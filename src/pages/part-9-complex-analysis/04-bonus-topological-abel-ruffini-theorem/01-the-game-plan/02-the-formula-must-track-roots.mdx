<SlideLayout title="The formula must track roots">

Any proposed quintic formula $F$ is a function of the coefficients. When two polynomials share the same coefficients, $F$ gives the same output — it can't tell them apart.

Now suppose we **continuously move** the roots $z_1, \dots, z_5$ along smooth paths in $\CC$. As the roots move, the coefficients (which are symmetric functions of the roots) also move. Since $F$ is continuous and depends only on the coefficients, it must continuously track one of the roots.

<Callout kind="remark" title="The key constraint">
Any continuous formula $F$ must maintain a consistent labeling of the roots as they move. Moving the roots along a smooth path forces $F$ to follow one root along that path — it cannot spontaneously jump to a different root.
</Callout>

This means $F$ induces a <Term k="rootPermutation">root permutation</Term>: as we rearrange the roots via smooth paths, $F$ must keep track of which root is which, giving a well-defined element of <Term k="S5">$S_5$</Term>.
