<SlideLayout title="The derived series">

We have reduced the problem to pure group theory. The question is whether repeatedly taking commutator subgroups of <Term k="S5">$S_5$</Term> ever kills the group.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Derived series">
For a group $G$, define the <Term k="derivedSeries">derived series</Term>:
$$G = G^{(0)} \supseteq G^{(1)} \supseteq G^{(2)} \supseteq \cdots$$
where $G^{(1)} = [G, G]$ is the <Term k="commutatorSubgroup">commutator subgroup</Term>, $G^{(2)} = [G^{(1)}, G^{(1)}]$, and so on.
</Callout>

Each $G^{(k+1)}$ is normal in $G^{(k)}$, and the quotient $G^{(k)} / G^{(k+1)}$ is abelian (it's the abelianization of $G^{(k)}$).

The topological argument tells us: if a quintic formula of nesting depth $d$ existed, then after $d$ steps of the derived series of $S_5$ we'd reach the trivial group. We need a permutation to survive all $d$ levels of commutators; if $S_5$ were solvable, there'd be no such permutation left at depth $d$, and the argument would fail to produce a contradiction.

But our goal is to show a contradiction **does** persist, meaning $S_5$ is **not** solvable — the derived series never reaches $\{e\}$.

</SlideLayout>
