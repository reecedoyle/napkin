<SlideLayout title="Why nth roots fix the problem">

Adding $\sqrt[n]{\,}$ to our toolkit breaks the rational-function argument. Here's why.

The $n$th root $\sqrt[n]{R}$ is **multi-valued**: for any nonzero $w$, there are $n$ values $v$ with $v^n = w$. When we pick a branch and move continuously, the branch might not return to where it started after a loop.

<Callout kind="example" title="Quadratic formula">
The quadratic formula gives $z_1, z_2 = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$. When we swap $z_1$ and $z_2$ smoothly, the coefficients $a, b, c$ return to their starting values — but the value of $\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}$ might travel along a path that ends at $-\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}$. That sign flip is exactly what causes the $+$ branch to become the $-$ branch, explaining why the formula can track the swap rather than being contradicted by it.
</Callout>

More precisely: as we move the roots, the expression $b^2 - 4ac$ (the discriminant) traces a path in $\CC$. If that path winds once around the origin, the <Term k="liftingPath">lifting</Term> of $\sqrt{\,}$ picks up a <Term k="phaseAccumulation">phase</Term> of $e^{i\pi} = -1$, swapping the two branches. The formula accommodates the root swap by switching branches.

</SlideLayout>
