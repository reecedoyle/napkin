<SlideLayout title="The cubic formula — a nested radical example">

The cubic formula provides a concrete example of a degree-2 <Term k="nestedRadical">nested radical</Term>. One of the key terms in the cubic formula contains:

$$\sqrt[3]{\frac{2b^3 - 9abc + 27a^2d + \sqrt{(2b^3 - 9abc + 27a^2d)^2 - 4(b^2 - 3ac)^3}}{2}}$$

Inside the cube root, there's a square root. This is nesting depth 2.

<Callout kind="example" title="Why the cubic formula works">
The cubic's Galois group (the symmetric group $S_3$) has derived series $S_3 \supset A_3 \supset \{e\}$, which terminates at the trivial group. So $S_3$ is solvable. The nested radical structure of the cubic formula corresponds exactly to this two-step derived series.
</Callout>

For the quintic, we'd need the derived series of $S_5$ to eventually reach $\{e\}$ — meaning $S_5$ would need to be <Term k="solvableGroup">solvable</Term>. But $S_5$ is not solvable. The derived series gets stuck at <Term k="A5">$A_5$</Term>, which is a simple non-abelian group.

That's the punchline — and it's the group theory step we verify next.

</SlideLayout>
