<SlideLayout title="Phase accumulation is abelian">

The saving grace for our argument is that **phase shifts commute**. This might sound abstract, but the key point is:

When we take an $n$th root $\sqrt[n]{R}$ and move through a loop, the value picks up a phase of $e^{2\pi i k/n}$ for some integer $k$ (depending on how many times the loop winds around the branch point of $R$). These phases are elements of $\ZZ/n\ZZ$ under addition — a **commutative** (abelian) group.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Phases add">
If operation $\alpha$ causes a phase shift of $\phi_\alpha$ and operation $\beta$ causes a phase shift of $\phi_\beta$, then doing $\alpha$ then $\beta$ causes a total phase of $\phi_\alpha + \phi_\beta = \phi_\beta + \phi_\alpha$. Order doesn't matter.
</Callout>

Now here is the key idea: while <Term k="phaseAccumulation">phase accumulation</Term> is abelian, **root permutations are not**. The symmetric group $S_5$ is non-abelian: $(12)(23) \neq (23)(12)$.

We will exploit this mismatch. By taking a **commutator** of two root-swapping operations, we can cancel all <Term k="phaseAccumulation">phase</Term> effects while still producing a non-trivial permutation of the roots.
