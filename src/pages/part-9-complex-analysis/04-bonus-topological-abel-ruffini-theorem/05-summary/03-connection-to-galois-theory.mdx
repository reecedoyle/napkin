<SlideLayout title="Connection to Galois theory">

The topological and algebraic proofs of Abel-Ruffini say the same thing in different languages.

In Galois theory, a polynomial is solvable by radicals if and only if its Galois group is a <Term k="solvableGroup">solvable group</Term>. The Galois group of the general quintic is $S_5$, which is not solvable. Hence no formula exists.

The topological proof makes the same claim but grounds it in geometry: the fundamental group of the space of quintic polynomials with labeled roots captures the root permutations achievable by continuous paths. The condition for a formula to exist is precisely that the appropriate quotient of this fundamental group is solvable — and it isn't.

<Callout kind="remark" title="The derived series connection">
The <Term k="derivedSeries">derived series</Term> in both proofs is the same object: $S_5 \supset A_5 \supset A_5 \supset \cdots$. The topological proof says "commutators correspond to nesting depth"; Galois theory says "radical extensions correspond to steps in the derived series." Same obstruction, different words.
</Callout>
