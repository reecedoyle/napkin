<SlideLayout title="Path dependence for non-holomorphic functions">

For a general complex function, two paths with the same endpoints can give very different integrals. This makes <Term k="contourIntegral">contour integrals</Term> path-dependent — just like a 2D vector field with nonzero curl.

<Callout kind="example" title="A path-dependent integral">
Consider $\Psi(z) = z - \Re[z]$ (which extracts the imaginary part). Let $\beta$ be the segment from $-1$ to $1$ along the real axis, and $\alpha$ the upper semicircle.

- Along $\beta$: $\Psi(z) = 0$ (the imaginary part of real numbers is $0$), so $\oint_\beta \Psi(z)\,dz = 0$.
- Along $\alpha$: a direct computation gives $\oint_\alpha \Psi(z)\,dz = \tfrac\pi 2 i \neq 0$.

The two paths give different results because $\Psi$ is not holomorphic — it involves $\Re[z]$, violating the complex structure.
</Callout>

This is the setup for the big theorem coming next: if $f$ *is* holomorphic, then all paths between two fixed points give the same integral. Equivalently, loop integrals are zero. That's the <Term k="cauchyGoursat">Cauchy-Goursat theorem</Term>.

</SlideLayout>
