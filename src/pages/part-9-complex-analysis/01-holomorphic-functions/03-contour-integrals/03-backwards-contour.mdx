<Callout kind="definition" title="Backwards contour">
For a contour $\alpha \colon [0,1] \to \CC$, the **<Term k="backwardsContour">backwards contour</Term>** $\ol\alpha$ is defined by $\ol\alpha(t) = \alpha(1-t)$. It traces the same path in the reverse direction.
</Callout>

The key relationship: reversing a contour negates the integral.

$$\oint_{\ol\alpha} f(z) \; dz = -\oint_\alpha f(z) \; dz.$$

This follows directly from the definition (substitute $s = 1-t$, noting the limits of integration swap and the derivative changes sign by the chain rule).

<Callout kind="remark" title="Why this matters">
If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are two paths from $A$ to $B$, then following $\alpha$ and then the backwards path $\ol\beta$ forms a **loop**. If the loop integral is zero (as Cauchy-Goursat will tell us for holomorphic functions), then $\oint_\alpha f\, dz = \oint_\beta f\, dz$. Path-independence is equivalent to zero loop integrals.
</Callout>
