<SlideLayout title="Proof outline — holomorphic implies analytic">

This section is optional — it sketches the proof that holomorphic functions equal their Taylor series. The steps are each intuitive.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Proof outline">
1. **Write down a power series.** Using Cauchy's differentiation formula, define $c_k = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma \frac{f(z)}{z^{k+1}}\,dz$. This gives a formal series $\sum c_k z^k$.
2. **Show the series equals $f$.** Use the Cauchy integral formula plus the geometric series to verify that $\sum c_k a^k = f(a)$ for $|a|$ inside the disk.
3. **Prove $f$ is analytic.** Knowing $f$ equals a power series in each disk, differentiate term-by-term to show all derivatives exist — completing the definition of "analytic".
</Callout>

Step 2 is the non-trivial step: we interchange an integral and an infinite sum, justified by the Weierstraß M-test (uniform convergence of the geometric series on the circle).

The hard part of Cauchy-Goursat is handled by first proving it for rectangles (via a "binary search" argument), then building an antiderivative $F$ with $F' = f$, and using the fundamental theorem of calculus for loops.
