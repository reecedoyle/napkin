<SlideLayout title="Stronger than real differentiability">

For a real function $f \colon \RR \to \RR$, the limit $\lim_{h \to 0} (f(x_0+h)-f(x_0))/h$ only needs to agree from two directions: $h \to 0^+$ and $h \to 0^-$ (left and right).

For a <Term k="complexDifferentiable">complex derivative</Term>, $h$ approaches zero as a complex number — from infinitely many directions simultaneously. All must give the same limit.

<Callout kind="example" title="Conjugation is NOT holomorphic">
Let $f(z) = \ol z$ (complex conjugation). At $z_0 = 0$:
$$\frac{f(h) - f(0)}{h} = \frac{\ol h}{h}.$$
If $h = r \in \RR^+$, this ratio equals $r/r = 1$.
If $h = ri$ (purely imaginary), this ratio equals $\overline{ri}/(ri) = -ri/(ri) = -1$.
Two different directions give two different limits — the derivative does not exist.
</Callout>
