<SlideLayout title="What Cauchy-Goursat gives us">

<Term k="cauchyGoursat">Cauchy-Goursat</Term> unifies two ideas:

1. **Loop integrals are zero.** For holomorphic $f$ on a simply connected domain, any closed contour integrates to $0$.
2. **Path independence.** Any two paths from $A$ to $B$ in the domain give the same integral.

This is exactly the complex analogue of a conservative vector field: $f$ has a complex antiderivative $F$ (with $F' = f$) on $\Omega$, and the integral from $A$ to $B$ equals $F(B) - F(A)$.

We also get the consistency check: $\oint_\gamma z^m\,dz = 0$ for $m \geq 0$, since those are polynomials (holomorphic everywhere), and they check out against our explicit computation.
