<SlideLayout title="Cauchy-Goursat theorem">

We saw that the non-holomorphic function $\Psi(z) = z - \Re[z]$ gives path-dependent integrals. The miracle is that <Term k="holomorphic">holomorphic functions</Term> never have this problem.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Cauchy-Goursat theorem">
Let $f \colon \Omega \to \CC$ be holomorphic, where $\Omega$ is an open, <Term k="simplyConnectedOpen">simply connected</Term> subset of $\CC$. Then for any loop $\gamma$ in $\Omega$,
$$\oint_\gamma f(z) \; dz = 0.$$
</Callout>

Equivalently: if $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are any two paths from $A$ to $B$ inside $\Omega$, then $\oint_\alpha f\,dz = \oint_\beta f\,dz$.

The proof goes via Green's theorem (assuming continuous partials on $f$); Goursat's contribution was removing that extra assumption, proving the full theorem from holomorphicity alone.

</SlideLayout>
