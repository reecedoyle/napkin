<SlideLayout title="What Cauchy's formula means">

<Term k="cauchyIntegralFormula">Cauchy's integral formula</Term> says: knowing $f$ on a circle determines $f$ at every interior point. This is a fundamental rigidity property.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Consequences">
- **Boundary determines interior.** To find $f(a)$, you integrate $f(z)/(z-a)$ around a boundary circle.
- **No freedom inside.** Unlike real functions, a holomorphic $f$ cannot be freely modified inside a disk while keeping the boundary values fixed.
- **Higher derivatives too.** Differentiating the formula with respect to $a$ gives formulas for all derivatives $f^{(k)}(a)$ in terms of a contour integral — these form Cauchy's differentiation formula.
</Callout>

The formula generalizes: the circle can be any curve bounding a simply connected region. It does not require the domain $U$ to be simply connected — only the disk $D$ used needs to fit inside $U$.
