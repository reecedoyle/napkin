<SlideLayout title="Proof strategy — the keyhole contour">

The proof of Cauchy's integral formula is a clever application of Cauchy-Goursat. The idea: replace the big circle $\gamma$ around $a$ with a tiny circle $\gamma_\eps$ centered at $a$, then compute the small integral directly.

**Step 1: Construct a <Term k="keyholeContour">keyhole contour</Term> $\Gamma_{\delta,\eps}$.** This consists of the outer circle $\gamma$, an inner circle $\ol{\gamma_\eps}$ (centered at $a$, radius $\eps$, run clockwise), and two narrow corridors connecting them.

**Step 2:** The function $f(z)/(z-a)$ is holomorphic on $\Gamma_{\delta,\eps}$ (the point $a$ is in the inner hole, not on the contour). By Cauchy-Goursat, the integral over the entire keyhole is $0$.

**Step 3:** As the corridor width $\delta \to 0$, the two walls cancel (they run in opposite directions and $f$ is continuous). We're left with:
$$\oint_\gamma \frac{f(z)}{z-a}\,dz = \oint_{\gamma_\eps} \frac{f(z)}{z-a}\,dz.$$

We've replaced the big circle with a tiny one — and now the tiny integral is easy to control.
