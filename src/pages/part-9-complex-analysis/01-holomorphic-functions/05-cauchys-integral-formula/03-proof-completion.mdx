<SlideLayout title="Proof completion — the ML lemma">

We have reduced to showing $\oint_{\gamma_\eps} f(z)/(z-a)\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot f(a)$.

Split the integrand:
$$\oint_{\gamma_\eps} \frac{f(z)}{z-a}\,dz = \oint_{\gamma_\eps} \frac{f(z)-f(a)}{z-a}\,dz + f(a)\oint_{\gamma_\eps} \frac{1}{z-a}\,dz.$$

The second integral equals $2\pi i$ (by our unit-circle computation, shifted to center $a$). So we need the first integral to vanish as $\eps \to 0$.

<Callout kind="lemma" title="ML estimation lemma">
If $|f(z)| \leq M$ on a contour of length $L$, then $\left|\oint f(z)\,dz\right| \leq ML$.
</Callout>

As $\eps \to 0$, the quotient $(f(z)-f(a))/(z-a)$ approaches $f'(a)$ — so it is bounded by some $M$ for small $\eps$. But the length of $\gamma_\eps$ is $2\pi\eps$, so:
$$\left|\oint_{\gamma_\eps} \frac{f(z)-f(a)}{z-a}\,dz\right| \leq M \cdot 2\pi\eps \to 0.$$

Therefore $\oint_\gamma f(z)/(z-a)\,dz = 2\pi i \cdot f(a)$, which is <Term k="cauchyIntegralFormula">Cauchy's integral formula</Term>.

</SlideLayout>
