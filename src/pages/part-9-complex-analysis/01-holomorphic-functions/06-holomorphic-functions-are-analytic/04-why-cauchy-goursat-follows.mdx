<SlideLayout title="Why Cauchy-Goursat is plausible from Taylor series">

Now that we know holomorphic functions equal their <Term k="taylorSeries">Taylor series</Term>, we can see why <Term k="cauchyGoursat">Cauchy-Goursat</Term> should hold.

Assuming $f(z) = c_0 + c_1 z + c_2 z^2 + \cdots$, integrate around the unit circle $\gamma$:
$$\oint_\gamma f(z)\,dz = \oint_\gamma c_0\,dz + \oint_\gamma c_1 z\,dz + \oint_\gamma c_2 z^2\,dz + \cdots$$

We already know $\oint_\gamma z^m\,dz = 0$ for every integer $m \geq 0$. So every term in the series contributes $0$, giving the total integral $0$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Caveat">
This argument is not fully rigorous: it swaps the infinite sum and the integral without justification. Making that swap precise requires the Weierstraß M-test. But the heuristic captures why the theorem is true: term-by-term the integrals all vanish.
</Callout>
