<SlideLayout title="Surprising consequences">

Once we establish the two headline theorems, many striking results follow as corollaries.

<Callout kind="remark" title="What falls out for free">

- **Boundary determines interior.** Knowing $f$ on the boundary circle of a disk tells you $f$ everywhere inside.
- **Isolated values determine everything.** Knowing $f(1)$, $f(1/2)$, $f(1/3)$, … is enough to pin down the entire function on its domain.
- **Bounded entire functions are constant.** A <Term k="entireFunction">holomorphic function</Term> $f \colon \CC \to \CC$ that stays bounded must be constant (Liouville's theorem).

</Callout>

All of this flows from the Taylor series representation, which itself follows from the <Term k="cauchyIntegralFormula">Cauchy integral formula</Term>. The path through this chapter:

$$\text{holomorphic} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{Cauchy-Goursat} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{Cauchy integral formula} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{analytic (= Taylor series)}$$

Each arrow is a nontrivial theorem; each is surprisingly tractable once we have the right setup.

</SlideLayout>
