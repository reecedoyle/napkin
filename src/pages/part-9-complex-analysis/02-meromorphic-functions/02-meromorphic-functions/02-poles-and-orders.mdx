<SlideLayout title="Poles, orders, and residues">

Given the Laurent series at a pole $p$:

$$f(z) = \frac{c_{-m}}{(z-p)^m} + \cdots + \frac{c_{-1}}{z-p} + c_0 + c_1(z-p) + \cdots$$

<Callout kind="definition" title="Order and residue">
The integer $m$ is the <Term k="poleOrder">**order**</Term> of the pole. A pole of order 1 is called a <Term k="simplePole">**simple pole**</Term>.

The coefficient $c_{-1}$ is the <Term k="residue">**residue**</Term> of $f$ at $p$, written $\operatorname{Res}(f; p)$.
</Callout>

Poles are the "opposite" of <Term k="zeroMultiplicity">zeros</Term>. A zero of multiplicity $k$ at $p$ means $f$ looks like $c_k(z-p)^k + \cdots$. A pole of order $m$ means $f$ looks like $c_{-m}(z-p)^{-m} + \cdots$. Orders are **additive**: if $f$ has a pole of order 5 and $g$ has a zero of multiplicity 3, then $fg$ has a pole of order $5-3=2$.
