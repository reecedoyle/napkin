<SlideLayout title="Principal and analytic parts">

The Laurent series splits into two pieces at a pole $p$:

$$f(z) = \underbrace{\frac{c_{-m}}{(z-p)^m} + \cdots + \frac{c_{-1}}{z-p}}_{\text{principal part}} + \underbrace{c_0 + c_1(z-p) + \cdots}_{\text{analytic part}}$$

<Callout kind="definition" title="Principal and analytic parts">
The <Term k="principalPart">**principal part**</Term> carries all the singular behaviour. The **analytic part** is a regular power series (Taylor series) and is holomorphic near $p$.

The Laurent series is unique and converges in any punctured disk centred at $p$ that contains no other poles.
</Callout>

**Why this matters:** when we integrate $f$ around a loop, the analytic part contributes $0$ (Cauchy–Goursat). Only the principal part — and specifically the $c_{-1}/(z-p)$ term — contributes to the integral. That coefficient is the residue.
