<SlideLayout title="Meromorphic functions — definition">

Let $U$ be an open subset of <Term k="CC">$\mathbb{C}$</Term>.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Meromorphic function">
A function $f \colon U \to \mathbb{C}$ is <Term k="meromorphic">**meromorphic**</Term> if there exist holomorphic functions $A, B \colon U \to \mathbb{C}$ with $B$ not identically zero on any open neighbourhood, such that $f(z) = A(z)/B(z)$ whenever $B(z) \neq 0$.
</Callout>

**Equivalent view.** A meromorphic function is one that is holomorphic on $U$ except at an isolated set $S$ of <Term k="pole">**poles**</Term>, and at each pole $p \in S$ it admits a <Term k="laurentSeries">**Laurent series**</Term>:

$$f(z) = \frac{c_{-m}}{(z-p)^m} + \frac{c_{-m+1}}{(z-p)^{m-1}} + \dots + \frac{c_{-1}}{z-p} + c_0 + c_1(z-p) + \dots$$

with $m \geq 1$ and $c_{-m} \neq 0$. The series has finitely many negative-power terms but infinitely many non-negative ones.

</SlideLayout>
