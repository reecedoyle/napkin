<SlideLayout title="Cauchy's residue theorem">

A loop $\gamma$ is <Term k="regularCurve">**regular**</Term> if $\operatorname{Wind}(\gamma, p) = 1$ for every interior point $p$ — a counterclockwise simple closed curve, like a circle traversed once.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Cauchy's residue theorem">
Let $f \colon \Omega \to \mathbb{C}$ be <Term k="meromorphic">meromorphic</Term>, where $\Omega$ is <Term k="simplyConnected">simply connected</Term>. For any loop $\gamma$ not passing through any pole:

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_{\gamma} f(z) \, dz = \sum_{\text{pole } p} \operatorname{Wind}(\gamma, p) \cdot \operatorname{Res}(f; p)$$

In particular, if $\gamma$ is regular, this equals the sum of <Term k="residue">residues</Term> at all poles **inside** $\gamma$.
</Callout>

This is the engine of complex analysis. A closed contour integral — which looks like it should sum to zero — actually extracts the residues at every enclosed pole, weighted by how many times the loop winds around each one.

</SlideLayout>
