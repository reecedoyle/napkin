<SlideLayout title="Winding and zero multiplicity">

Consider the simplest case: $f(z) = z^n$. If $z$ traces a circle once counterclockwise, then $z^n$ traces the **same** circle $n$ times — so $\operatorname{Wind}(f \circ \gamma, 0) = n$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="The multiplicity–winding connection">
If $z$ moves around the origin once, $z^n$ winds around the origin $n$ times. Similarly, $z^{-n}$ winds $n$ times in the **opposite** direction. This is the geometric heart of the argument principle:

- A zero of multiplicity $k$ at a point contributes $+k$ to $\operatorname{Wind}(f \circ \gamma, 0)$.
- A pole of order $m$ contributes $-m$.
</Callout>

So $\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma f'/f \, dz = \operatorname{Wind}(f \circ \gamma, 0) = Z - P$. The algebraic count of zeros minus poles equals the geometric winding of the image curve.
