<SlideLayout title="The two-root problem">

Over the reals, $\sqrt{x}$ is unambiguous for $x \ge 0$: we just take the **positive** root. But over <Term k="CC">$\CC$</Term>, there is no notion of "positive," so we need a different strategy.

Every nonzero complex number $z$ has **exactly two** square roots: if $w^2 = z$ then $(-w)^2 = z$ as well. Picking one of the two consistently — so that the choice varies smoothly as $z$ moves — turns out to be surprisingly subtle.

<Callout kind="question" title="The central question">
Given a <Term k="holomorphic">holomorphic</Term> function $f \colon U \to \CC$, when does a holomorphic $g \colon U \to \CC$ exist with $f(z) = g(z)^2$ for all $z \in U$? More generally, when does an $n$th root exist?
</Callout>

The answer involves a beautiful mix of complex analysis and topology — specifically, the idea of a <Term k="liftingThm">lifting</Term> through a <Term k="coveringProjection">covering projection</Term>.
