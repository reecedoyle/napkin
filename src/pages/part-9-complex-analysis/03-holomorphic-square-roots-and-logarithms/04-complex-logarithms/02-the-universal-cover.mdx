<SlideLayout title="The universal cover and trivial π₁">

For the $n$th root, the covering space was $p = (-)^n : \CC \setminus \{0\} \to \CC \setminus \{0\}$ and we needed loops to wind in multiples of $n$. For the logarithm, the covering is different:

$$\exp \colon \CC \longrightarrow \CC \setminus \{0\}.$$

Since $\CC$ is simply connected — it has trivial <Term k="fundamentalGroup">fundamental group</Term> — this is the <Term k="universalCover">universal cover</Term> of the punctured plane. So $p_*(\pi_1(\CC))$ is the trivial group $\{0\} \subseteq \ZZ = \pi_1(\CC \setminus \{0\})$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="The condition tightens">
For the square root, we needed winding numbers divisible by $2$. For the logarithm, we need winding numbers to be zero — **no winding at all** is allowed. This is a strictly stronger condition.
</Callout>

Concretely: if $f$ has any zero, a logarithm cannot exist (since $f \circ \gamma$ would wind around $0$ for a small loop around the zero). And even among zero-free functions, the image curve must wind zero times around the origin for every loop.

</SlideLayout>
