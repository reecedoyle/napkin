<SlideLayout title="Monodromy and winding numbers">

When we transport a square root around a loop $\gamma$ in $U$, the root either returns to itself (success) or picks up a sign flip (failure). The loop succeeds if and only if $f \circ \gamma$ winds around $0 \in \CC$ an **even** number of times.

The <Term k="windingNumber">winding number</Term> of $f$ around $0$ along $\gamma$ is measured by the contour integral

$$\frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_\gamma \frac{f'}{f}\,dz.$$

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Existence of holomorphic nth roots">
Let $f \colon U \to \CC$ be holomorphic. Then $f$ has a <Term k="holomorphicNthRoot">holomorphic $n$th root</Term> if and only if
$$\frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_\gamma \frac{f'}{f}\,dz \in n\ZZ$$
for every closed <Term k="contour">contour</Term> $\gamma$ in $U$.
</Callout>

In other words: $f$ has an $n$th root exactly when every loop in $U$ sends the winding of $f$ around $0$ to a multiple of $n$.
