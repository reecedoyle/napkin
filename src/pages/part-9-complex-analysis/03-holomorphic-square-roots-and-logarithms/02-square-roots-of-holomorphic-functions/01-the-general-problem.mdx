<SlideLayout title="The general square root problem">

We now consider a more general problem. Let $f \colon U \to \CC$ be a <Term k="holomorphic">holomorphic</Term> function. We ask: does there exist a holomorphic $g \colon U \to \CC$ with

$$f(z) = g(z)^2 \quad \text{for all } z \in U?$$

<Callout kind="remark" title="Simplest non-example">
Taking $f = \text{id}$ (so $f(z) = z$) on $U = \CC \setminus \{0\}$: the motivation section showed this has no holomorphic square root, because any root must be discontinuous on some ray from the origin.
</Callout>

On the other hand, $f(z) = z^2$ definitely has a square root — namely $g(z) = z$. The question is: what exactly distinguishes these two cases?

The key is the behavior of $f$ near **zero**. If $f(z_0) = 0$, we must have $g(z_0) = 0$ too, and the situation near a zero can be controlled. The hard part is when $f$ is **nonvanishing**: does a consistent choice of $\pm\sqrt{f(z)}$ exist that varies holomorphically?

</SlideLayout>
