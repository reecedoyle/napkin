<SlideLayout title="Simply connected domains">

The winding-number condition simplifies dramatically when the domain has no holes.

<Callout kind="corollary" title="Nonvanishing functions on simply connected domains">
Let $f \colon \Omega \to \CC$ be continuous, where $\Omega$ is <Term k="simplyConnected">simply connected</Term>. If $f(z) \neq 0$ for every $z \in \Omega$, then $f$ has both a <Term k="holomorphicLog">holomorphic logarithm</Term> and a <Term k="holomorphicNthRoot">holomorphic $n$th root</Term> for every $n$.
</Callout>

**Why?** In a simply connected domain, every loop is null-homotopic — it can be shrunk to a point. That means any loop $\gamma$ in $\Omega$ has winding number $0$ around every point. So the condition $(1/2\pi i)\oint_\gamma f'/f\,dz = 0$ holds automatically.

<Callout kind="example" title="Open disks">
Any open disk in $\CC$ is simply connected. So: if $f$ is holomorphic and nonvanishing on an open disk, then $\sqrt{f}$, $\sqrt[3]{f}$, and $\log f$ all exist as holomorphic functions on that disk.
</Callout>

This corollary is the workhorse result in practice. The two previous theorems are the "when does it exist in general" answer; this is the "in the cases I actually care about" answer.
