Let us consolidate the chapter's main ideas with a concrete example.

<Callout kind="example" title="Square root of z(z−1)">
Consider $f(z) = z(z-1)$ on the domain $U = \CC \setminus [0,1]$, where $[0,1]$ is the closed unit interval on the real axis.

The zeros of $f$ are $0$ and $1$. Both are removed from $U$ by the cut $[0,1]$. On $U$, the cut forces any loop to wind *equally* around $0$ and $1$ — it cannot separate them — so $w_0 = w_1$ and the total $w_0 + w_1 = 2w_0$ is always even. By the $n$th root theorem, $f$ therefore has a holomorphic square root on $U$.
</Callout>
