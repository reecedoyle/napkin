Here's a covariant flavor of the same idea. Consider the forgetful functor $U \colon \Grp \to \Set$. It turns out $U$ is representable by $\ZZ$:
$$
\Hom_{\Grp}(\ZZ, G) \cong U(G), \qquad \phi \longmapsto \phi(1).
$$
Elements of $G$ correspond bijectively to homomorphisms $\ZZ \to G$, determined entirely by where $1 \in \ZZ$ lands. So "$U$ is represented by $\ZZ$" packages up something you already knew (a homomorphism out of $\ZZ$ is just a choice of one element) as an instance of the Yoneda machinery.

The general pattern: for a functor $X \colon \cA \to \Set$, a representation $H^A \cong X$ is a pair $(A, u)$ with $u \in X(A)$ satisfying a universal condition — for every $A'$ and every $x \in X(A')$, there's a *unique* $f \colon A \to A'$ with $(Xf)(u) = x$. Here, $A = \ZZ$ and $u = 1 \in U(\ZZ)$.
