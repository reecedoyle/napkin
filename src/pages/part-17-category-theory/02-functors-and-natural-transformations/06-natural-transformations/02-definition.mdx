<Callout kind="definition" title="Natural transformation">
Let $F, G \colon \cA \to \cB$ be two functors. A natural transformation $\alpha \colon F \Rightarrow G$ consists of, for each $A \in \cA$, an arrow $\alpha_A \in \Hom_\cB(F(A), G(A))$, called the **component** of $\alpha$ at $A$. These are subject to a **naturality** requirement: for every arrow $A_1 \taking{f} A_2$ in $\cA$,
$$
G(f) \circ \alpha_{A_1} = \alpha_{A_2} \circ F(f).
$$
That is, the square with corners $F(A_1), F(A_2), G(A_1), G(A_2)$ — sides $F(f)$, $G(f)$, and the two components $\alpha_{A_1}, \alpha_{A_2}$ — commutes.
</Callout>

Think of $\alpha_A$ as "the path $F(A)$ takes to get to $G(A)$," in direct analogy to a path-homotopy where each point $\alpha(t)$ gets deformed to $\beta(t)$. The naturality square is what upgrades "a bunch of arrows, one per object" into "a bunch of arrows that respect every relationship $\cA$ already has."
