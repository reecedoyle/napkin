<SlideLayout title="Natural isomorphism">

Suppose every component $\alpha_A$ of a natural transformation $\alpha \colon F \Rightarrow G$ happens to be an isomorphism in $\cB$.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Natural isomorphism">
In that case, the componentwise inverses $\beta_A \defeq \alpha_A^{-1}$ assemble into a natural transformation $\beta \colon G \Rightarrow F$ (naturality of $\beta$ follows from naturality of $\alpha$ by inverting both sides of each square). We call $\alpha$ a <Term k="fan67NaturalIsomorphism">natural isomorphism</Term>, write $F \cong G$, and say $F(A) \cong G(A)$ **naturally** in $A$.
</Callout>

This means $F$ and $G$ are "really the same" functor: not only is each $F(A)$ isomorphic to $G(A)$, but the whole family of isomorphisms is compatible with every arrow of $\cA$ at once — nothing was chosen ad hoc, object by object.
