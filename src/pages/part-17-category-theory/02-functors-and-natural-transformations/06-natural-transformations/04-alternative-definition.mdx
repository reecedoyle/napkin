There's an equivalent definition that looks much more like the path-homotopy picture from before.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Category 2, and natural transformations as functors out of 𝒜 × 2">
Let <Term k="fan67CategoryTwoPoset">$\mathbf 2$</Term> denote the category generated by the poset $0 \le 1$: objects $0, 1$, identities $\id_0, \id_1$, and one further arrow $0 \to 1$.

Then a natural transformation $\alpha \colon F \Rightarrow G$ (for $F, G \colon \cA \to \cB$) is the same data as a functor $\alpha \colon \cA \times \mathbf 2 \to \cB$ satisfying $\alpha(-,0) = F$ and $\alpha(-,1) = G$.
</Callout>

Concretely, the object-level data $\alpha(A,0) = F(A)$ and $\alpha(A,1) = G(A)$ recovers $F$ and $G$, and the arrow $(\id_A, 0 \to 1)$ in $\cA \times \mathbf 2$ gets sent to exactly the component $\alpha_A \colon F(A) \to G(A)$. Proving the two definitions agree is left as an exercise in the problems section — it hinges on the observation that every arrow of $\cA \times \mathbf 2$ factors as an arrow of $\cA$ composed with one of these "crossing" arrows.
