Right on schedule, products obey the same "unique up to isomorphism" pattern we saw for initial and terminal objects.

<Callout kind="proposition" title="Uniqueness of products">
When they exist, products are unique up to isomorphism: given two products $P_1$ and $P_2$ of $X$ and $Y$, there is an isomorphism between the two objects.
</Callout>

<ProofReveal
  client:load
  id="oam-prod-proof-uniqueness"
  prompt="Prove the proposition above."
  solution="Let P1 and P2 both be products of X and Y, with projections π¹_X, π¹_Y (for P1) and π²_X, π²_Y (for P2). Since P1 is a product and we have arrows π²_X: P2 → X, π²_Y: P2 → Y, the universal property of P1 gives a unique arrow f: P2 → P1 with π¹_X∘f = π²_X and π¹_Y∘f = π²_Y. Symmetrically, the universal property of P2 gives a unique g: P1 → P2 with π²_X∘g = π¹_X and π²_Y∘g = π¹_Y. Now consider f∘g: P1 → P1. It satisfies π¹_X∘(f∘g) = π¹_X and π¹_Y∘(f∘g) = π¹_Y — the same equations id_{P1} satisfies. By the uniqueness clause of P1's universal property (applied with A = P1, the pair of arrows being π¹_X, π¹_Y themselves), there is only one arrow P1 → P1 compatible with those projections, so f∘g = id_{P1}. The mirror argument gives g∘f = id_{P2}. Hence f and g are mutually inverse, and P1 ≅ P2."
