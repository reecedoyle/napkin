<SlideLayout title="Initial objects">

Certain objects in a category have special properties defined purely by the arrows in and out of them — no reference to their internal contents needed. The first example: an object that maps into everything, uniquely.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Initial object">
An <Term k="oam66InitialObject">initial object</Term> of $\mathcal{A}$ is an object $A_{\text{init}} \in \mathcal{A}$ such that for any $A \in \mathcal{A}$ (possibly $A = A_{\text{init}}$), there is *exactly one* arrow from $A_{\text{init}}$ to $A$.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" title="Initial objects, category by category">
- The initial object of $\catname{Set}$ is the empty set $\varnothing$: there's exactly one function $\varnothing \to S$ for any set $S$, the empty function.
- The initial object of $\catname{Grp}$ is the trivial group $\{1\}$.
- The initial object of $\catname{CRing}$ is $\ZZ$ — recall ring homomorphisms $R \to S$ must send $1_R \mapsto 1_S$, and a homomorphism $\ZZ \to R$ is then forced.
- The initial object of $\catname{Top}$ is the empty space.
- The initial object of a poset (as a category) is its smallest element, if one exists.
</Callout>

The prototype to hold onto: $\catname{Set}$ has initial object $\varnothing$ — there's a unique way to map "nothing" into anything.

</SlideLayout>
