<SlideLayout title="Monic maps">

"Injective" doesn't make literal sense in an arbitrary category, since arrows need not be functions — think of the poset category, where arrows are just instances of $\le$. We need a purely arrow-based substitute.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Monic">
A map $X \taking{f} Y$ is <Term k="oam66Monic">monic</Term> (or a monomorphism) if for any commutative diagram with $g, h \colon A \to X$ satisfying $f \circ g = f \circ h$, we must have $g = h$. In other words, $f$ is left-cancellable: $f \circ g = f \circ h \implies g = h$.
</Callout>

The idea: if post-composing with $f$ can never merge two distinct arrows into one, $f$ itself must not be "losing information" — which is exactly the spirit of injectivity, phrased with no reference to elements at all.

</SlideLayout>
