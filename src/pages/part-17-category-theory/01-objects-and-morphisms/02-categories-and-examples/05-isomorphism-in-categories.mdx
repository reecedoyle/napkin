<SlideLayout title="Isomorphism, categorically">

Now that we have a formal notion of category, we can finally define isomorphism in one shot, for *any* category at all.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Isomorphism">
An arrow $A_1 \taking{f} A_2$ is an <Term k="oam66CategoricalIsomorphism">isomorphism</Term> if there exists $A_2 \taking{g} A_1$ such that $f \circ g = \id_{A_2}$ and $g \circ f = \id_{A_1}$. We then say $A_1$ and $A_2$ are isomorphic, written $A_1 \cong A_2$.
</Callout>

This single definition specializes down to every isomorphism notion from the recap slide, depending only on which category we're working in.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Set">
In $\catname{Set}$, $X \cong Y \iff \left\lvert X \right\rvert = \left\lvert Y \right\rvert$ — two sets are isomorphic exactly when they have the same size.
</Callout>
