<SlideLayout title="The moral — arrows over objects">

Stop and appreciate what just happened. We defined whether two groups (or rings, or spaces) are isomorphic *solely* by the functions between them — never by peeking at what the elements of the groups actually are.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Moral">
One can learn about objects by the functions between them. Category theory takes this to the extreme by *only* looking at arrows, and ignoring what the objects themselves are.
</Callout>

This is one of the recurring themes of algebra generally, and category theory is what happens when you take it as the *entire* foundation rather than just a useful habit.

</SlideLayout>
