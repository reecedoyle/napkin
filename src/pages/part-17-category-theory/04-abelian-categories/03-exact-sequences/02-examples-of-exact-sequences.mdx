<Callout kind="example" title="Exact sequences">
The sequence $0 \to \Zc3 \overset{\times 5}{\injto} \Zc{15} \surjto \Zc5 \to 0$ is exact. More generally, $0 \to G \injto G \times H \surjto H \to 0$ is exact for any groups $G, H$ (here $0$ denotes the trivial group).
</Callout>

Two special cases are worth memorizing on their own, since they'll recur constantly:

<Callout kind="example" title="Exactness at the ends">
For groups, $0 \to A \to B$ is exact if and only if $A \to B$ is injective. Dually, $A \to B \to 0$ is exact if and only if $A \to B$ is surjective.
</Callout>

Both follow directly from the definition: exactness at $A$ in $0 \to A \to B$ says $\Img(0 \to A) = \Ker(A \to B)$, i.e. $\Ker(A \to B) = \{1\}$, i.e. injective. Exactness at $B$ in $A \to B \to 0$ says $\Img(A \to B) = \Ker(B \to 0) = B$, i.e. surjective.
