<SlideLayout title="Short exact sequences">

The prototype example from last slide, $0 \to G \to G \times H \to H \to 0$, is an instance of the single most useful shape an exact sequence can take.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Short exact sequence">
A <Term k="ac69ShortExactSequence">**short exact sequence**</Term> is an exact sequence of the form $0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="remark" title="Moral">
A short exact sequence is basically just an equation $C = B/A$.
</Callout>

Whenever you read "there is a short exact sequence $0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$," you can mentally translate it as "$C \cong B/A$." But there's a real difference: a group carries more structure than a number, so the sequence also records *which* maps realize this — the map identifying $A$ with a subgroup of $B$, and the map identifying $C$ with the quotient $B/A$.

</SlideLayout>
