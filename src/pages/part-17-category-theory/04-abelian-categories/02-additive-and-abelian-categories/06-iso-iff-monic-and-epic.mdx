One more sanity check that abelian categories behave the way "injective + surjective = bijective" would lead you to expect.

<Callout kind="proposition" title="Isomorphism ⟺ monic and epic">
In an abelian category, a map is an <Term k="ac69CategoricalIso">isomorphism</Term> if and only if it is both monic and epic.
</Callout>

We'll leave the proof aside — the Freyd-Mitchell embedding theorem, coming up next, will let us reduce this (and most other "obvious" facts about abelian categories) to a statement about $R$-modules, where "injective and surjective functions between sets" makes the claim immediate.
