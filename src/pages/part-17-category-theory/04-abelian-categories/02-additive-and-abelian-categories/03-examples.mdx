<Callout kind="example" title="Examples of abelian categories">
<Term k="ac69VectK">$\catname{Vect}_k$</Term> and <Term k="ac69Ab">$\catname{Ab}$</Term> are abelian categories, where $f + g$ takes its usual pointwise meaning. Generalizing both, the category <Term k="ac69ModR">$\catname{Mod}_R$</Term> of $R$-modules is abelian for any ring $R$. On the other hand, <Term k="ac69Grp">$\catname{Grp}$</Term> is not even additive, because there is no way to assign a commutative addition to pairs of homomorphisms between (possibly non-abelian) groups.
</Callout>

From here on, additive categories mostly recede into the background — essentially everything we do lives in an abelian category. Once you know a category is abelian, all the properties you'd hope kernels, cokernels, and images have actually do hold.
