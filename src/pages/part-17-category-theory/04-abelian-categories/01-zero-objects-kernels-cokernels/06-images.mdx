We now have both halves of the machine we need: a kernel construction and a cokernel construction. Composing them gives the categorical notion of image.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Image">
The <Term k="ac69Image">**image**</Term> of $A \taking f B$ is the kernel of its cokernel: $\Img f = \Ker(\coker f)$. This gives a unique map $\img f \colon A \to \Img f$ making everything commute, by universality of $\Img f$.
</Callout>

When it exists, $\Img f$ coincides with our concrete notion of "image." For a group homomorphism $\phi \colon G \to H$ with $\phi(G)$ normal in $H$, the cokernel is the honest quotient $H/\phi(G)$, and taking its kernel recovers $\phi(G)$ exactly — so $\Img \phi \cong \phi(G)$. This is precisely the well-behaved case flagged on the previous slide.

In $\catname{Ab}$ and $\catname{Mod}_R$, every subgroup is normal, so this subtlety never bites — the categorical image always agrees with the ordinary one.
