<SlideLayout title="The first isomorphism theorem, categorified">

Any exact sequence, no matter how long, turns out to break apart into a chain of short exact sequences. The building block that makes this work is a categorical version of a theorem you already know.

<Callout kind="proposition" title='"First isomorphism theorem" in abelian categories'>
Let $A \taking f B$ be a map in an abelian category. Then there is an exact sequence $0 \to \Ker f \taking{\ker f} A \taking{\img f} \Img f \to 0$.
</Callout>

Let's translate this back to the two running examples:

<Callout kind="example" title="Two familiar special cases">
In <Term k="ac69Ab">$\catname{Ab}$</Term>, this is essentially the first isomorphism theorem: $A / \Ker f \cong \Img f$. In <Term k="ac69VectK">$\catname{Vect}_k$</Term>, this amounts to the rank-nullity theorem — $\dim A = \dim \Ker f + \dim \Img f$.
</Callout>
