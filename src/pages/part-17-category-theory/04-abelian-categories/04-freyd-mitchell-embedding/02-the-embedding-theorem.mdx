<SlideLayout title="The Freyd–Mitchell embedding theorem">

Here's the theorem that makes abstract abelian categories tractable: they're never really as exotic as the axioms make them sound.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Freyd–Mitchell embedding theorem">
Let $\AA$ be a small abelian category. Then there exists a ring $R$ (with $1$, but possibly non-commutative) and a full, faithful, <Term k="ac69ExactFunctor">exact</Term> functor from $\AA$ into the category $\catname{Mod}_R$ of left $R$-modules.
</Callout>

Here a functor is <Term k="ac69FreydMitchell">exact</Term> if it sends exact sequences to exact sequences. The "small" hypothesis is a technical obstruction requiring $\obj(\AA)$ to actually be a set; we'll gloss over it, since it can almost always be worked around with enough set-theoretic care.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Moral">
You can basically forget about all the weird definitions that work in any abelian category.
</Callout>

Whenever you're faced with a statement about a general abelian category, it suffices to prove it in a "concrete" category where injective/surjective/kernel/image/exact all agree with the notions you already know — i.e. some category of modules.

</SlideLayout>
