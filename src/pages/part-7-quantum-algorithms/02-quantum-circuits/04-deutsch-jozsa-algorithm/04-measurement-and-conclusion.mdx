<SlideLayout title="Measurement and conclusion">

After discarding the bottom qubit, the top wire is in state $|x_+\rangle$ (if $f$ is constant) or $|x_-\rangle$ (if $f$ is balanced). We apply one final $H$ gate to distinguish them:

$$H|x_+\rangle = |0\rangle \qquad H|x_-\rangle = |1\rangle.$$

Then we measure in the $\{|0\rangle, |1\rangle\}$ basis.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Deutsch-Jozsa (n = 1)">
The circuit $|01\rangle \xrightarrow{H^{\otimes 2}} \xrightarrow{U_f} \xrightarrow{H \otimes I} \text{measure top}$ determines whether $f$ is constant or balanced using a single call to the oracle:
- If $f$ is **constant**, the measurement returns $|0\rangle$ (with certainty).
- If $f$ is **balanced**, the measurement returns $|1\rangle$ (with certainty).
</Callout>

Classically, worst-case requires $2^{n-1} + 1 = 2$ queries for $n = 1$. We just solved it with one.

<Callout kind="remark">
The general $n$-qubit algorithm applies $H^{\otimes n}$ to $n$ zero qubits, queries $U_f$, applies $H^{\otimes n}$ again, and measures all $n$ bits. The probability of measuring $|0 \cdots 0\rangle$ is exactly $1$ for constant $f$ and $0$ for balanced $f$.
</Callout>
