<SlideLayout title="Normalisation">

Two nonzero kets that differ only by a global scalar phase $c \neq 0$ are physically indistinguishable — no measurement can tell them apart. So we always normalise:

<Callout kind="definition" title="Normalised state">
A ket $\ket{\psi} = \alpha\ket{0} + \beta\ket{1}$ is **normalised** if
$$\braket{\psi|\psi} = |\alpha|^2 + |\beta|^2 = 1.$$
For an $n$-dimensional space, the condition becomes $|c_0|^2 + |c_1|^2 + \cdots + |c_{n-1}|^2 = 1$.
</Callout>

The coefficients $c_i$ are called **amplitudes**. The quantity $|c_i|^2$ will turn out to be the probability of observing outcome $i$ — which is why probabilities must sum to 1.
