<SlideLayout title="Simultaneous measurement of two qubits">

We can also measure both qubits simultaneously using a diagonal observable on the full $4$-dimensional space.

<Callout kind="example" title="Simultaneous readout">
For $\ket\psi = \alpha\ket{00} + \beta\ket{01} + \gamma\ket{10} + \delta\ket{11}$, define the diagonal observable $T$ by $T\ket{00} = 0$, $T\ket{01} = 1$, $T\ket{10} = 2$, $T\ket{11} = 3$. Then we observe $0, 1, 2, 3$ with probabilities $|\alpha|^2, |\beta|^2, |\gamma|^2, |\delta|^2$, and the state collapses to the corresponding basis vector.
</Callout>

This is the standard "computational basis measurement": read out both bits at once, getting a two-bit string with probabilities given by squared amplitudes.
