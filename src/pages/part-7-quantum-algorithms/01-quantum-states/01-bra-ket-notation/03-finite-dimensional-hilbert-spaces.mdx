<SlideLayout title="Finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces">

In the Fourier analysis chapter we worked with infinite-dimensional <Term k="hilbertSpace">Hilbert spaces</Term>. For quantum computation we only need the finite-dimensional case, which is much simpler.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Convention for this part">
Throughout Part 7, every Hilbert space $H$ is finite-dimensional. A finite-dimensional complex inner product space is automatically complete, so "Hilbert space" just means "finite-dimensional complex inner product space with orthonormal basis $\ket{0}, \dots, \ket{n-1}$."
</Callout>

The canonical example is $H = \CC^{\oplus 2}$: the two-dimensional complex space with orthonormal basis $\ket{0}$ and $\ket{1}$. This is the home of a single <Term k="qubit">qubit</Term>.
