When can we reliably tell two quantum states apart?

<Callout kind="proposition" title="Orthogonal states are distinguishable">
If two states $\ket{\psi}$ and $\ket{\phi}$ are **orthogonal** ($\braket{\psi|\phi} = 0$), we can find a Hermitian observable that tells them apart with 100% certainty — and measuring does not collapse either state.
</Callout>

The key observation: orthogonal states are eigenvectors of the same operator for **different** eigenvalues. Measuring that operator gives a deterministic outcome for each state — no probabilistic collapse occurs within each eigenspace.

In particular, the two computational basis states $\ket{0}$ and $\ket{1}$ are orthogonal, so measuring along $\sigma_z$ perfectly distinguishes them. That's why $\sigma_z$-measurement is the standard "readout" in quantum circuits.
