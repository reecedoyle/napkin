<SlideLayout title="Measurement example">

Let $H = \CC^{\oplus 2}$ and consider
$$\ket{\psi} = \frac{i}{\sqrt{5}}\ket{0} + \frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\ket{1}.$$

<Callout kind="example" title="Measuring along σ_z">
Take the observable
$$T = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}.$$
Its eigenvectors are $\ket{0}$ (eigenvalue $+1$) and $\ket{1}$ (eigenvalue $-1$). The state $\ket{\psi}$ is already written in this eigenbasis, so the Born rule gives:
- observe $+1$ with probability $|i/\sqrt{5}|^2 = 1/5$.
- observe $-1$ with probability $|2/\sqrt{5}|^2 = 4/5$.

After measurement the state collapses to $\ket{0}$ or $\ket{1}$ accordingly. The original amplitudes are gone — **measuring destroys information**.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" title="Measuring along the identity">
Take $T = \id$. Every vector is an eigenvector with eigenvalue $+1$, so we always observe $+1$ with probability 1. There is **no collapse** — no coefficients are destroyed, since we never distinguish which eigenspace we projected onto.
</Callout>

</SlideLayout>
