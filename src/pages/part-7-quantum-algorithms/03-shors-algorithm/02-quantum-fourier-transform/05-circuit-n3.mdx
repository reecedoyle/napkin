<SlideLayout title="The QFT circuit for n = 3">

For $n = 3$ qubits (so $N = 8$), the QFT circuit uses controlled-$R_k$ gates. Reading left to right:

1. Apply $H$ to qubit $x_3$. Then apply controlled-$R_2$ (control: $x_2$) and controlled-$R_3$ (control: $x_1$) to produce $\ket{0} + e^{2\pi i \cdot 0.x_3 x_2 x_1}\ket{1}$ on the top wire.
2. Apply $H$ to qubit $x_2$. Then apply controlled-$R_2$ (control: $x_1$) to produce $\ket{0} + e^{2\pi i \cdot 0.x_2 x_1}\ket{1}$ on the middle wire.
3. Apply $H$ to qubit $x_1$ alone to produce $\ket{0} + e^{2\pi i \cdot 0.x_1}\ket{1}$ on the bottom wire.

The output (bottom to top) is exactly the tensor product from the factorization proposition.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Controlled gates">
A controlled-$R_k$ gate applies $R_k$ to the target qubit only when the control qubit is $\ket{1}$. This is how the phase factor $e^{2\pi i \cdot 0.x_j \cdots x_1}$ accumulates contributions from multiple bits.
</Callout>
