<SlideLayout title="Extracting r via continued fractions">

We measure some $j$ with $j/N \approx s/r$ for some integer $s$. We don't know $r$ yet, but we do know $j$ and $N$.

The classical tool to recover $s/r$ from the rational approximation $j/N$ is the <Term k="continuedFractions">continued fraction algorithm</Term>. It finds the best rational approximations to $j/N$ with small denominators.

<Callout kind="example" title="Factoring 77: reading off r">
With $N = 2^{13} = 8192$ and measurement result $j = 4642$, we compute the continued fraction expansion of $4642/8192$. The first few convergents are:

$$0, \quad 1, \quad \tfrac{1}{2}, \quad \tfrac{4}{7}, \quad \tfrac{13}{23}, \quad \tfrac{17}{30}, \quad \tfrac{1152}{2033}, \quad \ldots$$

The convergent $17/30$ has a "reasonable" denominator. We guess $s = 17$ and $r = 30$.

Verification: $2^{30} \bmod 77 = 1$. Correct! The order of $2$ mod $77$ is indeed $30$.
</Callout>

Once we have $r$, compute $\gcd(x^{r/2} - 1, M)$: $2^{15} - 1 = 32767 = 77 \times 425 + 42$, so... we actually get $\gcd(2^{15}-1, 77) = 7$ or $\gcd(2^{15}+1, 77) = 11$ — yielding the factors $7$ and $11$ of $77$.

</SlideLayout>
