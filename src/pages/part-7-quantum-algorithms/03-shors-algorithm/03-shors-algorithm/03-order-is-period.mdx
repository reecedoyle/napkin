<SlideLayout title="Order-finding is period-finding">

The order $r$ of $x \pmod M$ is just the period of the sequence

$$x^0 \bmod M, \quad x^1 \bmod M, \quad x^2 \bmod M, \quad \ldots$$

This sequence is periodic: $x^k \bmod M = x^{k+r} \bmod M$ for all $k$.

<Callout kind="example" title="Order of 2 mod 15">
Computing powers of $2$ modulo $15$:

$$2^0 = 1,\; 2^1 = 2,\; 2^2 = 4,\; 2^3 = 8,\; 2^4 = 1,\; 2^5 = 2, \ldots$$

The sequence repeats with period $r = 4$. Since $r = 4$ is even: $2^2 - 1 = 3$ and $2^2 + 1 = 5$. Indeed $\gcd(3, 15) = 3$ and $\gcd(5, 15) = 5$ — we factored $15 = 3 \times 5$.
</Callout>

The plan: create a quantum state that encodes the periodic sequence $k \mapsto x^k \bmod M$, then apply the QFT to extract the period. The QFT is perfectly suited to this — it is designed to detect periodicity.

</SlideLayout>
