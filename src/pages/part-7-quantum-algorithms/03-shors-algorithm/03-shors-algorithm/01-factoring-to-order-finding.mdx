<SlideLayout title="Factoring reduces to order-finding">

Let $p, q > 3$ be distinct odd primes and $M = pq$. We want to find $p$ and $q$.

The key reduction is:

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Factoring via order">
If we can find the <Term k="multiplicativeOrder">multiplicative order</Term> $r$ of any $x \pmod M$ with $\gcd(x, M) = 1$, we can factor $M$ in polynomial time.
</Callout>

Here is why. If $r$ is even (which we can arrange), then $x^r \equiv 1 \pmod M$ means

$$(x^{r/2} - 1)(x^{r/2} + 1) \equiv 0 \pmod M.$$

Since $M = pq$, one of these factors is divisible by $p$ and the other by $q$ — as long as neither factor is $\equiv 0 \pmod M$. Then $\gcd(x^{r/2} - 1, M)$ gives us $p$ or $q$ directly, via the Euclidean algorithm.

The remaining question: does a suitable $x$ always exist, and can we find the order $r$ efficiently?

</SlideLayout>
