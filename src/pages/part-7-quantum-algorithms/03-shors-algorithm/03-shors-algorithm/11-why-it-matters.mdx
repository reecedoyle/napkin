<SlideLayout title="Why this matters — cryptographic implications">

RSA encryption relies on the assumption that factoring an $n$-bit integer is computationally hard. The best classical algorithm (the general number field sieve) runs in time

$$\exp\!\left(O\!\left(n^{1/3} (\log n)^{2/3}\right)\right)$$

— sub-exponential, but super-polynomial. Shor's algorithm runs in time $O(n^3)$ — polynomial.

<Callout kind="remark" title="The practical barrier">
Shor's algorithm requires a fault-tolerant quantum computer with thousands of logical qubits. A 2048-bit RSA key would need roughly 4000 logical qubits, each requiring hundreds of physical qubits for error correction — millions of physical qubits total. As of now, the largest quantum computers have only thousands of noisy physical qubits.

This is why the threat is real but not immediate — and why agencies began funding quantum computing research decades ago.
</Callout>
