<SlideLayout title="Why DIFT detects periodicity">

The whole point of the transform is this:

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Periodicity detection">
If the sequence $(x_0, \ldots, x_{N-1})$ is periodic with period $r$ — meaning $x_{j+r} = x_j$ for all $j$ — then the output amplitudes $y_k$ are concentrated at indices $k$ that are multiples of $N/\gcd(N, r)$.
</Callout>

Intuitively: a periodic signal has energy only at its fundamental frequency and its harmonics. The transform reveals those frequencies as large amplitudes.

We can think of the $y_k$ as measuring "how much $\omega_N^k$-frequency is present" in the input. A signal that repeats every $r$ steps excites exactly the frequencies that are integer multiples of $1/r$.

This is the bridge from periodicity to Fourier: if we can measure the output frequencies, we can read off the period $r$.
