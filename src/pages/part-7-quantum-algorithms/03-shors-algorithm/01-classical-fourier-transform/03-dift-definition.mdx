<SlideLayout title="The discrete inverse Fourier transform">

<Callout kind="definition" title="Discrete inverse Fourier transform (DIFT)">
Given a tuple of complex numbers $(x_0, x_1, \ldots, x_{N-1})$, its <Term k="dift">discrete inverse Fourier transform</Term> is the sequence $(y_0, y_1, \ldots, y_{N-1})$ defined by

$$y_k = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} \omega_N^{jk} x_j.$$
</Callout>

In matrix form this is a single multiplication:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} y_0 \\ y_1 \\ \vdots \\ y_{N-1} \end{bmatrix}
= \frac{1}{N}
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 1 & 1 & \cdots & 1 \\
1 & \omega_N & \omega_N^2 & \cdots & \omega_N^{N-1} \\
1 & \omega_N^2 & \omega_N^4 & \cdots & \omega_N^{2(N-1)} \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
1 & \omega_N^{N-1} & \omega_N^{2(N-1)} & \cdots & \omega_N^{(N-1)^2}
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} x_0 \\ x_1 \\ \vdots \\ x_{N-1} \end{bmatrix}.
$$

The transform and its inverse are related by:

$$x_k = \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} y_j \, \omega_N^{-jk}.$$

So the DIFT takes a signal $x$ and produces amplitudes $y$; the original signal is recoverable by the inverse.

</SlideLayout>
