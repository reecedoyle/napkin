<SlideLayout title="Why Fourier? The big picture">

Shor's algorithm factors an integer $M = pq$ in $O((\log M)^2)$ time on a quantum computer — exponentially faster than any known classical algorithm. This is arguably why agencies like the NSA have poured millions into quantum computing research.

The crux move is the <Term k="QFT">quantum Fourier transform</Term>. To understand it, we first need the classical version.

<Callout kind="remark" title="The central idea">
The Fourier transform detects **periodicity** in data. Shor's algorithm turns the integer-factoring problem into a period-finding problem, then uses the QFT to find that period blazingly fast.
</Callout>

Here is the chain of reductions:

1. Factoring $M$ reduces to finding the **order** $r$ of some $x \pmod M$.
2. Order-finding is a **periodicity** problem: the sequence $x^0, x^1, x^2, \ldots \pmod M$ repeats with period $r$.
3. The QFT extracts periodicity from a quantum superposition in $O(n^2)$ gates, where $N = 2^n \approx M^2$.

We build up from the classical case first.
