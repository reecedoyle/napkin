<SlideLayout title="Homology of the torus — statement">

Up to now, we've been lucky: some piece of every space we split has always been contractible, which makes the Mayer–Vietoris sequence collapse to isomorphisms immediately. The torus is where our luck runs out — and it's a much more instructive computation for it.

<Callout kind="proposition" title="Homology groups of the torus">
Let $X = S^1 \times S^1$ be the torus. Then
$$
\wt H_n(X) \cong \begin{cases} \ZZ^{\oplus 2} & n = 1 \\ \ZZ & n = 2 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}
$$
</Callout>

This time we'll have to actually pin down what the maps in the long exact sequence look like, rather than getting away with a zero on either side for free.
