<SlideLayout title="Short exact sequences of chain complexes">

We already know what a map of chain complexes is: a family of group homomorphisms $f_n \colon A_n \to B_n$ commuting with all the boundary maps. Now we impose exactness in every single degree at once.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Short exact sequence of chain complexes">
A map of chain complexes $0 \to A_\bullet \taking{f} B_\bullet \taking{g} C_\bullet \to 0$ is <Term k="tle71ChainSES">short exact</Term> if, for every $n$, the row
$$
0 \to A_n \taking{f_n} B_n \taking{g_n} C_n \to 0
$$
is a short exact sequence of groups — and these rows are stacked so that the boundary maps of $A_\bullet$, $B_\bullet$, $C_\bullet$ all commute with $f$ and $g$.
</Callout>

Picture it as an infinite tower of short exact rows, one for every degree $n$, with the vertical boundary maps $\partial_A, \partial_B, \partial_C$ commuting with the horizontal inclusions and projections.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Moral">
This basically says $C_\bullet = B_\bullet / A_\bullet$: apply "mod out by $A$" in every single degree, and the boundary maps descend automatically.
</Callout>

The whole rest of this chapter is driven by finding natural examples of such sequences, then converting them into information about homology groups.

</SlideLayout>
