We now check that cohomology has the same nice functorial behavior that homology did. First, let's name the categories involved.

<Callout kind="question" title="Define CoCmplx">
Define <Term k="sc74CoCmplxCat">$\catname{CoCmplx}$</Term>, the category of cochain complexes (objects: cochain complexes; morphisms: degree-0 maps commuting with $\delta$).
</Callout>

<Callout kind="exercise" title="Hom(−,G) is a contravariant functor">
Interpret $\Hom(-,G)$ as a contravariant functor
$$
\Hom(-,G) \colon \catname{Cmplx}\op \to \catname{CoCmplx}.
$$
This means: given a chain map $f \colon A_\bullet \to B_\bullet$, we naturally obtain a dual map $f^\vee \colon B^\bullet \to A^\bullet$ going the other way.
</Callout>
