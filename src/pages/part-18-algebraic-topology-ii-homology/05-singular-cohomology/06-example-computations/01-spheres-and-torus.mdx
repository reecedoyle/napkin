<SlideLayout title="Cohomology of spheres and the torus">

With the universal coefficient theorem in hand, computing cohomology from known homology is now routine — *provided* every relevant homology group is free, since then all the $\Ext$ terms vanish and only the $\Hom$ term survives.

<Callout kind="example" title="Cohomology groups of Sᵐ">
<Term k="sc74HnSm">$H_n(S^m)$</Term> is always free, so every $\Ext$ term vanishes and
$$
H^n(S^m) \cong \Hom(H_n(S^m), G) \cong
\begin{cases}
G & n=m,\ n=0 \\
0 & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" title="Cohomology groups of the torus">
Likewise <Term k="sc74HnTorus">$H_n(S^1\times S^1)$</Term> is always free (it's $\ZZ, \ZZ^{\oplus 2}, \ZZ$ in dimensions $0, 1, 2$), so
$$
H^n(S^1 \times S^1) \cong \Hom(H_n(S^1 \times S^1), G) \cong
\begin{cases}
G & n = 0,2 \\
G^{\oplus 2} & n = 1.
\end{cases}
$$
</Callout>

</SlideLayout>
