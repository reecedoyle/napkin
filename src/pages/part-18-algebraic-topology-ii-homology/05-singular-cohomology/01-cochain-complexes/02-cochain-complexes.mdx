<SlideLayout title="Cochain complexes">

A **cochain complex** is the same kind of gadget as a chain complex, just running the other way: the maps raise the index instead of lowering it.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Cochain complex">
A <Term k="sc74CochainComplex">cochain complex</Term> $A^\bullet$ is a sequence of abelian groups
$$
\dots \taking\delta A^{n-1} \taking\delta A^n \taking\delta A^{n+1} \taking\delta \dots
$$
such that $\delta^2 = 0$. We write superscripts instead of subscripts, and use <Term k="sc74Delta">$\delta$</Term> rather than $\partial$, purely as a notational signal that we're now in "co-" land.
</Callout>

Since $\delta^2 = 0$ still holds, we get to recycle the whole cycles-modulo-boundaries idea from homology, just with the arrows flipped.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Cohomology group of a cochain complex">
We define the <Term k="sc74CohomologyOfComplex">cohomology group</Term>
$$
H^n(A^\bullet) = \ker\left( A^n \taking\delta A^{n+1} \right) / \img\left( A^{n-1} \taking\delta A^n \right).
$$
</Callout>

This is *literally* the same formula as $H_n(A_\bullet) = \ker(\partial)/\img(\partial)$, with every arrow reversed. Nothing new algebraically — the content of this chapter is entirely about *where cochain complexes come from* and *what they mean geometrically*.

</SlideLayout>
