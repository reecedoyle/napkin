Not every cochain complex is a dualized chain complex. The oldest example predates this whole story:

<Callout kind="example" title="De Rham cohomology">
Let $M$ be a smooth manifold and $\Omega^k(M)$ the additive group of $k$-forms on $M$. Then
$$
0 \taking d \Omega^0(M) \taking d \Omega^1(M) \taking d \Omega^2(M) \taking d \dots
$$
is a cochain complex (with $\delta = d$, the exterior derivative — recall $d^2 = 0$). The resulting cohomology is <Term k="sc74DeRham">de Rham cohomology</Term>.
</Callout>

From here on, though, we set de Rham cohomology aside and focus entirely on cochain complexes built by dualizing — that's where the geometry we care about lives.
