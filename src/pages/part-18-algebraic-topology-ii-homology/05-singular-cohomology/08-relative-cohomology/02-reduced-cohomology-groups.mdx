We can also define reduced cohomology, mirroring reduced homology. One way: take the augmented singular chain complex
$$
\dots \taking\partial C_1(X) \taking\partial C_0(X) \taking\eps \ZZ \to 0
$$
and dualize it to get
$$
\dots \xleftarrow\delta C^1(X;G) \xleftarrow\delta C^0(X;G) \xleftarrow{\eps^\vee} \underbrace{\Hom(\ZZ,G)}_{\cong G} \leftarrow 0.
$$
Since the extra $\ZZ$ we tacked on is also free, the universal coefficient theorem still applies to this augmented complex — so this construction genuinely gives reduced cohomology groups.

However, since we've already defined relative cohomology, it's cleanest to just define reduced cohomology in terms of it:

<Callout kind="definition" title="Reduced cohomology groups">
The <Term k="sc74ReducedCohomology">reduced cohomology groups</Term> of a nonempty space $X$, denoted $\wt H^n(X;G)$, are defined as $H^n(X, \{\ast\}; G)$ for some chosen point $\ast \in X$.
</Callout>
