<SlideLayout title="Where does the formula come from?">

Here's the intuition behind the universal coefficient theorem, built up degree by degree.

Because $H^\bullet(A_\bullet;G)$ only depends on $H_\bullet(A_\bullet)$ (point 2, two slides back), if we're handed some homology groups $H_\bullet$ to realize, we can just construct **any** free chain complex $A_\bullet$ with that homology and dualize it — the answer won't depend on which one we picked.

<Callout kind="proposition" title="The Ext-only extreme case">
Suppose $H_k = 0$ for every $k$ except $H_{n-1} \neq 0$. Then <Term k="sc74TautologicalExt">$H^n \cong \Ext(H_{n-1}; G)$</Term> tautologically — because a free resolution of $H_{n-1}$ literally *is* a free chain complex with exactly this homology profile.
</Callout>
