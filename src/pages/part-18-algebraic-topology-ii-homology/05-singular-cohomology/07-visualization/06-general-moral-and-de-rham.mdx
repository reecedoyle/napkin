<SlideLayout title="The general moral, and de Rham revisited">

The Klein bottle computation generalizes completely:

<Callout kind="remark" title="Moral">
If a region $e^k \in C_k(X)$ has boundary $\partial e^k \in C_{k-1}(X)$ divisible by $n$, then we only care about the value assigned to $e^k$, modulo $n$.
</Callout>

That "modulo $n$" ambiguity, tallied up across all the regions of $X$, is exactly what $\Ext(H_{n-1}(X),G)$ measures.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Back to de Rham">
Compare with de Rham cohomology, from the very start of the chapter: there, $G = \RR$, which is a field. Dividing by any integer $n$ is always possible in a field, so "modulo $n$" ambiguities never survive — $\Ext(H_{n-1}(X), \RR)$ is always $0$. That's the geometric reason $\Ext$ terms only ever show up with non-field coefficients like $\ZZ$ or $\Zc p$.
</Callout>
