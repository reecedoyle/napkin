<SlideLayout title="Cocycles and coboundaries">

Recall the homology picture: $Z_n(X) \defeq \ker(\partial \colon C_n(X) \to C_{n-1}(X))$ are the **cycles**, $B_n(X) \defeq \img(\partial \colon C_{n+1}(X) \to C_n(X))$ are the **boundaries**, and $H_n(X) = Z_n(X)/B_n(X)$.

For this section, define the dual notions:

<Callout kind="definition" title="Cocycles and coboundaries">
$Z^n(X;G) \defeq \ker(\delta \colon C^n(X;G) \to C^{n+1}(X;G))$ — the <Term k="sc74Cocycle">cocycles</Term>. $B^n(X;G) \defeq \img(\delta \colon C^{n-1}(X;G) \to C^n(X;G))$ — the <Term k="sc74Coboundary">coboundaries</Term>. Once again $H^n(X;G) = Z^n(X;G)/B^n(X;G)$.
</Callout>

The names are less obviously geometric than "cycle" and "boundary," but they become clear when $G$ is a **field** (group operation = field addition):

<Callout kind="remark" title="Field-coefficient characterization">
When $G$ is a field: an $n$-cocycle is a cochain that sends every $n$-boundary to $0$. An $n$-coboundary is a cochain that sends every $n$-cycle to $0$ — though only in one direction: a coboundary always kills cycles, but (as we'll see with the Klein bottle) a cochain that kills every cycle need not itself be a coboundary.
</Callout>

</SlideLayout>
