Two quick computations using the lemma from the last slide: $\Ext(H\oplus H',G) = \Ext(H,G)\oplus\Ext(H',G)$, $\Ext(H,G)=0$ for $H$ free, and $\Ext(\Zc n,G) = G/nG$.

<Callout kind="example" title="Ext(ℤ^⊕2015, G)">
Since $\ZZ^{\oplus 2015}$ is free, $\Ext(\ZZ^{\oplus 2015}, G) = 0$ for *every* abelian group $G$ — regardless of what $G$ is.
</Callout>

<NumericInput
  client:load
  id="sc74-uct-num-ext-z2015"

  expected="1"
  explanation="Ext(H,G) = 0 for any free H, and ℤ^⊕2015 is free — so Ext(ℤ^⊕2015, G) is the trivial group, which has exactly 1 element."

Now the more interesting one: $\Ext(\Zc{30}, \Zc4)$. Using the lemma, $\Ext(\Zc{30}, \Zc4) = \Zc4 / 30\Zc4$. Since $30 \equiv 2 \pmod 4$, the subgroup $30\Zc4$ equals $2\Zc4 = \{0,2\}$, a subgroup of order $2$ inside $\Zc4$ (order $4$).

<NumericInput
  client:load
  id="sc74-uct-num-ext-z30-z4"

  expected="2"
  explanation="Ext(ℤ/30ℤ, ℤ/4ℤ) = (ℤ/4ℤ)/(30·ℤ/4ℤ). Since 30 ≡ 2 (mod 4), 30·(ℤ/4ℤ) = {0,2}, a subgroup of order 2 inside the order-4 group ℤ/4ℤ. The quotient (ℤ/4ℤ)/{0,2} has order 4/2 = 2, i.e. it's isomorphic to ℤ/2ℤ."
/>
