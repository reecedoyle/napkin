<SlideLayout title="Computing Ext">

Fortunately $\Ext$, despite its abstract definition, is easy to compute in practice — you just pick *any* free resolution.

<Callout kind="lemma" title="Computing the Ext functor">
For any abelian groups $G$, $H$, $H'$: <Term k="sc74ExtLemma">$\Ext$</Term> satisfies
- (a) $\Ext(H \oplus H', G) = \Ext(H, G) \oplus \Ext(H', G)$,
- (b) $\Ext(H,G) = 0$ for $H$ free, and
- (c) $\Ext(\Zc n, G) = G / nG$.
</Callout>

For (a): if $\dots \to F_1 \to F_0 \to H \to 0$ and $\dots \to F_1' \to F_0' \to H' \to 0$ are free resolutions, then $\dots \to F_1 \oplus F_1' \to F_0 \oplus F_0' \to H \oplus H' \to 0$ is too — apply $\Hom(-,G)$ degreewise and the direct sums pass through.

For (b): if $H$ is free, then $0 \to H \to H \to 0$ (identity map) is itself a free resolution — there's no room left for a nonzero $\Ext$ term to hide in.

For (c), take the free resolution $0 \to \ZZ \taking{\times n} \ZZ \to \Zc n \to 0$ and apply $\Hom(-,G)$.
