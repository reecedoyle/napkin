<SlideLayout title="Cohomology of a space">

Now for the geometric case we actually care about: dualize the singular chain complex $C_\bullet(X)$ of a space $X$.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Singular cochain complex">
For a space $X$ and abelian group $G$, define <Term k="sc74SingularCochain">$C^\bullet(X;G)$</Term> to be the dual of the singular chain complex $C_\bullet(X)$ — the **singular cochain complex** of $X$. Its elements are called **cochains**.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="definition" title="Cohomology groups of a space">
The <Term k="sc74HnXG">cohomology groups</Term> of $X$ are
$$
H^n(X; G) \defeq H^n(C_\bullet(X); G) = H^n(C^\bullet(X;G)).
$$
</Callout>

<Callout kind="remark" title="G-module structure">
If $G$ is also a ring (like $\ZZ$ or $\RR$), then <Term k="sc74GModule">$H^n(X;G)$</Term> is not just an abelian group — it's a $G$-module.
</Callout>

</SlideLayout>
