<SlideLayout title="Graded rings, and anticommutativity">

Two more names, layered on top of a graded pseudo-ring:

<Callout kind="definition" title="Graded ring">
A <Term k="aoc75GradedRing">graded ring</Term> is a graded pseudo-ring with a multiplicative identity $1$. If it is also commutative, we call it a **commutative graded ring**.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="definition" title="Anticommutative">
A graded (pseudo-)ring $R$ is <Term k="aoc75Anticommutative">anticommutative</Term> if for any homogeneous $r$ and $s$,
$$
rs = (-1)^{|r||s|} sr.
$$
</Callout>

The wedge product is the motivating example: it's exactly this sign rule. Note anticommutative and commutative are *not* opposites — a ring can be both, or neither, depending on what degrees its nonzero elements live in.

| Flavor | Need not have $1$ | Must have a $1$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No assumption | graded pseudo-ring | graded ring |
| Anticommutative | anticommutative pseudo-ring | anticommutative ring |
| Commutative | — | commutative graded ring |

  correct="b"
