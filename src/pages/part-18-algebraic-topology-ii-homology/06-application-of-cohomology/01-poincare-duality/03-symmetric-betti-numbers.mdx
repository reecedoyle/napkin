<SlideLayout title="Symmetric Betti numbers">

Recall the <Term k="aoc75BettiNumber">Betti number</Term> $b_k$ of a space is the rank of $H_k$ (informally, the number of independent $k$-dimensional holes, ignoring torsion). Poincaré duality forces these to be symmetric.

<Callout kind="corollary" title="Symmetry of Betti numbers">
Let $M$ be a smooth oriented compact $n$-manifold, and let $b_k$ denote its Betti number. Then
$$
b_k = b_{n-k}.
$$
</Callout>

This matches our warm-up: the torus ($n=2$) had ranks $1, 2, 1$, and $S^2$ ($n=2$) had ranks $1, 0, 1$ — both symmetric about the middle. The proof combines Poincaré duality with the universal coefficient theorem (which relates the *rank* of $H^k(M;\ZZ)$ to the rank of $H_k(M)$); we leave the full argument as a problem at the end of the chapter.

Since the corollary pins down $b_{n-k}$ once you know $b_k$, a single Betti number close to the "top" of the space tells you one near the bottom for free.
