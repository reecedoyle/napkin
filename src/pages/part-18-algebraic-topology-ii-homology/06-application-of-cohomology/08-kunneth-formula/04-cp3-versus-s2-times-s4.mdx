<SlideLayout title="ℂP³ versus S² × S⁴">

Time to resolve the second motivating question from the start of the chapter: telling apart $\CP^3$ and $S^2\times S^4$. Since $H_n(\CP^3)\cong H_n(S^2\times S^4)$ for every $n$, plain (co)homology groups can't distinguish them. The cohomology *ring* can.

We have $H^\bullet(\CP^3;\ZZ)\cong\ZZ[\alpha]/(\alpha^4) \cong \ZZ\oplus\alpha\ZZ\oplus\alpha^2\ZZ\oplus\alpha^3\ZZ$ with $|\alpha|=2$ — a graded ring generated by $1$ (degree $0$), $\alpha$ (degree $2$), $\alpha^2$ (degree $4$), $\alpha^3$ (degree $6$).

By Künneth, $H^\bullet(S^2\times S^4;\ZZ) \cong \ZZ[\beta]/(\beta^2) \otimes \ZZ[\gamma]/(\gamma^2)$, generated by $1\otimes1$ (degree $0$), $\beta\otimes1$ (degree $2$), $1\otimes\gamma$ (degree $4$), $\beta\otimes\gamma$ (degree $6$). Every graded piece matches the $\CP^3$ side in rank.

<Callout kind="example" title="Where the rings differ">
Squaring the degree-$2$ generator of $H^\bullet(S^2\times S^4;\ZZ)$: $(\beta\otimes1)(\beta\otimes1) = \beta^2\otimes1 = 0$, since $\beta^2=0$ already in $H^\bullet(S^2;\ZZ)$. But the degree-$2$ generator $\alpha$ of $H^\bullet(\CP^3;\ZZ)$ does *not* have this property — $\alpha^2 \neq 0$. So the two graded rings are not isomorphic, and $\CP^3$ is not homotopy equivalent to $S^2\times S^4$.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="remark" title="Moral">
The nontrivial $4$-cocycle $1\otimes\gamma$ of $S^2\times S^4$ is orthogonal to (unrelated to) the $2$-cocycle $\beta\otimes1$ — a genuinely different generator, from a different factor. But the $4$-cocycle $\alpha^2$ of $\CP^3$ *is* the cup product $\alpha\smile\alpha$ of the $2$-cocycle $\alpha$ with itself — the same shape of distinction we saw between $\CP^2$ and $S^2\vee S^4$.
</Callout>
