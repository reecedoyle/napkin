Here's the payoff: the two cohomology theories we just set up — singular cohomology with real coefficients, and de Rham cohomology built from differential forms — are the *same*.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="de Rham's theorem">
For any smooth manifold $M$, there is a natural isomorphism
$$
H^k(M; \RR) \cong \HdR^k(M).
$$
</Callout>

So the real cohomology groups of a manifold are literally governed by the behavior of its differential forms — an object you already have geometric intuition for from calculus.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Moral">
One can metaphorically think of elements of cohomology groups as $G$-valued differential forms on the space.
</Callout>
