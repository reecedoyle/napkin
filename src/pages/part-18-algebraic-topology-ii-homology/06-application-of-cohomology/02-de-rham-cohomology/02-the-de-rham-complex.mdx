Recall <Term k="diffFormOnManifold">differential forms</Term> on $M$: $0$-forms are smooth functions, $1$-forms eat a tangent vector and return a number, and so on. The <Term k="exteriorDerivative">exterior derivative</Term> $d$ raises degree by one and satisfies $d^2 = 0$, so we get a cochain complex:
$$
0 \to \Omega^0(M) \xrightarrow{d} \Omega^1(M) \xrightarrow{d} \Omega^2(M) \xrightarrow{d} \Omega^3(M) \xrightarrow{d} \cdots
$$

Its cohomology groups are exactly the <Term k="deRhamCohomology">de Rham cohomology</Term> groups $\HdR^k(M)$: <Term k="closedForm">closed forms</Term> modulo <Term k="exactForm">exact forms</Term>.

This is the differential-forms analogue of the cochain/cocycle/coboundary language you already know from singular cohomology:
$$
\text{Cochain} : \text{Cocycle} : \text{Coboundary}
\;=\;
\text{$k$-form} : \text{Closed form} : \text{Exact form}.
$$
