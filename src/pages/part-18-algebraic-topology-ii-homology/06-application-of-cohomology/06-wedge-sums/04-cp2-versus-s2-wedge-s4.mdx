<SlideLayout title="ℂP² versus S² ∨ S⁴">

We can finally resolve the first question posed at the start of the chapter: telling apart $\CP^2$ and $S^2 \vee S^4$.

We know $H^\bullet(\CP^2;\ZZ) \cong \ZZ[\alpha]/(\alpha^3)$, a graded ring generated by $1$ (degree $0$), $\alpha$ (degree $2$), and $\alpha^2$ (degree $4$). Meanwhile
$$
\wt H^\bullet(S^2 \vee S^4;\ZZ) \cong \wt H^\bullet(S^2;\ZZ) \times \wt H^\bullet(S^4;\ZZ),
$$
so the absolute ring $H^\bullet(S^2\vee S^4;\ZZ)$ is also generated by three elements: $1$ (degree $0$), $a_2$ (degree $2$, from the $S^2$ factor), and $a_4$ (degree $4$, from the $S^4$ factor). Every graded piece has the same rank on both sides, exactly as expected.

<Callout kind="example" title="Where the rings differ">
In $H^\bullet(\CP^2;\ZZ)$, squaring the degree-$2$ generator gives $\alpha \cdot \alpha = \alpha^2 \neq 0$. In $H^\bullet(S^2\vee S^4;\ZZ)$, squaring the degree-$2$ generator gives $a_2 \cdot a_2 = a_2^2 = 0$, because $a_2 \smile a_2 = 0$ in $H^\bullet(S^2;\ZZ)$ (we computed $\alpha\smile\alpha=0$ on any sphere two slides back) and the product-ring rule kills the cross terms with the $S^4$ factor too. So $\CP^2$ and $S^2 \vee S^4$ are **not** homotopy equivalent.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="remark" title="Moral">
The nontrivial $4$-cocycle $a_4 \in H^4(S^2\vee S^4;\ZZ)$ has nothing to do with the $2$-cocycle $a_2$ — they came from unrelated wedge factors. But the $4$-cocycle $\alpha^2 \in H^4(\CP^2)$ *is* the cup product $\alpha \smile \alpha$ of the $2$-cocycle $\alpha$ with itself. Same ranks in every degree, but a fundamentally different multiplicative relationship between the generators.
</Callout>
