<SlideLayout title="Relative and reduced cohomology pseudo-rings">

For $A \subseteq X$, the cup product also makes sense relatively:
$$
H^k(X,A;R) \times H^\ell(X,A;R) \to H^{k+\ell}(X,A;R).
$$

The reason this works: if a cochain vanishes on every chain living inside $A$, so does its cup product with anything — vanishing is "absorbing" under $\smile$.

This gives us two more graded structures:

<Callout kind="definition" title="Relative and reduced cohomology pseudo-rings">
The <Term k="aoc75RelativeCohomologyPseudoRing">relative cohomology pseudo-ring</Term> of the pair $(X,A)$ and the <Term k="aoc75ReducedCohomologyPseudoRing">reduced cohomology pseudo-ring</Term> (the special case $A = \{\ast\}$) are
$$
H^\bullet(X,A;R) = \bigoplus_{k\ge0} H^k(X,A;R), \qquad \wt H^\bullet(X;R) = \bigoplus_{k \ge 0} \wt H^k(X;R).
$$
</Callout>

Both are only **anticommutative pseudo-rings** — not full rings. The obstruction is real: it's common to have $\wt H^0(X;R) = 0$, so there's often no identity element at all to be found anywhere in $\wt H^\bullet(X;R)$.

</SlideLayout>
