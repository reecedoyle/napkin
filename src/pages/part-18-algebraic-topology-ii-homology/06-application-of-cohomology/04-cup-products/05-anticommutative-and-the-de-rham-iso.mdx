<SlideLayout title="Anticommutative, and matching de Rham exactly">

The cohomology ring inherits the same sign behavior as the wedge product, even though we just saw the cup product isn't literally the wedge product at the cochain level.

<Callout kind="proposition" title="Cohomology is anticommutative">
$H^\bullet(X;R)$ is an anticommutative ring: $\phi \smile \psi = (-1)^{k\ell}\psi\smile\phi$ for $\phi \in H^k(X;R)$, $\psi \in H^\ell(X;R)$.
</Callout>

We omit the proof (see Hatcher's *Algebraic Topology*, Theorem 3.11). And the isomorphism with <Term k="deRhamCohomology">de Rham cohomology</Term> isn't just an isomorphism of groups in each degree — it respects the whole ring structure at once:

<Callout kind="theorem" title="de Rham extends to a ring isomorphism">
For any smooth manifold $M$, the isomorphism of de Rham cohomology groups to singular cohomology groups is in fact an isomorphism
$$
H^\bullet(M;\RR) \cong \HdR^\bullet(M)
$$
of **anticommutative rings**.
</Callout>

So: if "differential forms" is how you should picture elements of a cohomology group, the wedge product is the correct way to picture the cup product.
