<SlideLayout title="Cohomology rings of the torus and spheres">

Let's compute a few cohomology rings — mostly without proof, but each is a nice sanity check on the machinery.

<Callout kind="example" title="Cohomology ring of the torus">
$H^\bullet(S^1 \times S^1; \ZZ)$ is generated by elements $\alpha, \beta$ with $|\alpha|=|\beta|=1$ satisfying $\alpha\smile\alpha = \beta\smile\beta = 0$ and $\alpha\smile\beta = -\beta\smile\alpha$ (plus an identity $1$). As a $\ZZ$-module,
$$
H^\bullet(S^1\times S^1;\ZZ) \cong \ZZ \oplus \left[\alpha\ZZ \oplus \beta\ZZ\right] \oplus (\alpha\smile\beta)\ZZ,
$$
of total rank $1+2+1=4$, matching the Betti numbers we saw at the start of the chapter. The elements $\alpha, \beta$ here are exactly the classes of "$dx$" and "$dy$" from the warning a couple slides back.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" title="Cohomology ring of Sⁿ">
For $n \ge 1$, the only nontrivial cohomology groups of $S^n$ are $H^0(S^n;\ZZ) \cong H^n(S^n;\ZZ) \cong \ZZ$, so $H^\bullet(S^n;\ZZ) \cong \ZZ \oplus \alpha\ZZ$ for a generator $\alpha$ of $H^n$. Now $|\alpha \smile \alpha| = 2n$, but $H^{2n}(S^n;\ZZ) = 0$ (there's no room for a class of degree $2n$ when $n \ge 1$), so $\alpha \smile \alpha$ must be $0$. Succinctly, $H^\bullet(S^n;\ZZ) \cong \ZZ[\alpha]/(\alpha^2)$.
</Callout>

Confusingly, $\ZZ[\alpha]/(\alpha^2)$ is *both* commutative and anticommutative here — because $\alpha \smile \alpha = 0 = -(\alpha\smile\alpha)$, the anticommutative sign rule holds vacuously.
