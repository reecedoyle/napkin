<SlideLayout title="Holes multiply">

We now build the **cross product** — a different kind of product from the cup product, this time combining classes from *two different spaces* into a class on their product space.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Moral, informally">
If $X$ has an $m$-dimensional hole and $Y$ has an $n$-dimensional hole, then $X \times Y$ has an $(m+n)$-dimensional hole.
</Callout>

This is true in most common cases, under a suitable reading of "hole" (whether you mean homology or cohomology). We'll formalize and prove a version of this statement over the next few slides.
