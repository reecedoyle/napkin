The last problem is the hardest — it pulls together functoriality, the product pseudo-ring rule, and the Künneth formula all at once.

<Problem
  client:load
  id="aoc75-prob-wedge-not-retract-of-product"
  prompt="Show that S^m ∨ S^n is not a deformation retract of S^m × S^n for any m, n ≥ 1."
  hint="Assume r: S^m × S^n → S^m ∨ S^n is such a map, with inclusion i: S^m ∨ S^n → S^m × S^n satisfying r∘i = id. Show that the induced map H^•(S^m ∨ S^n; ℤ) → H^•(S^m × S^n; ℤ) between cohomology rings is monic (injective), using that there exists an inverse-on-the-left map i."
  solution="Suppose such a retraction r existed, with i the inclusion satisfying r∘i = id on S^m∨S^n. Pulling back reverses composition order, so i*∘r* = id on H^•(S^m∨S^n;ℤ); in particular r* : H^•(S^m∨S^n;ℤ) → H^•(S^m×S^n;ℤ) is injective (it has a left inverse), and it is a ring homomorphism since it's induced by a continuous map. Let a be the generator of H^m(S^m∨S^n;ℤ) and b the generator of H^n(S^m∨S^n;ℤ). By the product-pseudo-ring rule for wedge sums, a and b come from different wedge factors, so a⌣b = 0. Since r* is a ring homomorphism, r*(a)⌣r*(b) = r*(a⌣b) = r*(0) = 0. But r* is injective and a, b are nonzero, so r*(a) and r*(b) are nonzero elements of H^m(S^m×S^n;ℤ) and H^n(S^m×S^n;ℤ). By Künneth, H^•(S^m×S^n;ℤ) ≅ H^•(S^m;ℤ)⊗H^•(S^n;ℤ), with generators α⊗1 (degree m) and 1⊗β (degree n) satisfying (α⊗1)² = (1⊗β)² = 0 and (α⊗1)⌣(1⊗β) = α⊗β, a generator of H^{m+n}(S^m×S^n;ℤ) ≅ ℤ. If m ≠ n, then H^m is generated by α⊗1 and H^n by 1⊗β, so r*(a) = k(α⊗1) and r*(b) = l(1⊗β) for nonzero integers k, l, whence r*(a)⌣r*(b) = kl(α⊗β) ≠ 0. If m = n, then r* restricted to degree m is an injective map ℤ² → ℤ² (basis a, b on the left; α⊗1, 1⊗β on the right), so it has nonzero determinant; and the cup-product pairing H^m × H^m → H^{2m} ≅ ℤ on the product is nondegenerate (its matrix in the basis α⊗1, 1⊗β is [[0,1],[(−1)^(m²),0]], of determinant ±1). A short check with this pairing shows two ℤ-independent degree-m classes can never have all three of u⌣u, u⌣v, v⌣v equal to zero — so r*(a) and r*(b) cannot all cup to zero. But in the wedge a⌣a = a⌣b = b⌣b = 0 (every positive-degree cup product vanishes there), and r* is a ring homomorphism, so all of r*(a)⌣r*(a), r*(a)⌣r*(b), r*(b)⌣r*(b) would have to vanish — a contradiction. Either way r*(a)⌣r*(b) ≠ 0 contradicts r*(a⌣b) = r*(0) = 0. So no such retraction r can exist: S^m∨S^n is not a deformation retract of S^m×S^n."
