Here's the payoff of the definition:

<Callout kind="proposition" title="Chain homotopic maps induce the same map on homology">
Let $f, g \colon A_\bullet \to B_\bullet$ be chain homotopic. Then the induced maps $f_\ast, g_\ast \colon H_n(A_\bullet) \to H_n(B_\bullet)$ coincide for every $n$.
</Callout>

It's equivalent to show $g - f$ induces the zero map on homology — i.e. that every cycle of $A_n$ becomes a boundary of $B_n$ under $g - f$. Take a cycle $c \in A_n$, so $\partial_A c = 0$. Then by the chain homotopy identity,
$$
(g-f)(c) = \partial_B(P_n(c)) + P_{n-1}(\partial_A(c)) = \partial_B(P_n(c)) + P_{n-1}(0) = \partial_B(P_n(c)),
$$
which is manifestly a boundary in $B_n$. So $(g-f)(c) = 0$ in $H_n(B_\bullet)$, i.e. $f_\ast(c) = g_\ast(c)$.
