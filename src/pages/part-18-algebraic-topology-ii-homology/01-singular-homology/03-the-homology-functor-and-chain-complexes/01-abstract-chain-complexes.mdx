<SlideLayout title="Abstract chain complexes">

We already know the homology groups are homotopy invariant, and we want to prove it — the same "song and dance" as building $\pi_1$ into a functor $\mathrm{hTop}_\ast \to \mathrm{Grp}$. This time, rather than working up to the category theory at the end, we'll start with it right away.

The first move is to strip away everything geometric and study the *algebra* of $C_\bullet(X)$ on its own terms — this works for any sequence of abelian groups (in fact for any category, not just abelian groups, though we won't need that generality).

<Callout kind="definition" title="Chain complex">
A <Term k="sh70ChainComplexAbstract">chain complex</Term> is a sequence of groups $A_n$ and maps
$$
\dots \taking{\partial} A_{n+1} \taking{\partial} A_n \taking{\partial} A_{n-1} \taking{\partial} \dots
$$
such that the composition of any two adjacent maps is the zero morphism. We denote this by $A_\bullet$.

The $n$th homology group $H_n(A_\bullet)$ is defined as $\ker(A_n \to A_{n-1}) / \img(A_{n+1} \to A_n)$. Cycles and boundaries are defined exactly as before.
</Callout>

This is just an algebraic generalization of the singular chain complex $C_\bullet(X)$ we already built, with all the original geometric content stripped away — a chain complex is any sequence of groups with a "$\partial^2 = 0$" map running through it.
