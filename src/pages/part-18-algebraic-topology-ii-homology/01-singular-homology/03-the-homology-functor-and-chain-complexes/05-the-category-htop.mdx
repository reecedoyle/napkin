Now let's state exactly what we're aiming to prove, in category-theoretic language.

<Callout kind="definition" title="The category hTop">
The category <Term k="sh70hTopCategory">$\mathrm{hTop}$</Term> has:
- Objects: topological spaces.
- Morphisms: *homotopy classes* of continuous maps $X \to Y$.

In particular, $X$ and $Y$ are isomorphic in $\mathrm{hTop}$ exactly when they are homotopy equivalent.
</Callout>

This is the same idea as $\mathrm{hTop}_\ast$ (used to build $\pi_1$ as a functor), just without basepoints.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Homology is a functor hTop → Ab">
For any $n$, $H_n$ is a functor $\mathrm{hTop} \to \mathrm{Ab}$. In particular:
- Given any map $f \colon X \to Y$, we get an induced map $f_\ast \colon H_n(X) \to H_n(Y)$.
- For homotopic maps $f, g \colon X \to Y$, $f_\ast = g_\ast$.
- Homotopy-equivalent spaces $X, Y$ have isomorphic homology groups: if $f \colon X \to Y$ is a homotopy equivalence, $f_\ast \colon H_n(X) \to H_n(Y)$ is an isomorphism.
- (Insert your favorite general fact about functors here.)
</Callout>

To prove this, we need to (1) turn a map $f \colon X \to Y$ into a map $H_n(f) \colon H_n(X) \to H_n(Y)$, and (2) show this doesn't depend on the choice of homotopy representing $f$'s class. Step (1) is easy — we did the analogous thing for $f_\sharp$ back when building $\pi_1$. Step (2) is the hard part, and it's where we go back to the chain complex $C_\bullet(X)$.

</SlideLayout>
