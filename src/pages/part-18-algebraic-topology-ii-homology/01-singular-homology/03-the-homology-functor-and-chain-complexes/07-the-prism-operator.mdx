<SlideLayout title="The prism operator">

Suppose $f, g \colon X \to Y$ are homotopic: there's a map $F \colon X \times [0,1] \to Y$ with $F(-,0) = f$ and $F(-,1) = g$. We want to build a chain homotopy $P_n$ turning every $n$-simplex of $X$ into an $(n+1)$-chain of $Y$.

Take a singular $n$-simplex $\sigma \colon \Delta^n \to X$, and feed it through $f$ and $g$ to get two "copies" of $\sigma$'s image sitting in $Y$. Picture the case $n=1$: $\sigma$ is a path, $f(\sigma)$ and $g(\sigma)$ are two paths in $Y$, and the homotopy $F$ connects endpoint to endpoint, tracing out a "square" region bounded by $v_0, v_1, w_1, w_0$ (where $v_i = f(x_i)$, $w_i = g(x_i)$ for the vertices $x_i$ of $\sigma$).

We split this square into two triangles $[v_0, v_1, w_0]$ and $[v_0, w_0, w_1]$ — that's our $2$-chain. Formally: take $\Delta^1 \times [0,1]$ (which really is a square), split it into two triangles, apply $\sigma \times \id$ to land in $X \times [0,1]$, then apply $F$ to land in $Y$.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Prism operator">
For an $n$-simplex $\phi = [x_0, \dots, x_n]$, set $v_i = f(x_i)$ and $w_i = g(x_i)$. The <Term k="sh70PrismOperator">prism operator</Term> is
$$
P_n(\phi) \defeq \sum_{i=0}^n (-1)^i \left(F \circ (\phi \times \id)\right)\left[v_0, \dots, v_i, w_i, \dots, w_n\right].
$$
</Callout>

This generalizes the picture above to any dimension: split $\Delta^n \times [0,1]$ into $n+1$ simplices, map into $X$ via $\phi \times \id$, then push everything into $Y$ via $F$. The alternating $(-1)^i$ signs make the "diagonal" internal faces cancel against each other, leaving only the outer boundary of the prism.
