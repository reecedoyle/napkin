<SlideLayout title="Chain maps and induced homology">

To compare chain complexes we need morphisms between them.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Morphism of chain complexes">
A <Term k="sh70ChainMap">morphism of chain complexes</Term> (or *chain map*) $f \colon A_\bullet \to B_\bullet$ is a sequence of maps $f_n \colon A_n \to B_n$ for every $n$ such that every square in the ladder
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\dots \to A_{n+1} \to A_n \to A_{n-1} \to \dots \\
&\dots \to B_{n+1} \to B_n \to B_{n-1} \to \dots
\end{aligned}
$$
(vertical maps $f_{n+1}, f_n, f_{n-1}$, horizontal maps $\partial$) commutes. Chain complexes and chain maps form a category, <Term k="sh70CmplxCategory">$\mathrm{Cmplx}$</Term>.
</Callout>

Since the squares commute, $f$ sends cycles to cycles and boundaries to boundaries: if $\partial_A(c) = 0$ then $\partial_B(f_n(c)) = f_{n-1}(\partial_A(c)) = 0$, and similarly if $c = \partial_A(d)$ then $f_n(c) = \partial_B(f_{n+1}(d))$. So:

<Callout kind="proposition" title="Chain maps induce homology maps">
Every chain map $f \colon A_\bullet \to B_\bullet$ gives a map $f_\ast \colon H_n(A_\bullet) \to H_n(B_\bullet)$ for every $n$.
</Callout>

  solution="Send each chain complex A• to its group Hₙ(A•), and send each chain map f: A• → B• to the induced map f∗: Hₙ(A•) → Hₙ(B•) constructed above (well-defined since f sends cycles to cycles and boundaries to boundaries, so it descends to the quotient Zₙ/Bₙ).
