Two more data points before we get back to the algebra.

<Callout kind="example" title="Contractible spaces">
Given any <Term k="sh70Contractible">contractible</Term> space $X$,
$$
H_n(X) \cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ & n = 0 \\
0 & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$
The reason: homology groups turn out to be homotopy invariant, just like homotopy groups (we'll prove this in the next section). So a contractible space has the same homology groups as a one-point space — and those are the ones above, with no interesting holes anywhere.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" title="Homology of the torus">
For the torus $S^1 \times S^1$,
$$
H_n(S^1 \times S^1) \cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ & n = 0, 2 \\
\ZZ^{\oplus 2} & n = 1 \\
0 & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$
The $n=1$ group matches $\pi_1(S^1 \times S^1) \cong \ZZ^2$ (already abelian, so Hurewicz changes nothing), and the $n=2$ group detects the torus's enclosed cavity — the same phenomenon we saw for $S^2$.
</Callout>

We haven't proven either of these yet — that requires machinery we build over the next two sections. But keep them in mind as target computations: everything from here is in service of actually being able to compute $H_n(X)$ for spaces like these.
