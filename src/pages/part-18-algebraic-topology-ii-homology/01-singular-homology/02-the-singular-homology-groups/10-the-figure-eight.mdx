Let $X_8$ be the figure-eight space (two circles joined at a point), with its two loops $a$ and $b$. Both $\pi_1$ and $H_1$ "see" these two loops — but they organize them differently.

<Callout kind="example" title="H₁ of the figure eight">
In $\pi_1(X_8, x_0)$, the loops $a$ and $b$ do *not* commute: $ab \neq ba$ in general, because the group operation is "concatenate paths," and concatenation order matters. So $\pi_1(X_8, x_0) = \langle a, b \rangle$, a free (nonabelian) group.

In $H_1(X_8)$, we add $a$ and $b$ *formally*, as a $1$-chain $a + b$ — chains are commutative by construction. So
$$
H_1(X_8) \cong \ZZ^{\oplus 2} \qquad \text{while} \qquad \pi_1(X_8, x_0) = \langle a, b \rangle.
$$
</Callout>

This is the Hurewicz theorem playing out concretely: $H_1(X_8)$ is literally the abelianization of the free group $\langle a, b\rangle$, which is $\ZZ^{\oplus 2}$.
