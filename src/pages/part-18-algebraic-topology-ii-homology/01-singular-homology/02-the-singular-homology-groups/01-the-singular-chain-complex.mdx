<SlideLayout title="The singular chain complex">

Let $X$ be a topological space, and let $C_n(X)$ be the free abelian group of $n$-chains of $X$ — exactly the object we built in the last section.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Singular chain complex">
The <Term k="sh70BoundaryOperator">boundary operator</Term> $\partial$ gives us a sequence of maps
$$
\dots \taking{\partial} C_3(X) \taking{\partial} C_2(X) \taking{\partial} C_1(X) \taking{\partial} C_0(X) \taking{\partial} 0,
$$
(writing $0$ for the trivial group, standard notation for abelian groups). We call this the <Term k="sh70SingularChainComplex">singular chain complex</Term> of $X$.
</Callout>

We already know $\partial \circ \partial = 0$ everywhere along this sequence. The whole rest of this chapter is about squeezing topological information out of that one algebraic fact.
