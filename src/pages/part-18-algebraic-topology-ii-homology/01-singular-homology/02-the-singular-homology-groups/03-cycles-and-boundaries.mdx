We can capture the annulus idea in any dimension.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Cycles and boundaries">
Given the singular chain complex $\dots \taking{\partial} C_2(X) \taking{\partial} C_1(X) \taking{\partial} C_0(X) \taking{\partial} 0$, we say $c \in C_n(X)$ is:
- a <Term k="sh70Cycle">cycle</Term> if $c \in \ker\left( C_n(X) \taking{\partial} C_{n-1}(X) \right)$, and
- a <Term k="sh70BoundaryGroup">boundary</Term> if $c \in \img\left( C_{n+1}(X) \taking{\partial} C_n(X) \right)$.

We denote the cycles and boundaries by $Z_n(X), B_n(X) \subseteq C_n(X)$ respectively. ($Z$ comes from the German *Zyklus*, since $C$ is already taken.)
</Callout>

Since $\partial \circ \partial = 0$, every boundary is automatically a cycle — a chain that's already the boundary of something has, itself, zero boundary. That inclusion $B_n(X) \subseteq Z_n(X)$ is the whole reason the next definition makes sense.
