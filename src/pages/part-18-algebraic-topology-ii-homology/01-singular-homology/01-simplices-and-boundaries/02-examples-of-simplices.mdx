Let's unpack what a singular $n$-simplex $\sigma \colon \Delta^n \to X$ looks like for small $n$.

<Callout kind="example" title="Singular simplices">
- Since $\Delta^0 = [v_0]$ is just a point, a singular $0$-simplex in $X$ is just **a point of $X$**.
- Since $\Delta^1 = [v_0, v_1]$ is an interval, a singular $1$-simplex in $X$ is just **a path in $X$**.
- Since $\Delta^2 = [v_0, v_1, v_2]$ is an equilateral triangle, a singular $2$-simplex in $X$ looks like **a "disk" in $X$**.
</Callout>

The arrows on $\sigma^1$ and around the edges of $\sigma^2$ aren't required by the definition — but they're a useful reminder that we've fixed an *order* on the vertices $v_0, v_1, v_2$. That order is exactly what will let us build a boundary operator in a moment.
