<SlideLayout title="The Mayer–Vietoris chain complex">

One last variant, useful when a space is naturally covered by two open pieces.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Chains subordinate to a cover">
Suppose $X$ is covered by two open sets $U$ and $V$. Define $C_n(U+V)$ to consist of chains where each individual simplex has image entirely inside $U$, or entirely inside $V$ (or both).
</Callout>

The usual $\partial$ restricts to give another chain complex on these,
$$
\dots \taking{\partial} C_{n+1}(U+V) \taking{\partial} C_n(U+V) \taking{\partial} C_{n-1}(U+V) \taking{\partial} \dots,
$$
and hence homology groups $H_n(U+V)$, built from exactly the same recipe as before — just applied to a smaller sub-complex of chains.

<Callout kind="remark" title="A near-miracle">
It will turn out that $H_n(U+V) \cong H_n(X)$: restricting attention to simplices that fit entirely inside $U$ or entirely inside $V$ loses no homological information at all. This fact is the engine behind the <Term k="sh70MayerVietorisComplex">Mayer–Vietoris sequence</Term>, one of the most powerful computational tools in the subject — we'll put it to work in the next chapter.
</Callout>

</SlideLayout>
