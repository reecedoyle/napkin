<SlideLayout title="Relative chain groups">

A second useful variant: instead of augmenting the complex, we can quotient it by a subspace.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Relative chain groups">
Suppose $X$ is a topological space and $A \subseteq X$ a subspace. Define <Term k="sh70RelativeChainGroup">relative chain groups</Term>
$$
C_n(X,A) \defeq C_n(X) / C_n(A)
$$
for every $n$ — so chains contained entirely in $A$ become trivial. The usual $\partial$ on $C_n(X)$ descends to a chain complex
$$
\dots \taking{\partial} C_{n+1}(X,A) \taking{\partial} C_n(X,A) \taking{\partial} C_{n-1}(X,A) \taking{\partial} \dots,
$$
well-defined since $\partial$ takes $C_n(A)$ into $C_{n-1}(A)$.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="definition" title="Relative homology groups">
The homology groups of the relative chain complex are the <Term k="sh70RelativeHomology">relative homology groups</Term> $H_n(X,A)$.
</Callout>

Intuitively, $H_n(X,A)$ measures holes in $X$ that are only visible once you're allowed to "cap them off" using pieces of $A$ — a chain that isn't a cycle in $X$ can become one modulo $A$, since its boundary might now land entirely inside $A$ and thus vanish in the quotient.

</SlideLayout>
