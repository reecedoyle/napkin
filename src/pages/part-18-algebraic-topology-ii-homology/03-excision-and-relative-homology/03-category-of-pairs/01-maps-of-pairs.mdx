<SlideLayout title="Maps of pairs">

We now have $H_n(X,A)$ instead of just $H_n(X)$. To get the same functorial setup we enjoyed for ordinary homology, we need a suitable category whose objects are <Term k="ear72PairOfSpaces">pairs of spaces</Term> $(X,A)$.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Map of pairs">
Let $\varnothing \neq A \subseteq X$ and $\varnothing \neq B \subseteq Y$ be subspaces, and let $f \colon X \to Y$ be continuous. If $f(A) \subseteq B$, we write
$$
f \colon (X,A) \to (Y,B)
$$
and call $f$ a <Term k="ear72MapOfPairs">map of pairs</Term> between $(X,A)$ and $(Y,B)$.
</Callout>

Concretely: $f$ must send every point of $A$ into $B$ — it's an ordinary continuous map $X \to Y$ with one extra constraint on where $A$ is allowed to land.

</SlideLayout>
