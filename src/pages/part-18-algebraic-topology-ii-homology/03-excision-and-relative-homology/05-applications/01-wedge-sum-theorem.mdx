<SlideLayout title="Homology of a wedge sum">

A first payoff of excision: computing the homology of a <Term k="ear72WedgeSum">wedge sum</Term> $X \vee Y$ from the homologies of $X$ and $Y$ separately.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Homology of wedge sums">
Let $X$ and $Y$ be spaces with basepoints $x_0 \in X$ and $y_0 \in Y$, each a deformation retract of some open neighborhood. Then for every $n$,
$$
\wt{H}_n(X \vee Y) = \wt{H}_n(X) \oplus \wt{H}_n(Y).
$$
</Callout>

This matches intuition: a wedge sum glues $X$ and $Y$ at a single point, so their "holes" shouldn't interact at all — the homology of the whole is just the direct sum of the two pieces.
