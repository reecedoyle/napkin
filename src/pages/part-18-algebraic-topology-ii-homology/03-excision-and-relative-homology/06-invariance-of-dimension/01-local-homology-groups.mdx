<SlideLayout title="Local homology groups">

Excision has one more striking application: detecting local structure of a space around a single point.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Local homology group">
Let $X$ be a space and $p \in X$ a point. The $k$-th <Term k="ear72LocalHomologyGroup">local homology group</Term> of $p$ in $X$ is
$$
H_k(X, X \setminus \{p\}).
$$
</Callout>

For any open neighborhood $U$ of $p$, excision (with $Z = X \setminus U$) gives
$$
H_k(X, X \setminus \{p\}) \cong H_k(U, U \setminus \{p\}).
$$
So the local homology group only depends on an arbitrarily small neighborhood of $p$ — it's a genuinely *local* invariant, even though it's built using all of $X$.

</SlideLayout>
