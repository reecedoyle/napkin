<SlideLayout title="Invariance of dimension">

A question that sounds like it should be obvious, but genuinely isn't without algebraic topology: could an open subset of $\RR^2$ be homeomorphic to an open subset of $\RR^3$? Local homology groups let us answer this cleanly, via <Term k="ear72InvarianceOfDimension">invariance of dimension</Term>.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Invariance of dimension (Brouwer, 1910)">
Let $U \subseteq \RR^n$ and $V \subseteq \RR^m$ be nonempty open sets. If $U$ and $V$ are <Term k="homeomorphism">homeomorphic</Term>, then $m = n$.
</Callout>

This is exactly the fact you probably assumed all along — "$n$-dimensional space really is $n$-dimensional, not $m$-dimensional for any other $m$" — but it's genuinely a theorem about the topology of $\RR^n$, not a triviality of linear algebra (which only tells you $\RR^n \cong \RR^m$ as *vector spaces* iff $n=m$; it says nothing directly about continuous, non-linear homeomorphisms).

</SlideLayout>
