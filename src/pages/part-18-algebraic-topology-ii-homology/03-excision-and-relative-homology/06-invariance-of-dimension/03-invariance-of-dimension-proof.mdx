<SlideLayout title="Proof of invariance of dimension">

The proof pins down the local homology groups of $\RR^n$ exactly, then compares.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Proof">
Fix $x \in U$. By excision (using the local homology group fact from the previous slide),
$$
H_k(\RR^n, \RR^n \setminus \{x\}) \cong H_k(U, U \setminus \{x\}).
$$
Since $S^{n-1}$ is a <Term k="ear72DeformationRetract">deformation retract</Term> of $\RR^n \setminus \{x\}$ (§3, example (d), shifted to be centered at $x$), the long exact sequence of the pair $(\RR^n, \RR^n \setminus \{x\})$ — using $\wt{H}_k(\RR^n) = 0$ for all $k$ — collapses to
$$
H_k(\RR^n, \RR^n \setminus \{x\}) \cong \wt{H}_{k-1}(S^{n-1}) \cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ & k = n \\
0 & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$
Analogously, for $y \in V$ we get $H_k(\RR^m, \RR^m \setminus \{y\}) \cong H_k(V, V \setminus \{y\})$, which is $\ZZ$ exactly when $k = m$ and $0$ otherwise.

If $U \cong V$, a homeomorphism carries the local homology groups of $x$ to those of its image, so $H_k(\RR^n, \RR^n\setminus\{x\}) \cong H_k(\RR^m, \RR^m\setminus\{y\})$ for **every** $k$. Comparing the two "exactly one nonzero degree" patterns, this forces $n = m$.
</Callout>
