<SlideLayout title="The long exact sequence of a triple">

The pair version generalizes further: nest a third space in between.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Long exact sequence of a triple">
For subspaces $A \subseteq B \subseteq X$, there is a <Term k="ear72LongExactSequenceOfTriple">long exact sequence</Term>
$$
\dots \to H_n(B,A) \to H_n(X,A) \to H_n(X,B) \to H_{n-1}(B,A) \to \dots.
$$
</Callout>

Taking $A = \varnothing$ recovers the ordinary long exact sequence of the pair $(X,B)$; taking $B = X$ recovers the pair $(X, A)$. So the triple version is the more general statement.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Moral">
This is the algebraic shadow of a fact about quotients: $X/B$ is homeomorphic to $\dfrac{X/A}{B/A}$ — collapsing $X$ by $B$ all at once is the same as first collapsing by $A$, then collapsing what remains by $B/A$. We're "cancelling the common factor in the fraction."
</Callout>

</SlideLayout>
