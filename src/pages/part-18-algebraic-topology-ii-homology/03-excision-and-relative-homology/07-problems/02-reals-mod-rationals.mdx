So H̃₁(ℝ,ℚ) ≅ H̃₀(ℚ). Now, ℚ is totally path-disconnected — no two distinct points of ℚ are joined by a path staying inside ℚ — so its path components are single points, one for each rational number. Since ℚ is countably infinite, H̃₀(ℚ) is a direct sum of countably infinitely many copies of ℤ (one free generator per path component beyond the first). Hence H̃₁(ℝ,ℚ) is also countably infinitely many copies of ℤ."
