<SlideLayout title="Cells_1(X) is the aligned version">

Recall $\Cells_1(X) = Z_1(X^1, X^0) / B_1(X^1, X^0)$ — relative cycles modulo relative boundaries, which is just $H_1(X^1, X^0)$ by definition. A typical element here is "relative cycles mod relative boundaries," and it's not hard to believe this is isomorphic to the free abelian group generated by the $1$-cells of $X$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Picture this concretely">
Think of each element of $\Cells_1(X)$ as an "aligned" $1$-chain: one where every endpoint lies inside a vertex (i.e. the chain's actual boundary is in $X^0$), and where for each $1$-cell we've chosen one canonical simplex covering it exactly. Different parametrizations of the same cell differ by an element of $B_1(X^1, X^0)$ — they're "the same, up to fuzz."
</Callout>

This is the key move: $\Cells_1(X)$ trades the unwieldy freedom of $C_1(X^1)$ for a clean basis indexed by cells, at the cost of only remembering chains up to relative boundary.

</SlideLayout>
