<SlideLayout title="d_k for k > 1: degrees of attach-then-collapse">

For higher $k$, the differential is built entirely out of the notion of <Term k="bch73Degree">degree</Term> from the start of this chapter.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Cellular boundary formula for k > 1">
Let $k > 1$. Let $e^k$ be a $k$-cell, and let $\{e_\beta^{k-1}\}_\beta$ be all the $(k-1)$-cells of $X$. Then $d_k \colon \Cells_k(X) \to \Cells_{k-1}(X)$ is given on basis elements by the <Term k="bch73CellularBoundaryFormula">cellular boundary formula</Term>
$$
d_k(e^k) = \sum_\beta d_\beta\, e_\beta^{k-1}
$$
where $d_\beta$ is the degree of the composed map
$$
S^{k-1} = \partial e^k \xrightarrow{\ \text{attach}\ } X^{k-1} \twoheadrightarrow S_\beta^{k-1}.
$$
</Callout>

Here the first arrow is the <Term k="bch73AttachingMap">attaching map</Term> of $e^k$, and the second is the <Term k="bch73CollapseMap">collapse map</Term> that crushes $X^{k-1} \setminus e_\beta^{k-1}$ down to a point, leaving a sphere built from $e_\beta^{k-1}$ alone.

</SlideLayout>
