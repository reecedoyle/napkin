<SlideLayout title="What the degree is really counting">

A basis element $e^k \in \Cells_k(X)$ is just a $k$-cell, and morally its "boundary" should be the cells that form its edge. But in a CW complex, the boundary of a $k$-cell can fold onto *arbitrary points* of $X^{k-1}$ — so an edge of a $k$-cell need not even be an entire $(k-1)$-cell, and worse, edges can be duplicated.

In the Klein bottle, each pair of opposing edges shown above is actually **the same edge**, traversed in possibly different orientations. So when we compute $d_k(e^k)$, we can't just count "how many $(k-1)$-cells border $e^k$" — we need to count the **multiplicity** with which the boundary wraps around each $(k-1)$-cell, accounting for cancellation from opposite orientations. That multiplicity is exactly what the degree of the attach-then-collapse map measures.
