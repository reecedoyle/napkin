<SlideLayout title="The Klein bottle's homology, and a sanity check">

With $d_1 = 0$ and $d_2(e^2) = 2e^1_a$ (so $\ker d_2 = 0$, since $2e^1_a \ne 0$ for any nonzero multiple of $e^2$), we read off:
$$
H_0(X) \cong \ZZ, \qquad H_1(X) \cong \ZZ \oplus \Zc{2}, \qquad H_2(X) = 0.
$$

That $\ZZ \oplus \Zc2$ is $\ZZ^{\oplus 2}$ modulo the subgroup generated by $2 e^1_a$ — exactly $\img d_2$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Sanity check: torsion made visible">
This says there's a $1$-cycle in the Klein bottle that is *not* a boundary on its own, but *becomes* a boundary once you double it. Picture a horizontal line cutting straight across the square: since the two vertical edges are glued with a flip, sliding this cycle around and doubling it lets you pull the two copies across the identified edge to face each other — bounding the shaded strip between them. A single copy bounds nothing; two copies bound the whole strip. That's the $\Zc2$ torsion showing up geometrically.
</Callout>
