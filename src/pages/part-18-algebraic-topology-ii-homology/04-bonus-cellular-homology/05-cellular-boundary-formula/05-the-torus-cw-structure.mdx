<SlideLayout title="Recalling the torus's CW structure">

Let's run the algorithm on the <Term k="bch73Torus">torus</Term>. Recall its standard CW structure: one $0$-cell $e^0$, two $1$-cells $e^1_a$ and $e^1_b$ (each a loop at $e^0$), and one $2$-cell $e^2$ attached via the word $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$.

The full torus $X$ is obtained by gluing a single $2$-cell $e^2$ (a square) onto this $1$-skeleton, so its boundary reads off $a$, then $b$, then $a$ backwards, then $b$ backwards.
