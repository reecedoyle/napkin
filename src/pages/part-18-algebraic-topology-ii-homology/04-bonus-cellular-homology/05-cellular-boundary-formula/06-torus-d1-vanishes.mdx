<SlideLayout title="Torus: the chain complex and d_1">

With one $0$-cell, two $1$-cells, and one $2$-cell, the cellular chain complex is
$$
0 \to \ZZ e^2 \xrightarrow{d_2} \ZZ e^1_a \oplus \ZZ e^1_b \xrightarrow{d_1} \ZZ e^0 \to 0.
$$

By the $k=1$ formula, $d_1(e^1)$ is the difference of the endpoints of $e^1$. But both $e^1_a$ and $e^1_b$ are *loops* — both endpoints are $e^0$ itself. So
$$
d_1(e^1_a) = e^0 - e^0 = 0, \qquad d_1(e^1_b) = e^0 - e^0 = 0,
$$

<Callout kind="remark" title="Conclusion so far">
$d_1 = 0$ on the torus. Every $1$-chain is automatically a cycle.
</Callout>
