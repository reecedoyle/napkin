$d_1$ is still zero (both $e^1_a$, $e^1_b$ are still loops), but this time

<Callout kind="example" title="A different d_2">
$d_2(e^2) = 2 e^1_a$.
</Callout>

<ProofReveal
  client:load
  id="bch73-boundary-proof-klein-d2-coefficient"
  prompt="Why does d_2(e^2) = 2e^1_a for the Klein bottle's abab⁻¹ attaching word?"
  solution="Trace abab⁻¹ around the square and collapse e^1_b to a point (to read off the e^1_a-coefficient). The two a-traversals both survive the collapse, and this time — because the two b-edges are identified in opposite orientations rather than the torus's matching orientation — the two a-traversals occur with the SAME sign instead of cancelling. So the degree of the collapsed map is 1 + 1 = 2, giving d_2(e^2) = 2e^1_a. Collapsing e^1_a instead shows the e^1_b-coefficient is 0, by the same cancellation argument as the torus."
