<SlideLayout title="Hopf's classifying theorem">

Since homotopic maps induce the same map on homology (that's baked into how homology is a functor on the homotopy category), we get for free:

If $f \simeq g$ (f is <Term k="homotopic">homotopic</Term> to $g$), then $\deg f = \deg g$.

What's genuinely surprising is that the converse also holds:

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Hopf's classifying theorem">
Two maps $f, g \colon S^n \to S^n$ are homotopic if and only if $\deg f = \deg g$.
</Callout>

So the single integer $\deg f$ is a **complete** invariant: it doesn't just detect *some* difference between self-maps of $S^n$ up to homotopy, it detects *all* of it. There is exactly one homotopy class of self-map of $S^n$ for every integer.

</SlideLayout>
