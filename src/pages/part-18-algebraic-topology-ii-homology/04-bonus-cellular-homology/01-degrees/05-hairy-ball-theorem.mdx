<SlideLayout title="The hairy ball theorem">

One nice application of degree: you cannot comb a hairy ball flat.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Hairy ball theorem">
<Term k="bch73HairyBall">If $n > 0$ is even, then $S^n$ does not have a continuous field of nonzero tangent vectors.</Term>
</Callout>

<Callout kind="remark" title="Proof">
Suppose such a vector field exists; we may rescale so every vector has norm $1$, i.e. for every $x \in S^n$ we have a unit vector $v(x)$ orthogonal to $x$ (tangent to the sphere there). Define a homotopy $F \colon S^n \times [0,1] \to S^n$ by
$$
(x,t) \mapsto (\cos \pi t)\, x + (\sin \pi t)\, v(x).
$$
At $t=0$ this is $\id$; at $t=1$ it is $-\id$ (the <Term k="bch73AntipodalMap">antipodal map</Term>). So $F$ is a homotopy from $\id$ to $-\id$, meaning $\deg(\id) = \deg(-\id)$. But $\deg(\id) = 1$ and $\deg(-\id) = (-1)^{n+1}$, forcing $1 = (-1)^{n+1}$, so $n$ must be odd. Contradiction.
</Callout>

The bound is tight: for every odd $n$ one can actually construct such a vector field.

</SlideLayout>
