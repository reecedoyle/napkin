<SlideLayout title="A lemma about skeletons">

Every CW complex $X$ is built in layers: the <Term k="bch73Skeleton">$n$-skeleton</Term> $X^n$ is the union of all cells of dimension $\le n$, and $X^0 \subseteq X^1 \subseteq X^2 \subseteq \cdots$ with $X = \bigcup_n X^n$.

Before doing anything else, we record what homology looks like at each layer:

<Callout kind="lemma" title="CW homology groups">
Let $X$ be a CW complex. Then
$$
H_k(X^n, X^{n-1}) \cong
\begin{cases}
\ZZ^{\oplus \#(n\text{-cells of } X)} & k = n \\
0 & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
$$
and
$$
H_k(X^n) \cong
\begin{cases}
H_k(X) & k \le n-1 \\
0 & k \ge n+1.
\end{cases}
$$
</Callout>

In words: the relative homology of consecutive skeletons is concentrated in exactly one degree (and it's free abelian, with basis the cells), while the absolute homology of a skeleton $X^n$ already agrees with $H_k(X)$ once $n$ is comfortably above $k$, and vanishes once $n$ is comfortably below $k$.

</SlideLayout>
