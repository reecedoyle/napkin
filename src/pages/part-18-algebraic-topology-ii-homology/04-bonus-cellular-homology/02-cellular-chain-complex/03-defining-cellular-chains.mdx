<SlideLayout title="Defining the cellular chain groups">

So the groups $H_k(X^k, X^{k-1})$ are unusually nice: they're free abelian, with basis given by the cells of $X$. That's worth naming.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Cellular chain group">
For a CW complex $X$, define
$$
\Cells_k(X) = H_k(X^k, X^{k-1}),
$$
where $\Cells_0(X) = H_0(X^0, \varnothing) = H_0(X^0)$ by convention. So <Term k="bch73CellularChains">$\Cells_k(X)$</Term> is an abelian group with basis given by the $k$-cells of $X$.
</Callout>

Contrast this with the singular chain group $C_k(X)$ from earlier chapters, which is generated by *every* continuous map $\Delta^k \to X$ — an absolutely enormous, uncountable group. $\Cells_k(X)$ is, by comparison, tiny: for a finite CW complex it's finitely generated, with rank equal to the number of $k$-cells.

</SlideLayout>
