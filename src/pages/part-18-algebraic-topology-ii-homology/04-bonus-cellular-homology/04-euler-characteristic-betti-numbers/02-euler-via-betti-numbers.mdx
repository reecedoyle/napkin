Here's the fix: express $\chi(X)$ in terms of homology, which we already know only depends on $X$ itself.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Euler characteristic via Betti numbers">
For any finite CW complex $X$,
$$
\chi(X) = \sum_n (-1)^n \operatorname{rank} H_n(X).
$$
</Callout>

The numbers <Term k="bch73BettiNumber">$b_n = \operatorname{rank} H_n(X)$</Term> are called the **Betti numbers** of $X$. Since the right-hand side manifestly doesn't reference any CW structure, this theorem tells us $\chi(X)$ doesn't depend on the choice of CW decomposition after all — every valid decomposition gives the same alternating cell count.

In fact, this lets us *define* $\chi(X)$ for any reasonable space at all (CW or not), and when $X$ happens to be a CW complex, the definition agrees with the naive cell-counting one.
