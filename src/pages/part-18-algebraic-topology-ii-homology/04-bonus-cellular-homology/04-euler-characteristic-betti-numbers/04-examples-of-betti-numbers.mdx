<Callout kind="example" title="Betti numbers of familiar spaces">
- The Betti numbers of $S^n$ are $b_0 = b_n = 1$, and zero elsewhere. So $\chi(S^n) = 1 + (-1)^n$.
- The Betti numbers of the <Term k="bch73Torus">torus</Term> $S^1 \times S^1$ are $b_0 = 1$, $b_1 = 2$, $b_2 = 1$, and zero elsewhere. So $\chi = 0$.
- The Betti numbers of <Term k="bch73CPn">$\CP^n$</Term> are $b_0 = b_2 = \cdots = b_{2n} = 1$, and zero elsewhere. So $\chi = n+1$.
- The Betti numbers of the <Term k="bch73KleinBottle">Klein bottle</Term> are $b_0 = 1$, $b_1 = 1$, and zero elsewhere. So $\chi = 0$ — the same as the sphere, and also the same as the torus's cell count structure.
</Callout>

Notice: in $S^n$, the torus, and $\CP^n$, the Betti numbers are symmetric, $b_k = b_{n-k}$. This isn't a coincidence — it's a shadow of Poincaré duality, which we'll see more of later.
