<SlideLayout title="Almost everywhere — definition">

You will see the phrases "almost everywhere" and "almost surely" constantly in measure theory. Here is the definition.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Almost everywhere / almost surely">
We say a property $P$ holds <Term k="almostEverywhere">**almost everywhere**</Term> (a.e.) or **almost surely** (a.s.) on a measure space $(\Omega, \mathcal{A}, \mu)$ if the set of exceptions

$$\{\, \omega \in \Omega \mid P(\omega) \text{ does not hold} \,\}$$

has <Term k="measureZero">measure zero</Term>.
</Callout>

**Examples:**
- "$f = g$ almost everywhere" means $\mu(\{ \omega : f(\omega) \neq g(\omega) \}) = 0$.
- "The sequence $f_n$ converges almost everywhere" means the set of $\omega$ where $f_n(\omega)$ fails to converge has measure zero.
- In probability: "$X = 0$ almost surely" means $\mu(\{\omega : X(\omega) \neq 0\}) = 0$, even though individual worlds with $X(\omega) \neq 0$ may exist.
