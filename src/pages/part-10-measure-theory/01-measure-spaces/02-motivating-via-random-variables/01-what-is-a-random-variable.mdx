<SlideLayout title="What is a random variable?">

Our ultimate goal is a rigorous theory of probability powerful enough to prove the law of large numbers and the central limit theorem. To get there we need to handle <Term k="randomVariable">random variables</Term> — quantities that depend on chance.

Examples of random variables:
- The result of a coin flip (discrete, two outcomes).
- The high temperature in Boston on Saturday (continuous on some range).
- The amount of rain next weekend (can be correlated with temperature).

A single coin flip is easy: "50% heads, 50% tails." Continuous outcomes need calculus. But the moment two variables **talk to each other** — temperature tells you something about rain — the naive approach breaks down. Conditional probability gets messy fast.

<Callout kind="remark" title="The key insight">
We will build a single space of **worlds** and define random variables as functions on it. Dependence between variables is then just the fact that two functions share the same input.
</Callout>
