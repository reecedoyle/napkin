<SlideLayout title="Equivalent weaker axioms">

When you want to **verify** that something is a measure, you don't always need to check the full countable-additivity axiom. Weaker conditions suffice.

<Callout kind="exercise" title="Equivalent weaker definitions">

**(a)** The $\sigma$-algebra axioms can be weakened to: (i) $\varnothing \in \mathcal{A}$, and (ii) $\mathcal{A}$ is closed under complements and countable unions. (Closure under intersections follows by de Morgan.)

**(b)** The measure axioms can be weakened to:
1. $\mu(\varnothing) = 0$,
2. finite additivity: $\mu(A \sqcup B) = \mu(A) + \mu(B)$,
3. continuity from above: for $A_1 \supseteq A_2 \supseteq \cdots$ with $\mu(A_1) < \infty$, we have $\mu\!\bigl(\bigcap_n A_n\bigr) = \lim_n \mu(A_n)$.

These three together are equivalent to countable additivity.
</Callout>
