Let's put measures on the $\sigma$-algebras we already know.

<Callout kind="example" title="Three measures">

**(a) Counting measure.** On any $\sigma$-algebra, define <Term k="countingMeasure">$\mu(A) = |A|$</Term>. On a finite $\Omega$, normalize to $\mu(A) = |A|/|\Omega|$ to get a probability space.

**(b) The countable/co-countable measure.** On the coarse $\sigma$-algebra where $\mathcal{A}$ = countable sets and their complements, define
$$\mu(A) = \begin{cases} 0 & A \text{ is countable} \\ 1 & \Omega \setminus A \text{ is countable.} \end{cases}$$
One can check this satisfies the axioms.

**(c) Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{R}$.** The elephant in the room: constructing <Term k="lebesgueMeasure">Lebesgue measure</Term> on $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$ is the topic of the next chapter. For now, we just intend that every interval $[a, b]$ has measure $b - a$.
</Callout>
