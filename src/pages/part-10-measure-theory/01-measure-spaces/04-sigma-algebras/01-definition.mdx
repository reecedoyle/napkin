Here is the formal definition of the "nice family of measurable sets."

<Callout kind="definition" title="Measurable space and σ-algebra">
A **measurable space** $(\Omega, \mathcal{A})$ consists of a set $\Omega$ and a <Term k="sigmaAlgebra">$\sigma$-algebra</Term> $\mathcal{A}$ of subsets of $\Omega$. The set $\mathcal{A}$ must satisfy:

1. $\mathcal{A}$ contains $\varnothing$ and $\Omega$.
2. $\mathcal{A}$ is **closed under complements**: if $A \in \mathcal{A}$ then $\Omega \setminus A \in \mathcal{A}$.
3. $\mathcal{A}$ is **closed under countable unions**: if $A_1, A_2, \ldots \in \mathcal{A}$ then $\bigcup_n A_n \in \mathcal{A}$.
</Callout>

The $\sigma$ in $\sigma$-algebra signals the countable-union condition. (In algebra, a $\sigma$-something often means "countably infinite version of something".) Closure under countable intersections follows by de Morgan's law.

A word on naming: a *measurable* space merely has a $\sigma$-algebra. A *measure* space additionally has a measure $\mu$ assigned to each set in $\mathcal{A}$.
