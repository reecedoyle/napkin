<SlideLayout title="Examples of Pontryagin duals">

The four most important LCA groups and their duals:

<Callout kind="example" title="Computing Pontryagin duals">
- $\wh{\ZZ} \cong \TT$. A homomorphism $\ZZ \to \TT$ is determined by where $1$ goes, and it can go anywhere in $\TT$.
- $\wh{\TT} \cong \ZZ$. The characters of $\TT$ are $\theta \mapsto e^{2\pi i n\theta}$ for $n \in \ZZ$.
- $\wh{\RR} \cong \RR$. Each $\xi \in \RR$ gives the character $x \mapsto e^{2\pi i \xi x}$.
- $\wh{\Zc{n}} \cong \Zc{n}$. Characters are determined by the image of the generator $1$.
- $\wh{G \times H} \cong \wh G \times \wh H$.
</Callout>

Notice the symmetry: $\ZZ$ and $\TT$ are dual to each other (one discrete, one compact). $\RR$ is self-dual.

The <Term k="pontryaginDualityThm">Pontryagin duality theorem</Term> says this is always true:

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Pontryagin duality">
For any LCA group $G$, there is a natural isomorphism
$$G \cong \wh{\wh G} \qquad \text{by} \qquad x \mapsto \bigl(\xi \mapsto \xi(x)\bigr).$$
</Callout>

This is the group-theoretic analogue of the double-dual isomorphism $V \cong (V^\vee)^\vee$ for finite-dimensional vector spaces.
