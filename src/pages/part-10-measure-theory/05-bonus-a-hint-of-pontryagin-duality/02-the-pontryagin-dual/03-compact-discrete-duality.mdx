<SlideLayout title="Compact ↔ discrete duality">

The examples reveal a striking pattern: $\TT$ is compact and $\wh{\TT} = \ZZ$ is discrete; $\ZZ$ is discrete and $\wh{\ZZ} = \TT$ is compact. This is not a coincidence.

<Callout kind="proposition" title="Compact ↔ discrete">
Let $G$ be an LCA group. Then $G$ is compact if and only if $\wh G$ is discrete.
</Callout>

Unpacking with our examples:
- $\TT$ is compact $\Rightarrow$ $\wh{\TT} = \ZZ$ is discrete. ✓
- $\ZZ$ is discrete $\Rightarrow$ $\wh{\ZZ} = \TT$ is compact. ✓
- $\RR$ is neither compact nor discrete $\Rightarrow$ $\wh{\RR} = \RR$ is also neither. ✓
- $\Zc{n}$ is finite (compact and discrete) $\Rightarrow$ $\wh{\Zc{n}} = \Zc{n}$ is also finite. ✓
