The non-compact Fourier inversion formula also unifies two classical transforms.

<Callout kind="example" title="Non-compact Fourier transforms">
- **$G = \RR$** (self-dual: $\wh G = \RR$). The formula gives the classical **continuous Fourier transform**:
  $$f(x) = \int_\RR \wh f(\xi)\, e^{2\pi i \xi x}\, d\xi.$$
- **$G = \ZZ$** (dual: $\wh G = \TT$). The formula gives the **discrete-time Fourier transform (DTFT)**:
  $$f(n) = \int_\TT \wh f(\xi)\, e^{2\pi i \xi n}\, d\xi.$$
</Callout>

Both are special cases of the same abstract theorem — with different Haar measures (Lebesgue on $\RR$, counting measure on $\ZZ$, arc-length on $\TT$).

<Callout kind="remark" title="Why so many names?">
Every combination of a group $G$ and its dual $\wh G$ has historically been given its own Wikipedia article and its own name. If we called them all "$G$-Fourier transforms," life would be simpler. The zoo of names — continuous Fourier transform, Fourier series, DFT, DTFT — reflects history more than mathematics.
</Callout>
