Here is the central theorem of this chapter. It unifies all the Fourier theories we have seen.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Characters of G-hat form an orthonormal basis">
Assume $G$ is LCA and compact (so $\wh G$ is discrete). The characters
$$e_\xi(x) = \exp(2\pi i\, \xi(x)), \qquad \xi \in \wh G,$$
form an **orthonormal basis** of $L^2(G)$.

Thus every $f \in L^2(G)$ has a Fourier expansion
$$f = \sum_{\xi \in \wh G} \wh f(\xi)\, e_\xi$$
where the <Term k="fourierCoeffLCA">**Fourier coefficients**</Term> are
$$\wh f(\xi) = \langle f, e_\xi \rangle = \int_G f(x)\, \exp(-2\pi i\, \xi(x))\, d\mu.$$
</Callout>

The orthonormality $\langle e_\xi, e_{\xi'} \rangle = [[\xi = \xi']]$ follows directly from character integration: integrating a nontrivial character over a compact group gives zero.

This theorem says: **on any compact LCA group, Fourier analysis works perfectly** — every square-integrable function expands as a (possibly infinite) series in the characters.
