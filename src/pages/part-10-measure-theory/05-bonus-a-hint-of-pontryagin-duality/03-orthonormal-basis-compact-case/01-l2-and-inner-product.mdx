<SlideLayout title="L²(G) and its inner product">

Let $G$ be a compact LCA group with its normalized <Term k="haarMeasure">Haar measure</Term> $\mu$ (so $\mu(G) = 1$). We can now set up a Hilbert space of functions on $G$.

<Callout kind="definition" title="L²(G)">
The space <Term k="lTwoGroup">$L^2(G)$</Term> consists of all measurable functions $f \colon G \to \CC$ with
$$\int_G |f|^2 \, d\mu < \infty,$$
with the inner product
$$\langle f, g \rangle = \int_G f \cdot \ol{g} \, d\mu.$$
</Callout>

This is exactly the Hilbert space framework we need to state a general Fourier theorem.

For each character <Term k="character">$\xi \in \wh G$</Term>, define
$$e_\xi \colon G \to \CC \qquad \text{by} \qquad e_\xi(x) = \exp(2\pi i \, \xi(x)).$$

These are the "complex exponentials" of the group $G$ — the analogues of $e^{2\pi i n x}$ on $\TT$.

Since $G$ is compact and $\wh G$ is discrete (by the compact $\Leftrightarrow$ discrete proposition), the sum $\sum_{\xi \in \wh G}$ makes sense as a countable sum.

</SlideLayout>
