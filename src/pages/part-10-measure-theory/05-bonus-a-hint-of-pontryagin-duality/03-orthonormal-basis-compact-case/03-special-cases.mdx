<SlideLayout title="Special cases of the compact theorem">

The general theorem recovers every compact Fourier theory as a special case.

<Callout kind="example" title="Recognizing familiar theories">
- **$G = \TT$** (the circle). Then $\wh G = \ZZ$ and the characters are $e_n(\theta) = e^{2\pi i n\theta}$. This is exactly the classical **Fourier series**.
- **$G = \Zc{n}$** (cyclic group of order $n$). Then $\wh G \cong \Zc{n}$ and the characters are $\xi_k(j) = e^{2\pi i jk/n}$. This is the **discrete Fourier transform (DFT)**.
- **$G$ = any finite abelian group**. This is the general **Fourier transform on finite groups**.
</Callout>

All of these are just different compact LCA groups plugged into the same theorem. The "scary" looking DFT formula is just a special case of the universal pattern.
