For Fourier analysis we need one additional topological condition beyond being a topological group: **local compactness**.

<Callout kind="definition" title="LCA group">
A <Term k="lcaGroup">**locally compact abelian (LCA) group**</Term> $G$ is a topological group whose group operation is abelian, and whose topology is locally compact: every point $p \in G$ has a compact neighborhood.
</Callout>

Our key examples:

- Any finite group $Z$ with the discrete topology is LCA (every point has the compact neighborhood $\{p\}$).
- The circle group <Term k="circleGroup">$\TT$</Term> is LCA — and in fact **compact** (the whole space is compact).
- $\RR$ is LCA but **not** compact: any compact subset of $\RR$ is bounded, so $\RR$ itself is not compact.
