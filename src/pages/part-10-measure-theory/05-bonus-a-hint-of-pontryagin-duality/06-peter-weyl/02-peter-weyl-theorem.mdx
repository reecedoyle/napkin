<SlideLayout title="The Peter–Weyl theorem">

For a compact Lie group, <Term k="matrixCoefficient">matrix coefficients</Term> replace characters as the building blocks of $L^2(G)$.

Fix a compact Lie group $G$ and an irreducible unitary representation $(V, \rho)$ with $\dim V = d$ and an orthonormal basis $e_1, \dots, e_d$. The $(i,j)$th matrix coefficient is
$$\rho_{ij}(g) = \text{the }(i,j)\text{ entry of the matrix }\rho(g).$$

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Peter–Weyl">
Let $G$ be a compact Lie group. Let $\Sigma$ be a set of representatives of all isomorphism classes of irreducible finite-dimensional unitary representations of $G$. Then the functions
$$\bigl\{\sqrt{\dim V}\, \rho_{ij} \;\big\vert\; (V,\rho)\in\Sigma,\; 1\le i,j\le\dim V\bigr\}$$
form an **orthonormal basis** of <Term k="peterWeyl">$L^2(G)$</Term>.
</Callout>

When $G$ is abelian, all irreducible representations are 1-dimensional (maps $G \to \CC^\times$) and the unitary condition forces them into $G \to \TT$ — they are exactly the characters in $\wh G$. So Peter–Weyl recovers the abelian theorem as a special case.

</SlideLayout>
