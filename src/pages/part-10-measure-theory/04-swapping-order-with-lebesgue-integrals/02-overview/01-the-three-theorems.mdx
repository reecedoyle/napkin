<SlideLayout title="The three big convergence theorems">

Three results classify when you can swap $\lim$ and $\int$:

<Callout kind="remark" title="The hierarchy">

| Theorem | Hypothesis | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| <Term k="fatousLemma">Fatou's lemma</Term> | functions are nonneg. | most general — just a bound |
| <Term k="monotonoConvergence">Monotone convergence</Term> | nonneg. and $f_n \le f$ | a quick corollary of Fatou |
| <Term k="dominatedConvergence">Dominated convergence</Term> | $\lvert f_n \rvert \le g$, $g$ integrable | the everyday workhorse |

</Callout>

The logical flow is elegant: Fatou's lemma does the hard work once, and the other two
fall out as corollaries in just a few lines.

Fatou says: for nonneg. functions, the integral cannot
*suddenly jump up* when you pass to the limit — but it can drop.
The dominated convergence theorem adds: if the functions can't wiggle beyond a fixed integrable bound, there's no room for any anomaly at all.
