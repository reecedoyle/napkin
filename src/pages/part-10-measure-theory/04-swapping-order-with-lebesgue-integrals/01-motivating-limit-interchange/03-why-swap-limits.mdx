<SlideLayout title="Why we want to swap limits and integrals">

Here is a typical problem: compute

$$\lim_{k \to \infty} \int_1^\infty \frac{1}{k}\, e^{-x^2}\; dx.$$

The integral $\int e^{-x^2} dx$ has no closed form in elementary functions, yet
intuitively the factor $1/k \to 0$ should make the whole thing zero. We would love to say:

$$\lim_{k \to \infty} \int_1^\infty \frac{e^{-x^2}}{k}\; dx = \int_1^\infty \lim_{k \to \infty} \frac{e^{-x^2}}{k}\; dx = \int_1^\infty 0\; dx = 0.$$

Moving the <Term k="limitInterchange">$\lim$ inside the integral sign</Term> is called
interchanging the limit and the integral. When is this legal?
