<SlideLayout title="Pointwise convergence">

Define the sequence of functions $g_1, g_2, \dots$ by

$$g_n(x) = \begin{cases} 1 & (n!)x \in \ZZ \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$$

Each $g_n$ picks out the rationals with denominator dividing $n!$.
Every $g_n$ is piecewise constant, hence <Term k="riemannIntegrable">Riemann integrable</Term>,
with $\int_0^1 g_n \; dx = 0$.

But as $n \to \infty$, $g_n$ approaches $\mathbf{1}_\QQ$ in the following sense.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Pointwise convergence">
A sequence of functions $f_1, f_2, \dots \colon \Omega \to \RR$
<Term k="pointwiseConvergence">converges pointwise</Term> to $f \colon \Omega \to \RR$
if, for each fixed $\omega \in \Omega$,
$$f_1(\omega),\; f_2(\omega),\; f_3(\omega),\; \dots \longrightarrow f(\omega).$$
We write $\lim_{n \to \infty} f_n = f$.
Similarly we can define $\liminf_{n \to \infty} f_n$ and $\limsup_{n \to \infty} f_n$ pointwise.
</Callout>

For our sequence, every rational $x = p/q$ is eventually hit by $g_n$ (once $n \geq q$),
so $g_n(x) \to 1$. Every irrational is never hit, so $g_n(x) \to 0$.
Thus $\lim_{n \to \infty} g_n = \mathbf{1}_\QQ$ pointwise, even though the limit is not Riemann integrable.

</SlideLayout>
