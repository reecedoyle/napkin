<Callout kind="lemma" title="Fatou's lemma">
Let $f_1, f_2, \dots \colon \Omega \to [0,+\infty]$ be a sequence of
<Term k="nonnegMeasurable">nonneg. measurable functions</Term>.
Then $\displaystyle\liminf_{n \to \infty} f_n$ is measurable and
$$\int_\Omega \!\left(\liminf_{n \to \infty} f_n\right) d\mu
  \;\le\; \liminf_{n \to \infty} \left(\int_\Omega f_n\; d\mu\right).$$
Both sides may be $+\infty$.
</Callout>

Two points worth emphasising:

- **No extra hypotheses.** The only requirement is that the functions are nonneg. and measurable.
  This makes Fatou's lemma surprisingly powerful for proving general results.
- **Converging vs. non-converging sequences.** When the sequence actually converges (pointwise),
  $\liminf_{n\to\infty} f_n = \lim_{n\to\infty} f_n$ and the statement collapses to
  $\int f\; d\mu \le \lim \int f_n\; d\mu$, the "preliminary version."
