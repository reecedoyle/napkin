<Callout kind="theorem" title="Tonelli's theorem">
Let $(X, \mu)$ and $(Y, \nu)$ be <Term k="sigmaFinite">$\sigma$-finite</Term> measure spaces,
and let $f \colon X \times Y \to [0,+\infty]$ be <Term k="nonnegMeasurable">nonneg. measurable</Term>.
Then
$$\int_{X \times Y} f\; d(\mu \otimes \nu)
  = \int_X \!\left(\int_Y f(x,y)\; d\nu(y)\right) d\mu(x)
  = \int_Y \!\left(\int_X f(x,y)\; d\mu(x)\right) d\nu(y).$$
All three expressions may be $+\infty$, and they are always equal.
</Callout>

Tonelli is the "Fatou's lemma" of double integrals: *no* absolute integrability required,
just nonnegativity. The price is that the integral might be infinite.

The $\sigma$-finiteness condition is mild: both Lebesgue measure on $\RR^n$ and any
probability measure satisfy it. It prevents pathological cases where the product measure
isn't even well-defined.
