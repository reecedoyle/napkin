A second kind of "swapping" question arises when we integrate over a product space:
is the order of integration interchangeable?

Suppose we want to integrate a function $f(x, y)$ over $\RR^2$.
There are three natural candidates:
- The double integral $\displaystyle\int_{\RR^2} f\; d(\mu \otimes \nu)$ over the
  <Term k="productMeasure">product measure</Term>.
- The iterated integral $\displaystyle\int_\RR \!\left(\int_\RR f(x,y)\; d\nu(y)\right) d\mu(x)$:
  integrate over $y$ first, then over $x$.
- The iterated integral in the other order: integrate over $x$ first, then over $y$.

**Do these always agree?** In Riemann calculus, calculus students learn to switch the order of integration
when the region is nice. In Lebesgue theory, Fubini and Tonelli give a precise answer.

<Callout kind="example" title="Why the question matters">
The integral $\int_0^1 \!\int_0^1 \frac{x^2 - y^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2}\; dx\; dy$ equals $\pi/4$,
but integrating in the other order gives $-\pi/4$. The function is not absolutely integrable,
so Fubini does not apply — and the two orders genuinely disagree.
</Callout>
