<Callout kind="theorem" title="Fubini's theorem">
Let $(X, \mu)$ and $(Y, \nu)$ be $\sigma$-finite, and let $f \colon X \times Y \to \RR$
be <Term k="absolutelyIntegrable">absolutely integrable</Term>:
$\displaystyle\int_{X\times Y} |f|\; d(\mu\otimes\nu) < \infty$.
Then the three integrals
$$\int_{X \times Y}\! f\; d(\mu \otimes \nu),\quad
  \int_X \!\left(\int_Y f(x,y)\; d\nu(y)\right)\! d\mu(x),\quad
  \int_Y \!\left(\int_X f(x,y)\; d\mu(x)\right)\! d\nu(y)$$
are all finite and equal.
</Callout>

The standard strategy is: **use Tonelli on $|f|$ first** to verify $\int |f| < \infty$,
then apply Fubini to $f$ to swap the order of integration freely.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Fubini–Tonelli in one step">
Together, Tonelli and Fubini give a clean recipe for double integrals:
compute one iterated integral of $|f|$ — if it's finite, then all three integrals of $f$
agree and you may switch the order at will.
</Callout>
