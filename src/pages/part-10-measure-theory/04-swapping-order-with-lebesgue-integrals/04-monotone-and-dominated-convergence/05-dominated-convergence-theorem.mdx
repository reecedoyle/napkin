<SlideLayout title="Dominated convergence theorem">

When the sequence actually converges (not just has a $\liminf$), the Fatou–Lebesgue inequalities collapse to an equality.

<Callout kind="corollary" title="Dominated convergence theorem">
Let $f_1, f_2, \dots \colon \Omega \to \RR$ be measurable with
$f = \lim_{n} f_n$ existing pointwise.
Suppose $g \colon \Omega \to \RR$ is <Term k="absolutelyIntegrable">absolutely integrable</Term>
with $\lvert f_n(\omega)\rvert \le \lvert g(\omega)\rvert$ for all $n$ and $\omega$.
Then
$$\int_\Omega f\; d\mu \;=\; \lim_{n \to \infty} \int_\Omega f_n\; d\mu.$$
</Callout>

**Why it follows from Fatou–Lebesgue:** When $f_n \to f$ pointwise, all four quantities in the
Fatou–Lebesgue chain are equal:
$\liminf_n f_n = f = \limsup_n f_n$.
So the leftmost and rightmost integrals agree, forcing all the inequalities in between to be equalities.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Moral">
If there is only finite "space" for the functions to wiggle around — bounded by an integrable
dominator $g$ — then no anomaly can occur.
</Callout>

</SlideLayout>
