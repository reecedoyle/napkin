<SlideLayout title="The Fatou–Lebesgue theorem">

Fatou's lemma only covers nonneg. functions. What if $f_n$ can be negative?
We need a dominating bound.

<Callout kind="corollary" title="Fatou–Lebesgue theorem">
Let $f_1, f_2, \dots \colon \Omega \to \RR$ be measurable, and suppose
$g \colon \Omega \to \RR$ is <Term k="absolutelyIntegrable">absolutely integrable</Term>
with $\lvert f_n(\omega)\rvert \le g(\omega)$ for all $n$ and $\omega$.
Then
$$\int_\Omega\!\Bigl(\liminf_{n} f_n\Bigr)\; d\mu
  \;\le\; \liminf_{n} \int_\Omega f_n\; d\mu
  \;\le\; \limsup_{n} \int_\Omega f_n\; d\mu
  \;\le\; \int_\Omega\!\Bigl(\limsup_{n} f_n\Bigr)\; d\mu.$$
</Callout>

The proof applies Fatou's lemma twice — once to $g + f_n$ (nonneg. by assumption) and once to $g - f_n$ (also nonneg.). The middle inequality $\liminf \le \limsup$ is always true and is there only to make the chain easy to remember.

The key role of $g$: it lets us write $g + f_n \ge 0$ and $g - f_n \ge 0$ so that Fatou applies.
Without a dominator we cannot guarantee nonnegativity, so the lemma would not apply.
