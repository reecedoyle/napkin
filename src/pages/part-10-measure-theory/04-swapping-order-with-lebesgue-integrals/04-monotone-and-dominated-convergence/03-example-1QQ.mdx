<SlideLayout title="Example — recovering the integral of 1_ℚ">

We already know $\int_{[0,1]} \mathbf{1}_\QQ\; d\mu = 0$ directly from the definition.
Monotone convergence gives a second derivation.

<Callout kind="example" title="Monotone convergence applied to 1_ℚ">
Recall the sequence $g_n$ (the indicator of rationals with denominator dividing $n!$).
We have:
- $g_n \le \mathbf{1}_\QQ$ for every $n$ (each $g_n$ picks out only some rationals).
- $g_n \to \mathbf{1}_\QQ$ pointwise as $n \to \infty$.
- $\int_{[0,1]} g_n\; d\mu = 0$ for each $n$ (each $g_n$ is nonzero only on a finite set).

By the <Term k="monotonoConvergence">monotone convergence theorem</Term>:
$$\int_{[0,1]} \mathbf{1}_\QQ\; d\mu = \lim_{n\to\infty} \int_{[0,1]} g_n\; d\mu = \lim_{n\to\infty} 0 = 0.$$
</Callout>

This is how the theorem earns its keep: instead of computing a complicated integral directly,
we approximate by simpler functions whose integrals we already know.

</SlideLayout>
