<SlideLayout title="Monotone convergence theorem">

The first application of Fatou's lemma gives a clean result when the sequence is "bounded from above by its own limit."

<Callout kind="corollary" title="Monotone convergence theorem">
Let $f$ and $f_1, f_2, \dots \colon \Omega \to [0,+\infty]$ be
<Term k="nonnegMeasurable">nonneg. measurable functions</Term> with
$\lim_{n} f_n = f$ pointwise and $f_n(\omega) \le f(\omega)$ for each $n$.
Then
$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \int_\Omega f_n\; d\mu \;=\; \int_\Omega f\; d\mu.$$
Both sides may be $+\infty$.
</Callout>

The proof is four lines:

$$\int_\Omega f\; d\mu
 = \int_\Omega \!\Bigl(\liminf_n f_n\Bigr)\; d\mu
 \le \liminf_n \int_\Omega f_n\; d\mu
 \le \limsup_n \int_\Omega f_n\; d\mu
 \le \int_\Omega f\; d\mu.$$

The first inequality is Fatou's lemma. The last uses $f_n \le f$ for all $n$, so $\int f_n \le \int f$.
All four quantities are therefore equal.

</SlideLayout>
