<SlideLayout title="Where is the dominator used?">

In the proof of the <Term k="fatouLebesgue">Fatou–Lebesgue theorem</Term> we applied Fatou's lemma
to $g + f_n$ and to $g - f_n$, using the fact that both are nonneg. because $|f_n| \le g$.

But there is a subtlety: why does the calculation work out even when we subtract integrals of $g$?
The answer is that $g$ must be finite: $\int g < \infty$.

<Callout kind="exercise" title="Where is absolute integrability used?">
In the Fatou–Lebesgue proof, we apply Fatou to $g + f_n \ge 0$ to get
$\int \liminf(g + f_n)\; d\mu \le \liminf \int(g + f_n)\; d\mu$.
Expanding: $\int g + \int \liminf f_n \le \int g + \liminf \int f_n$.
We then cancel $\int g$ from both sides. At what point does $\int g < \infty$ become essential?
</Callout>
