<SlideLayout title="The monotone name is misleading">

The standard textbook version of the <Term k="monotonoConvergence">monotone convergence theorem</Term>
states a stronger hypothesis: $f_1(\omega) \le f_2(\omega) \le \dots \le f(\omega)$ for all $\omega$.
That pointwise chain is where the name "monotone" comes from.

<Callout kind="remark" title="The monotone hypothesis is superfluous">
Our version only requires $f_n(\omega) \le f(\omega)$ for all $n$ — the functions need not be
increasing in $n$. This weaker condition is actually sufficient, and implies the usual version.
(The implication goes: a monotone increasing chain bounded above by $f$ automatically satisfies
each $f_n \le f$.)

This observation appears on MathOverflow in a thread titled
"Do you know of any very important theorems that remain unknown?" — a testament to how rarely
the cleaner version is taught.
</Callout>

For everyday use, both formulations give the same result. The cleaner version is simply more versatile
when you are trying to prove something general and monotonicity of the sequence is hard to verify.

</SlideLayout>
