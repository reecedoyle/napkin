<SlideLayout title="From now on — the Borel measure">

We have constructed both the <Term k="borelSigmaAlgebra">Borel $\sigma$-algebra</Term> $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ and the <Term k="lebesgueSigmaAlgebra">Lebesgue $\sigma$-algebra</Term> $\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^n)$. Going forward in analysis, we mostly work with the Borel measure — for good reasons.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Why prefer Borel over Lebesgue?">
- **Stability under maps.** The preimage of a Borel set under a continuous map is again Borel. This makes Borel the natural setting for measurable functions.
- **Simpler description.** $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is generated by open sets — a clean, intrinsic description. $\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ requires the abstract completion procedure.
- **Sufficiency.** The extra Lebesgue-but-not-Borel sets are subsets of null sets. For integration these have measure zero and contribute nothing.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="remark" title="The payoff">
The hard work of this chapter was to show that a measure on $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ consistent with volume **exists**. Having done that, we can now work with it without thinking about Carathéodory or outer measures. The construction is behind us — the measure is in hand.
</Callout>
