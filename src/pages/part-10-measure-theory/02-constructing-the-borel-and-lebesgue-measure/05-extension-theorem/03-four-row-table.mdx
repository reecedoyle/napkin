Adding the generated $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{A}$ as a fourth row, the full picture is:

| Domain | Function | Step | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| $2^\Omega$ | $\mu^\ast$ | 2 | Outer measure from $\mu_0$ |
| $\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{cm}}$ | $\mu^{\mathrm{cm}}$ | 3 | Complete; completion of $(\mathcal{A}, \mu)$ when $\sigma$-finite |
| $\mathcal{A}$ | $\mu$ | 4 | $\sigma$-alg.\ generated by $\mathcal{A}_0$; unique extension when $\sigma$-finite |
| $\mathcal{A}_0$ | $\mu_0$ | 1 | Pre-measure on algebra |

For the Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{R}^n$:

| Domain | Function | Step | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| $2^{\mathbb{R}^n}$ | $\lambda^\ast$ | 2 | Lebesgue outer measure |
| $\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ | $\lambda$ | 3 | Lebesgue $\sigma$-algebra (complete) |
| $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ | $\mu$ | 4 | <Term k="borelSigmaAlgebra">Borel $\sigma$-algebra</Term>, generated by $\mathcal{L}_0$ |
| $\mathcal{L}_0$ | $\lambda_0$ | 1 | Rectangle pre-measure |

Going down one row is restriction; each upward step is a unique extension (when $\sigma$-finite).
