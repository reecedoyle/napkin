<SlideLayout title="σ-finite measures">

Often we want to extend $\mu_0$ not to all of $\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{cm}}$ but to the $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{A}$ generated by $\mathcal{A}_0$. The full Carathéodory theorem for pre-measures gives uniqueness when the pre-measure is $\sigma$-finite.

<Callout kind="definition" title="σ-finite measure">
A pre-measure or measure $\mu$ on $\Omega$ is **$\sigma$-finite** if $\Omega = \bigcup_n A_n$ for countably many sets $A_n$ with $\mu(A_n) < \infty$.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" title="Lebesgue pre-measure is σ-finite">
On $\mathbb{R}^n$: write $\mathbb{R}^n = \bigcup_{k=1}^\infty [-k,k]^n$. Each cube $[-k,k]^n$ has $\lambda_0$-measure $(2k)^n < \infty$. So $\lambda_0$ is <Term k="sigmaFinite">$\sigma$-finite</Term>.
</Callout>

Most measures encountered in practice are $\sigma$-finite. The Lebesgue measure, counting measure on $\mathbb{N}$, probability measures — all are $\sigma$-finite. The total measure of $\mathbb{R}^n$ is infinite, but we can cover it by finite-measure pieces.

</SlideLayout>
