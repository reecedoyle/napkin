<Callout kind="theorem" title="Carathéodory extension theorem (full version)">
Let $\mu_0$ be a pre-measure on algebra $\mathcal{A}_0 \subseteq 2^\Omega$, and let $\mathcal{A}$ be the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $\mathcal{A}_0$. Let $\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{cm}}, \mu^{\mathrm{cm}}$ be as before. Then:

**(a)** Restricting $\mu^{\mathrm{cm}}$ to $\mathcal{A}$ gives a measure $\mu$ extending $\mu_0$.

**(b)** If $\mu_0$ is $\sigma$-finite, then $\mu$ is the **unique** extension of $\mu_0$ to $\mathcal{A}$.

**(c)** If $\mu_0$ is $\sigma$-finite, then $\mu^{\mathrm{cm}}$ is the **completion** of $\mu$, and the unique extension of $\mu_0$ to $\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{cm}}$.
</Callout>

Parts (b) and (c) are why $\sigma$-finiteness matters: without it, extensions may not be unique. With it, there is a canonical "smallest complete extension" and a canonical "extension to the generated $\sigma$-algebra" — they are related by completion.
