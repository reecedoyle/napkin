<SlideLayout title="Compatibility with the algebra">

We need to know that the sets we started with — the elements of the algebra $\mathcal{A}_0$ — survive as <Term k="caratheodoryMeasurable">Carathéodory measurable</Term> sets. Otherwise the extension would throw away all our geometric information.

<Callout kind="proposition" title="𝒜₀ ⊆ Carathéodory measurable sets">
Suppose $\mu^\ast$ was obtained from a pre-measure $\mu_0$ on an algebra $\mathcal{A}_0$. Then every $A \in \mathcal{A}_0$ is $\mu^\ast$-measurable.
</Callout>

This is the **second compatibility result** (the first was $\mu^\ast = \mu_0$ on $\mathcal{A}_0$). Together they say: the full construction extends $\mu_0$ from $\mathcal{A}_0$ to a strictly larger $\sigma$-algebra, without losing any of the original values.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Proof idea">
Given $A \in \mathcal{A}_0$ and any test set $E$, we need $\mu^\ast(E) \ge \mu^\ast(E \cap A) + \mu^\ast(E \setminus A)$. For any $\varepsilon > 0$ take a covering $E \subseteq \bigcup_n A_n$ with $\sum \mu_0(A_n) < \mu^\ast(E) + \varepsilon$. Since $A \in \mathcal{A}_0$, the sets $A_n \cap A$ and $A_n \setminus A$ are also in $\mathcal{A}_0$, and $\mu_0$ is additive on them. This gives the needed inequality; then let $\varepsilon \to 0$.
</Callout>

</SlideLayout>
