<Callout kind="definition" title="Pre-measure">
A **pre-measure** $\mu_0$ on an algebra $\mathcal{A}_0$ is a function $\mu_0 : \mathcal{A}_0 \to [0,+\infty]$ satisfying:

1. $\mu_0(\varnothing) = 0$
2. **Countable additivity (when applicable):** if $A_1, A_2, \ldots$ are pairwise disjoint sets in $\mathcal{A}_0$ whose union $\bigsqcup_n A_n$ also belongs to $\mathcal{A}_0$, then $\mu_0\!\left(\bigsqcup_n A_n\right) = \sum_n \mu_0(A_n)$.
</Callout>

The parenthetical "(when applicable)" is the key difference from a measure. In an algebra we are **not** guaranteed that a countable disjoint union stays in $\mathcal{A}_0$. The pre-measure axiom only fires when it does.

<Callout kind="example" title="Lebesgue pre-measure on ℝ²">
Let $\mathcal{L}_0$ be the algebra generated by rectangles $[a_1,a_2]\times[b_1,b_2]$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$.
Define $\mu_0([a_1,a_2]\times[b_1,b_2]) = (a_2-a_1)(b_2-b_1)$ — just the area.
Extending this to finite unions of rectangles gives a pre-measure $\lambda_0$ on $\mathcal{L}_0$.
</Callout>
