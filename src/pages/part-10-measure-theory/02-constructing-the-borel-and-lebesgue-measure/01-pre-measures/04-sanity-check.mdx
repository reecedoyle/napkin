<SlideLayout title="The sanity check — geometry">

All the abstract machinery that follows will produce a measure starting from any pre-measure. But we need the initial pre-measure to be sensible. For $\mathbb{R}^n$ this sanity check is a geometric fact.

<Callout kind="proposition" title="Lebesgue rectangle pre-measure exists">
For $\Omega = \mathbb{R}^n$ and $\mathcal{L}_0$ the algebra generated by rectangular prisms, one can define a pre-measure $\lambda_0$ on $\mathcal{L}_0$.
</Callout>

We won't prove this (it reduces to checking that areas of finite unions of rectangles are unambiguous), but it is the **only geometric input** the rest of the construction needs.

<Callout kind="remark" title="The insane scientist">
Suppose someone tries to define a notion of area in which every rectangle has area $1$. This is geometrically absurd — a rectangle can be split into two halves, and we'd need $1+1=1$.

The only barrier stopping them is that they cannot extend their "measure" to a pre-measure on $\mathcal{L}_0$: the countable-additivity condition instantly fails. Once a pre-measure exists, the abstract construction produces a valid measure — but the geometry has already been checked at this step.
</Callout>

This is why $\lambda_0$'s well-definedness matters: it is the gatekeeper that ensures our eventual measure is geometrically sane.

</SlideLayout>
