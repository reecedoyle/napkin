<SlideLayout title="Lebesgue is the completion of Borel">

The relationship between the Lebesgue and Borel $\sigma$-algebras is captured cleanly by the notion of completion.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Completion of a measure space">
Given $(\Omega, \mathcal{A}, \mu)$, the **completion** is $(\Omega, \overline{\mathcal{A}}, \overline{\mu})$ where

$$\overline{\mathcal{A}} = \{A \cup N : A \in \mathcal{A},\; N \subseteq \text{null set}\}.$$

and $\overline{\mu}(A \cup N) = \mu(A)$. This forces every subset of a <Term k="nullSet">null set</Term> to be measurable (with measure zero).
</Callout>

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Lebesgue is completion of Borel">
For $\mathbb{R}^n$, the <Term k="lebesgueMeasure">Lebesgue measure</Term> is the <Term k="completionMeasure">completion</Term> of the <Term k="borelSigmaAlgebra">Borel measure</Term>.
</Callout>

This gives us a clean description: $\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^n) = \overline{\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)}$. Every Lebesgue measurable set is a Borel set plus a subset of a Borel null set.
