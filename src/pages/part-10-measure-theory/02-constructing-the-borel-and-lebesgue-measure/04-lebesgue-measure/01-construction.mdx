We now run the three-step pipeline on $\mathbb{R}^n$ to get the <Term k="lebesgueMeasure">Lebesgue measure</Term>.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Lebesgue measure">
Create a measure on $\mathbb{R}^n$ as follows:

1. **Step 1 (pre-measure):** Start with the algebra $\mathcal{L}_0$ generated by rectangular prisms and the area pre-measure $\lambda_0$ on it.

2. **Step 2 (outer measure):** Apply the infimum-covering construction to get the <Term k="lebesgueOuterMeasure">Lebesgue outer measure</Term> $\lambda^\ast$ on $2^{\mathbb{R}^n}$.

3. **Step 3 (Carathéodory):** Restrict $\lambda^\ast$ to the $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ of $\lambda^\ast$-measurable sets, giving a complete measure $\lambda$.

The result $\lambda$ is called the **Lebesgue measure**. The $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is the <Term k="lebesgueSigmaAlgebra">**Lebesgue $\sigma$-algebra**</Term>; sets in it are **Lebesgue measurable**.
</Callout>
