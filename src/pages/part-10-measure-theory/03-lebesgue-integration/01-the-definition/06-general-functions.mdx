<SlideLayout title="Step 4 — general (signed) functions">

So far we have only handled nonneg functions. For functions that can be positive or negative, we split.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Absolutely integrable (general case)">
A measurable function $f \colon \Omega \to [-\infty, \infty]$ is <Term k="absolutelyIntegrable">**absolutely integrable**</Term> if $|f|$ is absolutely integrable, i.e. $\int_\Omega |f| \; d\mu < \infty$.
</Callout>

**Step 4.** When $f$ is absolutely integrable, define the positive and negative parts:

$$f^+(x) = \max\{ f(x), 0 \}, \qquad f^-(x) = \min\{ f(x), 0 \}.$$

Both $|f^+|$ and $|f^-|$ are nonneg, and we set

$$\int_\Omega f \; d\mu = \int_\Omega |f^+| \; d\mu - \int_\Omega |f^-| \; d\mu,$$

which is finite. We really are splitting $f$ into its positive and negative contributions, integrating each separately (using Step 3), and subtracting.
