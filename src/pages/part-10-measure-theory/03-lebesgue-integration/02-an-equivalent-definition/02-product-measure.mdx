<SlideLayout title="Making Ω × ℝ into a measure space">

The region $R(f) \subseteq \Omega \times \mathbb{R}$ lives in a product space. We need a measure on it.

**Step 2 (Equivalent version).** We define a <Term k="productMeasure">product measure</Term> on $\Omega \times \mathbb{R}$ by assigning

$$|X \times Y| = |X| \times |Y|$$

to any measurable $X \subseteq \Omega$ and $Y \subseteq \mathbb{R}$. This pre-measure extends to a full measure on $\Omega \times \mathbb{R}$ by Carathéodory's theorem (which we used to build Borel measures in the previous chapter).

<Callout kind="remark" title="Intuition">
On $\Omega = \mathbb{R}$ with Borel measure, the product space $\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R} = \mathbb{R}^2$ gets the usual 2-dimensional Borel measure — the measure of a rectangle $[a,b] \times [c,d]$ is $(b-a)(d-c)$, which is its area. So "volume of the region under the graph" really is the usual area for functions $\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$.
</Callout>

**Step 3 (Equivalent version).** For each nonneg measurable $f \colon \Omega \to [0,+\infty]$:

$$\int_\Omega f \; d\mu \defeq |R(f)|.$$

This is well-defined whenever $R(f)$ is measurable in $\Omega \times \mathbb{R}$.

</SlideLayout>
