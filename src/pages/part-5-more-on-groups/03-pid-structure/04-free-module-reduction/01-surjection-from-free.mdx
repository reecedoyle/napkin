<SlideLayout title="Reducing to free modules — the surjection trick">

The proof of the structure theorem has two main steps. Step 1 reduces everything to linear algebra over $R$.

Suppose $M$ is generated by $d$ elements $g_1, \dots, g_d$. Map the free module $R^{\oplus d}$ onto $M$ by sending the $i$-th basis vector $e_i$ to $g_i$:
$$\phi \colon R^{\oplus d} \twoheadrightarrow M.$$

Let $K = \ker(\phi) \subseteq R^{\oplus d}$. By the first isomorphism theorem:
$$M \cong R^{\oplus d} / K.$$

So understanding $M$ reduces to understanding the submodule $K$ of the <Term k="freeModule">free module</Term> $R^{\oplus d}$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="K is also finitely generated">
Because $R$ is a PID, $R^{\oplus d}$ is <Term k="noetherianModule">Noetherian</Term>, so every submodule — in particular $K$ — is finitely generated. Thus there is also a surjection $R^{\oplus f} \twoheadrightarrow K$.
</Callout>

Composing the two surjections gives a linear map $T \colon R^{\oplus f} \to R^{\oplus d}$ whose <Term k="cokernel">cokernel</Term> is $M$:
$$M \cong R^{\oplus d} / \operatorname{im}(T).$$

</SlideLayout>
