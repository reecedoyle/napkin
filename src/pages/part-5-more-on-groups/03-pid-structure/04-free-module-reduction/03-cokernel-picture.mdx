<SlideLayout title="M as a cokernel — the key picture">

Putting it together, we have reduced the problem to linear algebra:

<Callout kind="remark" title="The key reduction">
Every finitely generated $R$-module $M$ over a PID $R$ is the <Term k="cokernel">cokernel</Term> of some linear map $T \colon R^{\oplus f} \to R^{\oplus d}$:
$$M \cong R^{\oplus d} / \operatorname{im}(T).$$
</Callout>

Step 2 of the proof will show that, by choosing the right bases for $R^{\oplus f}$ and $R^{\oplus d}$, we can put $T$ into **Smith normal form** — a diagonal matrix
$$T \sim \operatorname{diag}(s_1, s_2, \dots, s_k, 0, \dots, 0)$$
with $s_1 \mid s_2 \mid \dots \mid s_k$.

Once $T$ is in Smith form, the cokernel reads off immediately:
$$M \cong R/(s_1) \oplus R/(s_2) \oplus \dots \oplus R/(s_k) \oplus R^{\oplus (d-k)}.$$

This is exactly the invariant form of the structure theorem.

</SlideLayout>
