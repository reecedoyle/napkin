<SlideLayout title="Direct sums of Noetherian modules are Noetherian">

To justify that $K$ is finitely generated, we need:

<Callout kind="lemma" title="Direct sum of Noetherian modules is Noetherian">
If $M$ and $N$ are <Term k="noetherianModule">Noetherian</Term> $R$-modules, then $M \oplus N$ is also Noetherian.
</Callout>

The naive guess — that any submodule $L \subseteq M \oplus N$ splits as $P \oplus Q$ — fails. For example $M = N = \ZZ$ has the submodule $L = \{(n,n) \mid n \in \ZZ\}$ which is not a direct sum.

The correct proof uses an asymmetric construction. Define:
$$A = \{x \in M \mid (x, 0) \in L\}, \quad B = \{y \in N \mid \exists x : (x,y) \in L\}.$$

Both $A \subseteq M$ and $B \subseteq N$ are submodules, so they are finitely generated (since $M$ and $N$ are Noetherian). Generators for $A$ and $B$ together generate all of $L$.
