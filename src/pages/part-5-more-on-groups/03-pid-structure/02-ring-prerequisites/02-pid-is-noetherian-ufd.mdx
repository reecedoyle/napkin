<SlideLayout title="PIDs are Noetherian UFDs">

<Callout kind="proposition" title="PIDs are Noetherian UFDs">
If $R$ is a <Term k="pid">PID</Term>, then $R$ is <Term k="noetherian">Noetherian</Term> and also a <Term k="ufd">UFD</Term>.
</Callout>

**Why Noetherian?** Any ideal $I \subseteq R$ is principal: $I = (a)$ for some $a$. So there is no strictly ascending infinite chain — every ideal is already finitely generated by one element.

**Why a UFD?** The proof repeats the argument for $\ZZ$. Because every ideal is principal, the ideal $(a, b)$ is also principal — its generator is $\gcd(a, b)$. Using this GCD one extracts a unique factorisation by the usual divisibility argument.

This gives us the Chinese Remainder Theorem for ring elements:

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Chinese Remainder Theorem for rings">
Let $R$ be a PID and $m, n \in R$ with $(m) + (n) = (1)$ (i.e. $m$ and $n$ are **relatively prime**). Then
$$R/(mn) \cong R/(m) \times R/(n).$$
</Callout>

The proof copies the familiar proof for $\ZZ$: write $am + bn = 1$, then the map $(r, s) \mapsto r \cdot bn + s \cdot am$ is an isomorphism.

</SlideLayout>
