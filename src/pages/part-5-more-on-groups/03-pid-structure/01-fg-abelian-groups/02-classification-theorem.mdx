The examples from the previous slide turn out to be the only examples:

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups">
Let $G$ be a <Term k="fgAbelian">finitely generated abelian group</Term>. Then there exist an integer $r \geq 0$ and prime powers $q_1, \dots, q_m$ (not necessarily distinct) such that
$$G \cong \ZZ^{\oplus r} \oplus \Zc{q_1} \oplus \dots \oplus \Zc{q_m}.$$
This decomposition is unique up to permutation of the $\Zc{q_i}$.
</Callout>

The integer $r$ is called the <Term k="abelianRank">**rank**</Term> of $G$; it counts the "free" copies of $\ZZ$.

This theorem is actually a corollary of a much more general fact — the **PID structure theorem** — which applies to any finitely generated module over any PID. Our chapter proves that general theorem, and the abelian group classification falls out as a special case (take $R = \ZZ$).
