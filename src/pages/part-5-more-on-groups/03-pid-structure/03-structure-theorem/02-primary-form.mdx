<SlideLayout title="Structure theorem — primary decomposition form">

The Chinese Remainder Theorem lets us refine the invariant form further by splitting each $R/(s_i)$ along its prime factors:

<Callout kind="corollary" title="Structure theorem — primary form">
Let $R$ be a <Term k="pid">PID</Term> and $M$ a finitely generated $R$-module. Then
$$M \cong R^{\oplus r} \oplus R/(q_1) \oplus R/(q_2) \oplus \dots \oplus R/(q_m)$$
where each $q_i = p_i^{e_i}$ for some <Term k="primeElement">prime element</Term> $p_i \in R$ and integer $e_i \geq 1$.
</Callout>

**Proof sketch.** Factor each invariant factor $s_i$ into prime powers. Apply CRT: $R/(p^a q^b) \cong R/(p^a) \times R/(q^b)$ when $(p)$ and $(q)$ are coprime prime ideals.

The quantities $r$ and $q_1, \dots, q_m$ are unique up to permutation (we prove this in the uniqueness section).

<Callout kind="remark" title="Two forms of one theorem">
- **Invariant form**: summands linked by $s_1 \mid s_2 \mid \cdots$; good for theoretical arguments.
- **Primary form**: summands are prime-power cyclic modules; good for concrete computations.
</Callout>
