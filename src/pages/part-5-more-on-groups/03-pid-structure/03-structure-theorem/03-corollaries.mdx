<SlideLayout title="What the structure theorem explains">

The PID structure theorem is a single result with several famous corollaries:

<Callout kind="corollary" title="Classification of finitely generated abelian groups">
Every <Term k="fgAbelian">finitely generated abelian group</Term> is isomorphic to $\ZZ^{\oplus r} \oplus \Zc{q_1} \oplus \dots \oplus \Zc{q_m}$ with the $q_i$ prime powers.
Take $R = \ZZ$.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="corollary" title="All finite-dimensional vector spaces are isomorphic">
Every finite-dimensional vector space over a field $k$ is isomorphic to $k^{\oplus n}$ for some $n$.
Take $R = k$ (a field is a PID). Every ideal is $(0)$ or $(1)$, so every $k$-module is free.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="corollary" title="Jordan and Frobenius normal forms">
Let $T \colon V \to V$ be a linear map. Viewing $V$ as a $k[x]$-module (where $x$ acts by $T$), the structure theorem gives a decomposition. This yields Jordan form (if $k$ is algebraically closed) or Frobenius normal form (for general $k$).
</Callout>

All four corollaries from the chapter introduction follow from one proof.

</SlideLayout>
