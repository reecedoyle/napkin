Here is a beautiful application of the Sylow theorems — a counting argument that rules out certain group structures entirely.

<Callout kind="proposition" title="Triple product of primes">
If $|G| = pqr$ is the product of three distinct primes, then $G$ must have a normal <Term k="sylowSubgroup">Sylow subgroup</Term>.
</Callout>

**Proof idea.** Assume $p < q < r$ and suppose for contradiction that $n_p, n_q, n_r > 1$.

Since $n_r \mid pq$ and $n_r \equiv 1 \pmod{r}$, and $r > p, q$, we get $n_r = pq$.

Any two distinct Sylow subgroups for the same prime intersect trivially (by Lagrange, since their orders are distinct prime powers). So the non-identity elements from different Sylow subgroups don't overlap.

Counting distinct non-identity elements: at least $n_p(p-1) + n_q(q-1) + n_r(r-1) \geq (1+p)(p-1) + (1+q)(q-1) + pq(r-1)$, which exceeds $pqr = |G|$. Contradiction.
