<SlideLayout title="Classification of finite simple groups">

Since every finite group is built from simple groups via composition series, classifying all finite simple groups is a central goal. Remarkably, this classification is complete.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Classification of finite simple groups (CFSG)">
Every finite simple group is one of:
- $\Zc{p}$ for $p$ prime,
- the alternating group $A_n$ for $n \geq 5$,
- a **group of Lie type** (families analogous to matrix groups over finite fields), or
- one of **26 sporadic groups** that fit into none of the above families.
</Callout>

The proof spans tens of thousands of journal pages and was completed around 2004.

The two largest sporadic groups have playful names. The <Term k="babyMonster">**baby monster group**</Term> has order approximately $4 \times 10^{33}$. The <Term k="monsterGroup">**monster group**</Term> (also called the **friendly giant**) has order approximately $8 \times 10^{53}$ and contains 20 of the 26 sporadic groups as subquotients — these 20 are called the **happy family**.

Math is weird.
