Just as primes are integers that cannot be factored further, simple groups are the "atoms" of group theory.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Simple group">
A <Term k="simpleGroup">**simple group**</Term> is a group with no <Term k="normalSubgroup">normal subgroups</Term> other than the trivial group and itself.
</Callout>

The cyclic group <Term k="Zc">$\Zc{p}$</Term> is simple precisely when $p$ is prime — its only subgroups are $\{0\}$ and $\Zc{p}$ itself, and for abelian groups every subgroup is normal.

For $n \geq 5$, the **alternating group** $A_n$ (even permutations in $S_n$) is also simple. This is the key obstruction to solving quintic equations by radicals.
