<SlideLayout title="Steps 3 and 4 — Counting n_p">

Let $\mathcal{S}$ be the set of all Sylow $p$-subgroups, so $|\mathcal{S}| = n_p$.

**Step 3: $n_p \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$.**

Fix a Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$ and let $P$ act on $\mathcal{S}$ by conjugation.

- $P$ is a $p$-group, so fixed points account for $n_p \pmod{p}$.
- We claim $P$ is the **only** fixed point. If $Q$ is also a fixed point ($xQx^{-1} = Q$ for all $x \in P$), then both $P$ and $Q$ lie in the <Term k="normalizer">normalizer</Term> $N_G(Q)$. Applying Step 2 inside $N_G(Q)$ shows $P$ and $Q$ are conjugate in $N_G(Q)$, hence $P = Q$.

So exactly $1$ fixed point, giving $n_p \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$.

**Step 4: $n_p \mid m$.**

Let $G$ act on $\mathcal{S}$ by conjugation. Step 2 says this action has one orbit (all Sylow subgroups are conjugate). By orbit-stabilizer, $n_p = |\mathcal{S}|$ divides $|G| = p^n m$. Since $n_p \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$, we get $\gcd(n_p, p^n) = 1$, so $n_p$ divides $m$.
