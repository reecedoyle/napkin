<SlideLayout title="Conjugation — G acting on itself">

The most important self-action of a group is conjugation.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Conjugation action">
A group $G$ acts on itself by **conjugation**:
$$g \colon h \mapsto ghg\inv.$$
The orbit of $h$ under this action is called the <Term k="conjugacyClass">**conjugacy class**</Term> of $h$.
</Callout>

The element $ghg^{-1}$ is called the <Term k="conjugate">conjugate of $h$ by $g$</Term>. At first this looks artificial, but the next slide reveals why it's natural.

Note that the stabilizer of $h$ under conjugation is exactly the set of $g$ that commute with $h$: $g \cdot h = ghg^{-1} = h \iff gh = hg$. This is the **centralizer** $C_G(h) = \{g \in G \mid gh = hg\}$.

</SlideLayout>
