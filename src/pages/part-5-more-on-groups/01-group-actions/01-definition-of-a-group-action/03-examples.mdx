<Callout kind="example" title="Examples of group actions">
- <Term k="Zc">$\Zc{4}$</Term> acts on the 7×7 checkerboard colorings by rotation (the AIME example).
- $\Zc{4} = \langle r \rangle$ acts on <Term k="RR">$\RR^2$</Term> by $r \cdot (x,y) = (y,-x)$ — a $90°$ rotation.
- The <Term k="Dn">dihedral group $D_{2n}$</Term> acts on the set of colorings of the vertices of a regular $n$-gon.
- The <Term k="Sn">symmetric group $S_n$</Term> acts on $\{1, 2, \ldots, n\}$ by applying the permutation: $\sigma \cdot x \defeq \sigma(x)$.
- Any group $G$ acts on itself by left multiplication: $g \cdot g' \defeq g \star g'$.
</Callout>

The last example — $G$ acting on itself — is deceptively powerful. It is the key ingredient in Cayley's theorem, which embeds every finite group into a symmetric group.
