There is a cleaner way to say what a group action is.

<Callout kind="proposition" title="Actions as homomorphisms">
A group action of $G$ on $X$ is equivalent to a <Term k="homomorphism">group homomorphism</Term>
$$\phi \colon G \to S_X,$$
where $S_X$ is the <Term k="Sn">symmetric group</Term> of all bijections $X \to X$.
</Callout>

Given an action, define $\phi(g) = (x \mapsto g \cdot x)$. The compatibility axiom $(g_1 g_2) \cdot x = g_1 \cdot (g_2 \cdot x)$ is exactly $\phi(g_1 g_2) = \phi(g_1) \circ \phi(g_2)$, i.e., $\phi$ is a homomorphism. The identity axiom $1_G \cdot x = x$ says $\phi(1_G) = \id_X$, so the identity lands on the identity permutation.

Conversely, any homomorphism $\phi \colon G \to S_X$ defines an action via $g \cdot x \defeq \phi(g)(x)$.
