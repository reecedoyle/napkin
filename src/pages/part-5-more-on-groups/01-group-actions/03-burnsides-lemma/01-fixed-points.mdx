<SlideLayout title="Fixed points of an action">

To count orbits we introduce a key quantity for each group element.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Fixed points of g">
For an element $g \in G$ acting on $X$, define
$$\FixPt(g) \defeq \{ x \in X \mid g \cdot x = x \}.$$
This is the set of points of $X$ that are fixed by $g$.
</Callout>

Note the contrast: the <Term k="stabilizer">stabilizer</Term> $\Stab_G(x)$ fixes a point and varies over $G$; here $\FixPt(g)$ fixes a group element and varies over $X$.

There is a double-counting identity connecting the two:
$$\sum_{g \in G} |\FixPt(g)| = \sum_{x \in X} |\Stab_G(x)|.$$
Both sides count pairs $(g, x)$ with $g \cdot x = x$, just in different order.

This double-count is the engine behind Burnside's lemma.
