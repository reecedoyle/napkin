<Callout kind="definition" title="Orbit">
The <Term k="orbit">**orbit**</Term> of a point $x \in X$ under the action of $G$ is the set of all points reachable from $x$:
$$\OO_x \defeq \{ g \cdot x \mid g \in G \} \subseteq X.$$
</Callout>

Orbits partition $X$ into disjoint equivalence classes — every element of $X$ belongs to exactly one orbit. The AIME problem asks for the number of orbits.

For example, if $G = \Zc{4}$ acts on the checkerboard colorings by rotation:
- All four rotations of a given configuration land in the same orbit.
- Configurations that cannot be rotated into each other are in different orbits.

The number of orbits is the answer to "how many truly distinct configurations are there?" — this is what Burnside's lemma computes.
