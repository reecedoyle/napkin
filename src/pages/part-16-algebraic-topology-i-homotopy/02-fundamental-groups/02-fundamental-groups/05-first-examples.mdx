None of these are trivial to *prove* yet, but try to see intuitively why the claimed answer "should" be correct.

<Callout kind="example" title="Three fundamental groups">
- The fundamental group of <Term k="CC">$\mathbb{C}$</Term> is trivial: every loop in the plane is null-homotopic (imagine it's a piece of rope and reel it in).
- The fundamental group of $\mathbb{C} \setminus \{0\}$ — the punctured plane — with any basepoint is $\mathbb{Z}$! A loop is essentially determined by its <Term k="fg64WindingNumber">winding number</Term>: how many times it circles the puncture.
- Similarly, the fundamental group of <Term k="complexUnitCircle">$S^1$</Term> (the unit circle) is also $\mathbb{Z}$, again via winding number.
</Callout>

By symmetry, the basepoint doesn't matter for any of these three spaces (we'll see exactly why on a later slide).
