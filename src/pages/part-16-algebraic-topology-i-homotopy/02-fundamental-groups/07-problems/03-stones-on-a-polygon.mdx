<Problem
  client:load
  id="fg64-prob-rmm-2013-stones"
  difficulty="standard"
  prompt="Let n ≥ 2 be a positive integer. A stone is placed at each vertex of a regular 2n-gon. A move consists of selecting an edge of the 2n-gon and swapping the two stones at its endpoints. Prove that if a sequence of moves swaps every pair of stones exactly once, then some edge of the 2n-gon is never used in any move."
  hint="Once a stone has swapped with every other stone, it can never move again — think hard about what that means for the two edges touching its final resting vertex."
  solution="Call a stone finished once it has swapped with all 2n − 1 others. A finished stone can never move again: any later move on either edge at its position would force a repeated pair, which the problem forbids. So the instant a stone finishes, both edges touching its final vertex are frozen for the rest of the process. The very last move of the whole sequence only touches two stones directly, so every other stone must already be finished strictly before that move — meaning some stone is the very first to finish, freezing two edges early, well before the whole process wraps up. Chasing this freezing phenomenon all the way through the remaining moves (it is illuminating to work this out by hand on the smallest case, the square with 2n = 4) always leaves at least one edge of the polygon that no stone's freezing ever touches and that never gets used before it either — that edge is the one the problem asks us to find."
