<Problem
  client:load
  id="fg64-prob-hanging-picture"
  difficulty="standard"
  prompt="How do you hang a picture frame on a wall with two nails and one string, such that if you remove either nail, the picture frame falls?"
  hint="Represent the string's path around the two nails as an element of the fundamental group of the plane with two points removed, which is free on two generators — one loop per nail. Think about the element aba⁻¹b⁻¹."
  solution="Think of the wall with two nails as the plane with two punctures, so its fundamental group is the free group on two generators a and b, where a is a small loop around nail A and b a small loop around nail B. Wind the string around the nails so it traces the commutator a∗b∗ā∗b̄ — loop around A, then around B, then around A backwards, then around B backwards. If nail A is removed, the puncture at A disappears, so a and ā each become null-homotopic, and the whole path collapses to b∗b̄, which is also null-homotopic — so the string slides right off and the picture falls. The same thing happens if nail B alone is removed. But with both nails present, a∗b∗ā∗b̄ is a nontrivial element of the free group ⟨a,b⟩ — commutators in a free group on two generators are never the identity — so the string stays hooked around both nails and the picture hangs."
