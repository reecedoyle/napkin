<SlideLayout title="Telling spaces apart">

Topologists like to say they can't tell the difference between a coffee cup and a doughnut — both are, after all, homeomorphic (a solid with one handle-hole). So how do you tell *anything* apart?

One strategy: find *invariants* — quantities attached to a space that agree whenever two spaces are <Term k="homeomorphismTop">homeomorphic</Term>. Compare with groups: two groups are clearly non-isomorphic if they have different orders, or if one is simple and the other isn't.

Two major invariants for a <Term k="topologicalSpace">topological space</Term> $X$ are:

- **Homology groups** $H_1(X), H_2(X), \dots$ — hard to define, easier to compute.
- **Homotopy groups** $\pi_1(X), \pi_2(X), \dots$ — easy to define, harder to compute.

This chapter is about the first and most important homotopy group: the **fundamental group** $\pi_1$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Where we're headed">
We'll build $\pi_1(X)$ out of loops in $X$ that start and end at the same point, glued together like a group. Along the way we'll see why $\pi_1(\mathbb{C}) $ is trivial while $\pi_1(\mathbb{C} \setminus \{0\})$ is a copy of <Term k="ZZ">$\mathbb{Z}$</Term> — the "winding number" group.
</Callout>
