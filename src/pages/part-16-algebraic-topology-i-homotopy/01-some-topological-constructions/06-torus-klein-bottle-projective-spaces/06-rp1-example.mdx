The $n=1$ case is small enough to see completely, and it's a good sanity check on all three definitions from the last slide.

<Callout kind="example" title="ℝℙ¹, three ways">
$\RP^1$ is $S^1$ modulo the relation that identifies antipodal points. Projecting onto a tangent line, we get a copy of $\RR$ plus a single point at infinity (corresponding to the one direction parallel to that tangent line).

So the points of $\RP^1$ come in two forms: $(x:1)$, which we think of as the real number $x$, and $(1:0)$, which we think of as $1/0 = \infty$. Literally,
$$
\RP^1 = \RR \cup \{\infty\}.
$$
</Callout>

Topologically, $\RP^1 \cong S^1$ — it's "the real line with its two ends fused together into a single point at infinity." (This also matches the boundary of $\RP^2$ from the last slide's edge-square picture: a circle with antipodal points identified is exactly what a single edge, doubled back on itself, produces.)
