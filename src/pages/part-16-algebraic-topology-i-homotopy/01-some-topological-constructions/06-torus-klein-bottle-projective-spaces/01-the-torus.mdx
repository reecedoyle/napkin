We now walk through four of the most important spaces built from everything above. First: the **torus**, which you can build by taking a square and gluing opposite edges together in matching directions — walk off the right edge, you reappear at the corresponding point on the left (think *Asteroids*).

<Callout kind="remark" title="Torus = square / edge identifications">
The <Term k="tc63Torus">torus</Term> is $(\RR/\ZZ)^2 \cong S^1 \times S^1$. Note that all four corners of the square get identified together to a single point.
</Callout>

You can realize this in actual 3-space: treat the square as a sheet of paper, tape the solid edges together to make a cylinder, then bend the cylinder around and fuse the two dashed-edge circles at its ends — that's the familiar donut surface.
