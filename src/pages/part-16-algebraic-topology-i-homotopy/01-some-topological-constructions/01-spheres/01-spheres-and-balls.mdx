This chapter is a toolbox: a handful of standard constructions — spheres, quotients, products, wedges, CW complexes — that show up constantly once we start doing algebraic topology. None of it is deep on its own; the payoff is having the vocabulary ready.

We start with the two shapes you already know from calculus, but now viewed purely as topological spaces.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Sphere and closed ball">
The **<Term k="tc63Sphere">$n$-sphere</Term>** is
$$
S^n = \left\{ (x_0, \dots, x_n) \mid x_0^2 + \dots + x_n^2 = 1 \right\} \subset \RR^{n+1}.
$$
The **<Term k="tc63ClosedBall">closed ball</Term>** is
$$
D^{n+1} = \left\{ (x_0, \dots, x_n) \mid x_0^2 + \dots + x_n^2 \le 1 \right\} \subset \RR^{n+1}.
$$
</Callout>

So $S^n$ is only the *surface* — the shell — while $D^{n+1}$ is the solid ball including its interior. Note the index shift: $S^n$ is the boundary of $D^{n+1}$, one dimension up.

Two small cases are worth having a mental picture of immediately: $D^2$ is an ordinary disk in the plane, and $S^1$ is the unit circle bounding it. One dimension down, $S^0$ is just the two points $\{-1, 1\}$, and $D^1$ is the interval $[-1, 1]$ that they bound.
