<SlideLayout title="Exercise — the open ball is homeomorphic to ℝⁿ">

Removing the boundary sphere from the closed ball leaves the **open ball** $D^n \setminus S^{n-1}$ — every point strictly inside, none on the shell. Intuitively this should feel "the same size" as all of $\RR^n$: both are unbounded in the sense that you can never quite reach an edge.

<Callout kind="exercise" title="Open ball ≅ ℝⁿ">
Show that the open ball $D^n \setminus S^{n-1}$ is <Term k="homeomorphismTop">homeomorphic</Term> to $\RR^n$.
</Callout>

<ProofReveal
  client:load
  id="tc63-sph-proof-open-ball-rn"
  prompt="Show that the open ball D^n \ S^{n-1} is homeomorphic to ℝ^n."
  solution="Use the radial stretching map f(x) = x / (1 - |x|), sending a point x in the open ball to a point of ℝⁿ. As |x| → 1 (approaching the missing boundary), the denominator → 0, so |f(x)| → ∞ — the map stretches the open ball out to cover all of ℝⁿ. It is continuous, and it has a continuous inverse g(y) = y / (1 + |y|), which maps all of ℝⁿ back into the open ball (check |g(y)| < 1 always). Since f and g are mutually inverse continuous maps, f is a homeomorphism."
