<SlideLayout title="The product topology">

You've already met $\RR \times \RR = \RR^2$ as a set of pairs. What's the natural topology to put on a product of two topological spaces?

<Callout kind="definition" title="Product topology">
Given topological spaces $X$ and $Y$, the **product topology** on $X \times Y$ is the space whose
- points are pairs $(x, y)$ with $x \in X$, $y \in Y$, and
- topology has as a **<Term k="basisTop">basis</Term>** the sets $U \times V$, for $U \subseteq X$ open and $V \subseteq Y$ open.
</Callout>

That is: the basic open sets of $X \times Y$ are exactly the "rectangles" built from an open set of each factor, and a general open set of <Term k="productSpace">$X \times Y$</Term> is a union of such rectangles. This does exactly what you'd hope: $\RR \times \RR$ with the product topology recovers the ordinary topology on the plane.
