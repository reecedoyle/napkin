  hint="For the Mobius strip, take a square and glue just ONE pair of opposite edges with a half-twist — the other pair is left free and becomes the strip's boundary edge. Count the resulting 0-, 1-, and 2-cells (and note: if you instead attach a 2-cell with no free boundary edge, you close the surface up into ℝℙ², not the strip). For R and R^n, tile by unit intervals or unit cubes with vertices at the integers (or integer lattice points)."
  solution="(a) Mobius strip: build it from the square [0,1]² by gluing only the left and right edges with a flip, (0,y) ~ (1, 1−y); the top and bottom edges are left unglued and together form the strip's single boundary circle. This gives two 0-cells (the two corner classes), three 1-cells (the glued side edge plus the free top and bottom edges), and one 2-cell (the square), so its Euler characteristic is χ = 2 − 3 + 1 = 0 — consistent with the strip deformation-retracting onto its core circle S¹. Beware the tempting wrong answer of a single loop a with a 2-cell attached by the word 'aa': that leaves no free boundary and closes the surface up into the projective plane ℝℙ², which has χ = 1, not the Mobius strip. (b) R: take a 0-cell at every integer n, and a 1-cell joining each n to n+1. This infinite (but locally finite) 1-dimensional CW complex is exactly the real line. (c) R^n: take a 0-cell at every point of the integer lattice Z^n, a 1-cell joining each pair of adjacent lattice points, a 2-cell filling each unit square face, and so on up to n-cells filling each unit hypercube — the standard cubical CW structure on R^n."
