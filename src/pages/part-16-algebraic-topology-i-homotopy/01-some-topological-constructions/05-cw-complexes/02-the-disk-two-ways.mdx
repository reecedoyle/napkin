<SlideLayout title="Example — the disk, two ways">

The same space can be realized as a CW complex in more than one way — with more or fewer cells. Here's $D^2$ built two different ways.

<Callout kind="example" title="D² with 2+2+1 cells">
Start with $X^0$ having two points $e^0_a, e^0_b$. Join them with two 1-cells $e^1_c, e^1_d$, whose endpoints get attached to distinct points of $X^0$ — so $X^1 \cong S^1$. Finally weld in one 2-cell $e^2$, whose boundary fits around that $S^1$.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" title="D² with 1+1+1 cells">
Start with a single point $e^0$. Attach one 1-cell $e^1$, with *both* its endpoints glued to that single point (so $X^1 \cong S^1$, using just one loop). Then weld in one 2-cell $e^2$ as before.
</Callout>
