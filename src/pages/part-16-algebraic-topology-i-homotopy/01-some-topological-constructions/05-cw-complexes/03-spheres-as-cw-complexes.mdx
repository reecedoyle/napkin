<SlideLayout title="Spheres as CW complexes">

Two different CW recipes for $S^n$ ($n \geq 1$), each useful in different arguments later on.

<Callout kind="example" title="Two cells: the minimal recipe">
Take a single point $e^0$ for $X^0$, then take $D^n$ and weld its *entire* boundary into that one point $e^0$. We already saw this at the very start of the chapter: $S^2$ is what you get fusing the boundary of $D^2$ to a point.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" title="Hemispheres: the inductive recipe">
Alternatively, build $S^n$ inductively with two cells in *each* dimension up to $n$: $S^0$ is two points; $S^1$ is obtained by joining those two points with two 1-cells (two arcs); $S^2$ is obtained by gluing two hemispheres (each a 2-cell) with that $S^1$ as their shared equator; and so on.
</Callout>

Both recipes build the same space $S^n$ — they're just different bookkeeping for how many cells you use and in what dimensions.
