<SlideLayout title="p♯ is always injective">

<Callout kind="proposition" title="Injectivity of p♯">
Let $p \colon (E, e_0) \to (B, b_0)$ be a covering projection of path-connected spaces. Then $p_\sharp \colon \pi_1(E, e_0) \to \pi_1(B, b_0)$ is **injective**. Hence $p_\sharp\im(\pi_1(E, e_0))$ is an isomorphic copy of $\pi_1(E, e_0)$, sitting inside $\pi_1(B, b_0)$ as a subgroup.
</Callout>

  prompt="Prove that p♯ is injective, i.e. that its kernel is trivial."
  solution="It suffices to show: if γ is a null-homotopic loop in B, its lifting is null-homotopic in E. By definition, a null-homotopic loop admits a homotopy F: [0,1] × [0,1] → B from γ to the constant loop 1_B. Lifting this homotopy (using the lifting-homotopies remark from §2) gives a homotopy F̃: [0,1] × [0,1] → E from γ̃ to some lift of the constant loop 1_B. But the constant loop at e₀ is itself a lift of 1_B, and lifts starting at a given point are unique — so F̃ exhibits γ̃ as null-homotopic. Hence the kernel of p♯ contains only the identity, so p♯ is injective."
