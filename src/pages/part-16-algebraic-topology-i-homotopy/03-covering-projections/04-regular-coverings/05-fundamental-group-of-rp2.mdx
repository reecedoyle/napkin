The lifting correspondence machinery from §3 lets us compute $\pi_1(\RP^2)$ immediately.

<Callout kind="example" title="Fundamental group of ℝP²">
We saw the covering projection $S^2 \to \RP^2$, with every fiber of size two. Since $S^2$ is simply connected, it's the universal cover, so the lifting correspondence gives a bijection between $\pi_1(\RP^2)$ and a set of size two:
$$
\left\lvert \pi_1(\RP^2) \right\rvert = 2.
$$
There's only one group of order two, so $\pi_1(\RP^2) \cong \Zc 2$.
</Callout>

This is a strikingly clean way to pin down a fundamental group without ever writing down an explicit loop: just count the fiber of a known universal cover.
