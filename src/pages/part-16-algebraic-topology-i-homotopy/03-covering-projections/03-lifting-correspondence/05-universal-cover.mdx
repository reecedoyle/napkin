<SlideLayout title="Universal covers">

The case where $\Phi$ is a bijection — $E$ simply connected — is important enough to name.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Universal cover">
A <Term k="universalCover">universal cover</Term> of a space $B$ is a covering projection $p \colon E \to B$ where $E$ is simply connected (and in particular path-connected).
</Callout>

When $p$ is understood from context, people often just say "$E$ is the universal cover of $B$" and leave $p$ implicit.

This is where the payoff is: if $E$ is a universal cover of $B$, the lifting correspondence gives a genuine **bijection**
$$
\Phi \colon \pi_1(B, b_0) \leftrightarrow p\inv(b_0).
$$
Computing $\pi_1(B)$ reduces to understanding a single fiber of a (hopefully simpler) space $E$.
