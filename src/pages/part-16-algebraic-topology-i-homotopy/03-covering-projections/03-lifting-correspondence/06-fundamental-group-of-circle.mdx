We finally have the tool to compute the thing we couldn't compute before.

<Callout kind="example" title="Fundamental group of S¹">
Our usual $p \colon \RR \to S^1$ has $\RR$ simply connected, so it's a universal cover of $S^1$. The fiber of any point in $S^1$ is a copy of $\ZZ$ — naturally in bijection with loops in $S^1$, since a loop that winds $n$ times lifts to a path ending at the integer $n$.

You can check that the bijection $\Phi \colon \pi_1(S^1) \leftrightarrow \ZZ$ is in fact a group homomorphism, once we equip $\ZZ$ with ordinary addition. A bijective homomorphism is an isomorphism, so
$$
\pi_1(S^1) \cong \ZZ.
$$
</Callout>

This matches the intuition: a loop in $S^1$ is entirely characterized (up to homotopy) by its net winding number, and winding numbers add when you concatenate loops.
