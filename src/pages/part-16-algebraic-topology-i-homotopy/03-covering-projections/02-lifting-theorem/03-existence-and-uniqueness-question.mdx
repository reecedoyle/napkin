<SlideLayout title="Exactly one lifting per starting point">

Take a path $\gamma$ in $S^1$ with $\gamma(0) = 1 \in \CC$. Convince yourself that once we select an integer $n \in \RR$ as a starting point, there is exactly **one** lifting of $\gamma$ starting at $n$.

Why? Locally, an evenly covered neighborhood $U$ of $\gamma(0)$ has finitely (or countably) many disjoint sheets in $\RR$ sitting over it, and $n$ picks out exactly one of them. As $\gamma$ moves, the lift is forced to stay in that one sheet — mimicking $\gamma$'s motion — until $\gamma$ leaves $U$, at which point the next evenly covered neighborhood again forces a single choice. There's never a branch point.
