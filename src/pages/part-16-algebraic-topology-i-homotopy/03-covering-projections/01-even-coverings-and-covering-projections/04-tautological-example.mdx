<SlideLayout title="The tautological covering">

Here is the silliest possible example of a covering projection — worth seeing once so you know what to rule out.

<Callout kind="example" title="Tautological covering projection">
Let $B$ be any space and take $n$ disconnected copies of it: formally $E = B \times \{1, \dots, n\}$, with the discrete topology on $\{1, \dots, n\}$. There is a tautological covering projection $E \to B$ by $(x, m) \mapsto x$ — we just project all $n$ copies down onto $B$ at once.
</Callout>

This works because *every* open set of $B$ is evenly covered: the preimage of $U$ is just $n$ disjoint copies of $U$, one in each layer.

It's not a very interesting example, though, because $E = B \times \{1, \dots, n\}$ is never <Term k="pathConnected">path-connected</Term> (assuming $n > 1$) — the $n$ layers have nothing to do with each other. The interesting examples are the ones where $E$ itself is connected.

</SlideLayout>
