<SlideLayout title="ℝ covers S¹">

Here's the interesting example. Take $p \colon \RR \to S^1$ by $\theta \mapsto e^{2\pi i \theta}$, viewing $S^1$ as the unit circle in $\CC$. This wraps the real line into an infinite helix and projects it straight down onto the circle.

<Callout kind="example" title="Covering projection of S¹">
The map $p \colon \RR \to S^1$, $\theta \mapsto e^{2\pi i \theta}$, is a covering projection.
</Callout>

Consider the point $1 \in S^1$. A small open arc $U$ around $1$ has preimage $p\inv(U) = \bigcup_{n \in \ZZ} (n - \eps, n + \eps)$ for small $\eps$: a disjoint union of small intervals, one around every integer, and $p$ maps each homeomorphically back onto $U$. So $U$ is evenly covered, and the same argument works around every point of $S^1$.

Not every open set works, though: if $U = S^1$ itself, its preimage under $p$ is all of $\RR$, which is not homeomorphic to $S^1$ (one is compact, the other isn't) — so $U = S^1$ is not evenly covered by $p$, even though $p$ is still a covering projection overall.
