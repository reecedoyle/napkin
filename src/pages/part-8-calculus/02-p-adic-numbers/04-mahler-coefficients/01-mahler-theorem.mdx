<SlideLayout title="Mahler's theorem — a basis for continuous functions">

One of the big surprises of $p$-adic analysis: we can describe **all** continuous functions $\ZZ_p \to \QQ_p$.

The building blocks are the <Term k="mahlerBasis">binomial coefficient functions</Term>
$$\binom{x}{n} \defeq \frac{x(x-1)\cdots(x-(n-1))}{n!}.$$

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Mahler's theorem">
Let $f \colon \ZZ_p \to \QQ_p$ be continuous. Define the **Mahler coefficients**
$$a_n = \sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k}(-1)^{n-k} f(k).$$
Then $a_n \to 0$ and $f(x) = \sum_{n \ge 0} a_n \binom{x}{n}$ for all $x \in \ZZ_p$.

Conversely, any sequence $(a_n)$ with $a_n \to 0$ defines a continuous function this way.
</Callout>

So continuous functions $\ZZ_p \to \QQ_p$ are in bijection with sequences $(a_0, a_1, a_2, \ldots)$ in $\QQ_p$ converging to $0$.

</SlideLayout>
