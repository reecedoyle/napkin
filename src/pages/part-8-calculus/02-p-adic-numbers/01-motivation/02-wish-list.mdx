<SlideLayout title="The p-adic wish-list">

Here is what we want from the $p$-adic numbers:

1. **Take mod $p^e$ for all $e$ at once.** No need to pick a modulus — a $p$-adic equation carries enough information to reduce mod $p^e$ for any $e$.
2. **Form a field.** The $p$-adic numbers <Term k="Qp">$\QQ_p$</Term> are a field, so $1/p$ makes sense. Contrast with $\mathbb{Z}/p^2\mathbb{Z}$, which isn't even an integral domain.
3. **Embed $\mathbb{Z}$ injectively.** Rather than the surjective collapse $\mathbb{Z} \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{Z}/p$, we get an injective map <Term k="ZinjZp">$\mathbb{Z} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Z}_p$</Term>. No information is discarded from $\mathbb{Z}$.
4. **Still ignore "irrelevant" data.** Just as mod $p$ focuses on one type of information, $\QQ_p$ zooms in on $p$-adic structure while becoming blind to others (e.g., the sign of a real number).
