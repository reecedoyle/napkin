<SlideLayout title="What are p-adic numbers?">

This is a **bonus chapter** at the intersection of algebra and analysis. Throughout, $p$ denotes a prime.

In elementary number theory we already wield two powerful tools:

1. **Taking modulo** $p$ or $p^e$ — reduce an equation to get a necessary condition.
2. **The $p$-adic valuation** $\nu_p$ — track divisibility by powers of $p$.

Both tools are indispensable in olympiad work. But each has a frustrating limitation: taking mod $p$ loses information (the map <Term k="surjtoZp">$\mathbb{Z} \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{Z}/p$</Term> is far from injective), while taking mod $p^2$ gives finer control at the cost of working in a ring that isn't even a field.

<Callout kind="remark" title="The p-adic promise">
The $p$-adic numbers $\QQ_p$ are designed to fix both problems at once: they carry "all mod $p^e$ information simultaneously" while remaining a field where $1/p$ makes sense.
</Callout>
