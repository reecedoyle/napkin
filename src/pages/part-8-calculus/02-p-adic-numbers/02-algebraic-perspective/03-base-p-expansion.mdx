<SlideLayout title="p-adic integers as infinite base-p expansions">

Every ordinary integer has a base-$p$ representation, like $50 = \overline{1212}_3 = 2 \cdot 3^0 + 1 \cdot 3^1 + 2 \cdot 3^2 + 1 \cdot 3^3$. For a $p$-adic integer, the same idea works — but the expansion is **infinite to the left**:
$$x = \sum_{k \ge 0} a_k p^k = \overline{\ldots a_2 a_1 a_0}_p, \quad a_k \in \{0, 1, \ldots, p-1\}.$$

So $1 + 3 + 3^2 + \cdots = \overline{\ldots 1111}_3$.

Amusingly, negative integers also have infinite expansions: $-4 = \overline{\ldots 222212}_3$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Think: Taylor series">
The right mental model is that a $p$-adic integer is a **Taylor series with base $p$**. Much of your intuition from power series in $K[\![X]\!]$ carries over to $\mathbb{Z}_p$ — including the next surprise.
</Callout>
