<SlideLayout title="ℤ₃ in action — examples">

Let $p = 3$. The integer $50$ embeds as:
$$50 = (2 \bmod 3,\; 5 \bmod 9,\; 23 \bmod 27,\; 50 \bmod 81,\; 50 \bmod 243,\; \ldots) \in \mathbb{Z}_3.$$

But <Term k="Zp">$\mathbb{Z}_3$</Term> has elements that don't come from any ordinary integer. For example:
$$(1 \bmod 3,\; 4 \bmod 9,\; 13 \bmod 27,\; 40 \bmod 81,\; \ldots)$$
is a valid compatible sequence. We can write it as $x = 1 + 3 + 3^2 + 3^3 + \cdots$ — we'll soon see this converges to $-\tfrac{1}{2}$ in the $3$-adic sense.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Expert corner: inverse limits">
Those familiar with category theory will recognize $\mathbb{Z}_p = \varprojlim\, \mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z}$, the inverse limit over the tower $\cdots \to \mathbb{Z}/p^3 \to \mathbb{Z}/p^2 \to \mathbb{Z}/p$.
</Callout>
