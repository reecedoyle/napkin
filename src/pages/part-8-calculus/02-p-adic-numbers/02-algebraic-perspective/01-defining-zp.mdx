<SlideLayout title="Defining ℤₚ — compatible residues">

The key idea: a $p$-adic integer "knows" all of its residues mod $p^e$ simultaneously, and those residues must be consistent with each other.

<Callout kind="definition" title="p-adic integer">
A **$p$-adic integer** is a sequence
$$x = (x_1 \bmod p,\; x_2 \bmod p^2,\; x_3 \bmod p^3,\; \ldots)$$
of residues satisfying the **compatibility relations**: $x_i \equiv x_j \pmod{p^i}$ for $i < j$.

The set <Term k="Zp">$\mathbb{Z}_p$</Term> of all $p$-adic integers forms a ring under componentwise addition and multiplication.
</Callout>

Every usual integer $n$ gives such a sequence: $(n \bmod p,\; n \bmod p^2,\; \ldots)$. So we get an injection $\mathbb{Z} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Z}_p$. But there are many more $p$-adic integers than ordinary ones.

</SlideLayout>
