We're finally ready to solve the poster problem from §1.

<Callout kind="example" title="USA TST 2002/2 — solution">
We compute $f_p(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{p-1} \frac{1}{(px+k)^2} \pmod{p^3}$.

Factor out $k^2$ and apply the <Term k="genBinomThm">generalized binomial theorem</Term> with $r = -2$:
$$f_p(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{p-1} \frac{1}{k^2}\left(1 + \frac{px}{k}\right)^{-2} = \sum_{k=1}^{p-1} \frac{1}{k^2} \sum_{n \ge 0} \binom{-2}{n}\left(\frac{px}{k}\right)^n.$$

Rearranging and keeping only terms with $n \le 2$ (higher powers have $p^3 \mid p^n$ for $n \ge 3$):
$$f_p(x) \equiv \sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac{1}{k^2} - 2x\left(\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac{1}{k^3}\right)p + 3x^2\left(\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac{1}{k^4}\right)p^2 \pmod{p^3}.$$

One checks (by symmetry $k \leftrightarrow p-k$) that $p^2 \mid \sum k^{-3}$ and $p \mid \sum k^{-4}$, making all $x$-dependent terms vanish. So $f_p(x)$ is indeed independent of $x$.
</Callout>
