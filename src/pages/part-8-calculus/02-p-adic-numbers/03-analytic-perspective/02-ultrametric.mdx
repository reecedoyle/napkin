<SlideLayout title="The ultrametric (strong triangle) inequality">

The $p$-adic absolute value satisfies something stronger than the usual triangle inequality.

<Callout kind="proposition" title="Ultrametric inequality">
For any $x, y \in \ZZ_p$:
$$\abs{x + y}_p \le \max\bigl\{\abs{x}_p,\, \abs{y}_p\bigr\}.$$
Moreover, **equality** holds whenever $\abs{x}_p \neq \abs{y}_p$.
</Callout>

This is called the <Term k="ultrametric">ultrametric</Term> (or strong triangle) inequality. It's much stronger than $\abs{x+y} \le \abs{x} + \abs{y}$.

One striking consequence: in an ultrametric space, **every triangle is isosceles**. The two largest sides are always equal.
