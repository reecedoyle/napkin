<SlideLayout title="Power series as functions">

If a <Term k="powerSeries">power series</Term> has radius of convergence $R > 0$, we can treat it as a function

$$f \colon (-R, R) \to \mathbb{R}, \quad f(h) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n h^n.$$

If $R = \infty$ we get a function $f \colon \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Series vs. function">
There is a subtle distinction here. A power series is initially just a sequence of coefficients $(a_0, a_1, a_2, \dots)$ — think of it like a polynomial. Cauchy–Hadamard lets us *also* read it as a function. We blur this distinction freely, just as we treat the polynomial $x^2 + 1$ both as a formal expression and as a function $x \mapsto x^2 + 1$.
</Callout>
