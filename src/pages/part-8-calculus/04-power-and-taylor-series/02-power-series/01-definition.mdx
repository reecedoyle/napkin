<Callout kind="definition" title="Power series">
A <Term k="powerSeries">power series</Term> is a formal expression

$$\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n z^n = a_0 + a_1 z + a_2 z^2 + \cdots$$

where $a_0, a_1, a_2, \dots \in \mathbb{R}$ are fixed coefficients and $z$ is a variable. (Convention: $0^0 = 1$ throughout.)
</Callout>

A power series is first an algebraic object — a sequence of coefficients. It becomes an analytic object when we plug in a specific number $h \in \mathbb{R}$ and ask whether $\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n h^n$ converges.

The Cauchy–Hadamard theorem gives a clean answer: there is always a <Term k="radiusOfConvergence">radius of convergence</Term> $R$ that determines exactly which values converge.
