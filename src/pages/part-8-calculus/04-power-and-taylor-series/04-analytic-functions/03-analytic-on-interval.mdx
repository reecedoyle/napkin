<Callout kind="proposition" title="Local analyticity extends to an interval">
Let $f \colon \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be <Term k="smoothFunction">smooth</Term>, and let $p \in \mathbb{R}$. Suppose:
1. the <Term k="taylorSeries">Taylor series</Term> of $f$ at $p$ has <Term k="radiusOfConvergence">radius of convergence</Term> $R > 0$, and
2. that series actually converges to $f(x)$ for every $x \in (p - R, p + R)$.

Then $f$ is <Term k="analyticFunction">analytic</Term> on the entire interval $(p - R, p + R)$.
</Callout>

This is not obvious: condition 2 says the Taylor series at $p$ agrees with $f$ throughout the disk; but analyticity at a different point $q \in (p-R, p+R)$ needs a separate series centered at $q$. The proposition says we can re-expand and still get a convergent series.

We will use this for $\exp$ on the next slide.
