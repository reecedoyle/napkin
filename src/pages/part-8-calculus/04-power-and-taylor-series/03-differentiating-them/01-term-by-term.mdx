<SlideLayout title="Differentiating term by term">

With ordinary finite sums, we differentiate term by term without a second thought. For <Term k="powerSeries">power series</Term>, this still works — but it is a theorem, not obvious.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Term-by-term differentiation">
Let $f(x) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n x^n$ have <Term k="radiusOfConvergence">radius of convergence</Term> $R > 0$. Then all derivatives of $f$ exist on $(-R, R)$ and are given by

$$f'(x) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty n\, a_n x^{n-1}, \quad f''(x) = \sum_{n=2}^\infty n(n-1)\, a_n x^{n-2}, \quad \dots$$

Each differentiated series also has radius of convergence $R$. In particular, $f$ is <Term k="smoothFunction">smooth</Term>.
</Callout>

The proof uses *uniform convergence* — the fact that inside any compact subinterval $[-r, r]$ with $r < R$, the series converges not just pointwise but uniformly. We omit the details.
