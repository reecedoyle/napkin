<SlideLayout title="exp is multiplicative">

<Callout kind="proposition" title="Functional equation">
For all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$,

$$\exp(x + y) = \exp(x) \cdot \exp(y).$$
</Callout>

The idea: multiply the two series $\sum \frac{x^n}{n!}$ and $\sum \frac{y^m}{m!}$ together. Collecting all terms with $m + n = k$ and using the binomial theorem:

$$\exp(x)\exp(y) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \sum_{\substack{m+n=k \\ m,n \ge 0}} \frac{x^n}{n!}\frac{y^m}{m!} = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{(x+y)^k}{k!} = \exp(x+y).$$

(There are technicalities about swapping the order of summation that we wave through.)

<Callout kind="corollary" title="exp is always positive">
Since $\exp(x) = \exp(x/2)^2 \ge 0$, and $\exp(x)\exp(-x) = \exp(0) = 1$ rules out zero, we get $\exp(x) > 0$ for all $x$. In particular $\exp$ is strictly increasing.
</Callout>

</SlideLayout>
