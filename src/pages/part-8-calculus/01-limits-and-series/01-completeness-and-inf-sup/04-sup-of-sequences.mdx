<SlideLayout title="Sup and inf of sequences">

We extend sup/inf notation to sequences in the natural way.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Sup and inf of a sequence">
For a sequence $a_1, a_2, \ldots$ we write

$$
\sup_n a_n \defeq \sup \{ a_n \mid n \in \NN \}, \qquad \inf_n a_n \defeq \inf \{ a_n \mid n \in \NN \}.
$$

We also call the sequence "bounded above/below" in the same sense as a set.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" title="Infimum of a sequence">
The sequence $a_n = \tfrac{1}{n}$ gives the set $\{1, \tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{3}, \ldots\}$. This set has no minimum — there is no smallest positive fraction — but its greatest lower bound is $0$. So $\inf_n a_n = 0$.
</Callout>

Note that $0$ is never actually achieved by the sequence, yet it is the infimum. This is exactly the same phenomenon as $\sup(0,1) = 1$ even though $1 \notin (0,1)$.
