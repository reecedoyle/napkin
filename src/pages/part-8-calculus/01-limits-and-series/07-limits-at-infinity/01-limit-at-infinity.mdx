<SlideLayout title="Limits of functions as x → ∞">

We need a separate definition for $\lim_{x\to\infty} f(x)$, because $\infty$ is not a point in $\RR$ — we can't just plug it into the previous definition.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Limit at infinity">
Let $f : \RR \to \RR$. We say $\lim_{x \to \infty} f(x) = L$ if:

For every $\eps > 0$ there exists a constant $M$ such that if $x > M$ then $|f(x) - L| < \eps$.

Similarly, $\lim_{x \to -\infty} f(x) = L$ replaces $x > M$ with $x < M$.
</Callout>

Since $\infty \notin \RR$, there is no "$f(\infty)$" to worry about — no analog of the "$f(p)$ is irrelevant" subtlety.

<Callout kind="example" title="Standard example">
$$\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{1}{x} = 0.$$

*Proof*: For any $\eps > 0$, take $M = 1/\eps$. If $x > M$ then $\left|\frac{1}{x} - 0\right| = \frac{1}{x} < \frac{1}{M} = \eps$.
</Callout>
