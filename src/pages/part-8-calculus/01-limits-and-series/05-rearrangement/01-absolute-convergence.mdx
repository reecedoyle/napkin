<SlideLayout title="Absolute convergence">

The definition of convergence depends on the *order* of the terms. Rearranging them might change the sum — or even the question of whether it converges. Understanding when rearrangement is safe starts with distinguishing two kinds of convergence.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Absolute and conditional convergence">
A series $\sum_k a_k$ **converges absolutely** if

$$\sum_k |a_k| < \infty,$$

i.e. the series of absolute values converges. If the series converges but not absolutely, it **converges conditionally**.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="proposition" title="Absolute implies ordinary convergence">
If $\sum_k a_k$ converges absolutely, then it converges.
</Callout>

The proof uses Cauchy: if the partial sums of $\sum |a_k|$ are Cauchy, so are the partial sums of $\sum a_k$, because $|s_m - s_n| \le \sum_{k=n+1}^m |a_k|$.

</SlideLayout>
