<Callout kind="example" title="Limsup of an oscillating sequence">
Consider $a_n = (-1)^n$: the sequence $-1, 1, -1, 1, -1, \ldots$

For any $N$, $\sup\{a_N, a_{N+1}, \ldots\} = 1$ (there are always later even terms). So

$$\limsup_{n\to\infty} (-1)^n = 1.$$

Similarly, $\inf\{a_N, a_{N+1}, \ldots\} = -1$ for all $N$, so $\liminf_{n\to\infty} (-1)^n = -1$.

The sequence does not converge, and $\limsup \ne \liminf$.
</Callout>

In fact, a sequence converges to $L$ if and only if $\limsup_n a_n = \liminf_n a_n = L$. When the two values agree, the sequence is being "squeezed" to a single limit.
