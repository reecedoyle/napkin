<SlideLayout title="The limit definition of the derivative">

Most of us learned derivatives as rules: "power rule, product rule, done." The point of this chapter is to show where those rules *come from* — and more importantly, to prove the theorems (Rolle, MVT) that make the whole machine work.

We start with the definition of the derivative as a limit.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Derivative">
Let <Term k="openInterval">$U$</Term> be an open subset of <Term k="RR">$\RR$</Term> and let $f \colon U \to \RR$ be a function. Let $p \in U$.

We say $f$ is <Term k="differentiable">**differentiable**</Term> at $p$ if the limit
$$\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(p+h) - f(p)}{h}$$
exists. If so, we denote its value $f'(p)$ and call it the <Term k="derivative">**derivative**</Term> of $f$ at $p$.

The function $f$ is **differentiable** (on all of $U$) if it is differentiable at every point, and we regard $f' \colon U \to \RR$ as a function in its own right.
</Callout>

The fraction $\frac{f(p+h)-f(p)}{h}$ is undefined at $h = 0$, so when we write the limit we mean the limit *as $h$ approaches $0$*, ignoring any value at $h = 0$ itself.
