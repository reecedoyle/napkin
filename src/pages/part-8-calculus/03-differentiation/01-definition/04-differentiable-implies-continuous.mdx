Before we move to rules, here is a fundamental relationship between differentiability and continuity.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Differentiable ⟹ continuous">
If $f$ is <Term k="differentiable">differentiable</Term> at $p$, then $f$ is <Term k="continuousFunction">continuous</Term> at $p$.
</Callout>

The converse is **false**: $f(x) = |x|$ is continuous everywhere but not differentiable at $x = 0$ (the left- and right-hand slopes disagree).

<ProofReveal

  solution="We need to show f(p+h) → f(p) as h → 0. Write f(p+h) − f(p) = h · [(f(p+h) − f(p))/h]. As h → 0, the first factor h → 0 and the second factor → f′(p) (which is finite). So the product → 0, giving f(p+h) → f(p)."
