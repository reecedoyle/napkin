<SlideLayout title="Proof of the product rule">

The product rule proof works by a clever algebraic split.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Proof">
For nonzero $h$ and point $p$, split the numerator by adding and subtracting $f(p+h)\,g(p)$: the difference quotient equals $\frac{f(p+h) - f(p)}{h} \cdot g(p+h) + \frac{g(p+h) - g(p)}{h} \cdot f(p).$ As $h \to 0$: the first ratio $\to f'(p)$, and $g(p+h) \to g(p)$ (since $g$ is continuous at $p$, as it is differentiable). The second ratio $\to g'(p)$, and $f(p)$ is a constant. So the whole expression $\to f'(p)\,g(p) + g'(p)\,f(p)$.
</Callout>

The key ingredient is that differentiability implies continuity: we need $g(p+h) \to g(p)$ to replace $g(p+h)$ by $g(p)$ in the limit.
