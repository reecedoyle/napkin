Now the rules. All three hold **point-by-point**: if $f$ and $g$ are differentiable at $p$, so are $f + g$, $f \cdot g$, and $g \circ f$.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="The core differentiation rules">
Let $f \colon U \to \RR$ and $g \colon U \to \RR$ be differentiable functions on open subsets of <Term k="RR">$\RR$</Term>.

- **Sum rule:** $(f + g)'(x) = f'(x) + g'(x).$
- **<Term k="productRule">Product rule</Term>:** $(f \cdot g)'(x) = f'(x)\,g(x) + f(x)\,g'(x).$
- **<Term k="chainRule">Chain rule</Term>:** If $f \colon U \to V$ and $g \colon V \to \RR$, then $(g \circ f)'(x) = g'(f(x)) \cdot f'(x).$
</Callout>

The sum rule is essentially immediate from limit arithmetic. The product and chain rules need a short trick each.
