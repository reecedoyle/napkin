Combining the rules and the library, any "artificial" function built from polynomials, exponentials, and trig functions can be differentiated step by step.

<Callout kind="example" title="Differentiating e^x + x sin(x²)">
$$f(x) = e^x + x \sin(x^2).$$

Applying the rules in sequence:
$$f'(x) = e^x + \sin(x^2) + x \cdot 2x \cos(x^2) = e^x + \sin(x^2) + 2x^2 \cos(x^2).$$

(Sum rule, then product rule on $x\sin(x^2)$, then chain rule on $\sin(x^2)$.)
</Callout>

The rules reduce differentiation to pattern matching. The deeper theorems in later sections are what make the rules *useful* — they let us extract information about $f$ from information about $f'$.
