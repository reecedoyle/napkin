The <Term k="meanValueThm">mean value theorem</Term> has a vivid real-world interpretation.

<Callout kind="remark" title="For people with driver's licenses">
A car travels along a road with position $f(t)$ at time $t$. Suppose you cover 900 km in 5 hours: $f(0) = 0$, $f(5) = 900$.

The MVT guarantees there exists some moment $c \in (0, 5)$ where the **instantaneous speed** was exactly $\frac{900}{5} = 180$ km/h. You cannot claim you were never speeding if the speed limit is below 180 km/h.
</Callout>

The deeper point: the MVT lets us convert **derivative information** (bounds on $f'$) into **information about $f$ itself**. This is something that is genuinely hard to do without it — even though the statement seems "obvious", the proof requires the full machinery of compactness.
