One classical use of calculus: finding where a function achieves its maximum or minimum. Let's set up the terminology precisely.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Local maximum and minimum">
Let $f \colon U \to \RR$ be a function. A **<Term k="localMaximum">local maximum</Term>** is a point $p \in U$ such that there exists an open neighbourhood $V$ of $p$ (with $V \subseteq U$) where $f(p) \ge f(x)$ for every $x \in V$.

A **<Term k="localMinimum">local minimum</Term>** is defined similarly. A **<Term k="localExtrema">local extremum</Term>** is a point that is either a local maximum or a local minimum.
</Callout>

Informally: $p$ is a local maximum if $f$ does not exceed $f(p)$ anywhere nearby. We do not require $f(p)$ to be the largest value over the entire domain — only locally.
