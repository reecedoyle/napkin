<SlideLayout title="Finding global extrema via compactness">

Fermat's theorem finds all *local* extrema inside an open domain. To find **global** extrema we need a bounded closed domain, where we know extrema exist (by <Term k="extremeValueThm">compactness</Term>).

<Callout kind="remark" title="Strategy for global optimization">
1. Work on a **compact** (closed and bounded) domain $Z = [a, b]$.
2. Any interior local extremum satisfies $f'(p) = 0$ — find all such $p$.
3. The global maximum and minimum must be among: the **stationary points** inside $(a,b)$, and the **endpoints** $a$, $b$.
4. Evaluate $f$ at each candidate and compare.
</Callout>

This is the rigorous version of the "set $f' = 0$ and check endpoints" procedure from high school.
