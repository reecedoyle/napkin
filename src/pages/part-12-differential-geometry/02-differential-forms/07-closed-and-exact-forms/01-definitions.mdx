<SlideLayout title="Closed and exact forms — definitions">

With $d^2 = 0$, any exact form is automatically closed:

<Callout kind="definition" title="Closed form">
A $k$-form $\alpha$ is <Term k="closedForm">**closed**</Term> if $d\alpha = 0$.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="definition" title="Exact form">
A $k$-form $\alpha$ is <Term k="exactForm">**exact**</Term> if there exists a $(k-1)$-form $\beta$ with $d\beta = \alpha$. (For $k = 0$, exact means $\alpha = 0$.)
</Callout>

Since $d^2 = 0$: if $\alpha = d\beta$, then $d\alpha = d^2\beta = 0$. So **every exact form is closed**.

The interesting question is the converse: is every closed form exact? Globally, the answer depends on the **topology** of $U$.
