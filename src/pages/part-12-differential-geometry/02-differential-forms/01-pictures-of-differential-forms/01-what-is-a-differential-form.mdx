<SlideLayout title="What is a differential form?">

Before we write down a single definition, let's think about integration. To integrate a function over a curve, a surface, or a solid blob, we need something that "knows how to be integrated" over objects of each dimension.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Joe Harris's dictum">
The definition of a differential form is: something you can integrate.
</Callout>

Let $U \subseteq V$ be an open set inside a <Term k="finDimRealVS">finite-dimensional real vector space</Term> $V$. Throughout this chapter, all functions are assumed <Term k="smoothFunction">smooth</Term> (infinitely differentiable).

The plan: we first build up geometric pictures of $k$-forms for $k = 0, 1, 2$, and the operators that connect them. Then we make everything precise.
