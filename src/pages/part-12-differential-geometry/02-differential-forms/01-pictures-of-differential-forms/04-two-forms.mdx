<SlideLayout title="2-forms — parallelogram measurements">

Next: integrate over **surfaces** — 2-dimensional patches. A surface is a map $c \colon [0,1] \times [0,1] \to U$. At each interior point $p$, a tiny piece of $c$ looks like a small parallelogram spanned by two tangent vectors $v$ and $w$.

A **2-form** reads off a number from each such parallelogram.

<Callout kind="definition" title="2-form (picture)">
A <Term k="twoForm">**$2$-form**</Term> $\beta$ on $U$ is a smooth assignment: to each point $p \in U$, we assign an element $\beta_p$ that takes **two** tangent vectors $v, w \in V$ and returns a real number, antisymmetrically: $\beta_p(v, w) = -\beta_p(w, v)$.
</Callout>

The antisymmetry captures orientation: flipping $v$ and $w$ negates the result, just as swapping rows of a matrix negates the determinant. Algebraically, $\beta_p \in V^\vee \wedge V^\vee$ — the second <Term k="wedgeProduct">exterior power</Term> of the dual space.
