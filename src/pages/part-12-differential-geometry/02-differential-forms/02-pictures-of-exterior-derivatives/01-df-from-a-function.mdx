Given a smooth function $f \colon U \to \mathbb{R}$ (a 0-form), there is a canonical 1-form built from it: the **exterior derivative** $df$.

At a point $p$ and in the direction of a tangent vector $v$, the value $(df)_p(v)$ is simply the **directional derivative** — how much $f$ changes as we move from $p$ in the direction $v$. In other words,

$$(df)_p = (Df)_p,$$

the total derivative of $f$ at $p$, viewed as a linear functional on tangent vectors.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Exterior derivative of a 0-form">
For a smooth function $f \colon U \to \mathbb{R}$, the <Term k="exteriorDerivative">**exterior derivative**</Term> $df$ is the 1-form whose value at $p$ is the total derivative $(Df)_p \in V^\vee$.
</Callout>

Geometrically: $df$ measures the infinitesimal change in $f$ in any direction.
