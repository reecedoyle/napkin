<SlideLayout title="Writing a k-form in coordinates">

Fix a basis $\mathbf{e}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{e}_n$ for $V$, with dual basis $\mathbf{e}_1^\vee, \ldots, \mathbf{e}_n^\vee$ for $V^\vee$. By the projection principle, $\bigwedge^k(V^\vee)$ has a basis consisting of all wedge products

$$\mathbf{e}_{i_1}^\vee \wedge \mathbf{e}_{i_2}^\vee \wedge \cdots \wedge \mathbf{e}_{i_k}^\vee, \quad 1 \le i_1 < i_2 < \cdots < i_k \le n.$$

There are $\binom{n}{k}$ such basis elements. So a general $k$-form in coordinates is:

$$\alpha_p = \sum_{1 \le i_1 < \cdots < i_k \le n} f_{i_1 \cdots i_k}(p) \cdot \mathbf{e}_{i_1}^\vee \wedge \cdots \wedge \mathbf{e}_{i_k}^\vee$$

where each $f_I \colon U \to \mathbb{R}$ is a smooth coefficient function.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Abbreviated notation">
We write $d\mathbf{e}_I$ for $\mathbf{e}_{i_1}^\vee \wedge \cdots \wedge \mathbf{e}_{i_k}^\vee$, and abbreviate the whole expansion as
$$\alpha = \sum_I f_I \cdot d\mathbf{e}_I.$$
For $V = \mathbb{R}^n$ with standard coordinates $x_1, \ldots, x_n$, this becomes the familiar $\alpha = \sum_I f_I \, dx_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge dx_{i_k}$.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" title="A 2-form on ℝ³">
In $\mathbb{R}^3$ with basis $\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2, \mathbf{e}_3$, a typical 2-form looks like
$$\alpha_p = f(p)\,\mathbf{e}_1^\vee \wedge \mathbf{e}_2^\vee + g(p)\,\mathbf{e}_1^\vee \wedge \mathbf{e}_3^\vee + h(p)\,\mathbf{e}_2^\vee \wedge \mathbf{e}_3^\vee$$
for smooth functions $f, g, h \colon U \to \mathbb{R}$.
</Callout>

</SlideLayout>
