<SlideLayout title="Level hypersurfaces — the m = 1 case">

The most useful special case of the regular value theorem is $m = 1$: a single smooth constraint cuts the ambient space down by one dimension.

<Callout kind="corollary" title="Level hypersurfaces">
Let $f \colon U \to \mathbb{R}$ be smooth on open $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$. If $M = \{p \in U \mid f(p) = 0\}$ is nonempty and $(Df)_p \neq 0$ for all $p \in M$, then $M$ is a smooth manifold of dimension $n - 1$.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" title="The circle">
Take $f(x,y) = x^2 + y^2 - c$ for $c > 0$. Then $Df = 2x\,dx + 2y\,dy$, which is nonzero as long as $(x,y) \neq (0,0)$. Since $c > 0$ the zero set $M = \{x^2 + y^2 = c\}$ is a circle with radius $\sqrt{c}$, and it avoids the origin. So the circle is a <Term k="levelHypersurface">smooth $1$-manifold</Term>.

When $c = 0$ the theorem fails: $M = \{(0,0)\}$, a point where $(Df)_0 = 0$.
</Callout>
