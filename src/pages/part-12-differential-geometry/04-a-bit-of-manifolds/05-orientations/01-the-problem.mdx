<SlideLayout title="Why we need orientations">

We want to integrate differential forms on manifolds. But there is an obstacle: the sign of an integral depends on a choice of direction.

Recall from calculus:
$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = -\int_b^a f(x)\,dx.$$

If $M$ is a space homeomorphic to an interval, there is no canonical "left" or "right". To integrate a $1$-form over $M$, we must first **pick a direction**.

<Callout kind="example" title="The circle">
If $M = S^1$ is a circle, there is no default "clockwise" or "counterclockwise" until we embed $M$ into $\mathbb{R}^2$. An abstract $S^1$ has no preferred orientation built in.
</Callout>

The fix is to require our manifolds to carry an extra piece of structure: an **orientation**. This is what lets us consistently integrate.

</SlideLayout>
