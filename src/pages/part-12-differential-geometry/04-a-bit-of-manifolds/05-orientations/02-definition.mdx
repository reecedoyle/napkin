<Callout kind="definition" title="Orientable manifold and volume form">
A smooth $n$-manifold $M$ is <Term k="orientableManifold">**orientable**</Term> if there exists a differential $n$-form $\omega$ on $M$ such that $\omega_p \neq 0$ for every $p \in M$.

Such an $\omega$ is called a <Term k="volumeForm">**volume form**</Term> on $M$.
</Callout>

Why does a volume form encode orientation? At each point $p$, the form $\omega_p$ is an element of $\bigwedge^n(T_p(M)^\vee)$. An $n$-form at $p$ takes a basis of the <Term k="tangentPlane">tangent space</Term> $T_p(M)$ and returns a nonzero real number. The sign of that number tells us whether the basis is "positively oriented" or not.

A volume form gives a **consistent global choice of sign** at every point. When no such global choice exists, the manifold is non-orientable.

<Callout kind="example" title="Orientable and non-orientable surfaces">
- Spheres $S^n$, planes, and the torus $S^1 \times S^1$ are orientable.
- The Möbius strip and Klein bottle are **not** orientable: they are "one-sided", and any orientation chosen locally must reverse somewhere as you travel around the strip.
- $\mathbb{CP}^n$ is orientable for any $n$.
- $\mathbb{RP}^n$ is orientable only when $n$ is odd.
</Callout>
