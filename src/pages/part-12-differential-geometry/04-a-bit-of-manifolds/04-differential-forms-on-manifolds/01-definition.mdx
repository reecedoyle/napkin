<SlideLayout title="Differential forms on manifolds">

We already know what a differential $k$-form is on an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$. To define one on a manifold $M$, we follow the same template: define it on each chart, and require compatibility across overlaps.

Let $M$ be a smooth manifold with atlas $\{U_i \xrightarrow{\phi_i} E_i\}$.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Differential k-form on a manifold">
A <Term k="diffFormOnManifold">**differential $k$-form**</Term> $\alpha$ on $M$ is a collection $\{\alpha_i\}$ of differential $k$-forms, one $\alpha_i$ on each $E_i$, such that for every overlapping pair $i$, $j$:
$$\alpha_j = \phi_{ij}^*(\alpha_i).$$
</Callout>

The condition $\alpha_j = \phi_{ij}^*(\alpha_i)$ says the forms are compatible: the <Term k="pullback">**pullback**</Term> of $\alpha_i$ along the transition map $\phi_{ij}$ gives exactly $\alpha_j$. This guarantees the collection "glues" to a globally well-defined object on $M$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="The pattern">
Whenever we extend something from Euclidean space to manifolds, we: (1) define it locally on each chart, and (2) require it to be compatible under pullbacks of transition maps. Differential forms are the first major example of this pattern in this chapter.
</Callout>

</SlideLayout>
