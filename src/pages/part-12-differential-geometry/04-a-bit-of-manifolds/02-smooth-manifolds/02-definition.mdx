<SlideLayout title="Smooth manifolds — definition">

Transition maps between chart domains land in $\mathbb{R}^n$, so we know exactly what it means for them to be smooth (infinitely differentiable). We use this to promote a topological manifold to a smooth one.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Smooth manifold">
A topological $n$-manifold $M$ is a <Term k="smoothManifold">**smooth manifold**</Term> if all its <Term k="transitionMap">transition maps</Term> $\phi_{ij}$ are smooth as maps between open subsets of $\mathbb{R}^n$.
</Callout>

This is clean because smoothness of a map between Euclidean spaces is already well-defined. We are not adding structure on top of the manifold — we are choosing an atlas whose overlaps are compatible in a smooth way.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Why this is the right setup">
Once we have smooth manifolds, we can port over definitions from $\mathbb{R}^n$. Any definition that makes sense locally (on each chart) and is compatible across overlaps (using the smooth transition maps) extends to all of $M$.
</Callout>

All the standard examples — spheres, tori, real projective spaces, $\mathbb{R}^n$ itself — carry natural smooth structures.

</SlideLayout>
