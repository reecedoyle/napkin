<SlideLayout title="Smooth maps between manifolds">

With smooth manifolds in hand, we can define what it means for a map between them to be smooth.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Smooth function">
A continuous function $f \colon M \to \mathbb{R}$ is **smooth** if for every chart $\phi_i \colon U_i \to E_i$, the composition
$$E_i \xrightarrow{\phi_i^{-1}} U_i \hookrightarrow M \xrightarrow{f} \mathbb{R}$$
is smooth as a function $E_i \to \mathbb{R}$.
</Callout>

More generally, a <Term k="smoothMap">**smooth map**</Term> $f \colon M \to N$ between two smooth manifolds is one where, in every pair of charts (one on $M$, one on $N$), the coordinate expression of $f$ is smooth as a map between Euclidean spaces.

The pattern is always the same: **pick charts, write everything in coordinates, demand smoothness there**. The smooth transition maps guarantee this is chart-independent.
