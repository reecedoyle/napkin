<SlideLayout title="The key idea — spaces that look locally flat">

Long ago, people thought the Earth was flat. They were wrong about the global shape, but right about something local: standing on the surface, it really does look like <Term k="RRn">$\mathbb{R}^2$</Term>.

This observation motivates the central definition of the chapter.

<Callout kind="remark" title="The guiding moral">
An $n$-manifold is a space which locally looks like $\mathbb{R}^n$.
</Callout>

There are two equivalent ways to make "locally looks like" precise:

- **Locally:** at every point $p \in M$, some open neighborhood of $p$ is homeomorphic to an open set of $\mathbb{R}^n$.
- **Globally:** there is an open cover $\{U_i\}$ of $M$ such that each $U_i$ is homeomorphic to some open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$.

These two conditions are equivalent. The global version is easier to work with formally, so it becomes the official definition.
