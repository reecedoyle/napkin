<SlideLayout title="Examples of topological manifolds">

<Callout kind="example" title="Standard examples">
- The sphere $S^2$ is a $2$-manifold: cover the Earth with two hemispheres, each homeomorphic to an open disk.
- The circle $S^1$ is a $1$-manifold: every point has a neighborhood looking like an open interval.
- The torus, Klein bottle, and $\mathbb{RP}^2$ are all $2$-manifolds.
- $\mathbb{R}^n$ is trivially an $n$-manifold (the atlas has one chart: the identity map).
</Callout>

A key non-example: the closed disk $D^n$ is **not** a manifold (without boundary). Points on the boundary $\partial D^n = S^{n-1}$ have neighborhoods that look like a half-space, not all of $\mathbb{R}^n$.
