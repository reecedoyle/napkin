<Callout kind="definition" title="Topological n-manifold">
A <Term k="topologicalManifold">**topological $n$-manifold**</Term> $M$ is a <Term k="hausdorff">Hausdorff</Term> space with an open cover $\{U_i\}$ where each $U_i$ is homeomorphic to an open subset $E_i \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$, via a homeomorphism
$$\phi_i \colon U_i \xrightarrow{\;\cong\;} E_i \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n.$$
Each $\phi_i$ is called a <Term k="chart">**chart**</Term>, and the collection $\{(U_i, \phi_i)\}$ is called an <Term k="atlas">**atlas**</Term>.
</Callout>

The name "chart" and "atlas" are geographic metaphors. A chart is like a map of one region; an atlas is the whole collection.

Note the Hausdorff condition: it rules out pathological spaces where points can't be separated by open sets.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Intrinsic, not embedded">
This definition does not assume $M$ sits inside some ambient $\mathbb{R}^N$. An abstract group $G$ is an intrinsic object rather than a subgroup of $S_n$; likewise, an abstract manifold $M$ is an intrinsic space rather than a subset of $\mathbb{R}^N$.
</Callout>
