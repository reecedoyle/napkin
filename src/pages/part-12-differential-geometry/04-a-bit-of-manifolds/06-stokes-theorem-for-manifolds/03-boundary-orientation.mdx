<SlideLayout title="Induced orientation on the boundary">

Stokes' theorem requires $\partial M$ to be oriented. When $M$ is oriented, its boundary inherits a canonical orientation.

The rule for the induced orientation: at each boundary point $p \in \partial M$, choose a normal vector pointing **outward** from $M$. This outward normal, together with a positively oriented basis of $T_p(\partial M)$, should give a positively oriented basis of $T_p(M)$.

<Callout kind="example" title="The interval [0,1]">
The interval $[0,1]$ with the standard orientation (positive direction: left to right) has boundary $\{0\} \cup \{1\}$. The induced orientation on $\partial [0,1]$ assigns $+1$ to the right endpoint and $-1$ to the left endpoint. So:
$$\int_{[0,1]} df = f(1) - f(0).$$
This is exactly the fundamental theorem of calculus.
</Callout>
