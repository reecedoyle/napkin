<SlideLayout title="Sanity check — the tangent space has dimension n">

It is reassuring to check that our abstract definition gives a tangent space of the right dimension.

Since every manifold is locally homeomorphic to an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$, it suffices to verify the claim for open sets in $\mathbb{R}^n$.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Dimension of the cotangent space">
Let $M \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ be open and $0 \in M$. Then:
$$\mathfrak{m}_0 / \mathfrak{m}_0^2 \cong \mathbb{R}^n \quad\text{via}\quad [f] \mapsto \nabla f(0) = \left(\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_1}(0), \dots, \tfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_n}(0)\right).$$
</Callout>

This is proven using Taylor's theorem: any smooth $f$ with $f(0) = 0$ can be written $f(x) = \sum_i a_i x_i + O(|x|^2)$, and only the first-order part survives in $\mathfrak{m}_0 / \mathfrak{m}_0^2$. The gradient $\nabla f(0) = (a_1, \dots, a_n)$ captures exactly this first-order data.

So the cotangent space — and hence the <Term k="tangentSpace">tangent space</Term> $T_p(M)$ — has dimension $n$, as expected.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Connection to algebraic geometry">
The definition of the cotangent space as $\mathfrak{m}_p / \mathfrak{m}_p^2$ uses only the algebraic structure of smooth functions. In algebraic geometry one repeats this construction with algebraic functions on varieties or schemes, getting the **Zariski cotangent space**. This is one reason the abstract definition is so valuable.
</Callout>

</SlideLayout>
