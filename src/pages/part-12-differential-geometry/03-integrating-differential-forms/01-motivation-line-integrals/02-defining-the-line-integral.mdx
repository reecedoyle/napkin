<SlideLayout title="Defining the line integral">

By type-checking, there is only one reasonable definition. We need a real number for each $t \in [a,b]$: feed the tangent vector $c'(t) = (Dc)_t(1)$ into $\alpha_{c(t)}$.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Line integral">
Let $c\colon [a,b] \to U$ be a <Term k="parametrizedCurve">parametrized curve</Term> and $\alpha$ a 1-form on $U$. We define

$$\int_c \alpha \;\defeq\; \int_{[a,b]} \alpha_{c(t)}\!\left(c'(t)\right) dt.$$

Here $c'(t) = (Dc)_t(1)$ is the **tangent vector** to the curve at time $t$.
</Callout>

That formula is a mouthful. We can package it more cleanly by defining the <Term k="pullback">**pullback**</Term> $c^\ast\alpha$, a 1-form on $[a,b]$:

$$(c^\ast\alpha)_t(\varepsilon) \;=\; \alpha_{c(t)}\cdot (Dc)_t(\varepsilon).$$

Then the line integral becomes simply $\int_c \alpha = \int_{[a,b]} c^\ast\alpha$.
