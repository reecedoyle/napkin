<SlideLayout title="Pullback properties">

The <Term k="pullback">pullback</Term> behaves as nicely as possible:

- **Linearity:** $\phi^\ast(c\alpha + \beta) = c\phi^\ast\alpha + \phi^\ast\beta$
- **Wedge compatibility:** $\phi^\ast(\alpha \wedge \beta) = (\phi^\ast\alpha)\wedge(\phi^\ast\beta)$
- **Naturality (chain rule):** $\phi_1^\ast(\phi_2^\ast\alpha) = (\phi_2 \circ \phi_1)^\ast\alpha$

All three can be verified by expanding in a basis.

The naturality property is the chain rule for pullbacks: composing maps corresponds to composing pullbacks in the opposite order. This is what lets us use pullbacks to reduce integration on arbitrary cells to integration on a standard box.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Pullback and exterior derivative commute">
A crucial fact (which you can verify by computation): for any smooth $\phi$ and any form $\alpha$,

$$\phi^\ast(d\alpha) = d(\phi^\ast\alpha).$$

This will be essential in the proof of Stokes' theorem.
</Callout>
