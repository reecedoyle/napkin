<SlideLayout title="Example — area of a circle">

Let's compute the area of a disk of radius $R$ using our machinery.

Take $V = \RR^2$, the 2-cell $c\colon (r,\theta)\mapsto (r\cos\theta)\mathbf{e}_1 + (r\sin\theta)\mathbf{e}_2$ on $[0,R]\times[0,2\pi]$, and the <Term k="volumeForm">volume form</Term> $\alpha_p = \mathbf{e}_1^\vee\wedge\mathbf{e}_2^\vee$.

Computing the pullback:

$$c^\ast\alpha = (\cos\theta\, dr - r\sin\theta\, d\theta)\wedge(\sin\theta\, dr + r\cos\theta\, d\theta)$$
$$= r(\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta)\,(dr\wedge d\theta) = r\, dr\wedge d\theta.$$

Therefore:

$$\int_c \alpha = \int_0^R\int_0^{2\pi} r\, dr\, d\theta = \pi R^2.$$

<Callout kind="remark">
The factor $r$ in $c^\ast\alpha = r\, dr\wedge d\theta$ is the **Jacobian** of polar coordinates — the standard change-of-variables factor. The pullback machinery gives it automatically.
</Callout>
