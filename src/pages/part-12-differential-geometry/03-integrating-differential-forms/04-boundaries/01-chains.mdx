To state the boundary operation cleanly, we need a way to add and subtract cells.

<Callout kind="definition" title="k-chain">
A <Term k="kChain">**$k$-chain**</Term> over $U$ is a formal real linear combination of $k$-cells:

$$c = a_1 c_1 + \dots + a_m c_m, \qquad a_i \in \RR.$$

Integration extends by linearity: $\displaystyle\int_c \alpha = \sum_i a_i \int_{c_i}\alpha$.
</Callout>

In particular, a $0$-chain is a weighted collection of points, and a $1$-chain is a weighted collection of curves. The coefficients can be negative — a negative weight means we traverse the cell in the "opposite" direction.

This formalism lets us talk about the boundary of a cell systematically. For a 1-cell $c\colon [a,b] \to U$, the boundary should be the $0$-chain $\{c(b)\} - \{c(a)\}$: the endpoint minus the starting point. The minus sign captures the orientation: the fundamental theorem of calculus says $\int_{[a,b]} dg = g(b) - g(a)$, and the boundary formula matches.
