The key idea this chapter establishes:

<Callout kind="remark" title="Summary">
For a differentiable function $f \colon U \to \mathbb{R}$ (with $U$ open in a normed vector space $V$),
the total derivative $Df$ is a smooth map
$$
Df \colon U \to V^\vee.
$$
It assigns to each point a covector — a <Term k="differentialForm">differential 1-form</Term>.
The familiar partial derivatives $\partial f/\partial x$, $\partial f / \partial y$ are the components of $Df$
in the dual basis $\{dx, dy\} = \{\mathbf{e}_1^\vee, \mathbf{e}_2^\vee\}$.
</Callout>

The natural next step is **integration**: how do we integrate a function over a region in $\mathbb{R}^n$?
The correct language for this is differential forms of higher degree — 2-forms, 3-forms, and so on.
A $k$-form assigns an antisymmetric multilinear functional to each point,
and integration over a $k$-dimensional surface is pairing a $k$-form with that surface.

This gives a unified framework for:
- Line integrals (1-forms over curves)
- Surface integrals (2-forms over surfaces)
- Volume integrals (3-forms over 3d regions)
- Stokes' theorem as a single statement $\int_M d\omega = \int_{\partial M} \omega$
