<SlideLayout title="Higher derivatives — the second derivative">

We have $Df \colon U \to \operatorname{Hom}(V, W)$.
To differentiate $Df$ itself, we need $\operatorname{Hom}(V,W)$ to be a normed vector space.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Operator norm">
For a linear map $T \colon V \to W$, the **operator norm** is
$$
\|T\| \;\defeq\; \sup\!\left\{ \frac{\|T(v)\|_W}{\|v\|_V} \;\middle|\; v \neq 0_V \right\}.
$$
This makes $\operatorname{Hom}(V,W)$ a normed vector space.
</Callout>

With this norm in place, we can differentiate $Df \colon U \to \operatorname{Hom}(V,W)$
to get the **second total derivative**

$$
D^2f \colon U \to \operatorname{Hom}(V, \operatorname{Hom}(V,W)).
$$

And iterating: $D^k f \colon U \to \operatorname{Hom}(V, \operatorname{Hom}(V, \cdots \operatorname{Hom}(V,W)\cdots))$.

Using the natural isomorphism $\operatorname{Hom}(V,W) \cong V^\vee \otimes W$,
we can write this more cleanly as

$$
D^k f \colon U \to (V^\vee)^{\otimes k} \otimes W.
$$

For $k=2$, $W=\mathbb{R}$: $D^2 f(p) \in (V^\vee)^{\otimes 2}$,
which can be represented as an $n \times n$ matrix — the <Term k="hessian">Hessian</Term>.

</SlideLayout>
