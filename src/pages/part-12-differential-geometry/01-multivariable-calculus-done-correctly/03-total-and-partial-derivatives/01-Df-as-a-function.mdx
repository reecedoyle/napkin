<SlideLayout title="Df as a function on U">

So far, $(Df)_p$ is the total derivative **at a single point** $p$.
If $f \colon U \to \mathbb{R}$ is differentiable everywhere,
we can collect all these derivative-maps into a single function:

$$
Df \colon U \to V^\vee, \quad p \mapsto (Df)_p.
$$

This is a little mind-bending: to every **point** $p \in U$
we associate a **linear functional** $(Df)_p \in V^\vee$.
We call $Df$ the <Term k="totalDerivative">total derivative</Term> of $f$ (as opposed to the total derivative at a single point).

<Callout kind="remark">
The total derivative $Df$ is a map from the open set $U$ into the dual space $V^\vee$.
For $f \colon U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$, we have $V^\vee \cong \mathbb{R}^n$ (via the standard inner product), so $Df$ is a vector field — the **gradient** in disguise.
</Callout>

The next step: use the <Term k="projectionPrinciple">projection principle</Term> to decompose $Df$
into $n$ scalar functions using a basis for $V^\vee$.

</SlideLayout>
