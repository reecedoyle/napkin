<SlideLayout title="Two upgrades from single to multivariable">

Going from single-variable calculus $f \colon \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$
to fully multivariable $f \colon \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$ involves two separate upgrades:

1. **Domain upgrade**: $\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^n$ (inputs become vectors)
2. **Codomain upgrade**: $\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^m$ (outputs become vectors)

These upgrades are **not equally hard**. The domain upgrade is the deep one —
it is what forces us to replace the scalar slope $f'(p)$ with a linear map.
The codomain upgrade, by contrast, turns out to be completely mechanical.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Where the difficulty lives">
All the interesting structure in multivariable calculus comes from upgrading the domain.
Upgrading the codomain is just doing the same thing coordinate-by-coordinate.
</Callout>

Once you accept that $Df$ for scalar-valued $f$ is a dual vector $(Df)_p \in V^\vee$,
extending to vector-valued functions $f \colon U \to \mathbb{R}^m$
follows immediately from the <Term k="projectionPrinciple">projection principle</Term>.

</SlideLayout>
