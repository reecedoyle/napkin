<Callout kind="theorem" title="Projection principle">
Let $U$ be an open subset of the normed vector space $V$.
Let $W$ be an $m$-dimensional real vector space with basis $w_1, \dots, w_m$.
Then there is a bijection between continuous functions $f \colon U \to W$ and
$m$-tuples of continuous scalar functions $f_1, \dots, f_m \colon U \to \mathbb{R}$,
via
$$
f(v) = f_1(v)w_1 + \cdots + f_m(v)w_m.
$$
The same holds with "continuous" replaced by "differentiable", "smooth", or "arbitrary".
</Callout>

In plain language: a function into $\mathbb{R}^m$ is determined by its $m$ coordinate functions,
and every interesting analytic property (continuity, differentiability) can be checked per-coordinate.

<Callout kind="remark">
This is why we mostly study functions $f \colon U \to \mathbb{R}$. Understanding scalar-valued functions is enough — the vector-valued case is just $m$ copies stacked together.
For $f \colon U \to \mathbb{R}$, the total derivative $(Df)_p$ lives in the <Term k="dualSpace">dual space</Term> $V^\vee$.
</Callout>
