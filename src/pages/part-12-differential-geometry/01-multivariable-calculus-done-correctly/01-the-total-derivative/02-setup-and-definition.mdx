<SlideLayout title="Definition of the total derivative">

Let $U \subseteq V$ be an open subset of a <Term k="normedVectorSpace">normed vector space</Term> $V$,
and let $f \colon U \to W$ be a continuous function into another normed space $W$.
We want the derivative at a point $p \in U$ to be a <Term k="linearMap">linear map</Term>
$T \colon V \to W$ that approximates $f$ near $p$:
$$
f(p + v) \approx f(p) + T(v)
$$
for small displacements $v \in V$.

The "$\approx$" is made precise using the norm.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Total derivative">
Suppose there exists a linear map $T \colon V \to W$ such that
$$
\lim_{\|v\|_V \to 0} \frac{\|f(p+v) - f(p) - T(v)\|_W}{\|v\|_V} = 0.
$$
Then $T$ is the **total derivative** of $f$ at $p$, written $(Df)_p$.
We say $f$ is **differentiable at $p$**.
If $(Df)_p$ exists at every point we simply say $f$ is **differentiable**.
</Callout>

Note: we need $U$ open so that for small enough $\|v\|$, the displaced point $p + v$ is still in the domain $U$.

</SlideLayout>
