Before stating the SLLN, here is a question that reveals why "almost every world" matters.

<Callout kind="example" title="Biased random walk">
Define a random walk: $X_0 = 1$, and at each step $i \ge 1$, move down by $1$ with probability $p = 0.6$ or up by $1$ with probability $1 - p = 0.4$. What is the expected number of steps to first reach $0$?
</Callout>

A natural approach: let $f(i)$ be the expected steps to reach $0$ from position $i$. Then we get an infinite system $f(1) = 1 + 0.6 f(0) + 0.4 f(2)$, $f(2) = 1 + 0.6 f(1) + 0.4 f(3)$, etc. — no closed form.

**Smarter approach:** let $x = f(1)$. From position $2$, we first reach $1$ (costing $x$ steps in expectation), then reach $0$ (another $x$ steps). So:
$$x = 1 + 0.6 \cdot 0 + 0.4 \cdot 2x.$$
