<SlideLayout title="Chebyshev and Kolmogorov's inequalities">

We need two tail inequalities. The first is classical:

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Chebyshev's inequality">
For $X$ with mean $0$ and variance $\sigma^2$:
$$\Pr[|X| \ge a] \le \frac{\Var[X]}{a^2}.$$
</Callout>

But Chebyshev only bounds the **final** partial sum $|S_n|$. To prove almost sure convergence of $T_n$, we need control over the **running maximum**:

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Kolmogorov's maximal inequality">
Let $X_1, \ldots, X_n$ be independent mean-zero random variables. Define $S_i = X_1 + \cdots + X_i$. Then
$$\Pr\!\left[\max_{1 \le i \le n} |S_i| \ge a\right] \le \frac{\Var[S_n]}{a^2}.$$
</Callout>

This is strictly stronger: Chebyshev bounds $\Pr[|S_n| \ge a]$; <Term k="llKolmogorov">Kolmogorov's inequality</Term> bounds $\Pr[\text{any } |S_i| \ge a]$ — with the **same** right-hand side. Positive correlation among partial sums is the key: knowing $S_i$ is large makes $S_n$ likely to stay large too.

</SlideLayout>
