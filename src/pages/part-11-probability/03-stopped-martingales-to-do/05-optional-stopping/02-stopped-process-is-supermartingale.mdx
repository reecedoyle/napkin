<SlideLayout title="Stopped supermartingales are still supermartingales">

Stopping a supermartingale doesn't make it worse.

<Callout kind="proposition" title="The stopped process is a supermartingale">
Let $(X_n)$ be a supermartingale and $\tau$ a stopping time. Then the <Term k="stoppedMartingale">stopped process</Term>

$$X_{\tau \wedge 0},\; X_{\tau \wedge 1},\; X_{\tau \wedge 2},\; \dots$$

is itself a supermartingale.
</Callout>

**Proof.** We compute almost everywhere:

$$\mathbb{E}(X_{\tau \wedge n} \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1}) = \mathbb{E}\!\left( X_{n-1} + \mathbf{1}_{\tau(\omega) \ge n}(X_n - X_{n-1}) \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1} \right).$$

Since $\{\tau \ge n\} = \{\tau \le n-1\}^c \in \mathcal{F}_{n-1}$ (the indicator is $\mathcal{F}_{n-1}$-measurable), we can pull it out:

$$= X_{n-1} + \mathbf{1}_{\tau(\omega) \ge n} \cdot \mathbb{E}(X_n - X_{n-1} \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1}) \le X_{n-1},$$

since $(X_n)$ being a supermartingale means $\mathbb{E}(X_n - X_{n-1} \mid \mathcal{F}_{n-1}) \le 0$.

</SlideLayout>
