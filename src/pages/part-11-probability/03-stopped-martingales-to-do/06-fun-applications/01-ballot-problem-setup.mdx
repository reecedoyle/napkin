<SlideLayout title="The ballot problem">

Here is the first of three applications of optional stopping. (The other two, on the ABRACADABRA problem and a USA TST problem, are marked "to be written" in the source — they are omitted here.)

**Setup.** Alice and Bob race in an election. Alice receives $a$ votes, Bob receives $b$ votes, with $a > b$. The $a + b$ ballots are counted in a uniformly random order. We ask:

> What is the probability that Alice **stays strictly ahead** of Bob throughout the entire count?

<Callout kind="proposition" title="Ballot problem">
The probability that Alice remains strictly ahead of Bob at every step of the count is

$$\frac{a - b}{a + b}.$$
</Callout>

The proof uses a clever martingale. We need to model the ballot count probabilistically, construct a martingale, and apply the optional stopping theorem.
