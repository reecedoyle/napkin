<SlideLayout title="Doob's convergence theorem">

A martingale that wanders freely can oscillate forever. But if it is bounded, it must eventually settle down.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Doob's martingale convergence theorem">
Let $(X_n)_{n \ge 0}$ be a supermartingale on a probability space $\Omega$ such that

$$\sup_{n \ge 0} \mathbb{E}[|X_n|] < \infty.$$

Then there exists a random variable $X_\infty \colon \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ such that $X_n \to X_\infty$ almost surely.
</Callout>

The <Term k="doobConvergence">convergence theorem</Term> is the probabilistic analogue of the monotone convergence theorem: a supermartingale that is bounded below is like a decreasing sequence bounded below — it must converge.

**Why this matters.** For a stopping time $\tau$ that equals $\infty$ on some worlds, we need $X_\tau(\omega) = \lim_{n \to \infty} X_n(\omega)$ to be well-defined. The convergence theorem gives us this for free whenever the supermartingale is bounded.

</SlideLayout>
