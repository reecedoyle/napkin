  difficulty="standard"
  prompt="The number $1$ is written on a blackboard. Every minute, if $a$ is on the board, it is erased and replaced by a real number chosen uniformly at random from $[0, 2.01a]$.

What is the probability that the resulting sequence of numbers approaches $0$?"
  hint="It occurs with probability $1$. Let $X_n$ be the number on the board at step $n$, and let $\\mu = \\frac{1}{2.01} \\int_0^{2.01} \\log t \\, dt$. Show that $\\log(X_n) - n\\mu$ is a martingale."
  solution="The answer is probability $1$ — the sequence almost surely approaches $0$.

**Martingale approach.** Fix $\\mu = \\mathbb{E}[\\log U]$ where $U$ is uniform on $[0, 2.01]$:

$\\mu = \\frac{1}{2.01} \\int_0^{2.01} \\log t \\, dt = \\frac{1}{2.01}[t\\log t - t]_0^{2.01} = \\log(2.01) - 1 < 0$

since $2.01 < e$. (We have $\\log(2.01) \\approx 0.699 < 1$, so $\\mu < 0$.)

The process $M_n = \\log(X_n) - n\\mu$ is a martingale: given $X_{n-1}$, we have $X_n = X_{n-1} \\cdot U_n / (2.01)$... actually $X_n$ is uniform on $[0, 2.01 X_{n-1}]$, so $\\log(X_n) = \\log(2.01 X_{n-1} \\cdot U)$ where $U$ is uniform on $[0,1]$. Then $\\mathbb{E}[\\log X_n \\mid X_{n-1}] = \\log(X_{n-1}) + \\mu$, so $M_n$ is a martingale.

By the law of large numbers (or by the convergence of $M_n$), $\\log(X_n)/n \\to \\mu < 0$ almost surely. Since $\\mu < 0$, we have $X_n = e^{\\log X_n} \\to 0$ almost surely."
