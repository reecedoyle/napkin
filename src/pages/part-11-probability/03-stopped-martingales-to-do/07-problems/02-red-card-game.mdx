  prompt="A deck has 52 cards: 26 red and 26 black. Cards are drawn one at a time. At any point (while cards remain), you may stop the dealer; you win if and only if the next card is red. If all cards are dealt without you stopping, you lose.

Across all possible strategies, determine the maximal probability of winning."
  hint="There is a cute elementary solution. For the martingale-based solution, show that the fraction of red cards remaining in the deck at time $n$ is a martingale."
  solution="The answer is 1/2, and every strategy achieves exactly 1/2.

**Intuition.** Whenever you stop, the next card is equally likely to be any of the cards you have not yet seen, so your instantaneous chance of winning is exactly the fraction of red cards left in the deck. The subtlety is that your stopping time depends on what you have already seen, so we cannot just say that half the unseen cards are red. The martingale argument below makes the symmetry precise and shows the fraction-red-remaining is unaffected, on average, by any stopping rule.

**Martingale argument.** Let $R_n$ be the number of red cards remaining and $B_n$ be the number of black cards remaining after $n$ draws. Let $X_n = R_n / (R_n + B_n)$ be the fraction of red cards in the remaining deck. We have $X_0 = 1/2$.

Check that $X_n$ is a martingale: given the state at time $n$, the $(n+1)$th card drawn is equally likely to be any of the remaining $R_n + B_n$ cards. So $\\mathbb{E}[R_{n+1}/(R_n+B_n-1) \\mid R_n, B_n] = (R_n(R_n-1)/(R_n+B_n) + B_n \\cdot R_n/(R_n+B_n)) / (R_n+B_n-1) = R_n/(R_n+B_n) = X_n$.

By the optional stopping theorem (finite time: at most 51 draws), $\\mathbb{E}[X_\\tau] = X_0 = 1/2$.

When you stop, $X_\\tau$ is the probability the next card is red (given what you've seen). So your winning probability is $\\mathbb{E}[X_\\tau] = 1/2$, regardless of strategy."
