<SlideLayout title="Filtrations — information over time">

As time advances, we learn more. A <Term k="filtration">filtration</Term> formalizes this.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Filtration">
A **filtration** on $\Omega = (\Omega, \mathcal{A}, \mu)$ is a nested sequence of sub-$\sigma$-algebras

$$\mathcal{F}_0 \subseteq \mathcal{F}_1 \subseteq \mathcal{F}_2 \subseteq \cdots$$

Each $\mathcal{F}_n$ represents the information available at time $n$.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" title="Coin flip filtration">
You flip a fair coin infinitely many times, so $\Omega = \{H,T\}^\infty$. Let $\mathcal{F}_n$ be the sub-$\sigma$-algebra generated by the first $n$ flips:

- $\mathcal{F}_0 = \{\varnothing, \Omega\}$ — no information yet.
- $\mathcal{F}_1 = \{\varnothing, \text{first flip H}, \text{first flip T}, \Omega\}$.
- $\mathcal{F}_2$ contains $\mathcal{F}_1$ plus all events determined by the first two flips.
- And so on.
</Callout>
