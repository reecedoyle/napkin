The moral of the filtration story:

<Callout kind="remark" title="Key intuition">
$X$ is $\mathcal{F}$-measurable if and only if $X$ is determined solely by the information encoded in $\mathcal{F}$.
</Callout>

In the coin flip filtration, let $X_3$ be the value (H or T) of the third flip.

- $X_3$ is **not** $\mathcal{F}_2$-measurable: knowing only flips 1 and 2 tells you nothing about flip 3. Formally, $X_3^{-1}(\{H\}) \notin \mathcal{F}_2$.
- $X_3$ **is** $\mathcal{F}_3$-measurable: after the third flip, you know its value.

This captures the intuition: a process is <Term k="adaptedProcess">adapted</Term> to a filtration $(\mathcal{F}_n)$ if $X_n$ is $\mathcal{F}_n$-measurable for each $n$ — you can observe $X_n$ from the information available at time $n$, without looking into the future.
