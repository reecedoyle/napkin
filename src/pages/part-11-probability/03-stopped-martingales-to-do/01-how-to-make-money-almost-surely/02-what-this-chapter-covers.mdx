The doubling strategy works — in theory — because you can quit at any time. You exploit the freedom to stop the game at a favorable moment. This chapter formalizes that freedom and shows its limits.

Two main threads:

1. **Optional stopping theorem**: under "sane" conditions (finite time, finite money, or bounded bets), you cannot extract a profit from a fair game by choosing when to stop. Las Vegas wins.

2. **Martingales as a proof tool**: once we accept we can't beat the casino, we can flip the idea around. Problems that have nothing to do with gambling can sometimes be solved by constructing a suitable martingale and applying the optional stopping theorem.

Along the way we need to build up the machinery: conditional expectation, filtrations, and the formal definition of a stopping time.
