The inequality direction of the §5 theorem falls right out of the fixed field theorem.

<ProofReveal
  client:load
  id="gal-opt-proof-aut-leq-degree"
  prompt="Using the fixed field theorem, show [K:F] ≥ |Aut(K/F)|, with equality iff the elements fixed by every σ ∈ Aut(K/F) are exactly F."
  solution="Apply the fixed field theorem with G = Aut(K/F): it gives [K : K^G] = |Aut(K/F)|, where K^G is the fixed field of the whole automorphism group. Since every σ ∈ Aut(K/F) fixes F by definition, F ⊆ K^G, so [K:F] = [K:K^G][K^G:F] ≥ [K:K^G] = |Aut(K/F)| by the tower law. Equality [K:F] = |Aut(K/F)| holds exactly when [K^G:F] = 1, i.e. K^G = F — precisely when the set of elements fixed by all of Aut(K/F) is nothing more than F itself."
/>

The remaining, harder question is exactly when equality holds — that's where splitting fields come in.
