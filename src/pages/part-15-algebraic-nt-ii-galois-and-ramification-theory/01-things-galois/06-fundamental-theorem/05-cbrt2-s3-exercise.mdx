Let's sanity-check part (d) of the fundamental theorem against a fact we already know: $E = \QQ(\cbrt2)$ is not Galois over $\QQ$.

<ProofReveal
  client:load
  id="gal-fun-proof-s3-normal-subgroup"
  prompt="Apply the fundamental theorem to K = ℚ(∛2, ω), which has Galois group G = Gal(K/ℚ) ≅ S₃. Verify that the fact E = ℚ(∛2) is not Galois over ℚ corresponds to the fact that S₃ has no normal subgroup of order 2."
  solution="Since [K:E] = [K:ℚ]/[E:ℚ] = 6/3 = 2, the subgroup H = Gal(K/E) corresponding to E has order 2 (by part (b) of the fundamental theorem) — concretely H = {id, τ} where τ fixes ∛2 and sends ω ↦ ω². By part (d), E/ℚ is Galois exactly when H is normal in G. In S₃, the order-2 subgroups are generated by transpositions, and no transposition subgroup is normal (conjugating a transposition by a 3-cycle gives a different transposition). So H is not normal in G ≅ S₃, and therefore E = ℚ(∛2) is not Galois over ℚ — matching what we already knew directly from |Aut(E/ℚ)| = 1 ≠ 3 = [E:ℚ]."
/>

This is a nice consistency check: two completely different ways of seeing "$\QQ(\cbrt2)$ is deficient" — counting automorphisms directly, versus checking normality inside $S_3$ — agree.
