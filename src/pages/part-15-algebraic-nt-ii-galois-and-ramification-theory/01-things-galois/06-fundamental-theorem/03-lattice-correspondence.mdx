<SlideLayout title="The two lattices line up">

Working out the fixed field of each subgroup of $G = \Gal(\QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)/\QQ)$, we get a complete picture: the lattice of intermediate fields between $\QQ$ and $K$ matches, rung for rung, the lattice of subgroups of $G$.

<Callout kind="example" title="Fields between ℚ and ℚ(√2,√3)">
At the top, $\QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$ itself; one level down, the three degree-2 subfields $\QQ(\sqrt2)$, $\QQ(\sqrt6)$, $\QQ(\sqrt3)$; at the bottom, $\QQ$.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" title="Subgroups of G = {id, σ, τ, στ}">
At the top, the trivial subgroup $\{\id\}$; one level down, the three order-2 subgroups $\{\id,\tau\}$, $\{\id,\sigma\tau\}$, $\{\id,\sigma\}$; at the bottom, all of $G$.
</Callout>

Line these up and the correspondence is exact — and it's *inclusion-reversing*: the biggest field ($K$ itself) corresponds to the smallest subgroup ($\{\id\}$), and the smallest field ($\QQ$) corresponds to the biggest subgroup ($G$). We saw $K^{\{\id,\sigma\}} = \QQ(\sqrt3)$ last slide; the other two pairings work out the same way, matching $\{\id,\tau\}$ to $\QQ(\sqrt2)$ and $\{\id,\sigma\tau\}$ to $\QQ(\sqrt6)$.

</SlideLayout>
