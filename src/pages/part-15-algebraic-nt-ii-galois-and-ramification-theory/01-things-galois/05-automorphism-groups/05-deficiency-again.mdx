<SlideLayout title="The deficiency of ℚ(∛2), precisely">

Let's look once more at $K = \QQ(\cbrt2)$, and pin down exactly what's "deficient" about it, in two equivalent ways.

**First**, of the three maps $\QQ(\cbrt2) \injto \CC$, only one lives in $\Aut(K/\QQ)$ — the identity. So $|\Aut(K/\QQ)|$ is **too small**: it's $1$, while $[K:\QQ] = 3$.

**Second**, $K$ is missing some Galois conjugates: $\omega\cbrt2$ and $\omega^2\cbrt2$ aren't in $K$.

These two symptoms are really the same disease. To capture the second one precisely, we need a name for "the smallest field containing all the roots of a given polynomial."
