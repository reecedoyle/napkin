<SlideLayout title="Galois closures">

<Callout kind="example" title="Examples and non-examples of Galois extensions">
- $\QQ(\sqrt2)/\QQ$ is Galois — it's the splitting field of $x^2-2$. The Galois group has order $2$: $\sqrt2 \mapsto \pm\sqrt2$.
- $\QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)/\QQ$ is Galois — it's the splitting field of $(x^2-5)^2-6$. The Galois group is $\Zc2 \times \Zc2$ (the Klein four group from §5).
- $\QQ(\cbrt2)/\QQ$ is **not** Galois.
</Callout>

But we can always fix a non-Galois extension by throwing in the missing conjugates.

<Callout kind="example" title="The Galois closure of ℚ(∛2)">
Let $K = \QQ(\cbrt2,\omega)$, with $[K:\QQ] = 6$. Consider $\sigma \colon \cbrt2 \mapsto \omega\cbrt2, \omega \mapsto \omega$ and $\tau \colon \cbrt2 \mapsto \cbrt2, \omega \mapsto \omega^2$. Then $\sigma^3 = \tau^2 = \id$, and one can check $|\Aut(K/\QQ)| \le 6$ (it always divides $[K:\QQ] = 6$) while also $\ge 6$ (by Lagrange the orders $3$ and $2$ of $\sigma$ and $\tau$ each divide $|\Aut(K/\QQ)|$, forcing $6 \mid |\Aut(K/\QQ)|$) — so $|\Aut(K/\QQ)| = 6 = [K:\QQ]$, and $K/\QQ$ **is** Galois! In fact $\Gal(K/\QQ) \cong S_3$, the symmetric group on 3 elements.
</Callout>

Starting from a non-Galois extension $\QQ(\cbrt2)$, adjoining $\omega$ produced a <Term k="gal58GaloisClosure">Galois closure</Term> — the smallest Galois extension containing the original field.
