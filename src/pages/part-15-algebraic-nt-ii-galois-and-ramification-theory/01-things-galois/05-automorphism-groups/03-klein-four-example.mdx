Here's an example with a genuinely more interesting group.

<Callout kind="example" title="Automorphism group of ℚ(√2,√3)">
Let $K = \QQ(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$. It turns out $\Aut(K/\QQ) = \{1,\sigma,\tau,\sigma\tau\}$, where
$$
\sigma \colon \sqrt2 \mapsto -\sqrt2,\ \sqrt3 \mapsto \sqrt3
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\tau \colon \sqrt2 \mapsto \sqrt2,\ \sqrt3 \mapsto -\sqrt3.
$$
In other words, $\Aut(K/\QQ)$ is the Klein four group.
</Callout>

Each generator can independently be sent to $+$ or $-$ its value, and the two choices don't interfere with each other (unlike the $\cbrt2$ case, where sending $\cbrt2$ to a non-real conjugate dragged the whole map outside $K$). That independence is exactly why we get $2 \times 2 = 4$ automorphisms here, matching $[K:\QQ] = 4$.

</SlideLayout>
