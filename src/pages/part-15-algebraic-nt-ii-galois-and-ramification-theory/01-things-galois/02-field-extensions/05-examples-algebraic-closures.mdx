<Callout kind="example" title="ℝ̄ = ℂ̄ = ℂ ⊋ ℚ̄">
$\CC$ is the algebraic closure of $\RR$ (and of itself, since it's already algebraically closed). But the algebraic closure $\ol\QQ$ of $\QQ$ — the field of algebraic numbers — is a **proper** subfield of $\CC$: some complex numbers, like $\pi$ and $e$, aren't the root of any polynomial with rational coefficients.
</Callout>

So $\ol\QQ \subsetneq \CC$, even though both are algebraically closed. This is exactly the distinction between "algebraic closure of $F$" (the smallest algebraically closed field containing $F$) and "some algebraically closed field containing $F$" (of which there can be many, of different sizes) — $\CC$ happens to be a convenient, oversized choice when $F = \QQ$.
