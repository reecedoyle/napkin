<SlideLayout title="Algebraically closed fields">

Given a field like $\QQ(\cbrt2)$, we want somewhere to embed it — for number fields, that was $\CC$. Let's ask what makes $\CC$ special for this purpose: it contains every root of every polynomial, no matter the coefficients.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Algebraically closed">
A field $E$ is <Term k="algClosed">algebraically closed</Term> if every polynomial with coefficients in $E$ factors as a product of linear polynomials over $E$ — equivalently, every non-constant polynomial in $E[x]$ has a root in $E$.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" title="ℂ is algebraically closed">
This is the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra: every non-constant polynomial with complex coefficients has a complex root, and hence factors completely over $\CC$.
</Callout>

Contrast with $\RR$: $x^2+1$ has no real root, so $\RR$ is not algebraically closed. That's exactly why we embed number fields into $\CC$ rather than $\RR$.

</SlideLayout>
