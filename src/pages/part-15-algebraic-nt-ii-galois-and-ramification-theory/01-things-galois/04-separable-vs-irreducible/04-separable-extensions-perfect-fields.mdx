<SlideLayout title="Separable extensions and perfect fields">

We really want this separability property at the level of extensions, not just single polynomials.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Separable extension, perfect field">
A <Term k="gal58SeparableExtension">separable extension</Term> $K/F$ is one where for every $\alpha \in K$, the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $F$ is separable (automatic if $F$ has characteristic zero). A field $F$ is <Term k="gal58PerfectField">perfect</Term> if every finite extension $K/F$ is separable.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Finite fields are perfect">
Suppose $F$ is a field with finitely many elements. Then $F$ is perfect.
</Callout>

Combine these two facts and something reassuring falls out: **every field we'll ever see in this book is either finite or characteristic zero**, so it's automatically perfect, and every extension of it is automatically separable. The word "separable" in the theorems that follow is mostly a formality — you almost never have to check it.

</SlideLayout>
