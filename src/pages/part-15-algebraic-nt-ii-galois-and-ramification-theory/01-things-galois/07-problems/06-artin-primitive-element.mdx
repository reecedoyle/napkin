<Problem

  id="gal-prob-artin-primitive-element"
  difficulty="standard"
  prompt="Let K be a number field. Show that K ≅ ℚ(γ) for some single γ."
  hint="By induction, it suffices to show ℚ(α,β) = ℚ(γ) for some γ built from α and β. For all but finitely many rational λ, the choice γ = α + λβ works."
  solution="Since K is a finite extension of ℚ, it's generated by finitely many elements, so by induction it suffices to handle two generators: show ℚ(α,β) = ℚ(γ) for some γ. Let f and g be the minimal polynomials of α and β over ℚ, with roots α = α₁, …, αₘ and β = β₁, …, βₙ in ℂ. Discard the finitely many rational λ for which α + λβ = αᵢ + λβⱼ for some i and some j ≠ 1 — there are only finitely many such bad λ, since each equation pins down at most one value of λ (as βⱼ ≠ β₁ for j ≠ 1). For any surviving λ, set γ = α + λβ. Then β is the unique common root of g(x) and h(x) := f(γ − λx), because a second common root βⱼ would force γ − λβⱼ to equal some αᵢ, contradicting the choice of λ. Since g and h both have coefficients in ℚ(γ), their greatest common divisor — computed by the Euclidean algorithm entirely within ℚ(γ)[x] — is the linear factor (x − β), so β itself lies in ℚ(γ). Then α = γ − λβ also lies in ℚ(γ), giving ℚ(α,β) ⊆ ℚ(γ) ⊆ ℚ(α,β), so they're equal."
