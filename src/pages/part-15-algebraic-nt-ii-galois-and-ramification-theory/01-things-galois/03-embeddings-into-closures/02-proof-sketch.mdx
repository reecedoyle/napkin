<SlideLayout title="Proof — build the embedding one root at a time">

The idea is genuinely fun: build up $K$ one generator at a time, and count the choices at each step.

Recall that any irreducible polynomial over $\QQ$ has distinct roots. Adjoin elements $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_m$ to $\QQ$ one at a time until we reach all of $K = \QQ(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_m)$:
$$
\QQ \subseteq \QQ(\alpha_1) \subseteq \QQ(\alpha_1,\alpha_2) \subseteq \cdots \subseteq K.
$$

<Callout kind="remark" title="Counting choices at each step">
There are exactly $[\QQ(\alpha_1):\QQ]$ ways to pick where $\alpha_1$ goes: an embedding $\tau_1$ of $\QQ(\alpha_1)$ is determined by $\tau_1(\alpha_1)$ (since it must fix $\QQ$), and there are $\deg p_1$ choices — one per distinct root of $\alpha_1$'s minimal polynomial $p_1$, and $\deg p_1 = [\QQ(\alpha_1):\QQ]$.

Given a choice of $\tau_1$, there are similarly $[\QQ(\alpha_1,\alpha_2):\QQ(\alpha_1)]$ ways to extend it to $\tau_2$ on $\QQ(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)$ — this time $\tau_1$ need not be the identity, but it's still true that $\tau_2$ is pinned down by where it sends $\alpha_2$, with one choice per root of $\alpha_2$'s minimal polynomial over $\QQ(\alpha_1)$.
</Callout>

Multiplying all these counts together telescopes (by the tower law from §2) to exactly $[K:\QQ] = n$.
