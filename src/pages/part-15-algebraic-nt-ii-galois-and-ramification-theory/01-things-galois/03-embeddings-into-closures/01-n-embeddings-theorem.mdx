<SlideLayout title="The n embeddings of a number field">

Time to prove the pattern we noticed in §1: the number of embeddings always equals the degree.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="The n embeddings of a number field">
Let $K$ be a number field of degree $n$. Then there are exactly $n$ field homomorphisms $K \injto \CC$, say $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_n$, which fix $\QQ$.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="remark">
A nontrivial homomorphism of fields is automatically injective (its kernel is an ideal, and a field has none but the trivial ones). This is why we can write "$\injto$" and call each $\sigma_i$ an embedding of $K$ into $\CC$.
</Callout>

It's common in number theory to see "let $K$ be a number field of degree $n$, and $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_n$ its $n$ embeddings" thrown around without further comment — this theorem is why that's always legitimate.

</SlideLayout>
