Now that we know a number field always has exactly $n$ embeddings, we can restate the trace and norm cleanly, in terms of them.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Trace and norm via embeddings">
Given a number field $K$ of degree $n$ with embeddings $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_n \colon K \injto \CC$, we set
$$
\Tr_{K/\QQ}(\alpha) = \sum_{i=1}^n \sigma_i(\alpha)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\Norm_{K/\QQ}(\alpha) = \prod_{i=1}^n \sigma_i(\alpha).
$$
</Callout>

This matches the definitions from the rings-of-integers chapters (there defined via a multiplication matrix) — the embeddings viewpoint is just a second, equally valid way to compute the same numbers, and it's the one that generalizes best to arbitrary base fields, as we'll see in §4.
