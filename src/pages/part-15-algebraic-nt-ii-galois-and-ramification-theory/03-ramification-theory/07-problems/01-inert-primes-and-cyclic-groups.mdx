  solution="Suppose p were inert in K, so p·𝒪_K stays prime: g=1 and e=1. With g=1 there is only one prime 𝔭 above p, so every σ ∈ Gal(K/ℚ) automatically fixes it — that is, D_𝔭 = Gal(K/ℚ), the whole group. Since e=1, the inertia group I_𝔭 is trivial, so the surjection θ: D_𝔭 → Gal((𝒪_K/𝔭)/𝔽_p) is in fact an isomorphism: D_𝔭 ≅ Gal((𝒪_K/𝔭)/𝔽_p). But the Galois group of any finite field extension 𝔽_{p^f}/𝔽_p is cyclic (generated by the Frobenius map x ↦ x^p). So D_𝔭, and hence Gal(K/ℚ), would have to be cyclic. For K = ℚ(∛2, ω), Gal(K/ℚ) ≅ S₃, which is non-abelian — not cyclic. Contradiction, so no rational prime can be inert in K. This generalizes: no rational prime can be inert in any Galois extension K/ℚ whose Galois group is not cyclic — in particular, in any non-abelian Galois extension."
