<SlideLayout title="Factoring 5 in the non-Galois case">

Let's see the theorem fail exactly when its hypothesis fails. Let $p=5$ and $E = \QQ(\cbrt2)$, which is **not** Galois (it doesn't contain the other roots of $x^3-2$).

One can show $\OO_E = \ZZ[\cbrt2]$, so we run the <Term k="an55factoringAlgorithm">Dedekind–Kummer</Term> recipe on the minimal polynomial $x^3-2$. Since

$$
x^3 - 2 \equiv (x-3)(x^2+3x+9) \pmod 5
$$

is the irreducible factorization mod $5$ (check: $3^3 = 27 \equiv 2 \pmod 5$), we get

$$
(5) = (5, \cbrt2 - 3)\,(5, \cbrt4 + 3\cbrt2 + 9),
$$

with inertial degrees $1$ and $2$ respectively. The fact that these are *different* reflects exactly that $E$ is not Galois — the efg theorem simply doesn't apply here.
