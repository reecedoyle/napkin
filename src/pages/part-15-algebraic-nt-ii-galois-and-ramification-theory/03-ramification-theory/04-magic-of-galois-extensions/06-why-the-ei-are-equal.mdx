<SlideLayout title="Why the ramification indices are equal too">

For the $e_i$, we first need one small fact: applying $\sigma \in G$ to the *whole* ideal $p \cdot \OO_K$ does nothing to it.

<ProofReveal
  client:load
  id="rt60-s4-proof-sigma-fixes-p-ok"
  prompt="Using that σ ∈ Gal(K/ℚ), show that σ(p · 𝒪_K) = p · σ(𝒪_K) = p · 𝒪_K."
  solution="σ is a ring automorphism of K, so it sends the ideal generated by p and 𝒪_K to the ideal generated by σ(p) and σ(𝒪_K). Since p ∈ ℚ is fixed pointwise by σ (σ fixes the base field), σ(p) = p. And σ restricts to a ring automorphism of 𝒪_K (algebraic integers map to algebraic integers), so σ(𝒪_K) = 𝒪_K. Hence σ(p · 𝒪_K) = p · σ(𝒪_K) = p · 𝒪_K."
/>

So for every $\sigma \in G$, applying $\sigma$ to both sides of $p \cdot \OO_K = \prod \kp_i^{e_i}$ gives

$$
p \cdot \OO_K = \prod_i \kp_i^{e_i} = \prod_i (\sigma \kp_i)^{e_i}.
$$

Since the action is transitive, every $\sigma\kp_i$ ranges over all the $\kp_j$ as $\sigma$ varies — comparing exponents in the two factorizations of the same ideal forces all the $e_i$ to be equal too.
