<SlideLayout title="The action is transitive">

Since every $\sigma$ is a bijection, primes in the same orbit of this action are closely related. But something much stronger is true: there's only **one orbit**.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Galois group acts transitively">
Let $K/\QQ$ be Galois with $G = \Gal(K/\QQ)$, and let $\{\kp_i\}$ be the distinct primes above a rational prime $p$. Then $G$ acts transitively on the $\kp_i$: for every $i,j$ there is some $\sigma \in G$ with $\sigma \kp_i = \kp_j$.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="remark" title="Moral">
All the $\kp_i$ above $p$ are Galois conjugates of each other.
</Callout>

</SlideLayout>
