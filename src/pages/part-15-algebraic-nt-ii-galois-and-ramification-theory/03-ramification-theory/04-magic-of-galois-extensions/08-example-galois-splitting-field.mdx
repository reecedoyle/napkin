Now let $K = \QQ(\cbrt2, \omega)$, the splitting field of $x^3-2$ over $\QQ$ — this time $K/\QQ$ **is** Galois.

<Callout kind="remark" title="A messier θ, but the same recipe">
It turns out $\OO_K = \ZZ[\eps]$ where $\eps$ is a root of $t^6+3t^5-5t^3+3t+1$ (finding this takes real work, so we won't derive it here). Modulo $5$, this factors as
$$
(x^2+x+2)(x^2+3x+3)(x^2+4x+1) \pmod 5,
$$
so by Dedekind–Kummer,
$$
(5) = (5, \eps^2+\eps+2)\,(5, \eps^2+3\eps+3)\,(5, \eps^2+4\eps+1).
$$
</Callout>

This time **all three** inertial degrees are $2$ — exactly as the efg theorem predicts for a Galois $K$. Compare with the previous slide: same prime $5$, same underlying cubic $x^3-2$, but going from the non-Galois $E$ to its Galois closure $K$ forces the inertial degrees to snap into uniformity.
