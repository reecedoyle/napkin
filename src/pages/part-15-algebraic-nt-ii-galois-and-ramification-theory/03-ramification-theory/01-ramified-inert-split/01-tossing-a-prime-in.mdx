<SlideLayout title="Tossing a prime into 𝒪_K">

We've already seen that rational primes can behave strangely once we throw them into a bigger ring of integers. In $\ZZ[i]$ (which is a UFD!) we have

$$
(2) = (1+i)^2, \qquad (3) = (3), \qquad (5) = (2+i)(2-i).
$$

Same prime $p$, three totally different shapes. This chapter is about naming and explaining that behavior — and eventually using it to prove Quadratic Reciprocity with weapons from Galois theory.

Let $p$ be a rational prime and toss it into <Term k="an55ringOfIntegers">$\OO_K$</Term>. Unique factorization of ideals gives us

$$
p \cdot \OO_K = \kp_1^{e_1} \cdots \kp_g^{e_g}
$$

for some distinct <Term k="an55primeIdealOK">prime ideals</Term> $\kp_1, \dots, \kp_g$ and exponents $e_i \ge 1$. We say each $\kp_i$ is <Term k="rt60above">above</Term> $(p)$ — picture $K \supset \OO_K$ sitting over $\QQ \supset \ZZ$, with $\kp_i$ sitting over $(p)$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Notation">
$p \cdot \OO_K$ and $(p)$ mean the same thing — the ideal generated by $p$ in $\OO_K$. We'll use both interchangeably.
</Callout>
