<SlideLayout title="The residue field extension">

This section is optional, but it's a beautiful refinement of the efg theorem. Fix a prime $p$, so $p \cdot \OO_K = (\kp_1 \cdots \kp_g)^e$ with every $\kp_i$ of inertial degree $f$. Fix one $\kp$ among the $\kp_i$.

We can compare $\OO_K/\kp$ with $\ZZ/p = \FF_p$. Since $\OO_K/\kp$ is a finite field, it's naturally a field extension of $\FF_p$ — in fact $\OO_K/\kp \cong \FF_{p^f}$. Picture the tower: $K \supset \OO_K \supset \kp$ sitting above $\QQ \supset \ZZ \supset (p)$, with residue fields $\OO_K/\kp \cong \FF_{p^f}$ sitting above $\FF_p$.

On the far right we have an extension of *finite fields*, which we already understand extremely well. So it's natural to ask:

<Callout kind="question" title="The question of the section">
How are $\Gal\left( (\OO_K/\kp) / \FF_p \right)$ and $\Gal(K/\QQ)$ related?
</Callout>
