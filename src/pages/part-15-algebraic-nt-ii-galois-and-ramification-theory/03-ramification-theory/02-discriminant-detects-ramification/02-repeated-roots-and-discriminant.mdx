<SlideLayout title="Detecting a repeated root mod p">

How do we detect whether $f$ has a repeated root mod $p$, without factoring it every time? We use the <Term k="an57polynomialDiscriminant">polynomial discriminant</Term>

$$
\Delta(f) = \prod_{i<j} (z_i - z_j)^2,
$$

the product of squared differences of the roots $z_1, \dots, z_n$ of $f$ (over $\CC$, say). This vanishes exactly when two roots collide.

So: $p$ ramifies (i.e. $f$ has a repeated root mod $p$) exactly when $\Delta(f) \equiv 0 \pmod p$ — that is, exactly when $p \mid \Delta(f)$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Why this generalizes">
It turns out the naïve generalization to *any* number field works, once we replace $\Delta(f)$ by the discriminant <Term k="an57discriminant">$\Delta_K$</Term> of $K$ itself. (These agree for monogenic $\OO_K$.)
</Callout>
