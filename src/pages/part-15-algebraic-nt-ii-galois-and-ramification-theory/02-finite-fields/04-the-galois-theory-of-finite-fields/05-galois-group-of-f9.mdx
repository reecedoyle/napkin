Back to $\mathbb{F}_9 = \mathbb{Z}[i]/(3)$, with $\sigma_3 \colon \mathbb{F}_9 \to \mathbb{F}_9$ given by $x \mapsto x^3$.

<Callout kind="example" title="Galois group of the finite field of order 9">

Watch what $\sigma_3$ does to all nine elements. The three elements $0$, $1$, $2$ are fixed — they live in $\mathbb{F}_3$, exactly as the theorem predicts. The other six elements pair up and swap: $\sigma_3$ exchanges $i \leftrightarrow -i$, $1+i \leftrightarrow 1-i$, and $2+i \leftrightarrow 2-i$.

As predicted, the Galois group has order two:
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_9/\mathbb{F}_3) = \{\operatorname{id}, \sigma_3\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.
$$

</Callout>

This concludes the proof of all four facts stated at the very start of the chapter.
