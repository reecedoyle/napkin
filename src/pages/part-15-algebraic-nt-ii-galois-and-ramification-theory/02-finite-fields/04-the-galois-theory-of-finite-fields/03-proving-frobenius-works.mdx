<SlideLayout title="Proving Frobenius is an automorphism">

Three things to check: $\sigma_p$ is a homomorphism, it's injective, and it fixes $\mathbb{F}_p$.

**Homomorphism.** $\sigma_p$ fixes $1$, respects multiplication ($(xy)^p = x^p y^p$ trivially), and respects addition — that's exactly the <Term k="ff59FreshmansDream">Freshman's dream</Term> identity from earlier.

**Injective.** If $x^p = y^p$ then $x^p - y^p = 0$, so $(x-y)^p = 0$ (Freshman's dream again, applied to $x + (-y)$), forcing $x = y$. Since $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ is finite, injective automatically means bijective.

**Fixes $\mathbb{F}_p$.** This is just Fermat's little theorem: for $x \in \mathbb{F}_p \subset \mathbb{F}_{p^n}$, we already know $x^p = x$.
