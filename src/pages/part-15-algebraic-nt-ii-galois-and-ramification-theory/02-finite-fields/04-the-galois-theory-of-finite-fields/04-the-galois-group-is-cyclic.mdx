<SlideLayout title="The Galois group is cyclic">

Now we can finish fact #4 from the chapter overview.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Galois group of 𝔽_{p^n}/𝔽_p">
$\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, with generator $\sigma_p$.
</Callout>

Since $[\mathbb{F}_{p^n} : \mathbb{F}_p] = n$, the Galois group $G$ has <Term k="groupOrder">order</Term> $n$. So it suffices to show $\sigma_p \in G$ has order exactly $n$.

Applying $\sigma_p$ $k$ times sends $x \mapsto x^{p^k}$. Applying it $n$ times gives the identity, since every element of $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ satisfies $x^{p^n} = x$ (Fermat's little theorem again). But if $k < n$, applying $\sigma_p$ $k$ times *can't* be the identity — that would mean every element of $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ satisfies $x^{p^k} - x = 0$, a polynomial of degree $p^k < p^n$ that can't have $p^n$ roots.

So $\sigma_p$ really does have order $n$, generating all of $G$.
