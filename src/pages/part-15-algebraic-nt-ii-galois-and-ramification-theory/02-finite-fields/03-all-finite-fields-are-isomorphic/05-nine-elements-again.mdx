Let's watch the classification theorem play out concretely.

<Callout kind="example" title="The finite field of order nine again">

The polynomial $x^9 - x$ is separable modulo $3$, and factors as
$$
x(x+1)(x+2)(x^2+1)(x^2+x+2)(x^2+2x+2) \pmod 3.
$$

So if $F$ has order $9$, we'd expect it to be generated by adjoining all the roots: $0$, $1$, $2$ (roots of the linear factors), together with $\pm i$, $1 \pm i$, $2 \pm i$ (roots of the quadratic factors, where $i$ denotes an element of <Term k="ff59Fpn">$\mathbb{F}_9$</Term> satisfying $i^2 = -1$ — deliberately named to echo the ordinary imaginary unit). That's exactly the field from our very first example.

</Callout>

This concludes fact #3 from the overview: all fields of a given order $p^n$ are the same field, wearing different clothes.
