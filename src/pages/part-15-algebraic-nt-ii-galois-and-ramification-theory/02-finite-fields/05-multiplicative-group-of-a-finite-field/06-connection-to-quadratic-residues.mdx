<Callout kind="remark" title="A better lens on an old fact">

If you've seen an elementary proof that there are exactly $\frac{p-1}{2}$ quadratic residues modulo a prime $p$, it probably used some version of "$x^d - 1$ has at most $d$ roots" — the same trick we just used. But stopping there, without exposing the cyclic structure of $F^\times$, hides *why* the count comes out to exactly half.

The proposition takes that extra step. Now, without any computation, you also know that the finite field $\mathbb{F}_{17^2}$ has exactly $\frac{17^2 - 1}{2} = 144$ nonzero quadratic residues.

</Callout>
