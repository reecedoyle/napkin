<SlideLayout title="What this chapter proves">

Fields don't have to be infinite. We've already met <Term k="finiteField">$\mathbb{F}_p = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$</Term> for a prime $p$. This chapter classifies **every** finite field, and along the way computes their symmetry groups. The proofs are all short — this is a "cash in your abstract algebra" chapter.

<Callout kind="remark" title="The whole point of this chapter">

Everything below builds toward four facts about a finite field $F$:

- $F$ must have order $p^n$, for some prime $p$ and integer $n \geq 1$.
- In that case $F$ has characteristic $p$.
- All finite fields of a given order are isomorphic — so it's customary to write $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ for "the" field of order $p^n$.
- The extension $F/\mathbb{F}_p$ is Galois, with Galois group cyclic of order $n$, generated by $x \mapsto x^p$.

</Callout>

If you're in a hurry, you can just remember these four bullets and skip ahead. If not, let's build up to them one piece at a time, starting with a concrete example.
