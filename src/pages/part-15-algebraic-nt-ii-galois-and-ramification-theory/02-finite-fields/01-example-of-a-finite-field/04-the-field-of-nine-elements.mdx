<SlideLayout title="Example: the field of nine elements">

Here's a finite field that is *not* just "integers mod a prime."

<Callout kind="example" title="The finite field of nine elements">

Let $F \cong \mathbb{F}_3[X]/(X^2+1) \cong \mathbb{Z}[i]/(3)$, using the <Term k="quotientRing">quotient ring</Term> construction on the <Term k="gaussianIntegers">Gaussian integers</Term> $\mathbb{Z}[i]$. We can think of its elements concretely as

$$
\{ a + bi \mid 0 \le a, b \le 2 \}.
$$

Since $(3)$ is prime in $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ (the ring of integers of $\mathbb{Q}(i)$), the quotient $F$ is a field, and it has $3^2 = 9$ elements.

</Callout>

Nine elements, built by adjoining a square root of $-1$ to $\mathbb{F}_3$ instead of to $\mathbb{Q}$. The construction is exactly the one you'd use to build $\mathbb{C}$ from $\mathbb{R}$ — only now everything is taken mod $3$.

</SlideLayout>
