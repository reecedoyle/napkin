<Callout kind="lemma" title="Nonzero characteristic is prime">
If the <Term k="ff59Characteristic">characteristic</Term> of a field $F$ isn't $0$, it must be a prime number.
</Callout>

Why can't the characteristic be, say, $6$? Because a field has no zero divisors — if $F$ had characteristic $ab$ with $1 < a, b < ab$, we could split the sum of $ab$ copies of $1_F$ into two nonzero pieces whose product is forced to vanish, which is impossible in a field.

<ProofReveal
  client:load
  id="ff59-s2-proof-char-prime"
  prompt="Try writing this out: suppose n = ab with 1 < a, b < n is the characteristic. Derive a contradiction."
  solution="Let A = 1_F + ... + 1_F (a times) and B = 1_F + ... + 1_F (b times). Since a, b are both smaller than the characteristic n, neither A nor B is 0. But AB is n copies of 1_F added together (by distributing the sum), which equals 0 by definition of the characteristic. So AB = 0 with A ≠ 0 and B ≠ 0 — a zero divisor, contradicting that F is a field."
/>
