<SlideLayout title="An amusing alternate proof of (c)">

Here's a slicker way to see that $|F|$ has no prime factors other than $p$, using group theory instead of linear algebra.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Alternate proof by contradiction">
Suppose a prime $q \neq p$ divides $|F|$. By Cauchy's theorem applied to the group $(F,+)$, there is a nonzero element $x$ of <Term k="elementOrder">order</Term> $q$. Then
$$
x \cdot (\underbrace{1_F + \dots + 1_F}_{q \text{ times}}) = 0,
$$
but $x \neq 0$, so the bracketed sum of $q$ copies of $1_F$ must be $0$ — meaning the characteristic divides $q$. Since $q$ is prime and the characteristic is already the prime $p \neq q$, that's impossible.
</Callout>

Two totally different toolkits — linear algebra, and group theory — landing on the same fact. That's a good sign the fact is really true.
