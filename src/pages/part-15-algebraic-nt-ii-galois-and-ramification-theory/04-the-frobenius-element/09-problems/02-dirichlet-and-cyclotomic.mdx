The first problem is a daggered (hard) capstone: Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions falls right out of Chebotarev applied to a cyclotomic field. The second is a companion fact about when cyclotomic polynomials can be irreducible mod p.

<Problem
  client:load
  id="frob-prob-dirichlet-density"
  difficulty="daggered"
  prompt="Let a and m be relatively prime positive integers. Show that the density of primes p ≡ a (mod m) is exactly 1/φ(m)."
  hint="Apply the Chebotarev density theorem on ℚ(ζ_m)."
  solution="Let K = ℚ(ζ_m). Exactly as in the cyclotomic setting, Gal(K/ℚ) ≅ (ℤ/mℤ)ˣ, of order φ(m). This group is abelian, so every conjugacy class is a singleton, giving φ(m) conjugacy classes total. For p sufficiently large — in particular unramified, since only primes dividing m ramify in K — we have σ_p = Frob_𝔭 for any 𝔭 above p, because the m-th roots of unity remain distinct modulo p (the derivative of x^m − 1 is m·x^(m−1), nonzero mod p once p does not divide m). By Chebotarev, the density of primes p with Frob_𝔭 = σ_a — equivalently, with p ≡ a (mod m), since σ_p = σ_a exactly when p ≡ a (mod m) — is |{σ_a}|/|G| = 1/φ(m)."
/>

<Problem
  client:load
  id="frob-prob-cyclotomic-not-irreducible"
  difficulty="standard"
  prompt="Let n be an odd integer that is not a prime power. Show that the n-th cyclotomic polynomial is not irreducible modulo any rational prime."
  hint="Split into the cases p divides n and p does not divide n. For the second case, relate irreducibility of the cyclotomic polynomial mod p to p being a primitive root mod n, and show that (ℤ/nℤ)ˣ is never cyclic when n is odd and not a prime power."
  solution="Write Φ_n for the n-th cyclotomic polynomial. First suppose p divides n. Write n = p^a·m with p ∤ m and a ≥ 1. A standard identity gives Φ_n(x) ≡ Φ_m(x)^(φ(p^a)) (mod p), where φ(p^a) = p^(a−1)(p−1) ≥ 2 because p is odd. So modulo p the polynomial Φ_n is a proper power of Φ_m, hence reducible (an irreducible polynomial is not a nontrivial power of another). Now suppose p does not divide n, so p is unramified in ℚ(ζ_n). By the cyclotomic Frobenius lemma, Frob_𝔭 = σ_p for 𝔭 above p, and Φ_n factors mod p into φ(n)/d irreducible factors of equal degree d, where d is the multiplicative order of p modulo n — this is exactly the cycle structure of multiplication-by-p acting on (ℤ/nℤ)ˣ. So Φ_n is irreducible mod p exactly when d = φ(n), i.e. exactly when p generates the group (ℤ/nℤ)ˣ, i.e. exactly when (ℤ/nℤ)ˣ is cyclic. But n is odd and not a prime power, so n has at least two distinct prime factors; by the Chinese remainder theorem, (ℤ/nℤ)ˣ decomposes as a direct product of the groups (ℤ/qℤ)ˣ for the prime power factors q of n, and each such factor is cyclic of even order (since q is an odd prime power). A direct product of two or more groups of even order always has more than one element of order dividing two, so it is never cyclic. Hence (ℤ/nℤ)ˣ is not cyclic, so no p generates it, and Φ_n is never irreducible modulo any prime not dividing n either. Combining both cases finishes the proof."
/>
