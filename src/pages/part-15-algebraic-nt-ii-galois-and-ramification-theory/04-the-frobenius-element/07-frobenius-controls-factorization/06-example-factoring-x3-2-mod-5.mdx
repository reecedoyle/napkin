<SlideLayout title="Example: factoring x³ − 2 mod 5">

Let $\alpha = \cbrt2$ and $f = x^3-2$, so $E = \QQ(\cbrt2)$. Set $p = 5$, and let $K = \QQ(\cbrt2, \omega)$ be the splitting field, as in our earlier §2 example (where we computed $\Frob_{\kP}$ there has cycle type $(\bullet)(\bullet\ \bullet)$, i.e. it fixes one root of $x^3-2$ and swaps the other two).

<Callout kind="example" title="All three descriptions agree">
All three of the theorem's descriptions have shape $2+1$:

(i) By the factoring algorithm, $(5) = (5, \cbrt2-3)(5, 9+3\cbrt2+\cbrt4)$ in $\OO_E$.

(ii) We have $x^3-2 \equiv (x-3)(x^2+3x+9) \pmod 5$.

(iii) We already saw $\Frob_{\kP} = (\bullet)(\bullet\ \bullet)$ in $S_3$, acting on the three roots of $x^3-2$.
</Callout>
