<SlideLayout title="Can we hit every conjugacy class?">

We now know that fixing $p$ pins down an entire conjugacy class of Frobenius elements. A natural question follows: does **every** conjugacy class of $G = \Gal(K/\QQ)$ actually show up this way? In other words, is every element of $G$ equal to $\Frob_\kp$ for *some* prime $\kp$?

<Callout kind="question" title="A natural question">
Given any $\sigma \in G$, can we always find a rational prime $p$ (unramified in $K$) and a prime $\kp$ above it with $\Frob_\kp = \sigma$?
</Callout>

Miraculously, not only is the answer "yes" — the Frobenius elements are spread out among conjugacy classes in the nicest way imaginable: **equally**, in proportion to class size.

</SlideLayout>
