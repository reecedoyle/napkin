<SlideLayout title="Intuition: throwing darts at G">

Here's the picture to keep in your head: if you pick a random unramified prime $p$ and look at its resulting conjugacy class, it's a lot like throwing a dart at $G$ — the probability of landing in any particular conjugacy class depends only on how big that class is, nothing else about its "shape" or which elements it contains.

<Callout kind="example" title="Throwing darts at S₃">
Take $G = S_3$, which appears in this chapter as $\Gal(\QQ(\cbrt2,\omega)/\QQ)$. Its six elements fall into three conjugacy classes: the identity alone (size $1$), the three transpositions (size $3$), and the two $3$-cycles (size $2$). Chebotarev says a random unramified prime has its Frobenius land in the transposition class $3/6 = 50\%$ of the time, in the $3$-cycle class $2/6 \approx 33\%$ of the time, and equal to the identity — meaning $p$ splits completely — just $1/6 \approx 17\%$ of the time.
</Callout>
