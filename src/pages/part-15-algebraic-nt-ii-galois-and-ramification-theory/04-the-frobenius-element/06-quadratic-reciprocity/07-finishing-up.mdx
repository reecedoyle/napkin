We already know, by the cyclotomic Frobenius lemma, that $\Frob_{\kP} = \sigma_p \in H$ if and only if $p$ is a quadratic residue mod $q$ (recall $H$ is exactly the subgroup $\{\sigma_n : n \text{ a QR mod } q\}$). One more exercise closes the loop on the $K$ side.

<Callout kind="exercise" title="Splitting in the quadratic field">
Show that $p$ splits in $\OO_K = \ZZ\left[\tfrac12(1+\sqrt{q^\ast})\right]$ if and only if $\left(\frac{q^\ast}{p}\right) = 1$. (Use the factoring algorithm; you'll need $p \neq 2$ here.)
</Callout>

<ProofReveal

  id="frob-qr-proof-splits-in-quadratic-field"

  solution="𝒪_K = ℤ[θ] where θ = ½(1+√q*) has minimal polynomial t² − t − ¼(1−q*). By the Dedekind–Kummer factoring algorithm, p splits in 𝒪_K iff this polynomial factors mod p (into two distinct linear factors, since p is unramified). Completing the square: the polynomial has roots ½(1±√q*), so mod p it splits iff q* is a nonzero square mod p, i.e. iff (q*/p) = 1 (using p ≠ 2 to divide by 4 safely)."
/>

Chaining everything together:
$$
\left( \frac pq \right) = 1
\iff \sigma_p \in H
\iff \Frob_{\kP} \in H
\iff \Frob_\kp = \id_K
\iff \ord(\Frob_\kp) = 1
\iff f_\kp = 1
\iff \text{$p$ splits in $\ZZ\!\left[\tfrac12(1+\sqrt{q^\ast})\right]$}
\iff \left( \frac{q^\ast}{p} \right) = 1.
$$

That's the reformulated theorem — and by the exercise two slides back, it's equivalent to quadratic reciprocity itself. Notice how little "new" number theory went in: essentially every step was either the order lemma, the restriction theorem, or the cyclotomic Frobenius lemma, all proved earlier in this chapter.
