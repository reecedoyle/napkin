<SlideLayout title="Step 2: a reformulation">

Rather than attack quadratic reciprocity head-on, we'll prove an equivalent-looking statement that turns out to be much more Frobenius-friendly.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Quadratic reciprocity, equivalent formulation">
For distinct odd primes $p$, $q$:
$$
\left( \frac pq \right) = \left( \frac{q^\ast}{p} \right).
$$
</Callout>

<Callout kind="exercise" title="Check the equivalence">
Using the fact that $\left(\frac{-1}{p}\right) = (-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}$, show that this reformulation is equivalent to quadratic reciprocity as we stated it.
</Callout>

<ProofReveal
  client:load
  id="frob-qr-proof-reformulation-equivalent"
  prompt="Using (−1/p) = (−1)^((p−1)/2), show (p/q) = (q*/p) is equivalent to quadratic reciprocity."
  solution="Recall q* = ±q with the sign chosen so q* ≡ 1 (mod 4); precisely q* = (−1)^((q−1)/2)·q. So (q*/p) = ((−1)^((q−1)/2)/p) · (q/p) = (−1/p)^((q−1)/2) · (q/p) = (−1)^((p−1)/2 · (q−1)/2) · (q/p), using (−1/p) = (−1)^((p−1)/2) and the multiplicativity of the Legendre symbol. So the reformulated statement (p/q) = (q*/p) becomes (p/q) = (−1)^((p−1)/2·(q−1)/2)·(q/p), i.e. (p/q)(q/p) = (−1)^((p−1)/2·(q−1)/2), which is exactly quadratic reciprocity."
/>

</SlideLayout>
