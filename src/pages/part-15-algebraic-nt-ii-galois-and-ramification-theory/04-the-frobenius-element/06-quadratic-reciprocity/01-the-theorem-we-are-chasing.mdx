Time to put all this machinery to work on a genuinely famous theorem — one you may have already met (and maybe proved by a completely different, more combinatorial route).

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Quadratic reciprocity">
Let $p$ and $q$ be distinct odd primes. Then
$$
\left( \frac pq \right)\left( \frac qp \right) = (-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2} \cdot \frac{q-1}{2}},
$$
where <Term k="frob61LegendreSymbol">$\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)$</Term> is the Legendre symbol.
</Callout>

<Term k="frob61QuadraticReciprocity">Quadratic reciprocity</Term> answers the question "if $q$ is a square mod $p$, does that tell us whether $p$ is a square mod $q$?" — remarkably, the two questions are (almost) the same question, up to a sign that depends only on $p, q \pmod 4$.

We're going to derive this from Frobenius elements and Chebotarev — a genuinely different proof than the classical Eisenstein lattice-point argument.
