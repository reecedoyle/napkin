<Callout kind="lemma" title="Cyclotomic Frobenius elements">
In the cyclotomic setting $L = \QQ(\zeta_q)$, let $p$ be a rational unramified prime and $\kp$ above it. Then
$$
\Frob_\kp = \sigma_p.
$$
</Callout>

The proof is a one-liner, and it's the whole point of the "uniqueness" remark from the very start of the chapter: **any** element of $\Gal(L/\QQ)$ satisfying the defining functional equation *is* the Frobenius element, no further work required.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Proof">
Observe that $\sigma_p$ satisfies the functional equation defining $\Frob_\kp$ — just check it on the generator $\zeta_q$: $\sigma_p(\zeta_q) = \zeta_q^p \equiv \zeta_q^p \pmod \kp$, trivially. By the "freshman's dream" argument from §1, checking on the generator $\zeta_q$ is enough. So $\sigma_p$ satisfies the same functional equation as $\Frob_\kp$, and by uniqueness, $\sigma_p = \Frob_\kp$. Done.
</Callout>

This is a wonderful shortcut: in the cyclotomic setting, we never have to think about decomposition groups or residue fields at all — the Frobenius element is just "raise everything to the $p$-th power symbolically."

</SlideLayout>
