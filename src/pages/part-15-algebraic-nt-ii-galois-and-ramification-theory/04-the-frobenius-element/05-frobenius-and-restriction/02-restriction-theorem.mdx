<SlideLayout title="Theorem: restrictions of Frobenius elements">

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Restrictions of Frobenius elements">
Assume $L/\QQ$ and $K/\QQ$ are both Galois. Let $\kP$ and $\kp$ be unramified as above. Then <Term k="frob61Restriction">$\Frob_{\kP}\restrict{K} = \Frob_\kp$</Term>, i.e. for every $\alpha \in K$,
$$
\Frob_\kp(\alpha) = \Frob_{\kP}(\alpha).
$$
</Callout>

<Callout kind="remark" title="Proof">
First, $K/\QQ$ is normal, so $\Frob_{\kP}$ fixes $K$ setwise, and $\Frob_{\kP}\restrict{K} \in \Gal(K/\QQ)$ is well-defined. We have a natural map $\phi \colon \OO_K \to \OO_L \to \OO_L/\kP$ and the quotient map $q \colon \OO_K \to \OO_K/\kp$. Since $\kp \subseteq \kP \cap \OO_K \subseteq \ker \phi$, the map $\phi$ factors through $q$, giving a natural field embedding $\OO_K/\kp \hookrightarrow \OO_L/\kP$. Since $\Frob_{\kP}$ induces the power-of-$p$ map on $\OO_L/\kP$, and this embedding is compatible with everything in sight, restricting to $\OO_K/\kp$ shows $\Frob_{\kP}\restrict{K}$ induces the power-of-$p$ map there too. By uniqueness, $\Frob_{\kP}\restrict{K} = \Frob_\kp$.
</Callout>

</SlideLayout>
