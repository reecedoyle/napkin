<Callout kind="remark" title="A sharper count">
In fact $K = \QQ(\sqrt[p]{p}, \zeta_p)$, and $|G| = p(p-1)$. With a little more group theory (counting the order-$p$ elements of $G$ and their conjugacy class), one can show the density of primes $q$ that work is exactly $\frac1p$.
</Callout>

This is a nice illustration of the general pattern in this chapter: Chebotarev doesn't just tell you *that* infinitely many primes have some Galois-theoretic property — it tells you exactly *how many*, as a clean fraction of all primes.
