<SlideLayout title="Setting up with Cauchy">

We draw the tower of fields $\QQ \subseteq \QQ(\sqrt[p]{p}) \subseteq K$, where $K$ is the splitting field of $f(x) = x^p - p$. Let $E = \QQ(\sqrt[p]{p})$ for brevity, noting $[E:\QQ] = p$, and let $G = \Gal(K/\QQ)$.

<Callout kind="question" title="A subgroup of order p">
Show that $p$ divides the order of $G$. (Look at $E$.)
</Callout>

<ProofReveal
  client:load
  id="frob-imo-proof-p-divides-order-g"
  prompt="Show that p divides |G| = |Gal(K/ℚ)|. (Hint: look at E.)"
  solution="E = ℚ(∛[p]{p}) has degree p over ℚ, and E sits inside K. By the tower law, [K:ℚ] = [K:E]·[E:ℚ], so p = [E:ℚ] divides [K:ℚ] = |G| (using K/ℚ Galois, so |G| = [K:ℚ])."
/>

Hence, by <Term k="frob61CauchysGroupTheorem">Cauchy's theorem</Term> — a purely group-theoretic fact — we can find $\sigma \in G$ of order exactly $p$.

</SlideLayout>
