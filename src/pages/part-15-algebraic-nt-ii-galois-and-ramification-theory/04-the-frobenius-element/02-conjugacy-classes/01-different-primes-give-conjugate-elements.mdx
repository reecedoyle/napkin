<SlideLayout title="Different primes above p give conjugate Frobenius elements">

Now suppose $\kp_1$ and $\kp_2$ are **two** primes above an unramified rational prime $p$. Then we get two Frobenius elements, $\Frob_{\kp_1}$ and $\Frob_{\kp_2}$ — and since the Galois group acts transitively on the primes above $p$, we can pick some $\sigma \in \Gal(K/\QQ)$ with $\sigma(\kp_1) = \kp_2$.

<Callout kind="proposition" title="Frobenius elements above the same p are conjugate">
With $\sigma$ as above,
$$
\Frob_{\kp_2} = \sigma \circ \Frob_{\kp_1} \circ \sigma^{-1}.
$$
This is an equation of group elements: $\Frob_{\kp_2}$ is the <Term k="conjugate">conjugate</Term> of $\Frob_{\kp_1}$ by $\sigma$.
</Callout>
