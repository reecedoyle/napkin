<SlideLayout title="Frobenius elements above p form a conjugacy class">

The previous slide generalizes cleanly: for a given unramified rational prime $p$, all the Frobenius elements over it fill out one entire <Term k="conjugacyClass">conjugacy class</Term> of $G$ — no more, no less.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Conjugacy classes in Galois groups">
The set $\{ \Frob_\kp \mid \kp \text{ above } p \}$ is one of the conjugacy classes of $G$.
</Callout>

We've already shown $\Frob_{\kp_1}$ and $\Frob_{\kp_2}$ are conjugate whenever both lie above $p$, so the set is *contained* in some conjugacy class. For the reverse inclusion: given any $\Frob_\kp$ and any $\sigma \in G$, we just need $\sigma \circ \Frob_\kp \circ \sigma^{-1}$ to itself be a Frobenius element above $p$ — and indeed it equals $\Frob_{\kp'}$ for $\kp' = \sigma\kp$ (another prime above $p$, since $\sigma$ fixes $\QQ$). So every conjugate of $\Frob_\kp$ shows up in our set, and the set is exactly a conjugacy class.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Moral">
$\Frob_\kp$ is determined up to conjugation by the rational prime $p$ from which $\kp$ arises. So even though the Gothic letters $\kp$ look scary, the *content* of $\Frob_\kp$ really just comes from the friendlier, more familiar rational prime $p$.
</Callout>

</SlideLayout>
