<SlideLayout title="The Hilbert class field">

From now on, let $K = \QQ(\sqrt{-5})$, and let $L$ range over abelian extensions of $K$. Recall $H(L/K,\km) = \ker\left(\frac{L/K}{\bullet}\right)$, and Artin reciprocity guarantees $P_K(\km) \subseteq H(L/K,\km)$.

Why bother with $H$ separately from the conductor moral? Because the *converse* is genuinely extra information:

<Callout kind="remark" title="Moral">
If $P_K(\kf) = H(L/K,\kf)$, then we can recover "$\kp \bmod \kf$" once we know $\left( \frac{L/K}{\kp} \right)$ — i.e. Artin reciprocity becomes an *if and only if*.
</Callout>

Take $L = K(i)$. It turns out (this is exactly the content of the Hilbert class field problem at the end of the chapter):

<Callout kind="definition" title="Hilbert class field">
$L = K(i)$ satisfies: $L/K$ is abelian, its conductor is $\kf(L/K) = 1$, and $H(L/K,1) = P_K(1)$. We call $L$ the <Term k="gr62HilbertClassField">Hilbert class field</Term> of $K$.
</Callout>

For a prime $\kp \subseteq K$, the following are all equivalent: $\kp$ is principal; $\left( \frac{L/K}{\kp} \right) = \id$; and $\kp$ <Term k="gr62SplitCompletely">splits completely</Term> when lifted to $L$.
