<SlideLayout title="Ramification of infinite primes">

Only *real* infinite primes get to do anything interesting when we lift them:

<Callout kind="definition" title="Ramified infinite prime">
For a real infinite prime $\sigma$ of $K$, if any $\tau$ above it (in a Galois extension $L/K$) is complex, we say $\sigma$ <Term k="gr62RamifiedInfinite">ramifies</Term> in $L/K$. Otherwise $\sigma$ is unramified in $L/K$.
</Callout>

A complex infinite prime, by contrast, can never ramify further — a complex embedding only ever extends to complex embeddings. So the only thing that can happen along the way is: a *real* embedding of $K$ might fail to extend to a real embedding of $L$, becoming complex instead.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Unramified Galois extension">
Combining this with the ramification you already know for finite primes: we call $L/K$ an **unramified** Galois extension when *every* prime of $K$ — finite or infinite — is unramified in $L/K$.
</Callout>

</SlideLayout>
