<SlideLayout title="Example: ray classes in ℚ with a finite modulus">

This is the example that shows why finite primes alone won't be enough later. Take $K = \QQ$, with $\km = 8$ (no infinite prime included yet).

Then $I_\QQ(8) \cong \left\{ \frac ab\ZZ \mid a/b \equiv 1,3,5,7 \pmod 8 \right\}$, and

$$
P_\QQ(8) \cong \left\{ \frac ab\ZZ \mid a/b \equiv 1 \pmod 8 \right\}.
$$

<Callout kind="remark" title="The tempting wrong answer">
You might guess the quotient is just $\Zm{8}$. But we're dealing with **ideals**, and ideals don't see signs: $7\ZZ = -7\ZZ$, and $-7 \equiv 1 \pmod 8$. So $7\ZZ \in P_\QQ(8)$ even though $7 \not\equiv 1 \pmod 8$!
</Callout>

Working it out correctly, we get

$$
C_\QQ(8) \cong \{1,3,5,7 \bmod 8\} / \{1,7 \bmod 8\} \cong \Zm{4}.
$$

More generally, $C_\QQ(m) = \Zm{m} / \{\pm 1\}$ — the sign ambiguity in ideal generators always collapses $\pm a$ to the same class.

</SlideLayout>
