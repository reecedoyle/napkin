<SlideLayout title="The ray class group">

Fix a modulus $\km$ of $K$. Now we can define the promised generalization of the class group.

<Callout kind="definition" title="I_K(m) and P_K(m)">
- Let $I_K(\km)$, or <Term k="gr62IK">$I_K(\km)$</Term>, denote the group of all fractional ideals of $K$ relatively prime to $\km$.
- Let $P_K(\km)$, or <Term k="gr62PK">$P_K(\km)$</Term>, be the subgroup of $I_K(\km)$ generated by
$$
\left\{ \alpha \OO_K \mid \alpha \in K^\times \text{ and } \alpha \equiv 1 \pmod{\km} \right\}.
$$
</Callout>

$P_K(\km)$ is sometimes called a "ray" of principal ideals — like a geometric ray, it extends infinitely in one direction, at least once an infinite prime is present in $\km$.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Ray class group">
The <Term k="gr62RayClassGroup">ray class group</Term> of $\km$ is $C_K(\km) = I_K(\km) / P_K(\km)$.
</Callout>

This group is always finite. Taking $\km = 1$ (the empty modulus) recovers the ordinary <Term k="an56classGroup">class group</Term>: $C_K(1) = \Cl_K$.

One more definition we'll need right after Artin reciprocity:

<Callout kind="definition" title="Congruence subgroup">
A <Term k="gr62CongruenceSubgroup">congruence subgroup</Term> of $\km$ is a subgroup $H$ with $P_K(\km) \subseteq H \subseteq I_K(\km)$.
</Callout>

So $C_K(\km)$ sits at the top of a whole lattice of quotients $I_K(\km)/H$, one for each congruence subgroup $H$.

</SlideLayout>
