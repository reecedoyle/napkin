<SlideLayout title="Example: a base field other than ℚ">

Let $L = \QQ(\omega, \sqrt[3]{2})$ and $K = \QQ(\omega)$, where $\omega$ is a primitive cube root of unity.

<Callout kind="example" title="Working it out">
Consider $\kp = (5)$, which is prime in $K$, with $\Norm(\kp) = 25$. The residue field $\OO_K/\kp$ is isomorphic to $\FF_{25}$.

In $L$, the ideal $\kp$ splits as $\kP_1 \kP_2 \kP_3$, and each residue field $\OO_L/\kP_i$ is also isomorphic to $\FF_{25}$.

The Frobenius element $\Frob_\kP \in \Gal(L/K)$ induces the power-of-$25$ map on the residue field — but that's the *identity* map on $\FF_{25}$ (since every element of $\FF_{25}^\times$ has order dividing $24$, and Fermat's little theorem for finite fields gives $x^{25} = x$). So $\Frob_\kP = \id$.
</Callout>

The lesson: once you replace "raise to the $p$-th power" with "raise to the $\Norm(\kp)$-th power", the Frobenius machinery works over any number field base, not just $\QQ$.

</SlideLayout>
