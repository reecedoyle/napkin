The same trick generalizes beyond quadratic residues. Suppose $\theta$ is a primary prime in $K = \QQ(\sqrt{-3}) = \QQ(\omega)$ (so $\OO_K = \ZZ[\omega]$, the Eisenstein integers). Then

$$
\left( \frac{K(\sqrt[3]{2})/K}{\theta \OO_K} \right)\!\left(\sqrt[3]2\right) \equiv \left(\sqrt[3]2\right)^{\Norm(\theta)} \equiv 2^{\frac{\Norm(\theta)-1}{3}} \cdot \sqrt[3]2 \equiv \left( \frac{2}{\theta} \right)_{\!3} \sqrt[3]2 \pmod \kP,
$$

where $\kP$ lies above $(\theta)$ in $K(\sqrt[3]2)$, and $\left( \frac{2}{\theta} \right)_3$ is the <Term k="gr62CubicResidueSymbol">cubic residue symbol</Term>.

<Callout kind="remark" title="A pattern emerging">
Quadratic reciprocity, cubic reciprocity — every classical reciprocity law you've met turns out to be a special case of one Artin symbol computation. That's the miracle Artin reciprocity is going to make precise.
</Callout>
