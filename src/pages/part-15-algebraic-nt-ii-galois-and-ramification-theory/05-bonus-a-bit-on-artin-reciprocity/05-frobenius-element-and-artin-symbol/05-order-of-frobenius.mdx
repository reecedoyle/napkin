<SlideLayout title="The order of the Frobenius element">

The order of the Frobenius element tells you exactly how $\kp$ splits in $L$.

<Callout kind="lemma" title="Order of the Frobenius element">
$\Frob_\kP \in \Gal(L/K)$ has order equal to the <Term k="gr62InertialDegree">inertial degree</Term> of $\kP$:
$$
\ord \Frob_\kP = f(\kP \mid \kp).
$$
In particular, $\Frob_\kP = \id$ if and only if $\kp$ **splits completely** in $L/K$.
</Callout>

Reading off exponents, this order is log base N(𝔭) of N(𝔓), which is f(𝔓|𝔭) by definition of the inertial degree."

Also worth remembering: the Galois group acts transitively on the primes $\kP$ above a fixed $\kp$, and $\Frob_{\sigma(\kP)} = \sigma \circ \Frob_\kP \circ \sigma^{-1}$. So $\Frob_\kP$ is determined by $\kp$ **up to conjugation** — it can genuinely depend on which $\kP$ you pick, unless conjugacy classes in $\Gal(L/K)$ are trivial.
