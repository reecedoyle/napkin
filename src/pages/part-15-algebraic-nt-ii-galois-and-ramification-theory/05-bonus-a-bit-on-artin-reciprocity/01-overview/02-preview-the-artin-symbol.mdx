<SlideLayout title="Preview — the Artin symbol">

By the end of §5 we will define, for an abelian extension $L/K$ and a prime ideal $\kp$ of $K$, the <Term k="gr62ArtinSymbol">Artin symbol</Term>

$$
\left( \frac{L/K}{\kp} \right).
$$

This notation is deliberately suggestive: it generalizes the <Term k="gr62LegendreSymbol">Legendre symbol</Term> $\left( \frac ap \right)$ from elementary number theory.

<Callout kind="remark" title="The dictionary">
- Above the solidus, instead of an integer $a$, we have a field extension $L/K$.
- Below the solidus, instead of a rational prime $p$, we have a <Term k="an55primeIdealOK">prime ideal</Term> $\kp$ of $K$.
</Callout>

One catch: the symbol is only defined when $\kp$ does **not** ramify in $L/K$ — you'll recall from the earlier ramification chapters that some primes behave badly in an extension, and the Artin symbol simply refuses to be defined at those.

We are heading toward the Artin reciprocity theorem, which — very roughly — says:

<Callout kind="remark" title="Moral">
For primes $\kp$, the symbol $\left( \frac{L/K}{\kp} \right)$ depends only on "$\kp$ modulo $\kf$" — where $\kf$ is a **modulus** depending only on the extension $L/K$, not on $\kp$.
</Callout>

The rest of the chapter is spent making "modulus" and "depends only on $\kp$ mod $\kf$" precise.

</SlideLayout>
