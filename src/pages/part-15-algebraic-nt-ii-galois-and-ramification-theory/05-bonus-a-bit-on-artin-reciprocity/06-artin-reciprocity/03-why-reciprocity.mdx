<SlideLayout title="Why it's called a reciprocity theorem">

Apply the theorem at $\km = \kf$ itself: it tells us $P_K(\kf) \subseteq H(L/K, \kf)$. So the Artin map factors through the quotient map $I_K(\kf) \surjto I_K(\kf)/P_K(\kf)$.

Recalling that $C_K(\kf) = I_K(\kf)/P_K(\kf)$, we get a chain of surjections

$$
I_K(\kf) \;\surjto\; C_K(\kf) \;\surjto\; \Gal(L/K),
$$

where the second map is the composite of $C_K(\kf) \cong I_K(\kf)/H(L/K,\kf)$ with the isomorphism from the Artin map. Every arrow in this chain only depends on which coset of $\kf$ you're in.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Moral">
For primes $\kp \in I_K(\kf)$, the symbol $\left( \frac{L/K}{\kp} \right)$ depends only on "$\kp \pmod \kf$".
</Callout>

That's the reciprocity: a Galois-theoretic quantity (which conjugacy class of Frobenius you land in) is completely determined by a purely arithmetic congruence condition on $\kp$ — no reference to $L$ needed once you know $\kf$.

</SlideLayout>
