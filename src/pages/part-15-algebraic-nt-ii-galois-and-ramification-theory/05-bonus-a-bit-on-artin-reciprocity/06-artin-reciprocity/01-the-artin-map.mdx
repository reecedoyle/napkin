<SlideLayout title="The Artin map">

Now we capitalize fully on $\Gal(L/K)$ being abelian. Write <Term k="gr62RamSet">$\Ram(L/K)$</Term> for the set of primes of $K$ (finite or infinite) that ramify in $L$.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Artin map">
Let $L/K$ be abelian and let $\km$ be divisible by every prime in $\Ram(L/K)$. Since the Artin symbol is defined at every unramified prime, and $\Gal(L/K)$ is abelian, we can extend it *multiplicatively* to a map

$$
\left( \frac{L/K}{\bullet} \right) \colon I_K(\km) \surjto \Gal(L/K).
$$

This is the <Term k="gr62ArtinMap">Artin map</Term>. It's surjective — for instance by the Chebotarev density theorem, which says every element of $\Gal(L/K)$ arises as $\Frob_\kP$ for infinitely many $\kp$.
</Callout>

Let <Term k="gr62HLK">$H(L/K, \km)$</Term> $\subseteq I_K(\km)$ denote the kernel of this map, so that

$$
\Gal(L/K) \cong I_K(\km) / H(L/K, \km).
$$

</SlideLayout>
