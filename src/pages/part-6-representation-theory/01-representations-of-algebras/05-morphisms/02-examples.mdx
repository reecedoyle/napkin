<SlideLayout title="Examples of intertwining operators">

Some natural <Term k="intertwinOp">intertwining operators</Term>:

- **Scalar maps.** For any $\lambda \in k$, the map $T(v) = \lambda v$ is intertwining.
- **Inclusions.** If $W \subseteq V$ is a <Term k="subrep">subrepresentation</Term>, the inclusion $W \hookrightarrow V$ is intertwining.
- **Projections.** The projection $V_1 \oplus V_2 \twoheadrightarrow V_1$ is intertwining.
- **Rotations.** Let $A = \mathbb{R}[x]$ act on $V = \mathbb{R}^{\oplus 2}$ by $\rho(x) = $ rotation by $90°$. Then rotation by $30°$ is also an intertwining operator (rotations commute).

<Callout kind="example" title="A non-example">
Let $A = \Mat_2(\mathbb{R}) \oplus \Mat_2(\mathbb{R})$. The two irreps $V_1 = \{(m,n,0,0)\}$ and $V_2 = \{(0,0,p,q)\}$ of $A$ are isomorphic as $\mathbb{R}$-vector spaces but **not** as representations of $A$ — any intertwining operator $V_1 \to V_2$ must be identically zero.
</Callout>
