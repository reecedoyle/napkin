<Callout kind="theorem" title="Schur's lemma">
Let $V$ and $W$ be representations of a $k$-algebra $A$. Let $T \colon V \to W$ be a **nonzero** <Term k="intertwinOp">intertwining operator</Term>. Then:
1. If $V$ is <Term k="irrep">irreducible</Term>, then $T$ is injective.
2. If $W$ is irreducible, then $T$ is surjective.

In particular, if both $V$ and $W$ are irreducible then $T$ is an isomorphism.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Schur's lemma — algebraically closed case">
Let $k$ be algebraically closed and $V$ a finite-dimensional irrep of $A$. Then every intertwining operator $T \colon V \to V$ is multiplication by some scalar $\lambda \in k$.
</Callout>

The proof uses eigenvalues: $T$ has an eigenvalue $\lambda$ over an algebraically closed field. Then $T - \lambda \cdot \mathrm{id}_V$ is an intertwining operator with nontrivial kernel — by Schur, it must be zero. So $T = \lambda \cdot \mathrm{id}_V$.
