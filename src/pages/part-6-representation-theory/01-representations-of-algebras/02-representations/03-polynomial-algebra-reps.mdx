<SlideLayout title="Representations of polynomial algebras">

Polynomial algebras give a neat perspective: a representation amounts to choosing a linear map satisfying some functional equation.

- $A = k$: a representation is just any $k$-vector space $V$.
- $A = k[x]$: a representation $(V, \rho)$ is a vector space $V$ plus a choice of linear map $T = \rho(x) \in \Mat(V)$ — any $T$ works.
- $A = k[x]/(x^2)$: a representation is a vector space $V$ plus $T \in \Mat(V)$ with $T^2 = 0$.
- $A = k[x,y]/(x^2 - x + y,\, y^4)$: a representation requires commuting operators $S, T$ with $S^2 = S - T$ and $T^4 = 0$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Representations as functional equations">
Representing an algebra $A$ on $V$ is the same as solving the relations of $A$ in the algebra of linear maps on $V$.
</Callout>
