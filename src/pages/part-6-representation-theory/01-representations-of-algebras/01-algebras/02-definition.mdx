<Callout kind="definition" title="k-algebra">
Let $k$ be a field. A <Term k="kAlgebra">**$k$-algebra**</Term> $A$ is a possibly noncommutative ring equipped with a ring homomorphism $k \hookrightarrow A$ (whose image is the "copy of $k$"), satisfying $\lambda \cdot a = a \cdot \lambda$ for each $\lambda \in k$ and $a \in A$.

If multiplication is also commutative, $A$ is a **commutative algebra**.
</Callout>

Equivalently, $A$ is a <Term k="vectorSpace">$k$-vector space</Term> with an associative, bilinear multiplication (with identity $1_A$). The "copy of $k$" comes from scaling the identity: $\lambda \mapsto \lambda 1_A$.

Think of a $k$-algebra as a ring that "knows about" the field $k$ — scalars from $k$ commute with everything and act as expected.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Convention">
Some authors require neither associativity nor a unit, calling what we have an "associative algebra with 1". We always assume both.
</Callout>
