<Callout kind="definition" title="Group algebra">
The <Term k="groupAlgebra">**group algebra**</Term> $k[G]$ is the $k$-vector space whose **basis elements** are the elements of $G$, with multiplication of basis elements given by the group multiplication in $G$, extended bilinearly.
</Callout>

**Example.** Take $G = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} = \{1_G, x\}$. Then

$$k[G] = \{ a 1_G + b x \mid a, b \in k \}$$

with multiplication $(a 1_G + bx)(c 1_G + dx) = (ac + bd) 1_G + (bc + ad)x$.

This is one of the most important algebras in representation theory: a **representation of the algebra $k[G]$** turns out to be exactly the same thing as a **representation of the group $G$**. We will make this precise in the next section.
