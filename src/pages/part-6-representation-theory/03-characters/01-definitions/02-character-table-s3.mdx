<SlideLayout title="Character table of S₃">

The three irreps of $G = S_3$ each give a character.
For one-dimensional representations the trace **is** the scalar the element acts by.

<Callout kind="example" title="Characters of S₃">
Write the three irreps in columns indexed by group elements:

| $\chi_V(g)$ | $\id$ | $(1\;2)$ | $(2\;3)$ | $(3\;1)$ | $(1\;2\;3)$ | $(3\;2\;1)$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\Ctriv$ | $1$ | $1$ | $1$ | $1$ | $1$ | $1$ |
| $\Csign$ | $1$ | $-1$ | $-1$ | $-1$ | $1$ | $1$ |
| $\refl_0$ | $2$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | $-1$ | $-1$ |

For $\refl_0$, use the basis $(1,0,-1)$ and $(0,1,-1)$ and compute traces directly.
</Callout>

This array is called the <Term k="characterTable">**character table**</Term> of $G$.
It encodes all finite-dimensional representation theory of $G$ over $\CC$.

Notice a few striking patterns in the table:

- The three transpositions $(1\;2)$, $(2\;3)$, $(3\;1)$ give identical columns.
- The two 3-cycles $(1\;2\;3)$ and $(3\;2\;1)$ give identical columns.
- The sum of squares in each row equals $|G| = 6$.
- Any two rows are "orthogonal" in a suitable inner-product sense.

These are not coincidences. The rest of the chapter explains why they must hold.

</SlideLayout>
