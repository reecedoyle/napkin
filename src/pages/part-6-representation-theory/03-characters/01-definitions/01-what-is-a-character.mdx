<SlideLayout title="What is a character?">

Every finite-dimensional representation carries a single scalar-valued function that captures its essential data — the **character**.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Character of a representation">
Let $V = (V, \rho)$ be a finite-dimensional representation of an algebra $A$ over a field $k$.
The <Term k="characterRep">**character**</Term> $\chi_V \colon A \to k$ is defined by
$$\chi_V(a) \defeq \Tr\!\bigl(\rho(a) \colon V \to V\bigr).$$
</Callout>

Since both $\Tr$ and $\rho$ are additive (i.e.\ $k$-linear), $\chi_V$ is itself a $k$-linear map — but **not** multiplicative.

Two immediate consequences:

- **Direct sums:** $\chi_{V \oplus W} = \chi_V + \chi_W$ for any representations $V$ and $W$.
- **Dimension readout:** $\chi_V(1_A) = \Tr(\id_V) = \dim V$.

For the group algebra $A = k[G]$ we only need to specify $\chi_V(g)$ for each $g \in G$;
the linearity of $\chi_V$ then determines the full map $k[G] \to k$.
So we often treat a character as a function $G \to k$.

</SlideLayout>
