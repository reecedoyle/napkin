<SlideLayout title="The character theorem for algebras">

We can now state the main structural result about characters of algebra representations.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Characters of algebra representations">
Let $A$ be an algebra over an algebraically closed field. Then:

**(a)** The characters of pairwise non-isomorphic finite-dimensional irreps are **linearly independent** in $(A^{\mathrm{ab}})^\vee$.

**(b)** If $A$ is finite-dimensional and semisimple, the characters of the irreps form a **basis** of $(A^{\mathrm{ab}})^\vee$.

In particular, in (b), the number of irreps of $A$ equals $\dim A^{\mathrm{ab}}$.
</Callout>

For part (a), linear independence uses the density theorem: a dependence $\sum_i c_i \chi_{V_i}(a) = 0$ for all $a$ forces each $c_i = 0$.

  solution="By semisimplicity A ≅ ⊕ᵢ Mat(Vᵢ). Each Mat(Vᵢ) has a one-dimensional abelianization, so dim A^ab = r (the number of irreps). Part (a) gives r linearly independent elements in a space of dimension r, so they form a basis."
/>

**Corollary for groups:** Since $k[G]^{\mathrm{ab}}$ has dimension equal to the number of conjugacy classes, the number of irreps of $G$ equals the number of conjugacy classes. This is Property (II).
