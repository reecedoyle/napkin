<SlideLayout title="Abelianization — a warning about group vs. algebra">

There are two different "abelianizations" in play. It is easy to confuse them.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Group abelianization ≠ algebra abelianization">
For a group $G$, the **group abelianization** is $G/[G,G]$, quotienting by the subgroup generated by all $ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$.

When $A = k[G]$, the algebra <Term k="abelianizationAlg">abelianization</Term> $A^{\mathrm{ab}} = k[G]/[k[G], k[G]]$
is **not** the same as the group algebra $k[G/[G,G]]$.

The group abelianization sets $ghg^{-1}h^{-1} = 1$ (a multiplicative condition), while the algebra abelianization sets $gh - hg = 0$ (an additive condition). These are different.

Moreover, $A^{\mathrm{ab}}$ does not inherit a $k$-algebra structure from $A$ — it is only a $k$-vector space.
</Callout>

**What $A^{\mathrm{ab}}$ does give us** for $G$ finite: its dimension equals the number of conjugacy classes of $G$.
Combining this with the character theorem coming next, we get Property (II) for free.
