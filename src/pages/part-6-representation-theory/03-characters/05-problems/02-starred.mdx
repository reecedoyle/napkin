<Problem
  difficulty="starred"
  id="chr-prob-second-orthog"
  prompt="Let $g, h \in G$ and let $V_1, \dots, V_r$ be the irreps of $G$. Prove the second orthogonality formula: $\sum_{i=1}^r \chi_{V_i}(g) \ol{\chi_{V_i}(h)} = \begin{cases} |C_G(g)| & \text{if } g \sim h \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$ Here $C_G(g) = \{x \in G : xg = gx\}$ is the centralizer."
  hint="Construct two square $r \times r$ matrices $A$ and $B$ such that $AB$ is the identity by the first orthogonality relation. Then use the product $BA$ to derive the second orthogonality relation."
  solution="Let $A$ be the $r \times r$ matrix with $A_{ij} = \chi_{V_i}(C_j) \sqrt{|C_j|/|G|}$ (rows indexed by irreps, columns by conjugacy classes). Let $B = A^*$ (conjugate transpose). The first orthogonality relation says $AB = I$. For square matrices, $AB = I$ implies $BA = I$, giving the second orthogonality relation after translating back: $\sum_i \chi_{V_i}(C_j) \ol{\chi_{V_i}(C_k)} = (|G|/|C_j|) \delta_{jk}$. Since $|C_j| = |G|/|C_G(g_j)|$ by the orbit-stabilizer theorem, this becomes $|C_G(g)|$ when $j = k$."
