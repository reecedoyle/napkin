<SlideLayout title="Character trace formulas">

We collect the three key trace identities in one place.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Character traces">
For finite-dimensional representations $V$ and $W$ of $G$ and any $g \in G$:

**(a)** $\chi_{V \oplus W}(g) = \chi_V(g) + \chi_W(g)$.

**(b)** $\chi_{V \otimes W}(g) = \chi_V(g) \cdot \chi_W(g)$.

**(c)** $\chi_{V^\vee}(g) = \ol{\chi_V(g)}$.
</Callout>

Parts (a) and (b) follow from the matrix identities $\Tr(S \oplus T) = \Tr(S) + \Tr(T)$ and $\Tr(S \otimes T) = \Tr(S)\Tr(T)$.

Part (c) was proved on the previous slide: the eigenvalues of $g$ acting on $V^\vee$ are the inverses of the eigenvalues on $V$, and since they are roots of unity, inversion equals complex conjugation.

<Callout kind="remark" title="These do not extend to algebra elements">
Identities (b) and (c) are special to *group* elements.
For a general $a \in \CC[G]$, it is **false** that $\chi_{V^\vee}(a) = \ol{\chi_V(a)}$.
The tensor product for algebras is also defined differently (via the diagonal action, not the naïve product).
</Callout>

Putting (b) and (c) together: $\chi_V(g)\ol{\chi_W(g)} = \chi_{V \otimes W^\vee}(g)$.
This is the key identity that lets us rewrite the inner product as a single character evaluation.

</SlideLayout>
