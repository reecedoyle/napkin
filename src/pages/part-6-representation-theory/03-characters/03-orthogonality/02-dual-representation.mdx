<SlideLayout title="The dual representation">

To handle the conjugate $\ol{\chi_W(g)}$ in the inner product, we need a representation whose character is the complex conjugate.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Dual representation">
Let $(V, \rho)$ be a representation of $G$.
The <Term k="dualRepresentation">**dual representation**</Term> $V^\vee$ acts on the dual space $V^\vee = \Hom(V, \CC)$.
For $\xi \in V^\vee$ and $v \in V$:
$$(g \cdot \xi)(v) \defeq \xi\!\left(\rho(g^{-1})(v)\right).$$
</Callout>

**Character of the dual:** The action of $g$ on $V^\vee$ has eigenvalues $\lambda_1^{-1}, \dots, \lambda_n^{-1}$ whenever $g$ acts on $V$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$.
Since $g^{|G|} = 1_G$, every $\lambda_i$ is a root of unity, so $\lambda_i^{-1} = \ol{\lambda_i}$.
Hence:

$$\chi_{V^\vee}(g) = \sum_i \lambda_i^{-1} = \sum_i \ol{\lambda_i} = \ol{\chi_V(g)}.$$

The formula $|\lambda| = 1 \iff \lambda^{-1} = \ol\lambda$ is the crucial step.

</SlideLayout>
