<SlideLayout title="Proof of orthogonality">

The proof reduces the inner product to a single character value via the averaging operator.

Using $\chi_V(g)\ol{\chi_W(g)} = \chi_{V \otimes W^\vee}(g)$, we rewrite:

$$\langle \chi_V, \chi_W \rangle = \frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{g \in G} \chi_{V \otimes W^\vee}(g) = \chi_{V \otimes W^\vee}\!\left(\frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{g \in G} g\right).$$

Let $P \colon V \otimes W^\vee \to V \otimes W^\vee$ be the action of the averaging element $\frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{g \in G} g$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="P is an idempotent projection">
A direct computation shows $P^2 = P$ (idempotent). So $V \otimes W^\vee = \ker P \oplus \img P$, and $P$ acts as $0$ on $\ker P$ and as $1$ on $\img P$. Hence $\Tr P = \dim \img P$.

The image $\img P$ is precisely the **$G$-fixed subspace**: $\{x \in V \otimes W^\vee : g \cdot x = x \text{ for all } g \in G\}$.
</Callout>

Under the natural isomorphism $V \otimes W^\vee \cong \Hom(W, V)$, an element $T \in \Hom(W, V)$ is $G$-fixed if and only if $T(g \cdot w) = g \cdot T(w)$ for all $w \in W$ — that is, $T$ is a morphism of representations.

Therefore $\dim \img P = \dim \Homrep(W, V)$, completing the proof.

</SlideLayout>
