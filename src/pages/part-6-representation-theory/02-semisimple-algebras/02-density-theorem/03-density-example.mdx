<SlideLayout title="Density theorem — explicit example">

Let $A = \Mat_2(k)$ with the obvious irrep $V = k^{\oplus 2}$.

The density theorem says: for any $e_1, e_2 \in V$ linearly independent and any $w_1, w_2 \in V$, there exists $a \in A$ with $a \cdot e_1 = w_1$ and $a \cdot e_2 = w_2$.

To see the proof's mechanism, choose instead **linearly dependent** $e_1 = (1,0)$ and $e_2 = (2,0)$. The map $\Reg(A) \to V^{\oplus 2}$ by $a \mapsto (a \cdot e_1, a \cdot e_2)$ has image $\{(v, 2v) : v \in V\}$, isomorphic to $V^{\oplus 1}$ via $v \mapsto (v, 2v)$.

The proof works because the basis assumption in the non-degenerate case prevents this from happening: a **true** basis cannot have such a dependence.
