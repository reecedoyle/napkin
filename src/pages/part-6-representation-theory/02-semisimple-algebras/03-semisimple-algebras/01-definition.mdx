<Callout kind="definition" title="Semisimple algebra">
A finite-dimensional algebra $A$ is <Term k="semisimpleAlgebra">**semisimple**</Term> if every finite-dimensional representation of $A$ is completely reducible.
</Callout>

Intuitively: $A$ is semisimple when there are no "weird" indecomposable-but-reducible representations. Every representation just decomposes cleanly into irreducible blocks.

The prototype of pathological behavior is a representation that has a proper subrepresentation but cannot be split as a direct sum — these are called indecomposable but reducible. Semisimplicity rules this out entirely.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Why this matters">
Once we know $A$ is semisimple, understanding all representations of $A$ reduces to two steps: (1) classify the irreps, and (2) count multiplicities. There are no exotic indecomposables to worry about.
</Callout>
