<SlideLayout title="Maschke's theorem — statement">

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Maschke's theorem">
Let $G$ be a finite group and $k$ an algebraically closed field whose characteristic does not divide $|G|$. Then $k[G]$ is semisimple.
</Callout>

In particular, over $\CC$ (characteristic 0), the <Term k="groupAlgebra">group algebra</Term> $\CC[G]$ is semisimple for every finite group $G$. So all representations of finite groups over $\CC$ are completely reducible.

The key hypothesis is that the characteristic of $k$ does not divide $|G|$. When it does, things break down — the averaging argument used in the proof requires dividing by $|G|$, which is impossible in characteristic dividing $|G|$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Characteristic matters">
Over a field of characteristic $p$ dividing $|G|$, the group algebra $k[G]$ need not be semisimple. This is the world of **modular representation theory**, which is significantly harder.
</Callout>

</SlideLayout>
