Let us verify the three key properties of $P = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g \in G} \rho(g^{-1}) \circ \pi \circ \rho(g)$.

<ProofReveal

  solution="For w ∈ W: since W is G-invariant, ρ(g)(w) ∈ W for each g. Since π is a projection onto W, π(ρ(g)(w)) = ρ(g)(w). Applying ρ(g⁻¹): ρ(g⁻¹)(π(ρ(g)(w))) = ρ(g⁻¹)(ρ(g)(w)) = w. Averaging |G| equal terms w: P(w) = (1/|G|) · |G| · w = w."
/>

<Callout kind="remark" title="Why G-equivariance holds">
For any $h \in G$ and $v \in V$: $\rho(h)(P(v)) = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_g \rho(h)\rho(g^{-1})\pi\rho(g)(v)$. Re-index by $g' = hg$ (a bijection on $G$): this equals $\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g'} \rho(g'^{-1})\pi\rho(g')(h \cdot v) = P(\rho(h)(v))$. So $P$ is $G$-equivariant.
</Callout>
