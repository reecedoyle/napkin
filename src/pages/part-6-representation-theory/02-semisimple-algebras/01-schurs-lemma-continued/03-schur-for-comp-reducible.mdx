<SlideLayout title="Schur's lemma for completely reducible representations">

The matrix-of-scalars picture extends to all <Term k="completelyReducible">completely reducible</Term> representations.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Schur's lemma for completely reducible representations">
Let $V = \bigoplus_i V_i^{\oplus n_i}$ and $W = \bigoplus_i V_i^{\oplus m_i}$ be finite-dimensional completely reducible representations, where each $V_i$ is an irrep. Then

$$\Homrep(V, W) \cong \bigoplus_i \Mat_{m_i \times n_i}(k).$$

An intertwining operator $T \colon V \to W$ amounts to, for each $i$, an $m_i \times n_i$ matrix of scalars giving the map $V_i^{\oplus n_i} \to V_i^{\oplus m_i}$.
</Callout>

The key point: maps between non-isomorphic irreps must be zero. So the full Hom-space breaks into independent blocks, one per isomorphism class of irrep. Different irrep types cannot "mix."

<Callout kind="corollary" title="Subrepresentations of completely reducible representations">
If $V = \bigoplus_i V_i^{\oplus n_i}$ is completely reducible and $W \subseteq V$ is a subrepresentation, then $W \cong \bigoplus_i V_i^{\oplus m_i}$ for some $m_i \le n_i$, and the inclusion is given by matrices on each block.
</Callout>
