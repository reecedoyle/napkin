<SlideLayout title="Hom-spaces between irreps">

Fix an algebraically closed field $k$ throughout. For representations $V$ and $W$ of an algebra $A$, write <Term k="Homrep">$\Homrep(V,W)$</Term> for the space of <Term k="intertwinOp">intertwining operators</Term> — the $A$-equivariant linear maps $T \colon V \to W$.

Schur's lemma (which we already proved) says: for finite-dimensional irreps $V$ and $W$,

$$
\Homrep(V,W) \cong
\begin{cases}
k & \text{if } V \cong W, \\
0 & \text{if } V \not\cong W.
\end{cases}
$$

So there is essentially one nonzero map between isomorphic irreps (up to scaling), and none at all between non-isomorphic ones.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Standing assumption">
Throughout this chapter $k$ is algebraically closed. This is what makes Schur's lemma give scalars rather than division algebras, and it is what drives everything that follows.
</Callout>
