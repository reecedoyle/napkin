Let $K = \QQ(\sqrt{5})$, $\mathcal{O}_K = \ZZ[\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}]$, fundamental unit $u = \frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$.

The powers of $u$ alternate between integers and half-integers:

| $n$ | $u^n$ | Norm |
|---|---|---|
| $-2$ | $\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2}$ | $1$ |
| $-1$ | $\frac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}$ | $-1$ |
| $0$ | $1$ | $1$ |
| $1$ | $\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$ | $-1$ |
| $2$ | $\frac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2}$ | $1$ |
| $3$ | $2 + \sqrt{5}$ | $-1$ |
| $4$ | $\frac{7+3\sqrt{5}}{2}$ | $1$ |
| $6$ | $9 + 4\sqrt{5}$ | $1$ |

The **integer** solutions to $a^2 - 5b^2 = \pm 1$ come from powers $u^n$ that land in $\ZZ[\sqrt{5}]$. The first is $u^3 = 2+\sqrt{5}$, giving $(a,b) = (2,1)$ with norm $-1$. Then $u^6 = 9+4\sqrt{5}$ gives $(9,4)$ with norm $+1$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Pattern">
Every third power of $u$ lands in $\ZZ[\sqrt{5}]$. The odd ones ($u^3, u^9, \dots$) solve $a^2 - 5b^2 = -1$; the even ones ($u^6, u^{12}, \dots$) solve $a^2 - 5b^2 = +1$.
</Callout>
