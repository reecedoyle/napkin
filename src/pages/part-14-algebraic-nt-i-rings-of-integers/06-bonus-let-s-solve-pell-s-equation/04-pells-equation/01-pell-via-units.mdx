<SlideLayout title="Pell's equation via units">

We have built all the machinery we need. Now the payoff.

<Term k="an58pellEquation">Pell's equation</Term> asks for all integer solutions to
$$a^2 - d \cdot b^2 = \pm 1.$$

But wait — this is just asking for elements $a + b\sqrt{d} \in \ZZ[\sqrt{d}]$ with <Term k="an58normNK">norm $\NK$</Term> equal to $\pm 1$. That is exactly the condition for being a unit in $\mathcal{O}_K$.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Pell's equation solved">
Let $d > 0$ be squarefree with $d \equiv 2$ or $3 \pmod{4}$, so $\mathcal{O}_K = \ZZ[\sqrt{d}]$. The integer solutions to $a^2 - db^2 = \pm 1$ are exactly the elements $\pm u^n$ for $n \in \ZZ$, where $u$ is the fundamental unit.
</Callout>

By Dirichlet's unit theorem, we know the full unit group is $\{\pm u^n : n \in \ZZ\}$. Every unit corresponds to a solution. There are **infinitely many** solutions, all generated by the single fundamental unit.
