<SlideLayout title="The big picture">

Let us step back and appreciate what we have done.

Pell's equation $a^2 - db^2 = 1$ was studied for centuries as a mysterious Diophantine puzzle. We now see it as a completely natural consequence of algebraic number theory:

1. **Reinterpret** the equation as asking for units in $\mathcal{O}_K = \ZZ[\sqrt{d}]$ (or its overring).
2. **Apply Dirichlet's unit theorem**: the unit group is $\cong \ZZ \times \{\pm 1\}$ for real quadratic fields.
3. **Conclude**: all solutions come from powers of the single <Term k="an58fundamentalUnit">fundamental unit</Term>.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Why this is satisfying">
We did not just find some solutions — we found ALL solutions, and proved they form a cyclic pattern generated by the smallest one. The algebraic structure explains everything at once.
</Callout>

This is a taste of how algebraic number theory "eradicates" classical problems by embedding them in a richer structure. The equation $a^2 - db^2 = \pm 1$ is not an isolated puzzle; it is the shadow of the unit group of a number field.
