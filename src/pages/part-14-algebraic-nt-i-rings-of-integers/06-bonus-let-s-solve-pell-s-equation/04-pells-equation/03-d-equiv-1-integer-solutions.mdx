<SlideLayout title="Integer solutions when d ≡ 1 (mod 4)">

When $d \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$, the fundamental unit $u$ lives in $\mathcal{O}_K = \ZZ[\frac{1+\sqrt{d}}{2}]$ but not necessarily in $\ZZ[\sqrt{d}]$. The integer solutions to <Term k="an58pellEquation">Pell's equation</Term> $a^2 - db^2 = \pm 1$ still form an infinite family, but they may only appear at every $k$th power of $u$ for some $k \geq 1$.

<Callout kind="example" title="d = 5 revisited">
The fundamental unit is $u = \frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}$. Integer solutions to $a^2 - 5b^2 = \pm 1$ first appear at $u^3 = 2+\sqrt5$, then every 3rd power thereafter. All solutions are:
$$\pm u^{3m} \quad (m \in \ZZ), \quad \text{plus sign changes } (-u^{3m}).$$
</Callout>

Despite the subtlety, the conclusion is the same: there are infinitely many integer solutions, and they are all generated by a single element. That is the power of Dirichlet's unit theorem.
