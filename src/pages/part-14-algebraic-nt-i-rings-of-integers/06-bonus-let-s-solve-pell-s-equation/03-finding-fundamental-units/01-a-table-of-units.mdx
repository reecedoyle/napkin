For real quadratic fields $\QQ(\sqrt{d})$ with $d \equiv 2$ or $3 \pmod{4}$, the <Term k="an58fundamentalUnit">fundamental unit</Term> is $x + y\sqrt{d}$ where $(x, y)$ is the smallest positive integer solution to $x^2 - dy^2 = \pm 1$.

Here are the fundamental units for small squarefree $d$:

| $d$ | Fundamental unit |
|-----|-----------------|
| $2$ | $1 + \sqrt{2}$ |
| $3$ | $2 + \sqrt{3}$ |
| $6$ | $5 + 2\sqrt{6}$ |
| $7$ | $8 + 3\sqrt{7}$ |
| $10$ | $3 + \sqrt{10}$ |
| $11$ | $10 + 3\sqrt{11}$ |

The case $d = 5$ is special: since $5 \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$, the ring of integers is $\ZZ[\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}]$, and the fundamental unit is $\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$ — the golden ratio!

In general, computing fundamental units is **hard**. But verifying a proposed fundamental unit is manageable.
