Now try $K = \QQ(\sqrt{3})$ with $\mathcal{O}_K = \ZZ[\sqrt{3}]$.

$K$ has two distinct real embeddings ($\sqrt{3} \mapsto +\sqrt{3}$ and $\sqrt{3} \mapsto -\sqrt{3}$), so signature $(r_1, r_2) = (2, 0)$:
$$s = 2 + 0 - 1 = 1.$$

<Term k="an58dirichletUnitTheorem">Dirichlet's theorem</Term> says $\mathcal{O}_K^\times \cong \ZZ^1 \times \{\pm 1\}$. There is exactly **one** <Term k="an58fundamentalUnit">fundamental unit</Term> $u$, and every unit is $\pm u^n$ for $n \in \ZZ$.

The fundamental unit of $\ZZ[\sqrt{3}]$ is $u = 2 + \sqrt{3}$:
- Norm: $\NK(2 + \sqrt{3}) = 2^2 - 3 \cdot 1^2 = 1$. ✓
- Its inverse is $2 - \sqrt{3} = u^{-1}$ (also in $\ZZ[\sqrt{3}]$). ✓

So the full unit group is:
$$\ZZ[\sqrt{3}]^\times = \bigl\{ \pm(2+\sqrt{3})^n \;\big|\; n \in \ZZ \bigr\}.$$
