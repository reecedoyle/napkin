<SlideLayout title="Infinitely many units from √2">

Consider $K = \QQ(\sqrt{2})$ with <Term k="an58ringOfIntegers">$\mathcal{O}_K = \ZZ[\sqrt{2}]$</Term>. The element $u = 3 - 2\sqrt{2}$ satisfies:
$$\NK(3 - 2\sqrt{2}) = 3^2 - 2 \cdot (2)^2 = 9 - 8 = 1.$$
So $u$ is a unit. Since it is not a root of unity (its powers grow without bound), the elements $u^n = (3 - 2\sqrt{2})^n$ give infinitely many distinct units.
