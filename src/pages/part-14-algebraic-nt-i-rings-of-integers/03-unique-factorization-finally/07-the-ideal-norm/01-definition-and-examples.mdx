<SlideLayout title="The ideal norm — definition and examples">

We need a notion of "size" for ideals, both to prove the factoring algorithm and to apply number-theoretic arguments.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Ideal norm">
The <Term k="an55idealNorm">**ideal norm**</Term> (or absolute norm) of a nonzero ideal $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \OO_K$ is
$$\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{a}) = |\OO_K / \mathfrak{a}|,$$
the number of elements in the quotient ring.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" title="Norm of (5) in ℤ[i]">
$\OO_K = \ZZ[i]$, $K = \QQ(i)$. The quotient $\ZZ[i]/(5)$ consists of residues $\{a + bi : a, b \in \Zc5\}$, so $\mathcal{N}((5)) = 25$.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" title="Norm of (2+i) in ℤ[i]">
Modulo $2+i$: we have $5 = (2+i)(2-i) \equiv 0$ and $i \equiv -2$. So $\ZZ[i]/(2+i) \cong \FF_5$, giving $\mathcal{N}((2+i)) = 5$.
(Since $(2+i)$ is prime, we get a field — as expected.)
</Callout>
