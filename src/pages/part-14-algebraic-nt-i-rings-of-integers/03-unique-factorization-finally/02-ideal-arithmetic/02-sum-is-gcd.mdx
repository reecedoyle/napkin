<SlideLayout title="Ideal sum is the GCD">

A crucial conceptual point: **ideal addition is not element addition**. In $\ZZ$:

$$
(4) + (6) = (4, 6) = (2)
$$

even though $4 + 6 = 10$. The ideal sum gives us the GCD, not the element sum.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Sum of ideals = GCD of generators">
In ℤ (or any PID), $(a) + (b) = (\gcd(a,b))$. Ideal sum is the genuine algebraic GCD.
Adding an element $(1) + (-1)$ doesn't make sense since $(1) = (-1)$ as ideals, so
ideal addition is the right notion, not element addition.
</Callout>

Because factorization involves **multiplying** ideals — not adding — the $\mathfrak{a} + \mathfrak{b}$ notation won't appear much in the main argument. But it is useful for computing GCDs and verifying that certain ideals coincide.
