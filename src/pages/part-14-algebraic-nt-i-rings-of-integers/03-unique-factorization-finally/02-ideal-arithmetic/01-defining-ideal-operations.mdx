<SlideLayout title="Defining ideal addition and multiplication">

To do factorization with ideals, we need to add and multiply them.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Ideal arithmetic">
Given ideals $\mathfrak{a}$ and $\mathfrak{b}$ of a ring $R$:

$$\mathfrak{a} + \mathfrak{b} = \{ a + b \mid a \in \mathfrak{a},\, b \in \mathfrak{b} \}$$

$$\mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathfrak{b} = \{ a_1 b_1 + \cdots + a_n b_n \mid a_i \in \mathfrak{a},\, b_i \in \mathfrak{b} \}.$$

We take **finite** sums in the product because infinite sums need not make sense in a general ring. Both $\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b}$ and $\mathfrak{a}\cdot\mathfrak{b}$ are ideals of $R$.
</Callout>

These definitions are more natural when stated in terms of generators.

<Callout kind="proposition" title="Ideal arithmetic via generators">
If $\mathfrak{a} = (a_1, \ldots, a_n)$ and $\mathfrak{b} = (b_1, \ldots, b_m)$, then:
- <Term k="an55idealSum">$\mathfrak{a} + \mathfrak{b}$</Term> is generated by $a_1, \ldots, a_n, b_1, \ldots, b_m$.
- <Term k="an55idealProduct">$\mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathfrak{b}$</Term> is generated by all products $a_i b_j$.
</Callout>

So: **sum = concatenate generators, product = take all pairwise products of generators**. For example, $(3) \cdot (5) = (15)$ in $\ZZ$, which matches ordinary multiplication of elements.

</SlideLayout>
