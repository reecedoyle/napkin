We need a notion of "one ideal divides another" to make sense of prime factorization.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Ideal divisibility">
We say $\mathfrak{a}$ **divides** $\mathfrak{b}$ and write <Term k="an55idealDivides">$\mathfrak{a} \mid \mathfrak{b}$</Term> if $\mathfrak{a} \supseteq \mathfrak{b}$.
</Callout>

The reversal of inclusion is counterintuitive at first, but it is consistent with elements: $(3) \mid (15)$ because $(15) \subseteq (3)$ — every multiple of 15 is a multiple of 3.

In $\ZZ[\sqrt{-5}]$, our ideal $\mathfrak{q}_1 = (3, 1-\sqrt{-5})$ divides both $(3)$ and $(1 - \sqrt{-5})$, because both are contained in $\mathfrak{q}_1$.

**Slogan:** "to contain is to divide." Bigger ideal = smaller divisor.
