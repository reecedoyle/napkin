We have arrived at the payoff.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Prime factorization works in Dedekind domains">
Let $\mathfrak{a}$ be a nonzero proper ideal of a Dedekind domain $\mathcal{A}$. Then $\mathfrak{a}$ factors as a finite product of nonzero prime ideals:
$$\mathfrak{a} = \mathfrak{p}_1^{e_1} \mathfrak{p}_2^{e_2} \cdots \mathfrak{p}_g^{e_g},$$
and this factorization is unique up to order of the $\mathfrak{p}_i$.

Moreover, $\mathfrak{a} \mid \mathfrak{b}$ if and only if every prime ideal exponent of $\mathfrak{a}$ is at most the corresponding exponent of $\mathfrak{b}$.
</Callout>

This is <Term k="an55idealFactorization">unique ideal factorization</Term> — the ideal-world analogue of the fundamental theorem of arithmetic.

The "divides = exponents dominated" clause mirrors the integer case: $m \mid n$ iff every prime power in $m$ also appears in $n$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="When the domain is a PID">
If $\mathcal{A}$ is also a PID (like $\ZZ$), then every ideal $(a)$ is principal, and the factorization of $(a)$ directly gives the factorization of $a$ into primes (up to units). So unique element factorization in $\ZZ$ is a corollary of unique ideal factorization.
</Callout>
