The key payoff of fractional ideals: we can invert ideals.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Fractional ideals form a group">
For any integral ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ of a Dedekind domain $\mathcal{A}$, define
$$\mathfrak{a}^{-1} = \{ x \in K \mid x\mathfrak{a} \subseteq \mathcal{A} \}.$$
Then $\mathfrak{a}^{-1}$ is a fractional ideal with $\mathfrak{a} \cdot \mathfrak{a}^{-1} = (1) = \mathcal{A}$.
</Callout>

The nonzero fractional ideals of $K$ form a group — the <Term k="an55idealGroup">ideal group $J_K$</Term> — under multiplication, with identity $(1) = \mathcal{A}$.

<Callout kind="example" title="Inverse of (3) in ℤ">
In $\ZZ$: $\mathfrak{a} = (3)$, so $\mathfrak{a}^{-1} = \{x \in \QQ : 3x \in \ZZ\} = \frac{1}{3}\ZZ$.
Check: $(3) \cdot \frac{1}{3}\ZZ = \ZZ = (1)$. ✓
</Callout>

Every fractional ideal factors uniquely as $J = \prod_i \kp_i^{n_i}$ for prime ideals $\kp_i$ and **integers** $n_i \in \ZZ$ (possibly negative). An integral ideal is exactly one where all $n_i \geq 0$ — perfect analogy with $\QQ$ vs $\ZZ$.
