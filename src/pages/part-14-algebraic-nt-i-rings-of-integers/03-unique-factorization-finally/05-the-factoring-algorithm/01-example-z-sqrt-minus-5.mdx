<SlideLayout title="Example — factoring in ℤ[√-5]">

Let $\OO_K = \ZZ[\sqrt{-5}]$. We claim:

$$
(6) = \mathfrak{p}^2 \mathfrak{q}_1 \mathfrak{q}_2
$$

where $\mathfrak{p} = (2, 1+\sqrt{-5})$, $\mathfrak{q}_1 = (1+\sqrt{-5}, 3)$, $\mathfrak{q}_2 = (1-\sqrt{-5}, 3)$.

Let us verify $\mathfrak{q}_1 \mathfrak{q}_2 = (3)$ directly:

$$
\mathfrak{q}_1 \mathfrak{q}_2 = \bigl((1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5}),\; 3(1+\sqrt{-5}),\; 3(1-\sqrt{-5}),\; 9\bigr).
$$

The generators simplify: $(1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5}) = 1 + 5 = 6$, and $9 - 6 = 3$. So the ideal contains $3$. Since $9 = 3 \cdot 3$ and $3(1 \pm \sqrt{-5}) = 3 \cdot (\ldots)$, all generators are multiples of $3$ once we include $3$ itself. Thus $\mathfrak{q}_1 \mathfrak{q}_2 = (3)$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Why is 𝔭 prime?">
We check $\OO_K/\mathfrak{p}$ is a field. Since $\OO_K \cong \ZZ[x]/(x^2+5)$, we get
$\OO_K/\mathfrak{p} \cong \FF_2[x]/(x^2+5, x+1) \cong \FF_2[x]/(x+1) \cong \FF_2$,
a field. So <Term k="an55primeIdealOK">$\mathfrak{p}$ is maximal (= prime)</Term>.
</Callout>

The element factorization $6 = 2 \cdot 3$ corresponds to $(6) = (2) \cdot (3) = \mathfrak{p}^2 \cdot \mathfrak{q}_1\mathfrak{q}_2$, and the other factorization $6 = (1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5})$ corresponds to $(6) = \mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q}_1 \cdot \mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q}_2$. Same ideal factorization, two element-level views of it.
