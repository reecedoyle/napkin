We now carve out the class of rings where ideal factorization works perfectly.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Dedekind domain">
An integral domain $\mathcal{A}$ is a <Term k="an55dedekindDomain">**Dedekind domain**</Term> if:
1. $\mathcal{A}$ is <Term k="noetherian">Noetherian</Term> (every ideal is finitely generated),
2. $\mathcal{A}$ is <Term k="an55integrallyClosed">integrally closed</Term>, and
3. every nonzero prime ideal of $\mathcal{A}$ is also a maximal ideal.
</Callout>

The third condition is the heart of the matter. In $\ZZ$: the nonzero prime ideals are $(p)$ for rational primes $p$, and these are also maximal (since $\ZZ/(p) \cong \FF_p$ is a field). So $\ZZ$ is a Dedekind domain.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Comparison with UFDs">
For elements: UFD $\iff$ prime elements = irreducible elements (plus Noetherian).
For ideals: Dedekind $\iff$ prime ideals = maximal ideals (plus Noetherian + integrally closed).
The analogy is exact.
</Callout>
