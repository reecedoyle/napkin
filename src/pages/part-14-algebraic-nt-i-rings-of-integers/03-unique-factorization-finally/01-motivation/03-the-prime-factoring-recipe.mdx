<SlideLayout title="The prime-factoring recipe (preview)">

Once we have ideal factorization, we can recover all representations of $n$ as $x^2 + 5y^2$ by factoring $n$ into prime ideals and then reading off element factorizations. The key step is factoring each rational prime $p \mid n$.

For $\OO_K = \ZZ[\sqrt{-5}]$ the recipe is:

- If $t^2 + 5$ factors as $(t+c)(t-c) \pmod{p}$, then $(p) = (p, c+\sqrt{-5})(p, c-\sqrt{-5})$.
- Otherwise, $(p)$ is already a <Term k="an55primeIdealOK">prime ideal</Term> in $\OO_K$.

This chapter develops the full theory for any <Term k="an55ringOfIntegers">ring of integers $\OO_K$</Term>.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Notation">
Throughout this chapter, lowercase Gothic letters $\mathfrak{a}, \mathfrak{b}, \mathfrak{p}, \mathfrak{q}$ denote ideals of $\OO_K$; capital $I, J$ denote fractional ideals.
</Callout>

The roadmap:
1. Define ideal addition and multiplication.
2. Define Dedekind domains — the class of rings where ideal factorization works.
3. Prove unique factorization of ideals.
4. Give the explicit factoring algorithm for rational primes.
5. Extend to fractional ideals and the ideal norm.
