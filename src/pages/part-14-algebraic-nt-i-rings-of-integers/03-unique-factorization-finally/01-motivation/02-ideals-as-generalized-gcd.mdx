<SlideLayout title="Ideals as generalized GCDs">

The fix: use <Term k="ideal">ideals</Term> as a "generalized GCD". In $\ZZ[\sqrt{-5}]$, write $(a, b)$ for the ideal $\{ax + by \mid x, y \in \ZZ[\sqrt{-5}]\}$.

- $(35, 77) = (7)$ — the ideal collapses to a principal ideal, revealing that $\gcd = 7$.
- $(3, 1+\sqrt{-5})$ does **not** collapse — it stays genuinely two-generator, because $3$ and $1+\sqrt{-5}$ have no common factor expressible as an element of $\ZZ[\sqrt{-5}]$.

The key insight: these non-principal ideals are the "missing primes". When we factor with ideals instead of elements:

$$
(6) = \mathfrak{p}^2 \mathfrak{q}_1 \mathfrak{q}_2
$$

where $\mathfrak{p} = (2, 1-\sqrt{-5})$, $\mathfrak{q}_1 = (1+\sqrt{-5}, 3)$, $\mathfrak{q}_2 = (1-\sqrt{-5}, 3)$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Unique factorization is restored">
The element $6$ has two factorizations into irreducible elements, but it has exactly one factorization into prime ideals. The element factorizations can then be recovered from the ideal factorization.
</Callout>
