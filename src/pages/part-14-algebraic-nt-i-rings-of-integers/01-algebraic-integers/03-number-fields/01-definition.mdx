<SlideLayout title="Number fields — definition">

We've been adjoining algebraic numbers to $\mathbb{Q}$ and getting bigger fields. Let's give this a name.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Number field">
A **number field** $K$ is a <Term k="field">field</Term> containing <Term k="QQ">$\mathbb{Q}$</Term> as a subfield that is finite-dimensional as a <Term k="vectorSpace">$\mathbb{Q}$-vector space</Term>. The <Term k="dimension">**degree**</Term> of $K$ is $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} K$, written $[K : \mathbb{Q}]$.
</Callout>

The prototype is $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}) = \{a + b\sqrt{2} \mid a, b \in \mathbb{Q}\}$.

- It contains $\mathbb{Q}$ (set $b = 0$).
- It's a field: $\frac{1}{3 - \sqrt{2}} = \frac{3 + \sqrt{2}}{7}$, so inverses stay inside $K$.
- As a $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space, a <Term k="basis">basis</Term> is $\{1, \sqrt{2}\}$, so $[K:\mathbb{Q}] = 2$.

<Callout kind="example" title="Non-examples">
$\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$ are fields containing $\mathbb{Q}$, but they are **not** number fields: they are infinite-dimensional over $\mathbb{Q}$ (no finite $\mathbb{Q}$-basis exists).
</Callout>
