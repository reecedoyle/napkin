<Problem

  difficulty="standard"
  prompt="[Brazil 2006] Let p be an irreducible polynomial in ℚ[x] of degree larger than 1. Prove that if p has two roots r and s whose product rs = 1, then the degree of p is even."
  hint="Note that p(x) is the minimal polynomial of r, but so is q(x) = x^(deg p) · p(1/x). Since the minimal polynomial is unique, q and p must be scalar multiples of each other."
  solution="Since p is irreducible and has r as a root, p is the minimal polynomial of r. Define q(x) = xⁿ · p(1/x) where n = deg p. Since p is monic with integer coefficients, q is also monic with integer coefficients, and q(r) = rⁿ · p(1/r) = rⁿ · p(s) = 0 (since rs = 1 means 1/r = s, and p(s) = 0). So q is a monic integer polynomial of degree n vanishing at r, hence q = p by uniqueness of the minimal polynomial. The equality q = p says the coefficients are palindromic (aₖ = aₙ₋ₖ). Pairing coefficients: if n were odd, the middle coefficient a_{n/2} would pair with itself but the degree is not an integer, contradiction. More directly: the roots of p are paired under r ↦ 1/r (since q = p). This pairing is a fixed-point-free involution on the roots, so the number of roots n must be even."
