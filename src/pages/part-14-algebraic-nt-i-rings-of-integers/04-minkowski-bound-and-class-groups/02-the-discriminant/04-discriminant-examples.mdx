<Callout kind="example" title="K = ℚ(√2)">
$\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]$ with basis $\{1, \sqrt{2}\}$ and embeddings $\sigma_1(\sqrt{2}) = \sqrt{2}$, $\sigma_2(\sqrt{2}) = -\sqrt{2}$. So

$$\Delta_K = \det \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ \sqrt{2} & -\sqrt{2} \end{bmatrix}^2 = (-2\sqrt{2})^2 = 8.$$
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" title="K = ℚ(i)">
$\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}[i]$ with basis $\{1, i\}$. The embeddings are the identity and complex conjugation, giving

$$\Delta_K = \det \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ i & -i \end{bmatrix}^2 = (-2i)^2 = -4.$$

Note that $\Delta_K$ can be negative for fields that "wander into $\mathbb{C}$".
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" title="K = ℚ(√5)">
Here $\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z} \oplus \frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\mathbb{Z}$. Applying the embeddings to basis $\{1, \frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\}$ gives $\Delta_K = (-\sqrt{5})^2 = 5$.
</Callout>

**Pattern for quadratic fields:** if $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ with $d$ squarefree, then

$$\Delta_K = \begin{cases} d & \text{if } d \equiv 1 \pmod{4} \\ 4d & \text{if } d \equiv 2, 3 \pmod{4}. \end{cases}$$
