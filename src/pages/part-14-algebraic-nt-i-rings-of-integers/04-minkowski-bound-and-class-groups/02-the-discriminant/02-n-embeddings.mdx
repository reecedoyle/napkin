The key structural fact we need:

<Callout kind="theorem" title="The n embeddings of a number field">
Let $K$ be a number field of degree $n$. There are exactly $n$ field homomorphisms $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_n \colon K \hookrightarrow \mathbb{C}$ that fix $\mathbb{Q}$. Moreover, for $\alpha \in K$, the values $\sigma_i(\alpha)$ as $i$ ranges from $1$ to $n$ are precisely the conjugates of $\alpha$.
</Callout>

This lets us express the trace and norm via embeddings:

$$\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha) = \sum_{i=1}^n \sigma_i(\alpha), \qquad \operatorname{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha) = \prod_{i=1}^n \sigma_i(\alpha).$$

These formulas make concrete why the trace and norm are rational (or even integer) when $\alpha \in \mathcal{O}_K$: the product/sum of all conjugates of an algebraic integer is always an integer.

</SlideLayout>
