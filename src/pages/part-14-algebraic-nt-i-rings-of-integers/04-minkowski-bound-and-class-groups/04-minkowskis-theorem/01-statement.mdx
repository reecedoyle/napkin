<SlideLayout title="Minkowski's theorem — statement">

Here is the beautiful geometric engine powering everything that follows.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Minkowski's theorem">
Let $S \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ be a **convex** set that is **centrally symmetric** (meaning $x \in S \Leftrightarrow -x \in S$). Let $L$ be a lattice in $\mathbb{R}^n$ with mesh $d$. If either

(a) the volume of $S$ exceeds $2^n d$, or

(b) the volume of $S$ equals $2^n d$ and $S$ is compact,

then $S$ contains a **nonzero** lattice point of $L$.
</Callout>

Note: any nonempty convex centrally symmetric set already contains $0 \in L$, since taking $x \in S$ and $-x \in S$ and averaging gives $0$. The theorem asks for a *nonzero* lattice point.

**Intuition:** if $S$ is "big enough" relative to the lattice spacing, it must overlap a lattice point other than the origin. The bound $2^n d$ is sharp — you need exactly that volume.
