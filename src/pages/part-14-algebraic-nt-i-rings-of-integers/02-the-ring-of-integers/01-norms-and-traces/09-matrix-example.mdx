Let's make the determinant definition concrete.

<Callout kind="example" title="Matrix of multiplication by a + b√2">
Let $K = \QQ(\sqrt{2})$ with <Term k="basis">basis</Term> $\{1, \sqrt{2}\}$. For $\alpha = a + b\sqrt{2}$:

$$\alpha \cdot (x + y\sqrt{2}) = (ax + 2yb) + (bx + ay)\sqrt{2}.$$

Writing this in the basis $\{1, \sqrt{2}\}$:
$$\mu_\alpha = \begin{bmatrix} a & 2b \\ b & a \end{bmatrix}.$$

Therefore:
$$\det \mu_\alpha = a^2 - 2b^2, \qquad \operatorname{Tr} \mu_\alpha = 2a.$$
</Callout>

This matrix approach explains why the norm formula $a^2 - 2b^2$ works even when $b = 0$: the <Term k="matrix">matrix</Term> $\mu_a = \begin{bmatrix} a & 0 \\ 0 & a \end{bmatrix}$ has determinant $a^2$ and trace $2a$, consistent with viewing $a \in \QQ$ as a degree-1 element of a degree-2 field with correction factor $k = 2$.
