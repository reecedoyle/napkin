<SlideLayout title="Irreducible polynomials have distinct roots">

Before we can count conjugates confidently, we need to know that the minimal polynomial never has a repeated root. This is a classical fact.

<Callout kind="lemma" title="No repeated complex roots">
An irreducible polynomial in $\QQ[x]$ cannot have a complex double root.
</Callout>

<ProofReveal
  client:load
  id="an54-s1-proof-distinct-roots"
  prompt="Why can't the minimal polynomial of an algebraic number have a repeated root?"
  solution="Suppose f(x) ∈ ℚ[x] is irreducible and has a double root α. Consider its derivative f′(x). The derivative has degree one less than f, and since α is a double root, α is also a root of f′. So f and f′ share the factor (x − α), meaning gcd(f, f′) is nonconstant. But gcd(f, f′) ∈ ℚ[x] by the Euclidean algorithm, and it has smaller degree than f while still dividing f — contradicting irreducibility of f."
/>

**Consequence:** $\alpha$ has **exactly** as many distinct Galois conjugates as its degree. So every algebraic number of degree $m$ has exactly $m$ conjugates.

</SlideLayout>
