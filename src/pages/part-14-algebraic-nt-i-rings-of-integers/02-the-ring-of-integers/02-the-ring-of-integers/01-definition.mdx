<SlideLayout title="The ring of integers of a number field">

Just as $\ZZ$ gives richer arithmetic than $\QQ$, focusing on the **algebraic integers** inside a number field $K$ gives much nicer structure than $K$ itself.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Ring of integers 𝒪_K">
Given a number field $K$, define
$$\OO_K \defeq K \cap \ol{\ZZ}$$
to be the **ring of integers** of $K$. Equivalently, $\OO_K$ consists of all elements of $K$ that are roots of some monic polynomial with integer coefficients.
</Callout>

The notation $\ol{\ZZ}$ means the ring of **all** algebraic integers (in $\CC$). We intersect with $K$ to stay inside our field.

**The analogy:**
| Number field $K$ | Rational numbers $\QQ$ |
|---|---|
| Ring of integers $\OO_K$ | Integers $\ZZ$ |

The prototype is $K = \QQ$ itself, where $\OO_K = \ZZ$. Every algebraic integer in $\QQ$ is an ordinary integer.

</SlideLayout>
