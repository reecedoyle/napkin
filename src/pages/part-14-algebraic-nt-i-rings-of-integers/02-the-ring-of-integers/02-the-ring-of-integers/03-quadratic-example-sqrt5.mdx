The case $K = \QQ(\sqrt{5})$ is more surprising.

<Callout kind="example" title="𝒪_ℚ(√5) = ℤ[(1+√5)/2]">
We claim
$$\OO_K = \ZZ\!\left[\tfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right] = \left\{m + n \cdot \tfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2} \;\middle|\; m, n \in \ZZ\right\}.$$

The number $\varphi = \frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$ (the golden ratio) satisfies $\varphi^2 - \varphi - 1 = 0$, a monic integer polynomial — so $\varphi \in \OO_K$.

The key difference: $5 \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$, so there are two classes of half-integer solutions. Working mod 4, if $\alpha = a + b\sqrt{5}$ with $2a \in \ZZ$ and $a^2 - 5b^2 \in \ZZ$, then either $a, b \in \ZZ$ or $a, b \in \ZZ + \tfrac{1}{2}$ (both half-integers).
</Callout>
