Let's work out $\OO_K$ for $K = \QQ(\sqrt{3})$.

<Callout kind="example" title="𝒪_ℚ(√3) = ℤ[√3]">
We claim $\OO_K = \ZZ[\sqrt{3}] = \{m + n\sqrt{3} \mid m, n \in \ZZ\}$.

Let $\alpha = a + b\sqrt{3}$ with $a, b \in \QQ$. When does $\alpha \in \OO_K$?

- If $b = 0$: the minimal polynomial is $x - a$, so $\alpha \in \OO_K$ iff $a \in \ZZ$.
- If $b \neq 0$: the minimal polynomial is $(x - a)^2 - 3b^2 = x^2 - 2ax + (a^2 - 3b^2)$. This has integer coefficients iff $2a \in \ZZ$ and $a^2 - 3b^2 \in \ZZ$.
</Callout>

From the conditions $2a \in \ZZ$ and $a^2 - 3b^2 \in \ZZ$, one can check (working mod 4) that when $3 \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$, the only solutions have $a, b \in \ZZ$. So $\OO_{\QQ(\sqrt{3})} = \ZZ[\sqrt{3}]$.

The condition "$d \equiv 2$ or $3 \pmod{4}$" is what makes the integers be the obvious $\ZZ[\sqrt{d}]$.

</SlideLayout>
