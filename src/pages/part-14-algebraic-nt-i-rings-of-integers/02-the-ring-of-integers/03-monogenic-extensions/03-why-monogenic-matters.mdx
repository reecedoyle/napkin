<SlideLayout title="Why monogenic extensions matter">

Monogenic extensions are the nicely-behaved case where a single algebraic integer generates everything.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Factoring primes is nicer in monogenic extensions">
When $\OO_K = \ZZ[\theta]$ for some $\theta$ with minimal polynomial $f(x) \in \ZZ[x]$, there is an elegant algorithm for factoring primes in $\OO_K$: to find how a prime $p$ splits, simply factor $f(x)$ modulo $p$. This is Dedekind's factoring theorem.

Without monogenicity, the factoring algorithm becomes more complicated.
</Callout>
