<SlideLayout title="Road map">

We will build up line bundles in four stages:

1. **Definition** — the formal data: projection map, open cover, bundle charts, and transition functions.
2. **Visualization** — what a line bundle looks like over $X = \mathbb{C}$ (flat) and over $X = \mathbb{C}_\infty$ (where the Möbius-strip twist appears).
3. **Morphisms** — maps that respect the bundle structure, giving us a notion of isomorphism.
4. **Relation to sheaves** — sections of a line bundle form the model for what sheaf theory calls "sections of an invertible sheaf." (We note the connection here; sheaves are developed later.)

<Callout kind="remark" title="Why work over a Riemann surface?">
We will take $X$ to be a <em>Riemann surface</em>. That means $X$ is locally like an open subset of $\mathbb{C}$, so "analytic" has a clear meaning. Everything here extends to higher-dimensional complex manifolds, but one complex dimension is enough to see all the key phenomena.
</Callout>
