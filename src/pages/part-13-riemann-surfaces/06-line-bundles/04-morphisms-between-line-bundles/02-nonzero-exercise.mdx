<SlideLayout title="Isomorphisms require nonzero scaling">

In a morphism $\alpha \colon L_1 \to L_2$, the local scaling function $f(p)$ can be zero at some points. But an **isomorphism** forces $f$ to be everywhere nonzero.

<Callout kind="exercise" title="Nonzero scaling for isomorphisms">
Let $\alpha \colon L_1 \to L_2$ be a line bundle morphism that is also an isomorphism (it has a two-sided inverse). Show that the scaling function $f$ above must be nonzero on all of $U_1 \cap U_2$.
</Callout>

<ProofReveal
  client:load
  id="rs52-morph-proof-nonzero"
  prompt="Prove that f(p) is nonzero for all p whenever α is an isomorphism."
  solution="Let β: L₂ → L₁ be the inverse of α. In the same charts, β acts as (s, p) ↦ (g(p)·s, p) for some analytic g. Since β ∘ α = id_{L₁}, we get g(p)·f(p) = 1 for all p in the overlap. Therefore f(p) is nonzero everywhere, and g(p) = 1/f(p). Conversely, if f is everywhere nonzero, the map (s, p) ↦ (s/f(p), p) gives a well-defined inverse morphism, so nonvanishing of f is also sufficient for α to be an isomorphism locally."
/>
