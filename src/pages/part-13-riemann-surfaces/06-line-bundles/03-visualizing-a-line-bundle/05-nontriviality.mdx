<SlideLayout title="Nontriviality and global sections">

We built a bundle over $\mathbb{C}_\infty$ whose real-part picture is a Möbius strip. It appears obviously <Term k="rs52nontrivialBundle">nontrivial</Term> — whatever "isomorphic" means, a Möbius strip and a cylinder are not the same.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Why nontriviality matters">
A line bundle is trivial if and only if it has a **nowhere-zero global analytic section**. For the trivial bundle $\mathbb{C} \times X$ the constant section $p \mapsto (1, p)$ is nowhere zero. On the Möbius bundle, any global real section must pass through zero — the bundle "cannot avoid" a zero.

Over the complex Riemann sphere, the story is the same: the transition function $(y, z) \mapsto (y/z, z)$ introduces a pole, and any global analytic section must have at least one zero.
</Callout>

Any 'correction' analytic function on ℂ \ {0} that is nonzero everywhere must be of the form cz^n for integer n (by the argument principle), and no such function simultaneously makes the transition function trivial on both the z-chart and the t-chart.
