<SlideLayout title="Checking intuition against the definition">

We have built up a visual picture of a line bundle:
- Flat graph paper over $X = \mathbb{C}$ — this is the <Term k="rs52trivialBundle">trivial bundle</Term>.
- Twisted graph paper over $X = \mathbb{C}_\infty$ — the Möbius bundle from the transition function $(y, z) \mapsto (y/z, z)$.

It is important to verify that the pictures actually match the formal definition, not just look plausible.

<Callout kind="question" title="Matching pictures to the definition">
The grid picture draws two families of lines over an open interval $U \subseteq X$: vertical lines (the fibers $\pi^{-1}(p)$) and horizontal lines (level sets of the chart coordinate in the $\mathbb{C}$-direction). How do these correspond to the formal data of a bundle chart $\phi \colon \pi^{-1}(U) \to \mathbb{C} \times U$?
</Callout>

<ProofReveal
  client:load
  id="rs52-viz-proof-grid-formal"
  prompt="Explain how the grid picture encodes the formal line bundle chart data."
  solution="A bundle chart φ: π⁻¹(U) → ℂ × U maps every point of L above U to a pair (fiber value, base point). The vertical grid lines in the picture are the preimages of the sets {c} × U under the base-point component — they mark constant base position. The horizontal grid lines are the preimages of ℂ × {c} — they mark constant fiber value. Together, the grid is literally the pullback of the coordinate grid on ℂ × U along φ. Compatibility between two charts means their grids overlay consistently on overlapping open sets, which is exactly the transition-function condition."
