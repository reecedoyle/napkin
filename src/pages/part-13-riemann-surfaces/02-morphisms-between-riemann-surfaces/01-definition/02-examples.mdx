Here are two basic examples.

<Callout kind="example" title="Two morphisms">
- The function $f \colon \CC \to \CC$ given by $f(z) = z^3$ is a <Term k="rs48morphism">morphism</Term>. It is holomorphic at every point of $\CC$ in the standard charts.

  Note that $f$ is not bijective: at each point $p \neq 0$ there is a neighborhood on which $f$ has an inverse, but at $0$ no inverse exists.

- The embedding $\CC \hookrightarrow \CC_\infty$ of the complex plane into the <Term k="rs48riemannSphere">Riemann sphere</Term> is a morphism. It sends every finite $z \in \CC$ to itself in $\CC_\infty = \CC \cup \{\infty\}$.
</Callout>
