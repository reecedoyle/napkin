<SlideLayout title="Why constant degree is surprising">

This proposition might seem obvious — after all, a polynomial of degree $k$ has $k$ roots counted with multiplicity. But it fails badly for smooth maps!

<Callout kind="example" title="Smooth maps can have variable fiber sizes">
Consider a smooth map $f \colon X \to Y$ between compact smooth real 1-manifolds (circles). We can draw such a map as a curve in a plane — and it is easy to construct examples where:

- Above one point, the fiber is empty (no preimages).
- Above another point, there is exactly one preimage.
- Above another point, there are two preimages.
- Above yet another point, infinitely many preimages (if $f$ is constant on an interval).

No such pathology is possible for holomorphic maps between compact Riemann surfaces.
</Callout>

The key reason it works in the holomorphic setting is the identity theorem: a nonconstant holomorphic map cannot be locally constant on any open set. This forces the fiber count to be locally constant, and then connectedness forces it to be globally constant.
