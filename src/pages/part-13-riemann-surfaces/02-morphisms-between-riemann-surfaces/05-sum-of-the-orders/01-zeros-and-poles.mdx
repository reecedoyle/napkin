<SlideLayout title="Zeros and poles of meromorphic functions on ℂ∞">

Let us look at some meromorphic functions extended to $\CC_\infty \to \CC_\infty$, and list their zeros and poles with multiplicity.

<Callout kind="example" title="Four meromorphic functions on ℂ∞">
| Function | Zeros | Poles |
|----------|-------|-------|
| $5$ (constant) | None | None |
| $(x+1)^2$ | $-1, -1$ | $\infty, \infty$ |
| $\dfrac{1}{x^2+1}$ | $\infty, \infty$ | $i, -i$ |
| $\dfrac{x+1}{x+2}$ | $-1$ | $-2$ |
</Callout>

Observe: in every case, the number of zeros equals the number of poles. The zeros and poles are counted with multiplicity — for instance, $(x+1)^2$ vanishes to order 2 at $-1$ and has a pole of order 2 at $\infty$ (since $(x+1)^2 \sim x^2$ for large $x$).

This pattern is not a coincidence.
