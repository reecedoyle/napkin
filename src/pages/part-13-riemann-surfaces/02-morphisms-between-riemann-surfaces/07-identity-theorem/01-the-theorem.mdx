<SlideLayout title="The identity theorem for Riemann surfaces">

In complex analysis on $\CC$, the identity theorem says that a holomorphic function is completely determined by its values on any convergent sequence — or equivalently, by its values on any open set. The same holds for holomorphic maps between Riemann surfaces.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Identity theorem">
Let $f, g \colon X \to Y$ be holomorphic maps between Riemann surfaces, with $X$ connected. If $f = g$ on a nonempty open subset of $X$, then $f = g$ everywhere on $X$.
</Callout>

The hypothesis that $X$ is connected is essential. The disjoint union of two copies of $\CC$ is a smooth 2-manifold, but it is not connected (and not a Riemann surface by the usual convention), so two maps that agree on one copy but differ on the other would be a counterexample without connectivity.

</SlideLayout>
