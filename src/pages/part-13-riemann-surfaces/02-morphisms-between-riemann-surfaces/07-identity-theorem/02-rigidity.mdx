The identity theorem captures a fundamental property of holomorphic maps: they are extraordinarily <Term k="rs48rigidity">rigid</Term>.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Rigidity of holomorphic maps">
Holomorphic maps are rigid — the value of a holomorphic map on any tiny open patch of the domain determines its value everywhere on the entire connected surface.
</Callout>

This is in stark contrast with smooth maps: we can easily construct a smooth function on $\RR$ that is $0$ on $(-\infty, 0]$ and positive on $(0, \infty)$ — the function is determined on $(-\infty, 0]$ but this does not constrain its behavior anywhere else.

For holomorphic maps, there is no such freedom. If you know $f$ on any open set, you know $f$ everywhere. This is why complex analysis, despite being "about functions of a complex variable," ends up with much stronger results than its real counterpart.
