<SlideLayout title="Defining multiplicity">

We want a notion of multiplicity that satisfies two axioms:

1. $z \mapsto z^m$ has multiplicity $m$ at $0$.
2. Multiplicity is invariant under analytic reparametrization of either the source or the target.

These two properties uniquely determine the multiplicity:

<Callout kind="proposition">
Let $f \colon X \to Y$ be a nonconstant holomorphic map, and let $p \in X$. There is a unique integer $m \geq 1$ such that: for every chart $\phi_2$ on $Y$ centered at $f(p)$ (meaning $\phi_2(f(p)) = 0$), there exists a chart $\phi_1$ on $X$ centered at $p$ such that the induced map

$$\phi_2 \circ f \circ \phi_1^{-1} \colon V_1 \to V_2$$

has the form $z \mapsto z^m$.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="remark" title="Proof sketch">
Expand $f$ in a Taylor series: the smallest nonzero term determines $m$. Then one can construct $\phi_1$ accordingly. The exponent $m$ is independent of the choice of chart on $Y$ because any two centered charts differ by a holomorphic reparametrization with nonzero derivative at $0$, which preserves the order of vanishing.
</Callout>

</SlideLayout>
