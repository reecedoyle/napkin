<SlideLayout title="ℂ∞ as ℂ plus a point at infinity">

The <Term k="rs48riemannSphere">Riemann sphere</Term> $\CC_\infty$ can be identified with $\CC$ together with a single extra point called $\infty$.

Concretely: use stereographic projection to identify $\CC_\infty \setminus \{N\}$ (the sphere minus the north pole) with $\CC$. Under this identification, the north pole $N$ becomes the point $\infty$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Why call it ∞?">
This name makes sense topologically: every sequence of complex numbers $z_i$ with $|z_i| \to +\infty$ converges to the point $\infty$ on $\CC_\infty$.

In other words, numbers that "go off to infinity" in $\CC$ actually converge to a specific point on $\CC_\infty$ — the one we've added to compactify the plane.
</Callout>

This compact picture turns out to be exactly what we need to handle meromorphic functions without singularities.
