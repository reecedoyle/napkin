<SlideLayout title="Smooth structure vs topological structure">

Consider the open interval $(0, 2) \subseteq \RR$ as a topological manifold. We can define two homeomorphisms from $(0,2)$ to open subsets of $\RR$:

$$
\phi_1(x) = x, \qquad \phi_2(x) = \begin{cases} x & x \leq 1 \\ 2x - 1 & x > 1 \end{cases}
$$

Both $\phi_1$ and $\phi_2$ are homeomorphisms — as maps of topological spaces they are equally good. But $\phi_2$ has a **corner** at $x = 1$, so the transition map $\phi_2 \circ \phi_1^{-1}$ is *not* smooth there. A homeomorphism need not be a diffeomorphism: the two charts are topologically interchangeable but not smoothly compatible.

<Callout kind="remark" title="The subtlety">
A topological space does not come with a unique smooth structure. Two different atlases can give the **same** topological manifold **different** smooth structures. The smooth structure is extra data — it is encoded in the choice of atlas, not in the topology alone.
</Callout>

The definition of a smooth manifold bakes in smooth structure by requiring all transition maps to be smooth. This way, a single atlas determines the structure consistently.

For Riemann surfaces, we replace "smooth" with "analytic" throughout.

</SlideLayout>
