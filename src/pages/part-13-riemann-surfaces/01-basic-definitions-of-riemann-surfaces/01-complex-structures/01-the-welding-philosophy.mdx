<SlideLayout title="The welding philosophy">

Riemann surface theory is complex analysis generalized with ideas from differential geometry. The key image is **welding**: we take pieces of the complex plane $\CC$ and glue them together analytically to form a more complicated space.

You already know two instances of this philosophy:

- A **topological manifold** is patches of $\RR^n$ welded by homeomorphisms.
- A **smooth manifold** is patches of $\RR^n$ welded by smooth maps (diffeomorphisms).

<Callout kind="remark" title="The moral">
We take countably many patches $\{U_i\}$ and weld them together while preserving the underlying structure. A topological manifold preserves topological structure; a smooth manifold preserves smooth structure; a Riemann surface preserves **complex-analytic structure**.
</Callout>

Each layer of structure makes the transition maps — the maps that say how patches overlap — more constrained. For Riemann surfaces, the transition maps must be **analytic functions** of a complex variable.
