<SlideLayout title="Genus and topology">

As an orientable real $2$-manifold, every Riemann surface has a <Term k="rs47genus">**genus**</Term> — a purely topological invariant that counts the number of "handles" on the surface. A sphere has genus $0$; a torus has genus $1$; a pretzel surface has genus $2$.

The remarkable fact is that the genus is **deeply connected to algebraic invariants**. You may have encountered elliptic curves in cryptography — they are Riemann surfaces of genus $1$. More generally, <Term k="rs47hyperellipticCurve">hyperelliptic curves</Term> form a family of Riemann surfaces of arbitrary genus $\geq 2$.

<Callout kind="remark">
Genus is a topological concept, yet it controls things like: how many independent holomorphic differential forms exist on the surface (the Riemann–Roch theorem), and which algebraic curves can be parametrized by which surfaces. Topology and algebra are intertwined in a way that has no analogue on the real line.
</Callout>
