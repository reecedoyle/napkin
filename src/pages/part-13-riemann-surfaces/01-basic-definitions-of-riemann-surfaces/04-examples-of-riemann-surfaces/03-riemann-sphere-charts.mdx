<SlideLayout title="The Riemann sphere — two charts">

We define two <Term k="rs47complexChart">complex charts</Term> on $\CC_\infty$ by stereographic projection:

$$
\phi_1 \colon \CC_\infty \setminus \{N\} \to E_1 \cong \CC
$$
projects each point of the sphere (except the north pole $N$) to the plane $E_1$ through $N$.

$$
\phi_2 \colon \CC_\infty \setminus \{S\} \to E_2 \cong \CC
$$
projects each point of the sphere (except the south pole $S$) to the plane $E_2$ through $S$.

These two charts **cover** the whole sphere:
- $\phi_1$ covers everything except $N$ (corresponding to $\infty$).
- $\phi_2$ covers everything except $S$ (corresponding to $0$ in $\phi_1$'s coordinates).

<Callout kind="remark">
The chart $\phi_1$ is the standard identification of $\CC$ sitting inside $\CC_\infty$: the complex number $z \in \CC$ corresponds to the point of the sphere with $\phi_1$-coordinate $z$. The north pole $N$ is the extra point $\infty$.

The chart $\phi_2$ covers the neighbourhood of $\infty$ — the region you need when studying the behaviour of functions "at infinity".
</Callout>

The local coordinate $z = \phi_1(p)$ is centered at $S$ (the south pole maps to $0$). The local coordinate $t = \phi_2(p)$ is centered at $N$.

</SlideLayout>
