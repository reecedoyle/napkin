<SlideLayout title="Why analytic transition maps?">

The requirement that transition maps be analytic is what distinguishes a Riemann surface from a smooth real $2$-manifold. But why analytic rather than just smooth?

In complex analysis, **holomorphic = analytic**: every holomorphic function equals its Taylor series locally. So asking for analytic transition maps and asking for holomorphic transition maps are the same thing.

<Callout kind="remark">
On $\RR$, smooth $\ne$ analytic: the function $e^{-1/x^2}$ (extended by $0$ at $x=0$) is smooth but not analytic at $0$. So the complex world is fundamentally more rigid — and this rigidity is what makes the global theorems about Riemann surfaces so powerful.
</Callout>
