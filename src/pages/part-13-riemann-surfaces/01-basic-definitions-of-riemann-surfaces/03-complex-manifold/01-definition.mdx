<SlideLayout title="Complex n-manifold — definition">

A Riemann surface is the $n=1$ case of a broader family. We can define complex manifolds of any dimension by replacing $\CC$ with $\CC^n$.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Complex n-manifold">
A <Term k="rs47complexManifold">**complex $n$-manifold**</Term> is a Hausdorff space with a countable open cover $\{U_i\}$ and homeomorphisms
$$
\phi_i \colon U_i \xrightarrow{\cong} E_i \subseteq \CC^n
$$
(each $E_i$ open in $\CC^n$) such that all transition maps $\phi_j \circ \phi_i^{-1}$ are analytic functions $\CC^n \to \CC^n$.
</Callout>

By analytic here we mean each component of the transition map is holomorphic in all $n$ complex variables — the right notion of analyticity in higher complex dimensions.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Dimension counting">
A complex $n$-manifold is naturally a smooth **real** $2n$-manifold, because $\CC^n \cong \RR^{2n}$ as a real vector space. Each complex direction contributes two real dimensions. So a Riemann surface (complex $1$-manifold) is a smooth real $2$-manifold, i.e. a smooth surface.
</Callout>

The extra structure on top of a smooth real $2n$-manifold — the analytic transition maps — is exactly what the complex structure adds.

</SlideLayout>
