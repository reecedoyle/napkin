<SlideLayout title="The rigidity of complex manifolds">

A complex $n$-manifold is a smooth real $2n$-manifold, but with much more structure. The analytic transition maps make complex manifolds far more rigid than their smooth counterparts.

One way to feel this: in smooth geometry, you can deform the metric freely; in complex geometry, the complex structure imposes constraints that prevent many deformations.

<Callout kind="remark" title="An analogy">
Compare $\RR^n$ (smooth geometry) and $\CC^n$ (complex geometry). Both have the same underlying topology, but $\CC^n$ has Cauchy–Riemann equations constraining its functions. A holomorphic function on $\CC^n$ determined by its values on a tiny open set is determined everywhere — there is no "bump function" trick that works for holomorphic functions.
</Callout>

This rigidity is a feature, not a bug. It means that global objects on complex manifolds — holomorphic functions, meromorphic functions, line bundles — are far more constrained and therefore far more classifiable.
