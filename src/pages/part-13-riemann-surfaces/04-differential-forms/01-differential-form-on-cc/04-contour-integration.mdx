<SlideLayout title="1-forms and contour integration">

The motivation for introducing $1$-forms on $\CC$ is to generalize <Term k="rs50contourIntegral">contour integration</Term>.

In complex analysis, the contour integral $\int_c f(z)\,dz$ integrates the function $f$ along a curve $c$. But what if we want to integrate something more general — not just $f(z)\,dz$, but an arbitrary $1$-form $\omega = f(z)\,d\Re + g(z)\,d\Im$?

<Callout kind="definition" title="Integration of a 1-form over a curve">
Let $c : [0, 1] \to U$ be a smooth curve and $\omega = f(z)\,d\Re + g(z)\,d\Im$ a $1$-form on $U$. The integral of $\omega$ along $c$ is:
$$\int_c \omega = \int_0^1 \bigl[f(c(t)) \cdot \Re(c'(t)) + g(c(t)) \cdot \Im(c'(t))\bigr]\,dt.$$
</Callout>

This recovers the familiar contour integral: if $\omega = f(z)\,dz = f(z)\,d\Re + if(z)\,d\Im$, then
$$\int_c \omega = \int_0^1 f(c(t))\bigl[\Re(c'(t)) + i\,\Im(c'(t))\bigr]\,dt = \int_0^1 f(c(t))\,c'(t)\,dt,$$
which is exactly $\int_c f(z)\,dz$.
