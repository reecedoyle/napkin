<SlideLayout title="The 1-form dz">

Why do we care about complex coefficients at all? Because we want a clean way to write <Term k="rs50dz">$dz$</Term>, the $1$-form that records the infinitesimal change in $z$ itself.

Let $z : \CC \to \CC$ denote the identity function $z \mapsto z$. Its total differential measures how $z$ changes as we move in a tangent direction $v$:

$$dz \cdot v = v.$$

Let's compute $dz$ in terms of $d\Re$ and $d\Im$. If $v = a + ib$ is a tangent vector, then:
$$\Re(v) = a, \qquad \Im(v) = b.$$

So $dz \cdot v = a + ib = d\Re \cdot v + i \cdot d\Im \cdot v$, which gives us:

<Callout kind="definition" title="The standard 1-form dz">
$$dz = d\Re + i\,d\Im.$$
More generally, for a holomorphic or smooth function $h$, the $1$-form $dh$ is defined by the rule
$dh \cdot v = $ (the directional derivative of $h$ in direction $v$).
</Callout>

The companion form is $d\ol{z} = d\Re - i\,d\Im$, which records the change in $\ol{z}$.
