Allowing $f$ and $g$ to be complex-valued might seem like a major change. In fact, it is mostly cosmetic.

<Callout kind="proposition" title="Projection principle">
A complex-valued $1$-form $\omega = f(z)\,d\Re + g(z)\,d\Im$ is equivalent to a **pair** of real-valued $1$-forms: its real part $\Re\,\omega$ and its imaginary part $\Im\,\omega$.
</Callout>

Concretely, write $f = f_1 + if_2$ and $g = g_1 + ig_2$ with $f_1, f_2, g_1, g_2 : U \to \RR$. Then:
$$\omega = (f_1\,d\Re + g_1\,d\Im) + i(f_2\,d\Re + g_2\,d\Im) = \Re\,\omega + i\cdot \Im\,\omega.$$

So a complex $1$-form is just two real $1$-forms glued together. Any result that holds for a pair of real $1$-forms extends to complex $1$-forms by applying it to each component.
