<SlideLayout title="Example — the 1-form dz">

The simplest example is <Term k="rs50dz">$dz$</Term>, the form that records the total displacement in $z$.

Recall $dz \cdot v = v$. So:
- Moving right by $1$: value $= 1$
- Moving up by $1$ (direction $i$): value $= i$
- Moving left by $1$: value $= -1$
- Moving down by $1$: value $= -i$

The quiver diagram for $dz$ looks the **same at every point** — it is translation-invariant. The rightward arrows are labeled $1$ and the upward arrows $i$.

<Callout kind="example" title="Integrating dz along a curve">
Suppose $c$ moves $2$ units right, then $1$ unit up. Adding the contributions:
$$\int_c dz = 2 \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot i = 2 + i.$$
This equals $c(\text{end}) - c(\text{start})$, which is exactly the total change in $z$ — confirming that $\int_c dz$ is path-independent on $\CC$.
</Callout>
