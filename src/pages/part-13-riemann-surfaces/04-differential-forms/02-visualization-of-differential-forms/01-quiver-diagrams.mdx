<SlideLayout title="Visualizing 1-forms — quiver diagrams">

A $1$-form $\omega$ on $\CC$ assigns, to each point $p$ and each tangent direction $v$, a complex number $\omega_p(v)$. How do we draw this?

The natural visualization is a <Term k="rs50quiverDiagram">quiver diagram</Term>: at a grid of sample points, draw short arrows in several directions and label each arrow with the value $\omega_p(v)$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="How to read a quiver diagram">
The label on an arrow at point $p$ pointing in direction $v$ tells you:
**"If I move along a tiny piece of curve in direction $v$ near $p$, the contribution to $\int_c \omega$ is approximately this value."**

To integrate $\omega$ along a curve $c$, mentally "add up" the labels on arrows that match the direction of travel.
</Callout>

We will look at four key examples — two holomorphic and two non-holomorphic — to build intuition for what differentiates them.
