<SlideLayout title="Why Riemann-Roch matters">

The Riemann-Roch theorem is one of the central results in the whole of complex geometry and algebraic geometry. Here is why it is so powerful.

<Callout kind="remark" title="The three reasons">
**1. It counts.** On any compact Riemann surface, it gives the exact dimension of any $L(D)$ space — solving a hard analytic problem by algebra.

**2. It generalises.** The same statement, almost word-for-word, holds for algebraic curves over any field (not just $\CC$), and there are versions for higher-dimensional varieties. You can even define the genus of a number field like $\QQ[\sqrt{2}]$.

**3. It classifies.** On an elliptic curve ($g = 1$), Riemann-Roch shows that $L(3 \cdot p)$ has dimension exactly $3$ for any point $p$. Three independent functions on a torus — and with the right normalization they satisfy a cubic relation. That relation is the Weierstrass equation of the elliptic curve.
</Callout>

The theorem connects analysis (spaces of functions), algebra (polynomial equations), and topology (the genus). That triple bridge is what makes it a cornerstone of modern mathematics.
