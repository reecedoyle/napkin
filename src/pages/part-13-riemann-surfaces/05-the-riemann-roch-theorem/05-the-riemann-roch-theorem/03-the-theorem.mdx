<SlideLayout title="The Riemann-Roch theorem">

We have all the pieces. Here is the theorem.

<Callout kind="theorem" title="Riemann-Roch">
Let $X$ be a compact algebraic curve (compact Riemann surface) of genus $g$, let $K$ be a <Term k="rs51canonicalDiv">canonical divisor</Term>, and let $D$ be any divisor on $X$. Then

$$
\dim L(D) - \dim L(K - D) = \deg(D) + 1 - g.
$$
</Callout>

The left side is a difference of two dimensions of spaces of meromorphic functions. The right side depends only on:

- $\deg D$ — a single integer you compute by adding up the coefficients of $D$;
- the genus $g$ — a topological invariant of $X$.

So Riemann-Roch converts a hard analytic question ("how many independent meromorphic functions satisfy these pole/zero conditions?") into an almost purely algebraic computation.
