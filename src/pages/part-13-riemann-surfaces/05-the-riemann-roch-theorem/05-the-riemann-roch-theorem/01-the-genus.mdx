Before stating the theorem we need one more ingredient: the **genus** of the surface.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Genus">
The <Term k="rs51genus">genus</Term> $g$ of a compact Riemann surface $X$ is its topological "number of holes." More precisely, as a topological surface, $X$ is homeomorphic to a sphere with $g$ handles attached.
</Callout>

| Surface | Genus $g$ |
|---|---|
| Riemann sphere $\CC_\infty$ | $0$ |
| Torus (complex elliptic curve) | $1$ |
| Double torus | $2$ |

The genus is the single number that governs how the dimension $\dim L(D)$ deviates from what you would naively expect. On the sphere ($g = 0$), the Riemann-Roch formula reduces to a clean formula; on higher-genus surfaces, there is a correction that grows with $g$.
