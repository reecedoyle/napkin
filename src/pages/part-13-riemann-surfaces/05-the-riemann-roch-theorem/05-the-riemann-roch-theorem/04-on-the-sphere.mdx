<SlideLayout title="Riemann-Roch on the sphere">

Let us verify the theorem on the simplest case: $X = \CC_\infty$, genus $g = 0$, canonical divisor $K = (-2) \cdot \infty$.

<Callout kind="example" title="D = 4·∞ on the Riemann sphere">
Take $D = 4 \cdot \infty$. Then:

- $\deg D = 4$.
- $K - D = (-2) \cdot \infty - 4 \cdot \infty = (-6) \cdot \infty$.
- $\deg(K - D) = -6 < 0$, so $L(K - D) = \{0\}$ and $\dim L(K - D) = 0$.

Riemann-Roch says:

$$
\dim L(D) - 0 = 4 + 1 - 0 = 5.
$$

So $\dim L(4 \cdot \infty) = 5$, confirming our earlier calculation that $L(4 \cdot \infty)$ is spanned by $\{1, z, z^2, z^3, z^4\}$.
</Callout>
