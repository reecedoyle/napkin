<SlideLayout title="The divisor of a meromorphic function">

We now connect divisors back to functions. Given any meromorphic function $f$ on a Riemann surface, its <Term k="rs51principalDiv">divisor $\Div(f)$</Term> records all its zeros and poles.

<Callout kind="definition" title="Divisor of a meromorphic function">
Let $f$ be meromorphic on a Riemann surface $X$. The divisor of $f$ is the function

$$
\Div(f)(p) = \ord_p(f)
$$

for each $p \in X$. Written as a formal sum: $\Div(f) = \sum_p \ord_p(f) \cdot p$.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="example" title="Prototype">
For $f(z) = \dfrac{(z-3)^2}{z - i}$ on $\CC_\infty$:

- Zero of order $2$ at $z = 3$: contributes $+2$.
- Pole of order $1$ at $z = i$: contributes $-1$.
- Pole of order $1$ at $z = \infty$ (since degree of numerator exceeds denominator by $1$): contributes $-1$.

$$
\Div(f) = 2 \cdot 3 + (-1) \cdot i + (-1) \cdot \infty.
$$
</Callout>

Notice: $\deg \Div(f) = 2 - 1 - 1 = 0$. This is not a coincidence.

</SlideLayout>
