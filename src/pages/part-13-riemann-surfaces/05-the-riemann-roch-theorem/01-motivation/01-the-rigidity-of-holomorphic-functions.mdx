<SlideLayout title="The rigidity of holomorphic functions">

Holomorphic functions on ℂ are already remarkably rigid: a function $\CC \to \CC$ is uniquely determined by its Taylor series at the origin. Knowing the function in a tiny neighborhood pins it down everywhere. Yet specifying it still requires infinitely many coefficients $c_0, c_1, c_2, \ldots$.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Liouville's theorem (Riemann sphere form)">
A holomorphic function <Term k="rs51riemannSphere">$\CC_\infty \to \CC$</Term> is constant. In other words, the $\CC$-vector space of such functions has dimension $1$ — you need just a single complex number to specify it completely.
</Callout>

So we have two extremes:

- **Holomorphic $\CC \to \CC$:** infinite-dimensional space of functions (one per power series).
- **Holomorphic $\CC_\infty \to \CC$:** one-dimensional space (only constants).

The Riemann-Roch theorem is the tool that interpolates between these two extremes.

</SlideLayout>
