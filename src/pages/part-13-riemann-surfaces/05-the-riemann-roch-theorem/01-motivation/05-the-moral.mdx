<SlideLayout title="The moral — interpolating between extremes">

We've seen two extremes and one middle:

| Space | Functions | Dimension |
|---|---|---|
| Holomorphic $\CC \to \CC$ | All power series | ∞ |
| $L(n \cdot \infty)$ on $\CC_\infty$ | Polynomials of degree $\leq n$ | $n + 1$ |
| Holomorphic $\CC_\infty \to \CC$ | Constants only | $1$ |

The $L(D)$ construction smoothly interpolates. By varying the divisor $D$ — which points we allow poles at, and how large those poles can be — we get every finite-dimensional space in between.

<Callout kind="remark" title="The central moral">
By allowing poles of bounded order at specified points, we produce finite-dimensional spaces of meromorphic functions. The Riemann-Roch theorem tells us the **exact dimension** of each such space in terms of the geometry of the surface.
</Callout>

In the next sections we make the notation precise by defining **divisors** — the bookkeeping device that tracks where poles and zeros are allowed, and by how much.
