Since <Term k="rs51divisor">divisors</Term> are integer-valued functions, we add them and scale them pointwise, just like vectors.

<Callout kind="example" title="Adding two divisors">
On $\CC_\infty$, let $D_1 = 2 \cdot 0 + (-1) \cdot \infty$ and $D_2 = (-3) \cdot i + 4 \cdot \infty$.

Their sum is:

$$
D_1 + D_2 = 2 \cdot 0 + (-3) \cdot i + (-1 + 4) \cdot \infty = 2 \cdot 0 + (-3) \cdot i + 3 \cdot \infty.
$$
</Callout>

The <Term k="rs51divisorGroup">group of divisors $\Div(X)$</Term> is isomorphic to the free abelian group generated by the points of $X$ — every divisor decomposes uniquely as a finite integer linear combination of points.
