<Callout kind="definition" title="Smooth projective plane curve">
A <Term k="rs49projectiveCurve">**smooth projective plane curve**</Term> is the zero set
$$X = \{[x:y:z] \in \mathbb{CP}^2 : g(x,y,z) = 0\}$$
where $g$ is a homogeneous polynomial, $X$ is connected, and $X$ is smooth: at every point of $X$, at least one of $\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}$, $\frac{\partial g}{\partial y}$, $\frac{\partial g}{\partial z}$ is nonzero.
</Callout>

**Complex structure.** The intersection $X \cap U_i$ with each affine chart is a smooth affine plane curve (in the $\mathbb{C}^2$ image of $U_i$). The complex charts on those affine pieces assemble into a complex structure on $X$.

**Why compact?** $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is compact (it is a continuous quotient of the compact sphere $S^5 \subset \mathbb{R}^{12}$), and $X$ is a closed subset of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ (the zero set of a continuous function). Closed subsets of compact spaces are compact.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Affine vs projective">
An affine curve $\{f(z,w) = 0\} \subseteq \mathbb{C}^2$ and its projectivisation $\{g(x,y,z) = 0\} \subseteq \mathbb{CP}^2$ are related by: the affine curve is the intersection of the projective curve with the chart $U_2 \cong \mathbb{C}^2$. The projective curve adds "points at infinity."
</Callout>
