We want to give $\mathbb{CP}^1$ the structure of a Riemann surface. The textbook definition uses two charts:

<Callout kind="definition" title="Complex structure of ℂℙ¹">
Let $U_1 = \{[x:y] \in \mathbb{CP}^1 : x \neq 0\}$ and $U_2 = \{[x:y] : y \neq 0\}$. Define:
$$\phi_1 \colon U_1 \to \mathbb{C}, \quad \phi_1([x:y]) = y/x$$
$$\phi_2 \colon U_2 \to \mathbb{C}, \quad \phi_2([x:y]) = x/y$$
These are well-defined (the ratio is unchanged by scaling) and together cover $\mathbb{CP}^1$.
</Callout>

The transition map on $U_1 \cap U_2$ (where both $x,y \neq 0$) is:
$$\phi_2 \circ \phi_1^{-1}(w) = 1/w$$
This is holomorphic on $\mathbb{C}^{\times}$, so the charts are compatible.

**Where do these charts come from?** They are exactly the affine curves $x = 1$ and $y = 1$ embedded in $\mathbb{CP}^1$. The chart $\phi_1$ records $y/x$, which is the $y$-coordinate on the affine line $x = 1$. This is not the only choice — you could use $x + y = 1$ and $x - y = 1$ just as well.
