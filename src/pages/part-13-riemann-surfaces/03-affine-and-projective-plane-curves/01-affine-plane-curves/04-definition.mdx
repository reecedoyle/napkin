<SlideLayout title="Affine plane curves — definition">

Two technical conditions prevent degenerate cases: singular points (handled by the smoothness condition) and disconnected zero sets (which would give multiple separate Riemann surfaces rather than one).

<Callout kind="definition" title="Smooth affine plane curve">
Given $f(z,w) \in \mathbb{C}[z,w]$, let $X = \{(z,w) \in \mathbb{C}^2 : f(z,w) = 0\}$. Suppose:
1. $X$ is **connected**.
2. For every $(z,w) \in X$: $\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}(z,w) \neq 0$ or $\frac{\partial f}{\partial w}(z,w) \neq 0$.

Then $X$ is a Riemann surface, called a <Term k="rs49affineCurve">**smooth affine plane curve**</Term>. Complex charts: where $\partial f/\partial z \neq 0$ use $\phi = w$; where $\partial f/\partial w \neq 0$ use $\phi = z$.
</Callout>

We call it **affine** because $\mathbb{C}^2$ is "flat" — the affine plane. Later we will work in the projective plane $\mathbb{CP}^2$ to gain compactness.
