<SlideLayout title="Nodes and genus">

Nodes matter in algebraic geometry partly because they are the simplest singularities to handle — and because they appear naturally in families of curves.

<Callout kind="remark" title="Nodal curves and their normalisation">
Given a projective curve $X$ with only <Term k="rs49node">nodes</Term> as singularities, we can form its **normalisation** $\tilde{X}$: pull each node apart into two distinct smooth points. The result $\tilde{X}$ is a smooth compact Riemann surface.

If $X$ has degree $d$ in $\mathbb{CP}^2$ and $\delta$ nodes, then the genus of $\tilde{X}$ is:
$$g(\tilde{X}) = \frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2} - \delta$$
</Callout>

The formula $(d-1)(d-2)/2$ is the maximum genus of a smooth degree-$d$ curve (achieved when there are no singularities). Each node reduces the genus by 1.
